# Regards

Regards (first called Seen Back, the name this repository and its address still carry) is a web app in which you photograph something alive and your camera view changes toward what is known of how that creature sees: its field of view, where its sight is sharp, the shape of its pupil, its colours and the pace at which it takes in movement. A recognizer inside the page names the kind of eye and, when it is sure enough, the species, from the 5,911 species recorded in Alachua County, Florida, through 2024. Every meeting is kept in a record in the browser (`src/cam.r.js` keeps up to 300), and eyes you have met can be looked through again for a week after the meeting.

The app is served at https://jampotful.github.io/seen-back/ . The page there is build 16, which carries the owner's edits of the wording, the type and the layout of About (`docs/LOG.md`, 16:20 and 17:15); it calls the larger model "the full database" and a meeting an "encounter", and this file still uses the older words. Build 12 renamed the app and cut most of its text, build 13 put all type on three levels, and build 14 replaced more words with icons and gave a typed name four outcomes (`docs/LOG.md`, 11:25, 12:40 and 13:20). Build 10 differs from build 9 (commit `013bb77`) by one sentence in a sheet, and build 11 adds the rule that eyes are lent for a week from a meeting; every figure and check below was made on build 9 unless it says otherwise. "Regards" is the title for now.

## How accurate it is

The test photos are the 4,197 photos of `data/eval/alachua-2025`: research-grade iNaturalist photos from Alachua County dated 2025 and 2026, none of them used to fit or tune a recognizer. Research-grade photos are ones that people on iNaturalist had already agreed on, often with iNaturalist's own suggestion in front of them, so they are easier than a photo taken in passing, and no figure here measures such a photo. Every figure below holds for one county and its own list of species. The brackets hold 95% bootstrap intervals over photos.

The pocket recognizer of build 9 ("pocket v3") gets the kind of eye right for 89.1% of the test photos (88.2 to 90.1). It asserts a kind for 81.7% of them and is right for 96.3% of those, and for the rest it says it is not sure. It names a species for 845 test photos (20.1%) and is right for 788 of them (93.3%, 91.6 to 94.9). These figures are in `docs/eval/shipped-alachua-pre2025-nc-tinyclip-39m-16.json`, made by `tools/eval/shipped.py` through `tools/fusion/fuse.py`, the reference for the page's arithmetic. How often its names are right for rarely recorded species has not been measured for these heads; for the heads of build 8 the audit found names right for 97.0% of 494 given to species with 200 or more local records and for 35.7% of 14 given to species with 10 to 49 (`docs/eval/audit.md`). On the 1,240 COCO test photos of the extra set (`data/extra/coco-kinds`) it gets the kind right for 96.9% (1,201), where the heads of build 8 got 98.1% (1,217), and it named no species on any of the 900 photos of people and rooms among them (`docs/eval/decision-pocket-v3.json`, `docs/eval/audit.md`). Those photos say nothing about a hand, a toy animal, a painting of a bird or a screen showing a heron, and in the page before build 9 shipped it called a brick wall a reptile and named a photo of old coins as a fungus (`docs/LOG.md`, 05:52).

The larger model, as the page runs it (`kind_at` 0.5), names a species for 3,533 of the test photos (84.2%) and is right for 3,469 of those (98.2%), scored from the original model's vectors by `tools/audit/recompute.py` (`tools/audit/results/recompute.json`). For species with fewer than ten local records its names were right about 88 times in 100 (87.7% of 73 names for species with 3 to 9 records, 87.5% of 24 for species with 1 or 2), and the level of 98 in 100 from which it names a species was chosen after the test outcomes of three levels had been seen (`docs/eval/audit.md`). The int8 file that the page runs, through the browser's WASM runtime on a build machine, named a species for 280 of 320 test photos and was right for 275 of those (98.2%; on so few photos the interval is several points wide), and no phone has run it.

The species list was built from records of wild things. It holds the domestic cat and the domestic horse, and it holds no dog, cattle or sheep, most houseplants or common garden flowers, so any species name the app gives one of those is wrong. On COCO's test photos the pocket recognizer named a species for 66 of 77 cats and 70 of 82 horses, each name right, and for 43 of 73 cows, each name wrong (`docs/eval/pets-livestock.json`). No number here measures a houseplant or a garden flower.

No comparison with iNaturalist's own model is possible, because its computer-vision route needs an application or user token and is left out of its published documentation, and the accuracy it publishes was measured on other photos by rules its posts do not fully state (`docs/eval/others.md`). `docs/eval/audit.md` recomputed the headline figures with its own code and corrected some of those first printed in `docs/EVAL.md`, which was regenerated afterwards and now agrees with it.

## Build it and run the checks

```
python3 -I build.py                                  # writes index.html, sw.js, selftest.js and dist/
python3 -I -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1      # serve the repository root, then open http://127.0.0.1:8765/
NODE_PATH=<where playwright lives> node test/check4.js record     # also lex, viewer, edge, sweep, film
node test/rec/serve.js 8782 &                        # the recognizer's checks have their own server
NODE_PATH=<where playwright lives> node test/rec/check_rec.js     # every section, or name the sections to run
```

A page may use a live camera only at an `https` address or on `localhost`. Opened as a file on a phone, it falls back to a photo of who you met and a clip of your own view. The page runs its self-test when its address carries `?selftest=1`. The checks on other browsers and on macOS run on GitHub's build machines, as `docs/HANDOFF.md` describes.

## Where things are

- `src/` holds the parts of the page, and `build.py` joins them into `index.html`, `sw.js`, `selftest.js` and `dist/seen-back.artifact.html`.
- `models/pocket/` and `models/naturalist/` hold the two recognizers' files. The larger model's 310 MB encoder is fetched in four parts from a pinned commit of this repository.
- `docs/PLAN.md` is the plan and `docs/LOG.md` the running log. `docs/CONTRACTS.md` says how the parts fit together, and `docs/ENGINES.md` says what ran on which browser. `docs/EVAL.md` holds the evaluation, `docs/eval/audit.md` an independent check of it, `docs/SOURCES.md` the sources behind every graded line about an animal's sight, and `docs/LICENCES.md` the licences. `docs/HANDOFF.md` says what someone carrying the work on needs.
- `data/` holds the species list and the manifests of the evaluation, training and extra photos, each with a `SUMMARY.md`. `service/` holds a species service that is built and has not been deployed. `native/` holds the two app shells and what they showed.

## What is measured and what is invented

Each line the app shows about an animal's sight carries a grade: measured in this animal, measured in a relative or a wider group, a guess, or left out. `docs/SOURCES.md` lists the source for every measured line and names the conventions that are this project's own invention, among them sharpness drawn against the viewer's own eye, a faster eye suggested by slowing the picture, depth guessed from height in the frame, and ultraviolet drawn as violet or blue.
