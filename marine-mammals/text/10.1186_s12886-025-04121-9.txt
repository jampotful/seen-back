# Therapeutic effect of suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection combined with 532 laser photocoagulation in treating rhegmatogenous retinal detachment

## Abstract
PurposeTo assess the therapeutic effect of suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection combined with 532 laser photocoagulation for the closure of retinal tears in the treatment of rhegmatogenous retinal detachment (RRD).MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed the clinical data of eight patients with RRD (eight eyes) who underwent suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection combined with 532-nm laser photocoagulation for the closure of tears at our hospital. The outcomes investigated were postoperative visual acuity and retinal reattachment.ResultsThe intraocular pressure (IOP) was significantly higher after surgery than before surgery (11.93 mmHg vs. 19.50 mmHg), and the best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) changed from 0.41 to 0.49, suggesting a significant improvement within 3 days post-surgery. In 7/8 eyes, the retina was well reattached postoperatively and follow-ups indicated stable condition. One patient experienced failed retinal reattachment and subsequently underwent vitrectomy combined with silicone oil tamponade. Three months after surgery, the silicone oil was removed, revealing successful reattachment of the retina.ConclusionIn patients with RRD characterized by smaller retinal tears, more localized retinal detachment, and a proliferative vitreoretinopathy grade of ≤ C1, treatment incorporating suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection and 532-nm laser photocoagulation to seal the tears resulted in good postoperative recovery, with all patients achieving favorable visual acuity. However, due to the retrospective, non-comparative design without a control group, it is difficult to fully evaluate the relative efficacy of this treatment compared to other methods.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

## Background
Rhegmatogenous retinal detachment (RRD) is the most common form of retinal detachment, with an estimated annual incidence of 1 in 10,000 individuals. If not properly and promptly managed, this condition can lead to blindness in the affected eye [1]. Multiple surgical treatment options are available for RRD, and a surgical plan tailored to the individual’s specific condition is core to successful treatment. RRD is characterized by the formation of retinal holes, a process involving pathological interaction between the retina and the vitreous body. When the neural sensory layer (RNL) of the retina separates from the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) and retinal changes caused by other ocular or systemic diseases have been excluded, RRD is diagnosed [2].
Traditional surgical treatments for RRD are primarily categorized into vitrectomy and scleral buckling surgery. Each approach has its specific indications, advantages, and limitations [3, 4]. Vitrectomy has advantages in managing complex cases of retinal detachment, such as those accompanied by choroidal detachment; however, it may entail risks of complications, such as secondary cataracts and glaucoma. In certain instances, additional surgery may be required for subsequent management [5, 6]. In contrast, scleral buckling surgery is renowned for its simplicity; however, patients may subsequently experience issues such as foreign body sensation within the eye, increased fragility due to scleral thinning, diplopia, and changes in refractive status [7, 8]. To ensure that patients attain optimal visual acuity and quality postoperatively, innovative therapeutic approaches are warranted.
Viscoelastic devices (ophthalmic viscosurgical devices [OVDs]) play a crucial role in ophthalmic surgery, particularly in maintaining space, protecting intraocular tissues, facilitating manipulation, and controlling hemorrhage during procedures [9]. Laser photocoagulation using a 532-nm wavelength precisely targets and irradiates the targeted tissue, effectively sealing off these vessels, reducing leakage, and preventing or minimizing further vision loss [10, 11]. When combined with 532-nm laser photocoagulation, the use of viscoelastic agents in the suprachoroidal facilitates the creation of a safer surgical environment, enabling ophthalmologists to perform laser photocoagulation more accurately while minimizing damage to the surrounding tissues. This strategy enhances the safety and efficacy of the surgery, promotes postoperative healing, and reduces the incidence of complications. This study evaluated the efficacy of combining suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection and 532-nm laser photocoagulation to seal retinal tears in patients with RRD.

## Methods

## Study design and population
This study retrospectively analyzed eight patients (eight eyes) with RRD who received specific treatments at our hospital between June 2018 and December 2020. All patients were born at term via natural delivery, and had no history of oxygen therapy at birth; patients with conditions related to familial exudative vitreoretinopathy (FEVR)-associated RRD were excluded. The therapeutic regimen comprised suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection combined with 532-nm laser photocoagulation to seal retinal tears. The classification system for proliferative vitreoretinopathy (PVR) used in this study was based on the international consensus established in 1983 [12]. The exclusion criteria were as follows: retinal tears extending beyond the 1-h position in clock hour terms; the presence of star-shaped folds in the retina exerting traction on the tear; tears located at the 4-h position inferiorly in the eyeball; the presence of extensive retinal tears or transparency indicating significant damage; uncontrolled severe glaucoma that might not be effectively managed; significant media opacities (such as those caused by cataracts or vitreous hemorrhage) that could severely impair comprehensive evaluation of the peripheral retina; and a history of previous retinal surgery. Written informed consent was obtained from all the patients, and the study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the People’s Hospital of Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region (no: 2019 − 132).

## Intervention
Each patient underwent a comprehensive baseline ophthalmic examination, which included the assessment of best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), B-scan ultrasonography of the eye, slit-lamp examination, slit-lamp-assisted anterior segment examination using a lens, and measurement of intraocular pressure (IOP). Before surgery, the individual condition of each patient was thoroughly explained to ensure full understanding, and consent was obtained from the patients before proceeding to the surgical intervention stage. Surgical preparation entailed standard sterilization and draping procedures, followed by retrobulbar nerve block anesthesia to alleviate pain. For the insertion of the needle into the suprachoroidal space, despite the location of the retinal tears, including those more posteriorly, the surgical incisions provided optimal access. The corneal-edge incision, extended posteriorly for approximately 4–5 mm, and the scleral incision made around 8–9 mm from the limbus, were carefully aligned with the meridian corresponding to the tear. Through these incisions, the viscoelastic needle was gently and accurately inserted into the suprachoroidal space directly above the retinal tear. An appropriate volume of viscoelastic material (ranging from 0.3 to 0.5 mL, depending on the size of the tear and the extent of retinal detachment) was injected into this cavity until the choroidal elevation adequately sealed the retinal hole. The viscoelastic agent used in the suprachoroidal injection was sodium hyaluronate (Healon GV). It has a molecular weight of 5 × 106 and a concentration of 1% (10 mg/ml). This highly cohesive viscoelastic material was selected for its excellent biocompatibility, appropriate viscosity, and prolonged duration of action within the eye, which enabled it to effectively maintain the space in the suprachoroidal cavity during the procedure, facilitating the subsequent 532-nm laser photocoagulation and promoting the closure of retinal tears. The viscoelastic agent (sodium hyaluronate, Healon GV) was injected using the needle that was originally provided with the product. To enhance the injection process, a hemostatic forceps was employed to carefully bend the needle, creating an optimal curvature that enabled easier handling and more accurate delivery of the viscoelastic material into the suprachoroidal space. This customized shaping of the needle was critical for ensuring the effectiveness of the viscoelastic injection and the subsequent sealing of the retinal tear. The scleral incision was sutured with 8 − 0 vicryl using interrupted stitches to facilitate healing. On the day after surgery, 532-nm laser photocoagulation was administered to reinforce the closure of the retinal tear, with laser parameters set at 200 µJ power, 0.2 s exposure time, and a suggestion to initiate with level 3 laser spot intensity.
Notably, in all cases, there was no need for subretinal fluid drainage during the surgical procedure. Before surgery, both eyes of each patient were immobilized, and strict body positioning instructions were given to ensure that the retinal tear was positioned at the lowest point. This preoperative measure effectively promoted the absorption of subretinal fluid, resulting in a state of shallow retinal detachment. In this condition, the injection of 0.5 ml of viscoelastic agent (sodium hyaluronate, Healon GV) was adequate to generate a pressure ridge that sealed the retinal tear, obviating the necessity for additional subretinal fluid drainage.

## Data collection and outcome definition
During the comprehensive baseline ophthalmic examination, Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT), B-scan ultrasonography of the eye was performed to assess the extent of retinal detachment and the location of retinal tears. High-resolution fundus photographs were also taken to document the initial status of the retina. Moreover, we collected the basic demographic information and clinical characteristics of the patients from the electronic medical record system, extracting data on sex, age, affected eye, preoperative IOP, preoperative BCVA, precise location of the retinal tear, tear size, extent of retinal detachment, and whether the macula was involved. The primary postoperative monitoring indices included IOP, BCVA, and successful retinal reattachment. The criteria for assessing vision changes were as follows: if the postoperative BCVA improved by one line or more compared with the preoperative levels, it was considered vision improvement; a decrease by one line or more was defined as vision decline; and no noticeable change in visual acuity was regarded as vision stability. Gradual absorption of subretinal fluid (SRF) was considered a positive indicator of successful retinal reattachment. Conversely, ineffective absorption of SRF suggested that retinal reattachment may not have been achieved.

## Statistical analysis
This study primarily employed descriptive statistics. For continuous variables, we used means (standard deviations), whereas for categorical variables, results were reported as frequencies and percentages. All computational analyses were conducted using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software, version 26, operating on Windows (IBM SPSS Statistics, Armonk, New York, USA).

## Results

## Baseline characteristics
Of the eight patients, three were male and five were female. They ranged in age from 13 to 61 years (mean: 35.25 (19.18) years). In terms of the affected eyes, two were left eyes and six were right eyes. PVR grading revealed seven eyes as grade B and one as grade C1. The size of the retinal tears varied from 0.5 to 2.5 PD, averaging at 1.38 (0.74) PD. Among the eight eyes, four had tractional tears, three had atrophic round holes, and one had a small sieve hole in the transformation zone. Five patients had retinal tears at the equator, whereas the remaining three had tears in the pre-equatorial region. The macula was involved in four patients. ## Investigated outcomes
The IOP, BCVA, and postoperative reattachment results of the patients are shown in Table 2. Preoperatively, the IOP ranged from 9.0 to 14.7 mmHg (average: 11.93 (2.13) mmHg). Postoperatively, the IOP spanned 14.0–28.0 mmHg (average: 19.50 (4.87) mmHg), indicating a notable increase after surgery. Furthermore, preoperative BCVA varied between 0.02 and 0.80, averaging at 0.41 (0.25). Three days after surgery, the BCVA ranged from 0.25 to 0.80, with an improved average of 0.49 (0.17), demonstrating a significant enhancement in visual acuity within 3 days post-surgery.

Investigated outcomes
The retinas of seven of the eight eyes that underwent surgery successfully achieved full reattachment. However, one patient’s retina did not reattach postoperatively, necessitating subsequent vitrectomy. Preoperatively, a retinal tear measuring 1.5 PD in size was detected at the superotemporal pre-equatorial region (around 10 o’clock position) of the right eye. However, 3 days after the initial surgery, there was no obvious absorption of subretinal fluid. After communication with the patient’s family, vitrectomy was performed. Intraoperatively, an additional retinal tear, 0.5 PD in size, was discovered at the inferotemporal pre-equatorial region (5 o’clock position) of the right eye, which was overlooked during the preoperative examination. This oversight was likely due to the tear’s position below the 3 o’clock position. At that time, considering the safety concerns of using air filling for tears in this lower position and the unavailability of inert gas in our region, silicone oil tamponade was selected as the treatment method. During the secondary surgery, it was discovered that the initial surgery had failed because of an overlooked small retinal tear. In this patient, the silicone oil was removed 3 months after surgery, and subsequent examinations confirmed favorable retinal reattachment.
All patients were followed-up over 3–6 months, during which no recurrence of retinal detachment was observed. Despite this, the patient who underwent vitrectomy followed by silicone oil tamponade developed secondary cataracts, leading to poorer visual acuity than in the preoperative state. Furthermore, three patients experienced elevated IOP; however, through topical administration of medications to reduce eye pressure for 2–3 days, their pressures returned to normal levels. No significant complications were observed.

## Imaging analysis

## Case 1
A 61-year-old female presented with 3-day history of metamorphopsia in the left eye. BCVA was 0.15 in the left eye. Slit-lamp examination revealed unremarkable anterior segment. Fundoscopy demonstrated a grayish-white elevated retina in the temporal quadrant with a 1 PD horseshoe-shaped retinal tear at the 1 o’clock position in the superotemporal periphery (Fig. 1A-C). The patient was diagnosed with RRD in the left eye and subsequently underwent suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection combined with 532-nm laser photocoagulation. Postoperative examination showed complete retinal reattachment with improved BCVA of 0.4 (Fig. 1D-F). Regular follow-up visits confirmed maintained retinal position.

## Case 2
A 21-year-old female reported 1-week history of blurred vision and metamorphopsia in the right eye. Best-corrected visual acuity with − 8.00DS/-1.00DC×15 was 0.25. Anterior segment examination was normal. Fundus examination revealed superotemporal retinal elevation with a 1.5-PD horseshoe-shaped tear at the 11 o’clock position (Fig. 2A-C). The diagnosis included RRD in the right eye and high myopia. The patient received the same surgical procedure. Complete retinal reattachment was achieved postoperatively with BCVA of 0.4 (Fig. 2D-F). Serial follow-ups demonstrated gradual resolution of subretinal fluid. At 6-month postoperative evaluation, corrected VA improved to 0.6 with complete fluid absorption and stable retinal position.

## Discussion
This study included eight patients (eight eyes), all of whom underwent treatment for RRD using a combination of suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection and 532-nm laser photocoagulation to seal retinal tears. While a notable increase in IOP was observed postoperatively, the BCVA demonstrated a significant improvement. In these patients, seven eyes achieved successful retinal reattachment with stability maintained during follow-up. However, in the patient in whom reattachment was not initially achieved, we used an alternative surgical approach involving vitrectomy combined with silicone oil tamponade, which ultimately led to successful retinal reattachment. Unfortunately, this patient subsequently developed secondary cataracts, which led to a decline in visual acuity. Three patients experienced elevated IOP postoperatively; however, through local medication administration, their IOP was effectively managed and returned to normal levels. This indicates that the therapeutic strategy combining suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection and 532-nm laser photocoagulation to seal retinal tears ensures therapeutic efficacy while also exhibiting a relatively favorable safety profile.
Surgery is the standard treatment for RRD with the pivotal objective of identifying and repairing all detachments. Scleral buckling is commonly advised in younger patients with small tears near the equator and mild PVR. This procedure alleviates vitreous traction on the retina by applying pressure to the sclera, thereby sealing tears and restoring the retinal position. However, it may lead to changes in ocular shape, affecting refraction and eye-axis dimensions [13]. Technological advancements have popularized minimally invasive vitrectomy in RRD management [14]. By employing silicone oil or gas as a tamponade, this surgery supports the adherence of the retinal neural layer to the RPE until a stable scar forms around the tear. Although effective, both gas and silicone oil tamponades can significantly alter the refractive status, causing visual blurring [15]. Furthermore, 77% of patients who undergo vitrectomy develop cataracts within the first year, and 17% develop iatrogenic tears [16]. Consequently, this study evaluated the efficacy of a novel therapeutic approach, combining suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection with 532-nm laser photocoagulation in treating RRD, with the aim of exploring more advantageous treatment alternatives for patients.
This study demonstrates that combined suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection and 532-nm laser photocoagulation for RRD exhibits favorable therapeutic outcomes and high safety profiles. In the early stages of the disease, the exacerbation of retinal detachment may coincide with choroidal detachment; however, when the choroid reaches a certain height and seals the retinal tears, the subretinal fluid is significantly reduced, and spontaneous retinal reattachment occurs. As the retina realigns and intraocular pressure increases, the choroidal detachment recovers naturally, restoring the eyeball to its original anatomical configuration, while effectively avoiding the complications associated with vitrectomy and scleral buckling procedures. This mechanism highlights the high reattachment rate achievable through choroidal cavity filling combined with laser photocoagulation, rivaling the efficacy of conventional scleral buckling for retinal detachment repair, and avoiding severe adverse reactions, such as explosive choroidal hemorrhage [13]. Notably, the patients included in this study presented for early diagnosis, with a limited extent of retinal detachment, and a PVR grade not exceeding C1, categorizing them as relatively straightforward cases of RRD. Given the potential of subretinal fluid to complicate dosing control during cryotherapy, potentially elevating the risk of postoperative epiretinal membrane formation, all patients underwent 532-nm laser photocoagulation to seal tears the day after complete retinal reattachment surgery. The advantages of laser therapy lie in its precise energy regulation and accurate targeting, ensuring the effective closure of retinal tears and reinforcing the therapeutic outcome.
The viscoelastic agent used for suprachoroidal filling is sodium hyaluronate, which is present in interstitial tissues and a major constituent of the extracellular matrix; it plays a crucial role in regulating osmotic pressure and cell adhesion [17]. Sodium hyaluronate has excellent biocompatibility and non-toxicity, leading to its widespread adoption in various ophthalmic surgeries in recent years [18–20]. An earlier studies have explored similar treatment approaches for RRD on the use of intravitreal air injection and laser photocoagulation, they reported pneumatic retinopexy with simple air tamponade combined with laser photocoagulation is a safe and effective method for treating patients with RRD. Patients may achieve a relatively high single-surgery success rate and good visual acuity recovery [21]. Moreover, sodium hyaluronate, which is characterized by pseudoplasticity and viscosity, provides lubrication, protection, and filling effects, significantly enhancing surgical safety and efficacy [22]. Additionally, healthy eyes demonstrate a high clearance rate for sodium hyaluronate, with a half-life in the anterior chamber of approximately 13 h, confirming the safety of its intraocular use [23]. Therefore, the combination of suprachoroidal infusion with sodium hyaluronate and 532-nm laser photocoagulation therapy may become one of the least invasive surgical methods of treating patients with simple RRD.
The case of failed initial retinal reattachment provides valuable insights into the challenges of treating RRD. The overlooked tear in the inferotemporal region highlights the importance of a meticulous preoperative examination, especially for tears located in less accessible areas. The anatomical position of the tear below the 3 o’clock position posed a significant challenge in terms of choosing the appropriate tamponade material. The absence of inert gas in our local setting limited our options, and we had to opt for silicone oil, which, although effective in achieving reattachment in the long run, led to secondary complications such as cataract formation in this patient. This case emphasizes the need for better access to a variety of tamponade materials in clinical practice and the importance of continuous improvement in preoperative diagnostic techniques to avoid similar oversights. Future studies could focus on developing more comprehensive imaging and examination protocols to detect all potential retinal tears accurately, thereby improving the success rate of primary surgeries for RRD.
This study faces several limitations: First, due to its retrospective, non-comparative design without a control group, which hinders our ability to accurately assess the relative efficacy of suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection combined with 532-nm laser photocoagulation compared to traditional treatments for RRD. Without a control group, it is challenging to isolate the specific effects of our treatment method from other factors that may influence treatment outcomes, such as patient-specific characteristics, disease severity, and the natural course of the disease. The lack of a control group also prevents us from making conclusive statements about whether our treatment approach offers significant advantages in terms of visual acuity recovery, retinal reattachment rates, or complication rates compared to established surgical techniques. Second, the most notable limitation is the small sample size of only 8 eyes. This limited number of patients restricts the generalizability of our findings, as the results may not be applicable to a broader population of RRD patients. The small sample size also reduces the statistical power of the study, increasing the likelihood of type II errors and making it difficult to detect true but small treatment effects. Additionally, with a small sample, there is a higher chance that the characteristics of the included patients may not be representative of all RRD patients. Third, the relatively short follow-up period of 3–6 months in this study, this duration is insufficient to fully evaluate long-term outcomes, such as the long-term stability of retinal reattachment, the sustained improvement of visual acuity, and the occurrence of late-onset complications. Last, the failure to comprehensively gather patients’ ophthalmic medical histories may have implications, as these histories could significantly influence the prognosis and treatment outcomes for patients with RRD. To address these limitations and more accurately evaluate the relative efficacy of our treatment approach, future research should employ a prospective, randomized controlled trial design. This would allow for a direct comparison of our treatment with standard therapies, taking into account potential confounding variables and providing more robust evidence for the effectiveness of suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection combined with 532-nm laser photocoagulation in the management of RRD. Moreover, long-term follow-up studies, preferably spanning 2–3 years or more, are essential to gain a more comprehensive understanding of the long-term efficacy and safety of suprachoroidal viscoelastic injection combined with 532-nm laser photocoagulation.
This study demonstrated that a combination of suprachoroidal infusion using sodium hyaluronate and 532-nm laser photocoagulation therapy shows favorable therapeutic effects for RRD, including visual acuity recovery and retinal reattachment. Although some patients may experience elevated IOP postoperatively, it can be promptly alleviated using topical pressure-lowering medications.
