# Hysteretic curve characteristics in rectangular shear walls predicted by machine learning

## Abstract
Rectangular reinforced concrete (RC) shear walls are crucial for seismic resistance in high-rise buildings. Characteristic points on the skeleton curve from pseudo-static tests are key for evaluating seismic performance. Traditional models struggle with the high-dimensional, nonlinear relationships between these points and component dimensions. An interpretable empirical guidance machine learning (IEG-ML) was proposed for predicting these feature points. IEG-ML aligns with empirical trends from extensive experiments and is explainable via explicit formulas. Trained on a self-built dataset of 184 samples, IEG-ML accuracy and efficiency are enhanced using a population optimization algorithm. The model identifies the importance of feature points and component factors, providing a dominant explicable formula. Results show IEG-ML high accuracy and efficiency, particularly with a backpropagation network optimized by the dung beetle algorithm (DBO), making it a robust tool for seismic evaluation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-88401-w.

## Introduction
Reinforced concrete (RC) shear walls are essential for providing lateral load capacity and resisting seismic forces in traditional and safety-related structures1,2. Due to their nonlinear plastic deformation under severe vibrations, it is crucial to analyze the nonlinear force-deformation relationship and accurately model the hysteresis behavior1,3–8. RC walls, including rectangular, barbell, and flange walls, have been extensively studied, with numerous empirical regression formulas developed to characterize the hysteresis behavior of rectangular walls9–14. While these formulas, derived from probability statistics and linear regression, offer satisfactory results, they suffer from limited generalization ability and high resource costs.
Empirical formulas derived from the statistical regression of enormous of datasets can identify characteristic points of the envelope but fail to capture complete hysteresis behavior10. Finite element (FE) analysis, while detailed, is time-consuming and complex. In contrast, machine learning (ML) methods can efficiently summarize relationships between component parameters and envelope characteristic points using collected data15–17. Recent research has leveraged backpropagation networks (BP) to process nonlinear experimental data, proving highly effective in seismic engineering18. Liu and Li19 model-free method based on BP networks effectively predicts crucial seismic characteristics of RC columns, offering a fast and innovative approach for evaluating the seismic performance of buildings and bridges. To overcome the local optimal trap in gradient descent, Wang et al.20 developed a BP network enhanced with nature-inspired evolutionary algorithms. This approach, used to predict bond strength between H-bar surfaces and concrete in CES structures, outperformed traditional BP networks and empirical formulas, demonstrating superior results even with small datasets. Using a small database of 150 shear walls, Mohammad et al.18 employed an optimized neural network to accurately predict the seismic hysteretic response of shear walls using 11 input features. This study demonstrates that even small datasets can serve as effective training inputs for machine learning models in predicting the seismic behavior of shear walls.
Yu et al.21 and Liu et al.16 developed a machine learning model based on experience and supervised by physical formulas to predict the seismic failure characteristics of RC columns so as to make the machine learning method reflect the relationship between component parameters and failure characteristics of RC columns. However, the model in these studies still cannot solve the problem of black box that machine learning cannot explain22, namely it cannot give the concrete expression between the parameters of RC components and the failure characteristics.
To address the shortcomings of previous studies, this research proposes an IEG-ML training scheme to tackle issues of model interpretability and alignment with component parameter trends and feature points. The complex nonlinear relationships between component parameters and feature points are elucidated through dominant expressions. The trained ML model is validated against empirical formulas to ensure it aligns with expected trends. Three swarm intelligence algorithms—Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Genetic Algorithm (GA), and the recently proposed Dung Beetle Optimization (DBO) algorithm—were compared using a dataset of 184 rectangular shear wall samples. The algorithm demonstrating the highest accuracy and iteration efficiency was selected and integrated into IEG-ML to address under-fitting in single neural layers. Additionally, the relative importance of dimensional parameters and feature points was assessed using a weighting method. This study provides a practical tool and theoretical foundation for seismic evaluation of rectangular shear walls by enabling the prediction of feature points based on input component parameters. As illustrated in the Table 1, the experimental data of rectangular shear wall are well distributed and extensive. It should be noted, no matter in the web area or the boundary area, the reinforcement force in some experimental shear walls is not set, so the yield strength of the steel bar and the reinforcement ratio here are 0. Moreover, the axial compression ratio range in the database is [− 0.1, 1.01], the negative axial ratio here means that the axial force is the tension, and the opposite is the pressure. The statistical properties of the input parameters are depicted in Fig. 3, with the average, median, standard deviation, and coefficient of determination denoted as Mean, Median, Stdev, R2, respectively. Manifold probability distributions are used to fit the data of each parameter to reflect the parameter distribution of the database so that the statistical characteristics of the parameters can be explicitly represented. The choice of the distribution function depicted in the figure is evaluated based on the magnitude of the determination coefficient. A determination coefficient closer to 1 indicates a stronger fit of the distribution function.
The objective of statistical distribution is to illustrate the equilibrium of data distribution, thereby preventing overfitting that may arise from data concentration within a specific interval. As depicted in Fig. 3, some data exhibit imbalance, and acquiring a balanced distribution of parameters in the database is challenging due to constraints such as economic cost, workload, and equipment limitations. Although numerical methods (interpolation) and synthetic data can augment the simulation data, an excessive amount of or unreliable simulation data can compromise the actual effectiveness of real algorithms. We compiled and constructed a database comprising 184 samples, which essentially encompasses most of the working conditions of the actual project. During the training process, cross-validation enhances the robustness of model evaluation and indirectly addresses the issue of data imbalance. K-fold cross-validation ensures that each fold contains samples from these rare classes, thereby facilitating an adequate evaluation of the model’s performance on rare classes. Since Fig. 3(g), (h), and (j) do not present common distribution laws, Sine distribution and polynomial distribution are used to fit.

## Hysteretic characteristics selection
The paper focuses on skeleton curves, namely backbone curves proposed by FEMA 27448 in 1997, which is a crux for measure the seismic performance of components. The x-axis of skeleton curves is drift in percent, and y-axis is the shear force in kN in the paper. Drift represents the vibration deformation characteristics of shear wall in pseudo-static test, while shear force is used to represent its resistance strength. As shown in Fig. 4, the skeleton curves model for RC shear walls in ASCE 4152 is described by five points, namely crack point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( {{\delta _{cr}},{F_{cr}}} \right)$$\end{document}, yield point\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( {{\delta _y},{F_y}} \right)$$\end{document}, peak point\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( {{\delta _m},{F_m}} \right)$$\end{document}, limit point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( {{\delta _u},{F_u}} \right)$$\end{document}and residual strength point, moreover, structural designers can use the formula of hysteretic rules and skeleton curves model to calculate the hysteretic curve of quasi-static experiments, namely the behavior of shear walls under each low cyclic displacement. As shown in Fig. 4, before the cracking point, the member presents an elastic state with unchanged stiffness. Between the cracking point and the yield point, the concrete cracks and cracks gradually spread to intersect the steel bar, while the rebar maintains an elastic-plastic state. Following the yield point, the reinforcement bar undergoes yielding, resulting in a reduction in the overall stiffness of the structural member. Subsequently, the load distribution gradually shifts towards the peak point. Upon reaching the peak point, the resistance strength of the wall diminishes until it reaches the limit point, indicating the failure of the shear wall. Figure 5 shows the skeleton curves of the different failure types in the dataset for representing the true reliability of the dataset. According to the failure mode principles outlined in literature, RC shear walls primarily exhibit three failure modes: flexural, flexural shear, and shear. In the flexural mode, walls experience significant deformation and demonstrate excellent energy dissipation, typically occurring in slender walls with a height-to-width ratio (λ) greater than 3. This mode is characterized by the yield cracking of longitudinal steel bars in the plastic hinge region. The shear mode is prevalent in short or low walls with λ less than 1.5, exhibiting high brittleness and poor energy dissipation. Failure in this mode includes diagonal tension, diagonal compression, and sliding shear, with sliding shear often occurring at the ends of walls with bending cracks or at the base where concrete breaks. For walls with a medium height-to-width ratio (1.5 ≤ λ ≤ 3), flexural shear failure typically occurs after bending yield, displaying nonlinear shear deformation and ultimately culminating in shear failure. These models delineate the mechanical behaviour of shear walls under varying load conditions. It can be seen that the initial stiffness of diagonal tensile failure is the largest, while the peak bearing capacity is also the largest, and the stiffness of falling section is the largest, which indicates that the shear wall is prone to brittle failure in this kind of failure. The skeleton curves of flexural shear failure and sliding failure show that the bearing capacity of these failures is moderate, and the drift of falling section is long, which fully reflects the ductile failure property. The skeleton curve of bending failure shows that this kind of failure belongs to ductile failure with low bearing capacity.
In this paper, the skeleton curve feature points described above are used as output values. According to a large number of literatures on pseudo-static tests of shear walls1,8,23–47, the linear relationship between feature points is not as simple as described by French et al.53, Carrillo et al.12 and Liu et al.16, namely\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{\:F}}_{\text{u}}\text{=}{\text{\:}\text{0.8}\text{F}}_{\text{m}}$$\end{document}. Therefore, the elements of the output value should not ignore one of them, but all of them should be trained as output values.
Although there are many factors affecting the hysteresis characteristics of the wall, a large number of empirical formulas have been summarized from the widely distributed and large number of databases, which are listed in Table 4. There is an extremely complex nonlinear relationship between the input parameters with hysteresis characteristics. As shown in Fig. 6, it is obvious that the empirical formula obtained by statistical regression cannot fit the experimental data well. A large number of scattered points are distributed outside the boundary between 20% and − 20%, which proves that the results are quite different from the experimental results, and most of the results predicted by the formula are overestimate. Therefore, machine learning methods using interpretable single layer perceptron are used in this paper to find models with better fit.

## Methodology

## BP neural network
BP neural networks date back to the 1940s and are derived from neural networks that mimic the human brain, which is also called the perceptron55. As shown in Fig. 7, the neural network is composed of multiple neurons, which gather into multiple clusters, namely input layer, hidden layer and output layer. Information propagates forward from the input layer in a sequential manner, and the weighted sum between layers is formed through weights and biases, and then the activation function of neurons provides nonlinear characteristics and outputs to the next neuron. Finally, the L2 difference between the output value and the experimental value acts as a loss function for backpropagation56, and the partial derivation of the output value to the input value is obtained. The learning rate guides the next input value and then carries on the next round of forward propagation. More specifically, the input layer is input with n-dimensional vector \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\varvec{x}}={\left[ {{x_1},{x_2},.,{x_n}} \right]^T}$$\end{document}, where n is the quantity of neurons in the input layer. ## Theory of SI optimization algorithm
It is difficult to solve the complex problem of global optimal solution of BP neural network by using only quasi-Newton method and conjugate gradient method. In this paper, the self-learning property of swarm intelligence (SI) algorithm is proposed to find the optimal solution. Thus, three algorithms, PSO, GA and DBO are used in this study to compare the optimization effects.

## PSO algorithm
Particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm57 is an algorithm inspired by bird foraging, with few parameters and fast convergence speed, but it is easy to fall into local optimal solution, which requires proper initialization parameters to avoid, so it is difficult to quickly apply in the solution. ## GA

To examine the intricacies of the iterative process of three swarm intelligence algorithms, Fig. 12 illustrates the trend of fitness value across iterations for predicting yield load. Specifically, the graph demonstrates a decrease in the average absolute error value as the number of iterations increases. The initial fitness of PSO is 31.922, GA is 31.019, and that of DBO is 32.26, indicating that the fitness value still needs to be optimized before iteration, and the traditional gradient descent method cannot obtain the global optimal solution. The figure clearly demonstrates that the fitness value of DBO decreases more significantly, indicating a higher rate of optimal solution discovery compared to PSO and GA. This enhanced performance is attributed to DBO’s division of the population into four types—ball-rolling dung beetles, brood balls, small dung beetles, and thieves. The incubation and stealing iteration mechanisms further facilitate the model ability to explore positions distant from the current optimal solution, thereby avoiding local optima traps.

## Conclusions
In this paper, a machine learning scheme of IEG-ML is proposed to solve the problems of model unexplainable and non-conforming to empirical trends, and three swarm intelligence algorithms are used to optimize the weight and bias. Method introduction, model training, result analysis, application comparison and analysis were carried out with self-built database. Finally, the following conclusions can be drawn:
A comprehensive database comprising 184 rectangular shear wall samples has been established. The distribution of 10 key features of these samples is characterized and fitted using various mathematical models. Additionally, the shear walls are categorized based on different failure modes. It is observed that shear walls exhibiting diagonal tensile failure demonstrate the highest initial stiffness, peak load-bearing capacity, and descending stiffness. This indicates a propensity for brittle failure in such failure modes.Comparing the DBO algorithm screened by the positive and negative ratio relationship given by the empirical formula with the unscreened model, it can be concluded that the results of the screened model are more consistent with the empirical rules and experimental phenomena, which makes the ML model conform to the concept of structural design.Both PSO and GA and DBO algorithms have more accurate prediction effect than the empirical formula of statistical analysis, and the DBO algorithm has more accurate prediction effect. Compared with the DBO algorithm, the results of RMSE, MAE and R2 of PSO and GA are all poor, and the convergence rate of PSO and GA is not as fast as that of DBO as the number of iterations increases. This indicates that DBO is more suitable for predicting the hysteretic effect of rectangular shear walls through the dimensional characteristics.
A comprehensive database comprising 184 rectangular shear wall samples has been established. The distribution of 10 key features of these samples is characterized and fitted using various mathematical models. Additionally, the shear walls are categorized based on different failure modes. It is observed that shear walls exhibiting diagonal tensile failure demonstrate the highest initial stiffness, peak load-bearing capacity, and descending stiffness. This indicates a propensity for brittle failure in such failure modes.
Comparing the DBO algorithm screened by the positive and negative ratio relationship given by the empirical formula with the unscreened model, it can be concluded that the results of the screened model are more consistent with the empirical rules and experimental phenomena, which makes the ML model conform to the concept of structural design.
Both PSO and GA and DBO algorithms have more accurate prediction effect than the empirical formula of statistical analysis, and the DBO algorithm has more accurate prediction effect. Compared with the DBO algorithm, the results of RMSE, MAE and R2 of PSO and GA are all poor, and the convergence rate of PSO and GA is not as fast as that of DBO as the number of iterations increases. This indicates that DBO is more suitable for predicting the hysteretic effect of rectangular shear walls through the dimensional characteristics.
The feature points predicted by IEG-ML model in this study can be used to calculate the restoring force model of shear walls, so as to intelligently obtain hysteretic curves of shear walls under different sizes and working conditions. This hysteresis curve plays a crucial role in assessing the seismic action of shear walls, and intelligent interactive software can be produced for use by structural designers based on this principle.
