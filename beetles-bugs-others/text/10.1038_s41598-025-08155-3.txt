# Multi-strategy dung beetle optimization for robust indoor object detection and tracking for visually impaired people with hybrid deep learning networks

## Abstract
AbstractVisually impaired people generally face many troubles in their everyday lives, and technical involvement might help them perform these tasks. Object detection is a significant aspect of computer vision (CV) and machine learning (ML), which plays a substantial part in recognizing and detecting objects in a video or image. Dissimilar objects are detected, and their shape, size, and position data are accurately acquired through object detection. Particularly, visually challenged people are not set to face sudden situations by themselves while moving. Hence, these situations are a significant attack on the protection of the visually impaired. A method is required all over the world to alleviate their day-to-day lives, since it is problematic to help visually impaired individuals in real life. This study proposes a novel Multi-Strategy Dung Beetle Optimization for Robust Object Detection and Tracking with Hybrid Deep Learning Networks (MSDBO-ODTHDLN) model. The proposed MSDBO-ODTHDLN model presents visually challenged people with real-time and reliable object detection and tracking capabilities. Initially, the median filter (MF) is employed in the image pre-processing stage to enhance edge detection and ensure the clarity of objects in diverse environments. Furthermore, the Mask R-CNN method is used for object detection to provide accurate localization and identification of objects within the environment. Moreover, the CapsNet model is the backbone for the feature extraction process to capture spatial hierarchies and intricate patterns. The hybrid method unites a convolutional neural network and a bidirectional long short-term memory (CNN-BiLSTM) model, which is utilized for object detection and classification. Finally, the multi-strategy dung beetle optimization (MSDBO)-based hyperparameter selection process is performed to adjust the analysis outcomes of the CNN-BiLSTM model. To exhibit the improved performance of the proposed MSDBO-ODTHDLN methodology, a comprehensive experimental analysis is conducted under the Indoor object detection dataset. The comparison analysis of the MSDBO-ODTHDLN methodology portrayed a superior accuracy value of 99.77% over existing models.Keywords: Dung beetle optimization, Object detection, Deep learning, Image pre-processing, Visually impaired peopleSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing

The lack of vision might impact professional, personal, and ecological relations and signify a restraint in everyday routines. Despite many developments in invention, blindness remains a crucial problem. Scholars are concentrating on this problem to develop supportive devices or helpers for visually challenged people3. ## Image pre-processing using MF
At first, the MSDBO-ODTHDLN method employs an MF during the image pre-processing stage to enhance edge detection and ensure the clarity of objects in diverse environments29. This model is chosen for its effectiveness in removing noise while preserving the edges of objects in an image, making it ideal for object detection tasks. Unlike other filtering techniques, such as GF or mean filters, MF is robust to salt-and-pepper noise, which is common in real-world scenarios. It works by replacing each pixel’s value with the median of the surrounding pixel values, thus mitigating image distortions while maintaining key structural data. This ability to preserve edges while mitigating noise makes MF an optimal choice for enhancing the quality of images before further processing. Furthermore, MF is computationally efficient and easy to implement, making it an ideal option in real-time applications where processing speed is crucial. MF presents a balanced tradeoff between simplicity and performance compared to more complex filtering methods.
The MF is a nonlinear image processing model generally employed in object detection and tracking models for visually challenged individuals. It aids in decreasing noise and improving the quality of an input image by substituting every pixel value with the average of its adjacent pixels. This pre-processing stage enhances the accuracy and clarity of object detection, particularly in environments with fluctuating lighting conditions. The MF certifies more accurate object tracking and localization by filtering out noise. These mains are more trustworthy and effective navigation support for blind people.

## Object detection using mask R-CNN
Also, the Mask R-CNN technique is employed for object detection to provide accurate localization and classification of objects within the environment30. This method is highly effective in localizing and segmenting objects. Unlike conventional object detection models such as Faster R-CNN or YOLO, Mask R-CNN not only detects the bounding box of an object but also generates pixel-wise segmentation masks, giving a more precise comprehension of the object’s shape. This ability to simultaneously detect and segment objects makes it appropriate for applications requiring detailed object recognition. Moreover, this method is built on a region-based CNN architecture, which allows it to process images with multiple objects, maintaining high accuracy effectively. Its capability in handling intrinsic and overlapping objects in diverse environments distinguishes it from other detection models. Compared to models with a single task focus, Mask R-CNN presents a robust, multitasking approach, making it an ideal choice for applications needing detection and segmentation.
According to CNN, Mask R-CNN (Regional CNN) is intended for sample segmentation tasks. It presents the abilities of Faster R‐CNN by enhancing Region of Interest (ROI) handling over the overview of the ROI alignment and adding a branch to predict segmentation masks on all ROIs. The Mask R‐CNN structure works in two phases: region proposal and sample segmentation with object detection. The following segments describe all phases.

## Region proposal
The region proposal phase classifies candidate ROIs in the image, which can comprise objects, thus permitting the following processing to concentrate on this region. The region proposal system initially handles the input image over a convolutional backbone network for removing features from the imagery. Usually, a variation of ResNet combined with a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) serves as the backbone structure. The FPN improves the backbone by making a multiple-scale feature pyramid that permits the system to detect objects at different scales efficiently. The FPN attains this by building feature maps from the ResNet on numerous scales. It utilizes a top‐down pathway over lateral links by the feature mapping from the ResNet, to combine higher‐level semantical characteristics with lower‐level, higher‐resolution characteristics. The representation of multiple scales allows the system to detect either small or large objects effectively.
Here, ResNeXt is applied as the backbone structure rather than the more normally applied ResNet in concrete crack detection investigations to gain a possible performance development. ResNeXt improves ResNet by utilizing numerous simpler paths in parallel inside all layers, permitting it to learn more different characteristics without making the complete approach much greater or more complicated. It is established that RexNeXt provides improved inference outcomes in various object detection tasks.
After feature extraction, a region proposal network (RPN) works on the feature mapping output from the backbone. It utilizes a smaller convolutional network, which passes over the feature mapping and performs evaluation. At all locations, the RPN utilizes a set of pre-defined anchor boxes of various scales and feature ratios to produce these designs, which cover a different range of possible object sizes and shapes. The RPN returns the predicted object score for every anchor box, which designates the probability of the object to be present, and the adjusted anchor box directs.

## Instance segmentation with bounding box
The next phase enhances the ideas produced in the initial phase. It outputs the bounding boxes for object detection and sample segmentation masks, which signify the crack region in this case. It starts with the ROI Align layer, which removes fixed-size feature maps from all presented areas. ROI alignment builds upon its predecessor, ROI pooling, which is applied to faster R‐CNN. Utilizing bilinear interpolation maintains spatial information with significantly higher precision. This inhibits the quantization problem in ROI pooling, whereas the nonstop coordinates of the presented areas are passed to separate bin positions. The aligned feature maps are then handled by dual network heads: the object recognition head and the mask generation head. The object recognition head performs the bounding box regression and object classification utilizing fully connected (FC) layers. The mask generation head makes a class‐specific binary mask for all bounding boxes. This mask outlines the object shape inside the bounding box, like the crack’s region.

## Feature extraction using CapsNet
In addition, the CapsNet model is employed as the backbone for the feature extraction process to capture spatial hierarchies and intricate patterns31. This model presents significant merit in extraction due to its capability to preserve spatial hierarchies between features. Unlike conventional CNNs, which rely heavily on pooling layers that can lose spatial data, CapsNet utilizes capsules to encode part-whole relationships, making it more effective at recognizing objects in diverse orientations, perspectives, and deformations. This unique capability makes CapsNet useful for extracting detailed features in images with varying object poses. Furthermore, the dynamic routing mechanism of the model allows it to adaptively route information between capsules, improving its robustness to transformations like rotation and scaling. This makes CapsNet more effective and accurate for feature extraction, specifically in intrinsic and cluttered environments, compared to conventional CNN models that might struggle with such discrepancies. Therefore, CapsNet provides a more accurate and versatile solution for feature extraction, particularly in tasks that involve complex and dynamic objects. ## Classification process using CNN-BiLSTM model
For the object detection and classification process, the hybrid method uniting a CNN-BiLSTM model is employed32. This method integrates the merits of CNN and BiLSTM networks, making it highly effective for classification tasks, specifically when spatial and temporal features are involved. CNNs outperform in automatically extracting hierarchical features from image data, allowing for effective pattern recognition. At the same time, BiLSTMs capture sequential dependencies in data by processing data in both forward and backwards directions. This technique benefits intrinsic tasks such as object detection and classification, as the model enables it to comprehend context and temporal relationships. Using both CNN for feature extraction and BiLSTM for sequence learning, the CNN-BiLSTM model provides enhanced accuracy and robustness compared to conventional single-network architectures. It is advantageous in scenarios where spatial and temporal data are critical, such as video or sequence-based image classification tasks, presenting a superior solution over simpler models like CNNs or LSTMs alone. ## Hyperparameter tuning using MSDBO approach
At last, the multi-strategy MSDBO-based hyperparameter selection process is performed to enhance the analysis outcomes of the CNN-BiLSTM method33. This method effectually explores the search space while balancing exploration and exploitation. Inspired by the dung beetle’s behaviour, this optimization technique adapts to intrinsic, high-dimensional optimization problems by utilizing multiple strategies for a more flexible and robust search process. The method can also effectually handle intrinsic and nonlinear parameter spaces better than conventional optimization techniques like grid or random search, which may get stuck in local minima. Furthermore, the ability of the method to fine-tune hyperparameters over various iterations results in an enhanced performance of the model, improving accuracy and generalization. Unlike gradient-based methods, MSDBO does not require gradients, making it more appropriate for problems where gradients may be challenging to compute. This makes it an ideal choice for optimizing DL methods with many hyperparameters, ensuring faster convergence and improved results compared to conventional tuning methods. ## Conclusion
This study develops a novel MSDBO-ODTHDLN model. The proposed MSDBO-ODTHDLN model presents visually challenged people with real-time and reliable object detection and tracking capabilities. At first, the MSDBO-ODTHDLN method employs an MF during the image pre-processing stage to enhance edge detection and ensure the clarity of objects in diverse environments. Next, the Mask R-CNN method is used for object detection to provide precise localization and identification of objects within the environment. In addition, the CapsNet model is used as the backbone for the feature extraction process to capture spatial hierarchies and intricate patterns. The hybrid method uniting a CNN-BiLSTM model is exploited for object detection and classification. Finally, the multi-strategy MSDBO-based hyperparameter selection process is performed to optimize the analysis outcomes of the CNN-BiLSTM model. A comprehensive experimental analysis was made to exhibit the enhanced performance of the presented MSDBO-ODTHDLN technique. The comparative results reported the improved characteristics of the MSDBO-ODTHDLN technique. The limitations of the MSDBO-ODTHDLN technique comprise challenges related to scalability across diverse environments and discrepancies in object appearances under diverse lighting or occlusion conditions. The model may also experience mitigated accuracy in highly cluttered or dynamic scenes due to restricted contextual awareness. Moreover, the reliance on specific datasets may affect generalizability to unseen domains or real-world deployment scenarios. Real-time processing on low-power devices remains a concern due to computational intensity. Furthermore, the technique lacks adaptability to user-specific navigation preferences or feedback mechanisms. Future works may integrate continual learning to adapt to new environments without retraining from scratch. Expanding the dataset diversity and integrating multi-sensory input could improve robustness. Enhancing interpretability and explainability of predictions for better user trust and understanding is also suggested.
