ABSTRACT To effectively suppress the horizontal vibration of a high-speed elevator car caused by
uncertainties such as the irregularity of guide rails and the piston wind in the hoistway, this paper proposes
a new variable universe fuzzy control method for semi-active guide shoes with magnetorheological (MR)
dampers based on the firefly algorithm and backpropagation (FA-BP) algorithm fuzzy neural network (FNN).
First, the dynamic model of a car system is constructed, and the FNN system based non-parametric model of
the MR damper is established with the original MR damper data collected from experiments. Then, a variable
universe fuzzy main controller is presented to control the input current of the MR damper. As for its variable
universe contraction-expansion factors, another FNN auxiliary controller is designed to accurately adjust
the universe. Furthermore, the FA-BP algorithm is utilized to train the FNN auxiliary controller. Finally,
the elevator experiment is carried out to analyze the characteristics of horizontal vibration, and simulation
tests are conducted using the measured excitation signal and two kinds of simulated excitation signals,
respectively. The results show that the proposed controller has a lower horizontal vibration acceleration,
tilt angle acceleration and other index values than the passive controller or the FNN controller, indicating
that the proposed control method can effectively suppress the horizontal vibration of a high-speed elevator.
This study can provide the technical foundation for intelligent vibration control of high-speed elevators.

control, vibration control.

I. INTRODUCTION

With the increasing number and height of high-rise buildings,
elevators with higher speeds are inevitably being developed.
However, the horizontal vibration of the lift car caused by
uncertainties such as irregular excitation of guide rail, piston
wind in the hoistway and the swinging of hoisting rope will
also intensify, which will greatly affect the riding comfort of
passengers and even cause severe reactions such as dizziness
and vomiting. In addition, the instruments on the car will also
be affected by vibration, which could cause life shortening
or even failure, eventually leading to more serious safety

control current is not more than 10 A, which currently makes
it an ideal choice for a damping element in semi-active guide
shoes [1], [2]. However, due to the non-Newtonian fluid
characteristics of MR fluid under the action of the magnetic
field, MR dampers have obvious strong nonlinear characteristics, such as hysteresis and yield, which greatly increase
the difficulty of establishing an accurate parametric model
and make it hard to calculate the ideal control current during

To this end, Stanway [3] constructed a Bingham plastic
model of an electrorheological damper. Although the model
is simple and easy to analyze, it cannot describe the hysteretic characteristics of the damper. Bouc and Wen [4], [5]
introduced hysteretic variables into the model, which can systematically describe the nonlinear hysteretic characteristics of
MR dampers. To further analyze the dynamic characteristics
of MR dampers, Spencer [6] proposed a phenomenological
model based on the Bouc-Wen model, which has greatly
advanced the parametric modeling of MR dampers. Although
the model can accurately describe the dynamic response of
the MR damper, there are too many parameters needed to be
measured and identified, which leads to the increased time
delays and makes it difficult to use for actual engineering.
After those, the parametric modeling still cannot be used
to establish a relatively accurate mathematical model with
fewer parameters [7]. Therefore, it is necessary to utilize new
modeling methods to ensure the accuracy of the model and
to be as simple and efficient as possible, thereby providing
an accurate and practical model basis for the next step in
researching vibration control methods.
In our previous study [8], the main factors causing the
horizontal vibration of high-speed elevator cars were rail
irregularity and unsteady air flow represented by piston wind.
The vibration under the influence of such complex coupling
factors determines the complexity and accuracy requirements
for the control method. Among the existing vibration control
methods, fuzzy control takes the both requirements above
into account and does not depend on the control model,
which has been widely studied by scholars. Kanjuro [9]
proposed a fuzzy-based multimodal vibration controller, and
the experimental results show that the controller has high
vibration suppression performance. Tairidis [10] proposed
a fuzzy logic vibration controller of intelligent plates and
used the genetic algorithm to improve the controller performance. However, the design of a fuzzy controller is highly
dependent on the expert experience. When there are deviations or even errors in the existing experience, the performance of the control system will be greatly degraded [11].
In addition, the accuracy of traditional fuzzy control is limited by fuzzy partition. If the number of fuzzy partitions is
added to improve the control accuracy, the steady-state error
will be increased and the reliability of the controller will
be reduced.
Therefore, to overcome the shortcomings of the above conventional fuzzy control, Li [12] proposed variable universe
fuzzy control, pointing out that the discourse universe of

fuzzy variables can be compact with decreasing system error,
and vice versa. To a certain extent, it solves the problem of
over-dependence on expert experience, and could precisely
densify the control of some target domains. The ability of
local targeted densifying control makes the variable universe
fuzzy method widely studied by scholars and successfully
applied to robot control [13], crane positioning control [14]
and other fields. However, the range of the discourse universe
depends on a parameter called the contraction-expansion
factor, which is not a variable that can be obtained by
expert experience. Moreover, in the current related research,
the control methods for the contraction-expansion factor are
not unified: mathematical functions, integral adjustment and
multi-population genetic algorithm are used in [15]–[17],
respectively, to describe and optimize the scaling of the factor.
Although they optimize the control effect to varying degrees,
the description of the factor has a crucial impact on the control
effect of the variable universe fuzzy control, and the control
methods mentioned above are still not accurate.
To this end, Pang [18] used fuzzy neural network (FNN)
to control the contraction-expansion factor, and improved the
control effect through the learning ability of FNN. However,
the commonly used backpropagation (BP) algorithm requires
a large number of iterations to train the network to approximate the ideal output. This seriously reduces the engineering
practicability of the controller due to the time and resources
required by the training. In addition, as a result of the poor
global optimization ability of the BP algorithm, once it falls
into the local optimum, it will need more iterations to jump
out of the pseudo-optimum point [19]. Therefore, the FNN
controller training needs improved algorithm to achieve a
higher convergence rate as far as possible on the guarantee
of the global optimization ability.
Therefore, based on the firefly algorithm and backpropagation (FA-BP) algorithm FNN, this paper proposes a new
variable universe fuzzy controller for MR damper semi-active
guide shoes, referred to as FAFNN-VFC. The originality of
this paper is as follows:
The non-parametric model of an MR damper is established based on the FNN system, and its accuracy is
verified by experimental comparison. This model does
not depend on the damper object and is more practical
in engineering.
• A variable universe fuzzy control method based on
FA-BP algorithm FNN is proposed. This method is suitable for complex excitation and control models, and the
accuracy and training speed of the controller are highly
improved by FAFNN, which provides an effective and
practical control method for the intelligent control of
complex vibration, such as the horizontal vibration of
high-speed elevators.
• A real elevator experiment is carried out to analyze the
horizontal vibration characteristics of the elevator car,
and the actual response data set is used for the controller
training. Furthermore, the effectiveness of the controller

B. NON-PARAMETRIC MODELING OF MR DAMPER

To suppress the vibration of the car in the above model,
the damping force needs to be adjusted in real time according
to the excitation and the displacement of the car. The internal
basic structure of the MR damper used in this paper is shown
in Figure 2. To continuously control the damping force,
the current in the coil should be adjusted continuously to
change the magnetic field, and to further modify the damping
coefficient of the MR damper.
However, the use of MR fluid also brings strong nonlinear
characteristics such as hysteresis and yield. If the conventional parametric modeling method is used, the identification
and measurement of the newly introduced parameters will
increase the time delay, which will reduce the practicability
and control effectiveness of the model. Compared with the
MR damper widely adopted in automobile suspension, the

MR damper for elevator guide shoes lacks typical products,
which usually needs independent design and customization.
This further increases the cost of parameter optimization and
trial-and-error in parametric modeling.
Therefore, this paper uses non-parametric modeling [20]
and introduces FNN to adaptively adjust the parameters,
so that the model does not depend on a specific damper.
It only needs to train FNN through measurement data to
quickly establish the response model of the target MR
damper. As shown in Figure 3, the model consists of two
parts: one is based on the FNN I to switch the output level
k of the damping force according to the control current input
into the coil; the other is based on the FNN II to estimate
the damping force u under standard current according to the
displacement and velocity of the MR damper piston. Finally,
the output damping force of the MR damper is obtained by
calculating f = k × u.
To verify the accuracy of the established model, experimental data were collected for comparative analysis in this
paper. Utilizing the sine wave with amplitude of 5 mm and
frequency of 2 Hz, 5 Hz and 10 Hz as the excitation sources,
respectively, the magnitude of MR damping force with control currents of 0, 1 A, 2 A, 3 A and 4 A was measured
repeatedly on the vibration mechanics testing platform. For
simplicity, this paper only shows the response comparison
between the MR damping force and displacement under three
kinds of control currents using typical 5 Hz frequency excitation, as shown in Figure 4.
It can be seen that the relationship curve of the established
model is highly consistent with the experimental response
and properly reflect the nonlinear characteristics of the MR
damper. This indicates that the FNN non-parametric model
can accurately simulate the actual output response of the MR
damper.

However, it should be pointed out that although FA has a
high global optimization efficiency, like most swarm intelligence algorithms, in the late iterations, its local convergence
speed slows and the optimization accuracy is insufficient.
As a result, when it is used as the training algorithm of
FNN alone, the final output performance is poor. Therefore,
the FA-BP algorithm is used in this paper to train FNN, and
its algorithm flow chart is shown in Figure 8.
In the early iterations, the FA is used for quick global
optimization. When iterating to the maximum number or the
output reaches the expected accuracy, the optimal firefly’s
position is substituted into the FNN as the optimized solution
for the initial parameter. Then, the BP algorithm is used to
learn and train, so that it can quickly approximate the ideal
output.

the FA-BP algorithm, the BP algorithm and the FA are used
to train the FNN auxiliary controller, respectively. The error
convergence comparison of the mean value from experimental results in multiple times is shown in Figure 9:
It can be seen that the FA-BP algorithm and the FA have
good convergence speeds at the initial stage of iterations.
However, after 20 iterations, the convergence speed of the
FA decreases, while the FA-BP algorithm and the BP algorithm still maintain a certain decline speed. At the end of
the iterations, the FA fails to make the network converge

V. CONCLUSION

To suppress the horizontal vibration of a high-speed elevator
lift car caused by uncertainties such as rail irregularity and

piston wind in the hoistway, a new variable universe fuzzy
controller based on FA-BP FNN is proposed for car system
using MR dampers. The main conclusions are as follows:
(1) The dynamic model of the car and the non-parametric
model of the MR damper based on the FNN system are
established. The accuracy of the MR damper model is verified
by comparison with experimental data, which shows that
the model can precisely simulate the horizontal vibration
response of the car.
(2) An experiment with a 7 m/s high-speed elevator is carried out, and the vibration characteristics of the experimental
data are analyzed to explore the requirements for controller
design. The results show that the car horizontal vibration
signal mainly presents as a low-amplitude high-frequency
signal and occasional strong-amplitude signal.
(3) Simulation tests are carried out by three kinds of
signals: the actual excitation, the random excitation and
the impulse excitation. The results show that the proposed
FAFNN-VFC can reduce the max and RMS values of the
car’s horizontal vibration acceleration by more than 62%,
and the tilt angle acceleration and required damping force
are reduced by more than 46% and 61%, respectively, which
are far better than the passive control and the FNN control.
The effectiveness of the proposed controller is fully proved,
and the control method is suitable for the horizontal vibration
control of high-speed elevators.
The proposed control method provides important guidance
for researchers who study vibration control for high-speed
elevators and other systems with similar vibration characteristics. In future research, we will further consider the
high-dimensional coupled vibration control of high-speed
elevators and the development of an actual controller.
