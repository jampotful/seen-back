# Explainable artificial intelligence with temporal convolutional networks for adverse weather condition detection in driverless vehicles

## Abstract
AbstractAutonomous driving has reached significant milestones in research and development over the last few decades. There is growing attention in the area as the utilization of autonomous vehicles (AVs) assurances safer and more environmentally-friendly transportation systems. Adverse environmental conditions like dust or sandstorms, fog, heavy snow, and rain conditions are unsafe limitations on the function of the cameras by decreasing visibility and influencing driving safety. These limitations affect the performance of tracking and detection methods applied in traffic surveillance systems and autonomous driving applications. Using the fast development in mathematically powerful artificial intelligence (AI) approaches, AVs could sense their atmosphere using higher accuracy, make safer real-world decisions, and work consistently without user intervention. Therefore, this paper proposes a Complex Data Analysis for Adverse Weather Detection in Autonomous Vehicles Using Explainable Artificial Intelligence (CDAAWD-AVXAI) approach. The CDAAWD-AVXAI approach improves the safety and reliability of AVs by ensuring robust weather detection. Initially, the presented CDAAWD-AVXAI approach applies image pre-processing by utilizing the median filter (MF) model to reduce noise and enhance the quality of input images. The CapsNet model is employed for feature extraction to recognize spatial hierarchies and capture intricate patterns from complex visual data. Moreover, the temporal convolutional network (TCN) model detects adverse weather conditions. To further enhance performance, the hyperparameter tuning of the TCN model is performed by implementing the improved dung beetle optimization (IDBO) model. Finally, the XAI using LIME provides transparency and interpretability, allowing stakeholders to understand the decision-making process behind weather condition predictions. The stimulation study of the CDAAWD-AVXAI technique is performed under the DAWN dataset. The experimental validation of the CDAAWD-AVXAI technique portrayed a superior accuracy value of 98.83% over existing methods.Keywords: Autonomous vehicles, Adverse weather detection, Dung beetle optimization, Temporal convolutional network, Explainable artificial intelligenceSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing

## Introduction
The world health organization (WHO) was alarmed by fatal injuries and death counts because of negligence, speeding, and intoxication1. Automation in transportation has some intriguing predictions, like reduction in fuel consumption, lane capacity improvement, reduced travel time, emissions reduction, and more2. Therefore, AVs have attained considerable attention as a novel transportation method in the past years. AVs, particularly self-driving cars, have significantly increased attention in intelligent transportation, AI, and computer vision (CV). No other invention, meanwhile, the vehicle’s manufacture, had such a dramatic effect on the automotive industry as self-driving vehicles3. These vehicles mainly depend on sensors and cameras. Samples contain radar, ultrasonic, light detection and ranging (LiDAR), and more that have some autonomous features, comprising penetrating carbon dioxide emissions by up to 10%, minimizing the usage of fuel, reducing road accidents, diminishing traffic congestion, and employing roads more effectively to enhance the transportation methods4. AVs might help study and manage traffic by collecting valuable and successful information. In addition to other road-related abilities, they can recognize non-static and static objects like pedestrians, road lanes, and vehicles5. Autonomous driving technology has substantially progressed over the last decade6. However, the safety and dependability of these methods under opposing weather conditions endure essential challenges. Opposing weather, like fog, rain, and snow, can substantially control the occurrence of environmental perception sensors, thus raising the operational risks of autonomous driving methods7.
In addition, opposing weather conditions are reasons for slippery road surfaces, complications in operating autonomous driving methods, and increasing braking distance. In opposing weather conditions, real-world recognition is essential for self-driving and traffic monitoring applications8. Object detection (OD) is significant for AVs as it guarantees security by avoiding and identifying difficulties in their pathway. Several studies have examined approaches for recognizing objects in AV, widely categorized into fully automated, manual, and semi-automated methods9. Nowadays, deep learning (DL) and machine learning (ML) models are critical for improving OD in AVs, particularly in opposing weather. Whereas classical AI depends on statistical learning, it handles boundaries because of limited flexibility to dynamic environments and manual feature engineering, necessitating overall hindering and retraining its efficiency in real-world applications10. DL and ML methods have exposed greater possibilities by challenging the difficulties modelled by adverse weather. The rapid growth of AI and ML technologies has paved the way for crucial advancements in the automotive industry, specifically in the development of AVs. As traffic-related accidents continue to pose a significant risk to human lives, the requirement for robust, automated systems to ensure safer driving conditions has become paramount. Weather conditions, however, remain a crucial challenge, as they mostly mitigate the performance of AV perception systems, resulting in potential hazards. Addressing these threats requires developing innovative solutions that adapt to dynamic environmental factors and provide reliable real-time weather condition detection. Utilizing AI, specifically through explainable models, can provide greater transparency, allowing systems to make informed decisions while maintaining user trust.
This paper proposes a Complex Data Analysis for Adverse Weather Detection in Autonomous Vehicles Using Explainable Artificial Intelligence (CDAAWD-AVXAI) approach. The CDAAWD-AVXAI approach improves the safety and reliability of AVs by ensuring robust weather detection. Initially, the presented CDAAWD-AVXAI approach applies image pre-processing by utilizing the median filter (MF) model to reduce noise and enhance the quality of input images. The CapsNet model is employed for feature extraction to recognize spatial hierarchies and capture intricate patterns from complex visual data. Moreover, the temporal convolutional network (TCN) model detects adverse weather conditions. To further enhance performance, the hyperparameter tuning of the TCN model is performed by implementing the improved dung beetle optimization (IDBO) model. Finally, the XAI using LIME provides transparency and interpretability, allowing stakeholders to understand the decision-making process behind weather condition predictions. The stimulation study of the CDAAWD-AVXAI technique is performed under the DAWN dataset. The key contribution of the CDAAWD-AVXAI technique is listed below.
The CDAAWD-AVXAI model applies an MF technique during the pre-processing stage to effectively mitigate noise caused by adverse weather conditions. This improves image quality before feature extraction, enhancing downstream processing. It confirms cleaner inputs for more reliable detection results.
The CDAAWD-AVXAI approach employs CapsNet for feature extraction, which preserves spatial hierarchies and captures pose associations within the data. This improves representation learning, particularly under complex weather distortions, and strengthens the technique’s capability to distinguish subtle visual patterns in adverse conditions.
The CDAAWD-AVXAI method integrates TCN into the model to accurately detect adverse weather conditions over time. Capturing temporal dependencies improves sequence-aware prediction performance, enhancing the approach’s robustness in dynamic weather scenarios.
The CDAAWD-AVXAI methodology incorporates the IDBO method to fine-tune its parameters effectively. This improves the convergence speed and averts local optima during training. As a result, classification accuracy is significantly enhanced under varying weather conditions.
The LIME-based explainable AI (XAI) is applied to provide clear insights into the model’s weather prediction decisions. This assists stakeholders in comprehending which features influenced specific outcomes and improves trust, transparency, and accountability in critical decision-making processes.
The CDAAWD-AVXAI approach introduces a novel hybrid DL framework that integrates CapsNet for spatial feature extraction, TCN for temporal pattern recognition, and IDBO for optimal parameter tuning. It also integrates LIME-based XAI to ensure the interpretability of predictions. This integration improves the accuracy and transparency of adverse weather condition detection systems.

## Related works
Aloufi et al.11 present a new multi-objective method utilizing only the AV camera sensing method. This method presents improved efficacy and possible performance advantages by incorporating image feature valuation, super-resolution generative adversarial network (SRGAN), and an enhanced form of You Only Look Once (YOLO) version 5. Moreover, the YOLO structure is enhanced to recognize six object classes (cyclist, car, motorcycle, bus, pedestrian truck) through adverse weather situations. Kondapally et al.12 introduced a model to describe and understand six average weather transition states: rainy to sunny, sunny to rainy, and others. Initially, this paper suggests a method for interpolating the average weather transition data utilizing a variational autoencoder and removing its spatial characteristics by VGG. Additionally, the temporal distribution of such spatial features is modelled by using a gated recurrent unit to classify the consistent transition state. Meftah et al.13 create considerable support for the domain of AV obstacle avoidance by presenting a new method that uses the generative adversarial networks (GANs) approach for data augmentation. Using a GAN approach to make training datasets and incorporate images describing difficult weather conditions is essential in improving the precision of the DL methods. Three DL techniques, VGG16, ResNet50, and ResNet101 transfer learning trained on these databases before or after using the data augmentation methods. Li et al.14 implement a new field adaptive OD structure for autonomous driving in cloudy conditions. This model utilizes either an image-level or object-level version to reduce the domain adaptation in an image’s object presence and style. The research also presents original adversarial gradient reversal layers to conduct adversarial mining for the complex case and domain discrepancy. Furthermore, this work presented an auxiliary field by data augmentation for enforcing novel domain-level metric regularization. In15, a DL method is accomplished to recognize dangerous road and outside weather situations, allowing a rapid response to novel environments and conditions. The work attains this by presenting an enhanced taxonomy and label hierarchy for an advanced adverse weather database, renaming it with a new semi-automated labelling pipeline. Applying the newly presented hierarchy and dataset, the work training RECNet, a DL approach for the classification of weather conditions from a solitary frame of RGB.
Chen et al.16 proposed an orchestrated method which permits real-world OD. In an edge-calculating manner, the YOLO-based OD method utilizes thermal imageries to correctly identify objects in poor-lighting situations. Moreover, an attention mechanism enhances the performance of the systems without considerably improving model complexities. An unidentified object detector spontaneously labels and classifies unidentified objects without accurate monitoring on edge tools; however, the roadside unit (RSU)-based mechanism is established to upgrade class labels and guarantee safe driving knowledge for AVs. Zhang et al.17 developed a new method to detect objects at various stages using sensors and DL-based solutions in the traffic circle. The recommended model increases the efficacy of single-stage object detectors. The enhanced architecture is derived from the one-stage object-detection method, including numerous lightweight backbones. Moreover, attention mechanisms are combined to refine the process of OD additionally. Madaram et al.18 propose a war strategy optimization (WSO) approach for effectual electric vehicle charge scheduling, enhancing waiting time and charging costs compared to existing methods. Golam et al.19 developed BlackIceNet, a multimodal CNN-based methodology that integrates visual, acoustic, and sensor data for accurate and early black ice detection to improve road safety. Nadana Ravishankar et al.20 developed the white shark optimizer with optimal dl-based effective unmanned aerial vehicles communication and scene classification (WSOODL-UAVCSC) technique for optimized UAV clustering and accurate scene classification utilizing DL and metaheuristic algorithms. Nagavarapu et al.21 developed a vision-based algorithm incorporating CNN-LSTM for rain removal and lane marker detection to improve AV navigation in rainy conditions. Selvarajan22 reviews and analyzes recent improvements in bio-inspired optimization techniques and assesses their efficiency in solving complex engineering problems. Khan et al.23 propose a Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based traffic congestion detection model improved with XAI for improved accuracy, adaptability, and interpretability in smart cities. Nazat, Li, and Abdallah24 introduce an end-to-end XAI framework to interpret AI-based anomaly detection in VANETs, improving transparency, trust, and feature explainability in AV networks. Othman and Ahmed25 improve real-time image processing in AVs utilizing EfficientNet and MobileNet techniques for enhanced accuracy and speed under diverse driving conditions. Thakur and Mishra26 analyze integrating multi-sensor technologies with AI-based DL methods for enhanced obstacle detection in AVs. Using DL, Khairnar et al.27 developed a modified CANet for accurate and effectual road scene segmentation in unstructured driving scenarios.
Despite crucial progress in DL-based AV perception models, various challenges still exist. Many techniques are restricted by dependence on single-modality data, mitigating robustness in adverse weather. GAN-based augmentation enhances performance but may introduce artefacts that affect model reliability. Methodologies, namely GRL and auxiliary fields, lack generalization across unknown domains. Diverse OD techniques comprising YOLO variants encounter difficulty with class imbalance and small object detection. Furthermore, real-time constraints are often overlooked in intrisic architectures with attention or hybrid backbones. Incorporating XAI mostly remains shallow, using actionable insights into the AV decision-making process. Comprehensive solutions incorporating multimodal fusion, XAI, and lightweight architectures have also not been explored adequately.

## Image Pre-processing using MF
Initially, the presented CDAAWD-AVXAI technique applies image pre-processing using an MF model to reduce noise and enhance the quality of input images28. This model is chosen due to its efficiency in removing salt-and-pepper noise while conserving crucial edge details. Unlike mean or Gaussian filters, which may blur critical features, MF maintains the integrity of structural data, which is substantial for precise feature extraction in weather-distorted images. Its non-linear approach makes it specifically appropriate for adverse weather conditions, where noise is mostly non-uniform and sharp transitions exist. Furthermore, MF is computationally efficient, making it ideal for real-time applications. Its simplicity and robustness against noise ensure cleaner inputs, thereby enhancing the overall performance of the subsequent DL models.
The MF is a real image pre-processing approach applied in adverse weather conditions recognition for AVs, mainly to minimize noise and improve image quality in challenging weather conditions. It operates by substituting every pixel value within the image using the median value of the closed pixels inside an identified window. This removes impulsive noise, like snow or rain, which may change sensor data. During AVs, utilizing an MF guarantees that the data connected to the system is cleaner and more valid for analyzing weather conditions. By enhancing the clarity of the image, the MF improves the precision of the following methods for classifying and detecting adverse weather conditions, namely snow, fog, or rain.

## CapsNet-based feature extraction
The CapsNet model is employed for feature extraction to recognize spatial hierarchies and capture intricate patterns from complex visual data29. This method is chosen due to its capability to preserve spatial hierarchies and associations between parts of an image, which is crucial for accurate weather condition detection. Unlike the conventional convolutional neural network (CNN) model, focusing majorly on finding patterns without considering their spatial alignment, CapsNet captures both spatial and pose data, enabling better handling of complex, varying image conditions. This makes the chosen technique specifically effectual in detecting objects under diverse weather conditions, where features may appear in various orientations. Furthermore, CapsNet’s dynamic routing mechanism enhances representation learning by highlighting relevant features, mitigating the risk of losing crucial data in adverse weather contexts. ## Weather detection using TCN
Moreover, the TCN model detects adverse weather conditions30. This technique is chosen because it can effectively capture long-range dependencies in sequential data. Unlike recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, the utilized model employs convolutional layers for processing temporal sequences, allowing for rapid training and parallelization. This characteristic of the model plays a crucial part in making the method appropriate for real-time weather detection. Furthermore, varying input lengths are handled by the TCN technique and also assist in giving improved stability during backpropagation, mitigating the vanishing gradient problem mostly faced in RNNs. This capability of the method to capture both short- and long-term patterns in data assists in improving the accuracy of detecting adverse weather conditions and giving more appropriate results. The TCN is built upon the 1D-CNN, combining residual module, causal convolution, and dilated convolution. This structure efficiently takes correlations inside the data, making it suitable for time-series prediction tasks. Causal convolution is a unidirectional convolution approach, which guarantees the output at a particular point of time in the preceding layer relies exclusively on the input from the present layer and the prior point of time. Therefore, the receptive area of causal convolution develops linearly by improving the depth of the network. Nevertheless, when addressing more extended sequences of historical data, the training problem increases as the network depth multiplies. ## IDBO-based parameter tuning
To further enhance performance, the hyperparameter tuning of the TCN model is performed by utilizing the IDBO method31. This model is chosen for its robust capability to effectively navigate complex, high-dimensional search spaces. IDBO specializes in balancing exploration and exploitation, ensuring a thorough search for optimal parameters. This adaptive behaviour of the method assists in avoiding local minima, making the technique more dependable than standard optimization methods in DL applications. This technique is appropriate for multimodal optimization issues, namely tuning DL techniques, where it can find optimum outcomes with fewer iterations. Additionally, it is computationally effectual and proper for models with massive hyperparameters. Its efficiency in diverse scenarios confirms enhanced performance and generalization of the model. ## XAI using LIME
Finally, the XAI using LIME provides transparency and interpretability, allowing stakeholders to understand the decision-making process behind weather condition predictions32. This mode is chosen for its capacity to give clear, interpretable explanations for complex models, making it appropriate for enhancing transparency in DL-based weather prediction systems. LIME specializes in generating local approximations of model behaviour, giving information into individual anticipations. This model-agnostic approach allows it to work with diverse ML techniques, confirming flexibility. The simplicity and interpretability of LIME assist the stakeholders in comprehending how specific features influence model decisions. The model also emphasizes supremacy in explaining black-box models, particularly in time-sensitive applications such as weather detection, assisting in enhanced decision-making transparency.
The LIME XAI model is used to offer wide-ranging explanations to predict what is provided by a black box method. The critical theory of LIME includes locally approximating the black box functionalities process using a strong, clear glass box approach, improving interpretation ability. This model gives perturbations by sensibly activating and deactivating particular superpixels through the instance. This model aims to describe the outcomes in a human-readable way and to determine the importance of the continual superpixels within the predicted examples. It improves the method, encourages transparencies and interpretability, and inspires assurance in DL methods by clarifying how the input features of a black box process affect its predictions. The first stage of using the LIME model on the new samples is to shatter it downwards into superpixels. This superpixel regulates the granularities of regions’ segmentation. A linked group of pixels with similar positions and colours is termed superpixels. The segmentation given by these methods is finer and more detailed, making it likely to recognize the critical areas to predict the result precisely.
The XAI is applied to provide local justifications. This local justification is a method offered for each prediction distinctly. It contains dual key points: (i) calculating how every input affected the output, and (ii) expressing this result in a human-understandable way. However, each of the 3 XAI techniques acknowledged contains this fundamental point, and their methods and succeeding outcomes vary. For example, since the SHAP model processes shapely values through the complete area of the input images, the importance of the amalgamation of DL features between regions is vague. In LIME, the estimated outcomes of a fundamental classifier are not always correct. It just specifies whether areas of the sample influenced the likelihood scores of the last predictions. Therefore, it is essential to present prediction clarifications that associate all 3 XAI models.

## Conclusion
In this article, a CDAAWD-AVXAI approach is introduced. The CDAAWD-AVXAI approach improves the safety and reliability of AVs by ensuring robust weather detection. Initially, the presented CDAAWD-AVXAI technique applies image pre-processing using an MF model to reduce noise and enhance the quality of input images. The CapsNet model is employed for feature extraction to recognize spatial hierarchies and capture intricate patterns from complex visual data. Moreover, the TCN model is utilized to detect adverse weather conditions. To further enhance performance, the hyperparameter tuning of the TCN model is performed by implementing the IDBO method. Finally, the XAI using LIME provides transparency and interpretability, allowing stakeholders to understand the decision-making process behind weather condition predictions. The stimulation study of the CDAAWD-AVXAI technique is performed under the DAWN dataset. The experimental validation of the CDAAWD-AVXAI technique portrayed a superior accuracy value of 98.83% over existing methods. The limitations of the CDAAWD-AVXAI technique comprise restricted evaluation on a single dataset, which may not fully depict the diversity of real-world weather scenarios. The absence of user-centered validation restricts insight into practical deployment challenges in autonomous systems. Furthermore, the model lacks adaptability testing under varying sensor configurations or degraded input quality. The method does not explore cross-domain transferability or multilingual environmental inputs. Real-time processing capabilities under constrained hardware conditions remain unexplored. Integration with broader vehicular decision-making systems was not addressed. Expanding the scope of testing, integrating multi-source inputs, and involving end-user feedback could enhance robustness and applicability.
