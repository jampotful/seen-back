ABSTRACT
Inspired by the vibration mitigation effect of kangaroo’s legs, a novel bio-inspired
kangaroo leg structure (BKLS) is proposed for low-frequency vibration isolation. The
BKLS is composed of two main rods with different lengths (simulating the calf bone
and thigh bone), two linear springs (simulating the internal and external muscles) and
two auxiliary rods. The dynamic model is established based on the Lagrange principle
to describe the nonlinear effects of BKLS, which are mainly contributed by the
internal and external springs and the rotary joints. Experiments were carried out to
verify the theoretical predictions and demonstrate that the proposed BKLS indeed
possess superior low-frequency isolation performance without sacrificing the carrying
capacity, which can effectively suppress vibration with frequencies higher than 1.06
Hz. Regardless of the isolation mass and the excitation conditions, the vibration
isolation performance can be significantly improved by selecting appropriate
structural parameters. The innovative and simple BKLS provides an effective method
for low-frequency vibration isolation.

Keywords: Bio-inspired structure, Low-frequency vibration isolation, Nonlinear
dynamics, High-static and low-dynamic stiffness

1 Introduction

Vibration control is one of the most urgent issues in many engineering structures,
especially in the fields of aerospace, construction and precision instruments [1, 2].
From the perspective of passive vibration isolation, one of the most common methods
is to use the traditional linear isolator to suppress vibration, which is efficient in
isolating excitation frequencies greater than

2 times the natural frequency [3, 4].

In order to improve the low-frequency isolation performance, the isolator have to
reduce the natural frequency of the system by decreasing the stiffness, resulting in low
carrying capacity cannot be supported the isolated mass. Therefore, the carrying
capacity and low-frequency isolation are always contradictory in linear isolators [5].

To solve the engineering requirements, nonlinear isolation systems with quasi-zero
stiffness (QZS) characteristics have been proposed and developed to obtain excellent
low-frequency isolation performance [6-8]. The QZS isolator has high static and low
dynamic stiffness (HSLDS) properties. Specifically, the QZS isolator has a large
carrying capacity in static equilibrium and low dynamic stiffness during vibration,
which solves the contradiction between carrying capacity and low-frequency isolation
[9]. The HSLDS isolators can be designed in various ways, such as inclined springs
[10, 11], bending beams [12, 13], cam rollers [14, 15], magnets [16, 17], origami [18,
19] and other structures [20, 21]. Carrella et al. [22, 23] designed a HSLDS isolator
composed of vertical springs and oblique springs, and confirmed that the isolation
performance of HSLDS systems is better than that of linear systems. The
low-frequency bandgap can be achieved by the snap-through behavior of the negative
stiffness beam and the bending dominated behavior of positive stiffness beam [24, 25]
and optimizing the geometry of the bending beam [26]. Zhou et al. [27, 28] and Zou
et al. [29] developed a series of HSLDS isolators using cam mechanisms and
optimized design parameters to suppress multi-directional vibrations. Paralleling

magnetic negative stiffness mechanism with positive stiffness has also been proved to
be one of an effective way to resolve the inherent contradiction between high load
capacity and low-frequency vibration isolation ability [30, 31]. The origami
mechanism with unique geometric nonlinear characteristics is used to achieve HSLDS
characteristics and excellentr low-frequency isolation performance [32, 33]. However,
most of HSLDS isolation systems can only appear in a relatively narrow displacement
region and apply to small amplitude vibrations.

Certain parts of creature usually has excellent vibration mitigation properties after
thousands of years of natural evolution, which provides an energetic perspective for
isolators design [34-37]. Jing and his co-authors [38-40] proposed a series of bionic
limb-like or X-shaped structures and systematically investigated their superior
nonlinear stiffness and damping to achieve low frequency and wide band vibration
isolation. More advantages in engineering applications due to the simplicity and
flexible tuning ability of the X-shaped structure. Inspired by the felid paws and the
body topology of cockroaches, a novel bio-inspired isolation structure consists of
multiple rods with different length and linear springs is developed and systematically
studied for low-frequency vibration isolation [41, 42]. Feng et al. [43] and Niu et al.
[44] present a unique human body inspired passive vibration isolation system. The
nonlinear characteristics can significantly improve the isolation performance at the
low frequency. Sun et al. [45] and Fang et al. [46] proposed a bio-inspired variable
stiffness structure to suppress vibrations that may be generated by on-orbit spacecraft.
Inspired by the neck of a bird, Deng et al. [47] and Sun et al. [48] established a
multilayer vibration isolation system, which can always maintain a small dynamic
stiffness to achieve low frequency effective isolation and high stability during large
amplitude vibration. The above bio-inspired isolators usually have a wider HSLDS
range and are easy to manufacture due to the simple structure. Besides, the carrying

capacity and isolation performance of bio-inspired isolators can be flexibly adjusted
and disassembled.

The average adult kangaroo is 1.4 m tall and weighs 50-70 kg. All kangaroos have one
thing in common: strong and powerful long hind legs. The hind legs are the balancer
for kangaroo walking and jumping and the main support limb for standing. It is with
the unique structure of the hind legs that kangaroos can jump up to 4 m, as far as 13 m,
beating and running at more than 50 km/h. The kangaroo's hind leg structure plays an
important role in maintaining movement stability and mitigating impacts and
vibrations during these beats and runs. The legs need to not only support the entire
body mass, but also adapt to the complicated change in body's center of mass during
beating and running by adjusting the internal and external muscle strength of the legs.
It is envisioned that the bionic kangaroo leg structure may serve as a possible
excellent candidate for vibration isolation. Here, a novel isolator is proposed inspired
by the kangaroo leg structure with simulating the leg bone and internal and external
muscles via two rods and two springs, respectively. A dynamic model is established to
predict the isolation performance, which is then verified by experiments. Both
experiments and theoretical predictions show that the proposed BKLS has excellent
low-frequency isolation performance. In addition, the influence of structural
parameters on the nonlinear equivalent stiffness and isolation performance of BKLS
is systematically studied.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, the BKLS topology design is
described in detail and the geometric relationship is analyzed. The static and
dynamics models are established in Section 3. The influences of parameters on
nonlinearity and isolation performance are comprehensively discussed in Section 4. In
Section 5, a prototype is fabricated and tested to verify the effectiveness of
low-frequency vibration isolation. The main conclusions are drawn in Section 6.

2 Design of bio-inspired kangaroo leg structure (BKLS)
2.1 Topological structure

The kangaroo usually jumps during its walking, and the jumping frequency is about
100 per/min. The vibration and impact during walking and jumping are mainly
mitigated by the unique structure of their legs. Therefore, the kangaroo's legs play an
important role in isolating vibration and impact, which is attributed to the good
stiffness adjustment ability and the beneficial nonlinear damping characteristics of the
leg structures. Muscle strength is adjusted by varying the angle between the leg bones
and the degree of muscle tension, and nonlinear damping is constrained by the friction
between the joints. To simplify the kangaroo leg structure as much as possible, the leg
structure is simply divided into the thigh bone, calf bone, internal and external
muscles, and knee joint, as shown in Fig. 1. The kangaroo leg can be abstracted as the
rod-spring system in Fig. 1(c). The longer rod (Rod 1) represents the calf bone; the
shorter rod (Rod 2) represents the thigh bone; the Spring 1 and Spring 2 represent the
internal and external muscles, respectively; the red rods (Rod 3 and Rod 4) are the
springs auxiliary rod; and the yellow dot is the joint.

Considering the stability and reliability requirements, a three-dimensional
bio-inspired kangaroo leg structure (BKLS) is obtained by installing four legs
symmetrically, as illustrated in Fig. 1(d), which limits the motion of the platform in
the vertical direction. When the kangaroo leg structure is designed as a vibration
isolation system, the body is replaced by the support platform together with the
carrying mass. The internal and external linear springs are the only elastic elements,
but the recovery force in the vertical direction is nonlinear due to the geometric
nonlinearity of the BKLS. The proposed BKLS is a polyhedral structure with an
internal cavity, so the pneumatic actuators, active actuators or other component

devices can be assembled internally to apply the semi-active or active control required
for further optimization.

4 Parameter influence and discussions

The BKLS can achieve a variety of carrying capacity and excellent isolation
performance by flexible design and adjustment of structural parameters. The effects of
structural parameters on carrying capacity, nonlinearity and isolation performance are

systematically analyzed and discussed in this section. The main parameters discussed
include non-dimensional bone length ratio β, non-dimensional connection length of
Spring 2 α, non-dimensional connection length of Spring 1 ε, initial angle between
Rod 1(CG) and the horizontal line θ0 and non-dimensional rotational damping ĉ2 .
And the other parameters are fixed in the following discussion: the geometric
parameters are

Lˆ0 = 1, Lˆ1 = 6,  = 1,  = 1,  = 1,  0 = 0.295 , Kˆ 1 = 0.4, Kˆ 2 = 0.4 ; the

isolation mass is M̂ =1; the non-dimensional damping is set to ĉ1 =3, ĉ2 =1 and the
excitation amplitude is ẑ0 =1. The effect of the parameters on the isolation
performance is evaluated by the transmissibility Td expressed by Eq. (37).

For the given isolated mass and excitation amplitude, the distance between the
platform and the base h1 can be obtained by Eq. (20) when the stiffness is 0. At the
same time, the resonance peak and starting frequency of effective isolation are solved
by Eq. (37) and submitted to Eq. (20), consequently the minimum distance between
the platform and the base h2 during vibration can be obtained. when h1<h2, the
structure will be unstable due to the negative stiffness, so the structure can effectively
work only when h1≥h2. The lowest starting frequency of effective isolation can be
achieved when h1=h2, and the corresponding parameter is the optimal one.

4.1 Effect of bone length ratio β
The restoring force and stiffness of the BKLS under different β are shown in Fig. 4.
The force-displacement curve is obtained from Eq. (20) and the stiffness curve in Fig.
4(b) can be obtained by deriving the displacement. It can be seen that with the
increase of β, the force-displacement curve becomes gentle, and the working range of
HSLDS characteristics becomes wider but the load-carrying capacity is weakened.

Fig.4. Effects of different bone length ratio β on (a) force-displacement curve and (b)
stiffness-displacement curve.

The dependence of the displacement transmissibility of the BKLS on the bone length
ratio β is shown in Fig. 5(a). When β=1, the resonance frequency is approximately 0.6
and the resonance peak is larger, about 2.15. For β=1.6, the resonance frequency is
lowered to 0.55 and the resonance peak also gets smaller, about 2. Both the resonance
peak and the transmissibility decrease with the increase of β, i.e., the resonance peak
is suppressed and the attenuation effect is better. The isolation frequency band widens
as β decreases, as shown in Fig. 5(b). The BKLS achieves the lowest starting
frequency of effective isolation fs=0.40 when β=2.35. Significantly, the platform offset
becomes smaller as β increases (Fig. 5(c)), since the asymmetric effects of the
restoring force and damping force are weakened. The platform offset helps to
determine whether the excitation amplitude will cause structural instability or even
collapse. However, the change in the offset is very small and has little effect on the
isolation performance. Overall, the larger bone length ratio β is, the better the
isolation performance of BKLS is. Since the kangaroo's leg structure not only needs
to mitigate the vibrations during movement, but also requires sufficient stiffness to
support the mass of the entire body, the isolation performance and carrying capacity
should be fully considered to reasonably design parameters β.

Fig.5. Effects of different bone length ratio β on (a) transmissibility, (b) the starting
frequency of effective isolation and (c) the offset of platform.

4.2 Effect of non-dimensional Spring 2 connection length α

Fig. 6 shows the changes of both the restoring force and the stiffness of the BKLS
with respect to the displacement under various α. It can be observed that the effective
working range of the BKLS remains almost constant with the increase of α, but the
carrying capacity and stiffness increase, which leads to a narrower HSLDS range.
Therefore, a smaller α is recommended when the carrying capacity is sufficient.

Fig. 6. Effects of different non-dimensional Spring 1 connection length α on (a)
force-displacement curve and (b) stiffness-displacement curve.

Fig. 7 displays the transmissibility and the starting frequency of effective isolation fs
under various α. The resonance peak decrease when α reduces from 3 to 1, indicating
that a smaller α is conducive to vibration attenuation. The fs reduces with
the decrease of α and the minimum fs is 0.52, as shown in Fig. 7(b). In Fig. 7(c), the
platform offset is stable about -0.035 when the excitation frequency larger than fs,
indicating the stable value is less affected by α. It is worth noting although the
influence trends of α and β are similar, the adjustment operation of α is obviously
more convenient than that of β. Therefore, α is recommended as an on-site
controllable parameter to quickly adjust the isolation performance.

The restoring force and stiffness of the BKLS under different ε are shown in Fig. 8.

carrying capacity and stiffness are, but it has little effect on the HSLDS range.

The effect of α on transmissibility and the starting frequency of effective isolation is
investigated in Figs. 9(a) and (b), respectively. With a larger ε, both the starting
frequency of effective isolation and the resonant peak increases on account of the
increasing nonlinear stiffness and the unchanged nonlinear damping. In addition, it

can be seen from the platform offset in Fig. 9(c) that has little effect on ε. Like α, ε

nonlinearity of the BKLS is significantly enhanced with the increase of θ0. Therefore,

reduces to 0.58, which is beneficial to low-frequency isolation of the BKLS. It can
also be seen from Fig. 11(c) that a larger θ0 significantly increases the platform offset
near the resonance frequency. Therefore, a smaller θ0 is conducive to cope with
large-amplitude excitation and improve the isolation performance of the BKLS when
the carrying capacity is sufficient.

4.5 Effect of nonlinear damping ĉ2

The nonlinear damping of the BKLS is mainly provided by the rotational friction of
the joints. Fig. 12 represents the transmissibility and platform offset under different
non-dimensional rotational damping ĉ2 . It can be seen that a larger damping ĉ2 is
beneficial to suppress both the resonance peak and the offset peak, although the
isolation effect is little weakened at the high frequency vibration. In addition, the
change in damping ĉ2 has no effect on the starting frequency of effective isolation.
Therefore, the resonance peak can be suppressed to broaden the effective isolation
frequency band by increasing the damping.

5 Experiments
The experimental prototype was fabricated according to the above parameter analysis.
Fig. 13 shows the experimental setup for testing the vibration isolation performance

of the BKLS. The BKLS was made of upper and lower polylactic acid panels,
aluminum rods and ball bearings. The springs were connected at the joints by the sub
rivets. The structural parameters are L0=60 mm, L1=360 mm, β=1, α=1, ε=1, θ0=0.28π,
K1=400 N/m, K2=200 N/m, and the vibration isolation mass M=1.2 kg. The BKLS was
fixed on an electromechanical shaker (LDS V721). The standard weights were added
to the platform and secured to the plate with double-sided tape to avoid dropping
during test. Two identical accelerometers (1A110E) were attached on the base and the
platform by magnets to monitor the excitation and the output acceleration responses,
respectively. The excitation signal was generated by the signal generation (VT-9002),
and then amplified by power amplifier (LDS LPA1000). Finally, the acceleration
signals were measured and acquired by the dynamic signal acquisition system
(DH5922D). The acceleration transmissibility for evaluating the vibration isolation
performance is defined by aout/ain, where ain and aout are the input and output
accelerations, respectively.

Fig. 13. The experimental setup for testing the vibration isolation performance of the
BKLS.

Fig. 14 displays the experimental and theorical transmissibility under various
excitation amplitude z0. It is shown that the starting critical frequencies are 1.50 Hz,

1.18 Hz and 1.05 Hz under z0=2.0 mm, 3.2 mm, 4.4 mm, respectively, indicating that
the BKLS isolator can effectively suppress the low-frequency and large amplitude
vibration. Overall, there is good agreement between the experimental data and the
theoretical prediction, which proves the validity of the theoretical modeling and the
effectiveness of low-frequency vibration isolation of the proposed structure. The
experimental transmissibility is slightly higher than the theoretical value in the high
frequency band. It is maybe caused by a small transverse deflection of the platform
during vertical moving in the experiments, which increases the damping at the joints,
and thus weakens the isolation effect. It can be found from Fig. 14(d) that with the
increase of the excitation amplitude z0, the resonant peak gradually decreases,
meanwhile the starting frequency of effective isolation fs slightly shifts to the left with
the isolation frequency band becoming wider. This phenomenon indicates that the
larger excitation amplitude leads to stronger nonlinear effects, which is beneficial to
the BKLS isolator.

Fig. 15 displays the experimental and theoretical transmissibility of BKLS under
different spring stiffness. The starting frequency of effective isolation fs of BKLS
decreases from 1.93 Hz to 1.14 Hz with the spring stiffness decreases, i.e., the
effective isolation frequency band is widened. These experimental results are
consistent with the theoretical ones. Since the nonlinear stiffness of the BKLS isolator
can be controlled by the spring stiffness, the BKLS is able to quickly adapt to
different loads by changing the spring stiffness, and it is not difficult to do, such as
quickly replacing different stiffness springs, which is significant beneficial for many
engineering practices.

6 Conclusions

In this paper, a novel bio-inspired kangaroo leg structure (BKLS) for low-frequency
isolation is proposed, which consists of a simple rod-spring system to simulate the leg
bones’ movement and the muscles’ extension of the kangaroo legs. Based on the
Lagrange equation, both the static and the dynamic models of the BKLS are
established. A prototype of BKLS was fabricated and experiments were carried out to
verify the theoretical predictions and the excellent low-frequency vibration isolation.
In addition, the influence of parameters on the carrying capacity and isolation
performance is comprehensively investigated. The following conclusions can be
drawn:
1) The dynamic analytical model is built to predicate the carrying capacity and the
isolation performance of the proposed BKLS as an isolator, which shows good
agreement with the experimental results. The theoretical model is benefit to find the
effective range and the optimal value of geometric parameters of the structure, in
order to ensure BKLS always working stably and showing excellent isolation
performance.
2) Both the experimental and the theoretical results indicate that the proposed
structure possess excellent low-frequency vibration isolation without sacrificing the
carrying capacity. The starting frequency of effective isolation can be reduced to as
low as 1.06 Hz without causing large resonant peak and frequency jump phenomena.
3) The proposed BKLS is simple in structure and flexible in adjustment, so the
required isolation performance and carrying capacity can be quickly achieved by
adjusting the parameters. Specifically, reducing the bone length ratio and the length of
the rod can enhance the effective stiffness of the BKLS. A smaller initial angle of Rod
1 and spring connection length can effectively reduce the starting frequency of

effective isolation and the resonance peak, which can be operate on-site to improve
the adaptability of the structure.
