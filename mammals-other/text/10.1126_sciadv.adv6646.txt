Enabling an ultraefficient lithium-selective construction through electric field-assisted ion control.

# Enabling an ultraefficient lithium-selective construction through electric field–assisted ion control

## Abstract
AbstractMembranes with precise ion transport behaviors are regarded as an alternative for lithium (Li) extraction from water streams. Current membranes demonstrate limited viability due to the lack of efficient Li+-selective architectures. We propose an electric field–assisted ion control hypothesis in reinforcing ultraefficient Li+-selective membranes, in which an ionized zeolitic imidazolate framework layer (Q-PEI@ZIF) is constructed via polyethylenimine (PEI) in situ confinement conversion and subsequent quaternization of 2,3-epoxypropyl trimethyl ammonium chloride. In electrodialysis at 5 milliampere per square centimeter, the resulting membrane Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM shows that the ion permeation rates follow the order of K+ ~ Li+ > Na+ > Ca2+ ~ Mg2+, corresponding to 0.31, 0.30, 0.25, 0, and 0 mole per square meter per hour in 120 minutes, respectively. With a 25-millimolar Li+/Mg2+ mixed solution, it exhibits an unprecedented Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity of 20,000 and 99.99% purity of Li+ product in 120 minutes. This study expands the hypothesis of electric field–assisted ion control in enabling an ultraefficient Li+-selective construction.

## INTRODUCTION
Lithium (Li+) recovery from water streams is a sustainable method for overcoming Li shortages and reducing the environmental impacts of exploitation from mineral ores (1). Membrane technologies, recognized for their efficient, low-energy, and scalable separation processes, have been extensively explored in harvesting ion resources from water streams (2–4). Among these, electrodialysis and nanofiltration are two of the most prominent methods for ion separation. In particular, electro-driven membranes, equipped with ion exchange capacity and the ability to mitigate challenges such as high-concentration gradients and osmotic pressure (which commonly affect nanofiltration membranes), have been increasingly developed and integrated into electrochemical systems for ion separation (5, 6). To enable ion recovery from water streams, research efforts are focused on constructing membranes with high-target ion selectivity. These membranes are prepared mainly via controlling membrane structures (e.g., pore size sieving) or functional groups (e.g., surface electrostatic repulsion and binding affinity) to exploit the interaction between hydrated ions and membranes (7). For example, membrane modification with positively charged layers on the surface (8, 9), construction of a membrane structure with controllable pore size (10), or fabrication of the membrane materials with high binding affinity groups (11), have been used to improve monovalent cation selectivity. However, current membranes still suffer from an extremely low efficiency in separating Li+ from other coexisting ions, such as the similar cations of Mg2+ and Na+ (12, 13). In addition, these ion-selective mechanisms tend to impede ion throughput, resulting in a low Li+ permeation rate and thus the permeability-selectivity trade-offs (14, 15).
With nanometer-sized pores and a powerful capacity for multifunctional construction, metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) open an avenue for ion-selective transport. Recent studies have explored them to be an ideal material that allows Li+ transport while rejecting other mixed cations in electro-driven processes (16–18). Nevertheless, it is limited in the separation of ions with similar hydration radius if only based on size sieving effects of MOFs (19). Besides, it is technically challenging to fabricate defect-free and stable MOF layers on a membrane surface (20). In addition, because of the Gibbs-Donnan effect, the insufficiency of ion exchange sites in MOFs leads to slow ion transport (21). Furthermore, ion transport through the non-charged cavities of MOFs may cause hydrolysis issues (22). Thus far, MOF-based membranes have had limited success in effective ion selectivity in electro-driven systems.
Recently, hybrid MOF-based composite membranes that combine the easy processing of functional polymers and tunable pores of MOFs have been explored to equip them with well-defined crystal structures and coordination functional groups (23–25). To some extent, MOFs can be constructed on the membrane surface via an in situ growth method. However, this structure is typically thick and easily disintegrates because of the stacking of the crystals (18), thereby largely limiting the ion permeation rate while suffering from stability issues (26). In our recent work, a zeolitic imidazolate framework (ZIF)–based anion exchange membrane with monovalent anion selectivity was facilely fabricated by coating a polydopamine on ZIFs under an alternating current electric field (27).
Theoretically, ion species exhibit unique ionic charge density and corresponding ion hydration shells, resulting in specific interactions with surrounding molecules/materials (7). Under an electric field, these ions gain kinetic energy, enabling precise control via an external electric field (5, 28). Inspired by biological cell membranes, where ion channels enable rapid and selective transport via tunable charge density, geometric filtering (pore size and charge compatibility) (29–32), herein, we design a class of composite MOF membrane via the in situ growth of ZIFs threaded with linear polyethylenimine (PEI) onto a commercial cation exchange membrane (CEM) substrate (Fig. 1A). This prepared PEI@ZIF#CEM has ample -NH2 groups and is further reacted with the 2,3-epoxypropyl trimethyl ammonium chloride (ETAC) monomer (with quaternary ammonium group) through an alternating current electric field–assisted Li+ control assembly process (28, 33) to construct the Q-PEI@ZIF#CEM (figs. S1 to S5). A catalyst, tris(dimethylaminomethyl)phenol (DMP-30), is used to accelerate the epoxy groups (in ETAC) reacting with amino groups (in PEI@ZIF). To investigate the effect of PEI and ETAC contents on the resulting membranes, the Q-PEI@ZIF#CEM membranes are prepared with varying PEI content of 0, 0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 g liter−1 (in the construction of the PEI@ZIF architecture process), and different ETAC content of 0, 5, and 10 wt % (in LiCl assembly process). This bioinspired design merges stimuli-responsive regulation with Q-PEI@ZIF structural precision and achieves ultraefficient ion selectivity via advancing separation systems. These membranes are denoted as pristine CEM, ZIF#CEM, Q(5%)-ZIF#CEM, PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM, Q(5%)-PEI(0.5)@ZIF#CEM, Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM, Q(5%)-PEI(2.0)@ZIF#CEM, and Q(10%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM, respectively (table S2).

## RESULTS
Figure 1B shows the cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of a pristine CEM [Fig. 1B (i)] and Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM [Fig. 1B (ii)], and a uniformly distributed layer (~4 μm thick) can be observed on the pristine CEM membrane surface (fig. S6). Attenuated total refraction–Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy spectra (ATR-FTIR; fig. S7) of these crystals indicate that secondary amine group characteristic peaks of 1567, 1418, and 954 cm−1 exist in both ZIF#CEM and Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM, mainly due to the C─N groups in the 2-methylimidazole (34). To further analyze these chemical components, x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of ZIFs and Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF and their high-resolution (C 1s, N 1s, and O 1s) are used to illustrate the element contents (atomic%) and existing chemical bonds, respectively (Fig. 1C). Compared to the pure ZIFs, the elements of O 1s and N 1s in Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF increased from 7.7 to 9.7% and from 16.1 to 18.2%, respectively, due to the ETAC monomer introduced into Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF. The bonds of C─O (287.4 eV) and C─N+ (286.1 eV) appearing in the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF confirm that the increased amount of O and N may come from -C─OH and -C─N+R3 in the structure, which may be generated by the substitution reaction between -NH2 (in PEI(1.0)@ZIF) and epoxy group in ETAC (figs. S8 and S9). Figure 1D shows a homogeneous, intergrown, and continuous Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF layer. The pure ZIF crystals with the homogeneous and hollow framework and the vacancies between ZIF crystals can be observed on the CEM surface (Fig. 1E). When in situ confinement conversion of PEI and alternating current electrical field–assisted Li+ control in assembling of ETAC is applied, the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF shows a much rougher crystal (Fig. 1F), which reflects the changed morphology of the crystal on the membrane surface.
Figure 2A shows a large area (a 6.0-cm diameter) of Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF layer constructed on the CEM surface. The roughness of this layer is characterized by atomic force microscopy (AFM), showing a maximum height of ~1.7 μm (Fig. 2B). It reflects an increase of membrane-specific surface area and a uniform distribution of a defect-free Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF layer. The increased roughness of the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF crystal is observed by the amplification of AFM images compared to that of the pure ZIF crystal (fig. S10). The x-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the ZIF and PEI@ZIF crystals are presented in Fig. 2C. Subtle shifts in the characteristic peaks of PEI@ZIF are observed compared to those of the unmodified ZIF, which can be attributed to the incorporation of the PEI polymer within the ZIF framework. Similarly, when the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF composite is fabricated on the pristine CEM membrane [Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM], the XRD pattern (Fig. 2D) retains the distinctive ZIF-derived peaks but exhibits minor shifts relative to the pristine ZIF#CEM. The threaded PEI polymer in ZIF followed by ETAC quaternization, may shift the pore size of the ZIF in Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM and form a cushion-shaped cavity, which is expected to be 0.94 by 0.70 by 0.54 nm (35). A nitrogen (N2) adsorption isotherm at 77 K is measured to determine the porous structure (36). Compared to the ZIFs, the Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) surface area of the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF increases from 3.0 to 5.8 m2·g−1 (Fig. 2E). It suggests the enhanced access of N2 molecules within the porous material in this Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF and confirms the retention of ZIF microporosity. The pore size distribution derived from N2 (3.40-Å kinetic diameter) sorption is calculated on the basis of the Horváth-Kawazoe (HK) method, showing smaller pores of the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF (14.6-Å pore width) than that of ZIF (16.8-Å pore width) (Fig. 2F). The zeta potentials of the membranes (Fig. 2G) are quantified to explore the influence of the electrostatic interaction of the membrane surface on the ion separation. In neutral conditions (pH 7), as shown in fig. S11, the negative charge (−2.4 mV) of the commercial CEM is due to the cation exchange groups in the membrane structure. The pure ZIF nanocrystals on the ZIF#CEM surface show an almost zero zeta potential, indicating the lack of charged groups in this layer. In contrast, a positive charge of +1.9 mV is determined for the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM, which is ascribed to the introduction of PEI and ETAC molecules with positive charges in the crystal layer. In addition, the positive charge effect is more pronounced in Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM with a rougher surface, and thus, multivalent cations face stronger electrostatic repulsion when being transported through the membrane.

The electrochemical behavior of the membrane is measured in a 0.10 M NaCl solution in a laboratory-designed device (fig. S12). The surface electrical resistance of CEM is 3.02 ohm·cm2, and all the prepared membranes show an increased surface electrical resistance (fig. S13). The surface electrical resistance of ZIF#CEM is as high as 13.50 ohm·cm2 due to the lack of charged groups, indicating a high resistance for ion transport. However, intercalation of PEI in ZIF crystals and further quantization with ETAC can minimize the increased surface electrical resistance due to the increased surface charge density. Thus, the surface electrical resistance of Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF is only increased by 1.8 ohm·cm2 compared to the CEM. Figure 2H shows a 15 mA·cm−2 limiting current density of the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM, which is lower than that of the CEM (i.e., 20 mA·cm−2 limiting current density, fig. S14). The decreased limiting current density is caused by Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF, and caution should be taken to choose the operating conditions of this resulting membrane under the limiting current density (Ohmic region) in electrodialysis.
Five cations (Li+, Na+, K+, Mg2+, and Ca2+) commonly found in water streams are selected, and their permeation is measured in electrodialysis by using a cocktail solution. The ion permeation rate and the Li+/Mz+ (Mz+: Na+, K+, Mg2+, and Ca2+) permselectivity are calculated to evaluate the transport behavior of different cations. In electrodialysis at a constant current density of 5 mA·cm−2, all cations transport through the pristine CEM (the commercial cation exchange membrane) and accumulate in the concentrated chamber (fig. S15A). During the 120 min, the transport order of this pristine CEM is K+ (0.31 mol m−2·hour−1) > Ca2+ (0.25 mol m−2·hour−1) ~ Mg2+ (0.25 mol m−2·hour−1) > Na+ (0.22 mol m−2·hour−1) > Li+ (0.18 mol m−2·hour−1) (Fig. 2I); while, the transport ability order about the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM is K+ ~ Li+ > Na+, and the divalent cations (Mg2+ and Ca2+) are rejected (fig. S15B and Fig. 2I) during the 120 min. To decrease the effect of the polarization phenomenon caused by excessive running time in the electro-driven system, we also calculate the ion transport ability order through the pristine CEM and Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM during the 60 min: The ion transport ability order through the pristine CEM is K+ (0.34 mol m−2·hour−1) > Ca2+ (0.26 mol m−2·hour−1) ~ Mg2+ (0.26 mol m−2·hour−1) > Na+ (0.23 mol m−2·hour−1) > Li+ (0.19 mol m−2·hour−1), and through the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM is K+ ~ Li+ > Na+ > > Ca2+ (0 mol m−2·hour−1) ~ Mg2+ (0 mol m−2·hour−1) (Fig. 2J). The synergistic effect of size sieving and electrostatic repulsions of Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF could impede the divalent cations (Ca2+ and Mg2+) transport, where both Ca2+ and Mg2+ permeation rates decrease close to 0 mol m−2·hour−1. Through the pristine CEM, monovalent cations with smaller Stokes radii are preferentially transported, which means that K+ is the easiest to be transported, followed by Na+ and then Li+ (37, 38). However, our prepared Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM shows a much enhanced Li+ permeation rate instead, making the transport of Li+ faster than Na+ and close to that of K+. This changed Li+ transport behavior can be explained by Li+ controlled Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF structure formation and the bonding affinity of reacted ETAC. Consequently, this enhanced Li+ ion permeation rate, together with high Li+/Mz+ permselectivity of Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM (Fig. 2K), could be used to achieve highly efficient Li+ recovery from water streams.
Theoretically, the design principles of the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM membrane for cation selectivity are based on the electrical field–assisted Li+ control synergizing with Donnan equilibrium, size sieving, electrostatic interaction, or bonding affinity (Fig. 3A): (i) Donnan equilibrium—Anion (i.e., Cl−) can move into the positively charged cavity of Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF but cannot transport through CEM; cations can transport from this cavity instead; (ii) electrostatic repulsion—Monovalent/multivalent cations is selectively separated; (iii) size sieving and dehydration—Smaller hydrated cations transport into the cavity with part-dehydration energy; (iv) electric field–assisted ion control—The Li+ controlled ETAC formation of the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF forms well-defined geometrical and chemical channels enabling fast and precise Li+ transport; (v) bonding affinity—Functional groups with high adsorption energy (i.e., bonding affinity) to Li+ enhance Li+ transport (figs. S16 to S19).

To investigate the Li+ transport ability through the Q-PEI@ZIF structure, on the basis of the results of cations permeation ability through CEM and Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM, we select representative monovalent (Li+, Na+, and K+) and divalent (Mg2+ and Ca2+) cations to study their transport mechanisms. Among those ions, Na+ and Mg2+ are the mono- and divalent ion species that often interfere with Li+ recovery from water streams (39), while K+ and Ca2+ are included to further validate the observed selectivity trend. The DFT calculations are performed to compare the bonding affinity (adsorption energy, Eads) of these cation species on the Q-PEI@ZIF. In the calculations, the active part of the Q-PEI@ZIF framework (Fig. 3B) is referred to Q+-PEI(Cl−), where several Cl− surround the Q+-PEI molecule (Fig. 3C and table S3). This model is constructed to be consistent with experimental results. Multiple potential binding sites are considered for each ion species, and the most energetically favorable configurations are identified (figs. S20 to S25). As shown in Fig. 3D, the calculated Eads values indicate a clear difference between monovalent and divalent cations. Li+ exhibits the strongest binding at −2.23 eV/ion, followed by Na+ (−1.86 eV/ion) and K+ (−1.55 eV/ion), while the divalent Mg2+ and Ca2+ show substantially weaker adsorption energies of −0.74t and −0.68 eV/ion, respectively. Further analyses reveal the differences in cation interaction with the Q+-PEI(Cl−) framework. Li+ forms a Li─N bond of 1.99 Å, close to the 2.03-Å bond length in cyclic Li₂N₂ (40, 41). Charge density difference analysis reveals substantial electron accumulation between Li+ and [N], confirming the strong ionic character of this interaction. Similarly, Na+ and K+ bind to [N] with bond lengths of 2.42 and 2.85 Å, respectively, and exhibit clear ionic interactions as evidenced by charge accumulation between the cations and [N]. In contrast, Mg2+ and Ca2+ adsorb near [N] with bond distances of 2.34 and 2.46 Å, respectively, but exhibit only minimal electron accumulation, indicating much weaker interaction between the divalent cations and the framework at the binding sites (Fig. 3D). The substantially stronger bonding affinity between Li+/Na+/K+ and Q+-PEI(Cl−), compared to Mg2+ or Ca2+, originates from enhanced charge transfer between these cations and Q+-PEI(Cl−). Bader charge analysis quantifies this effect, showing that Li+, Na+, and K+ transfer 0.44, 0.36, and 0.31 elementary charges ([q]), respectively, while Mg2+ and Ca2+ transfer only 0.07 [q] and 0.05 [q] (table S4). The strong ionic bonding formed between monovalent cations and [N] sites highlights the high affinity of Q+-PEI(Cl−) toward monovalent species, thereby explaining the superior selectivity for Li+, Na+, and K+ over divalent ions in electrodialysis (Fig. 2, I and J). Among the monovalent ions, Li+ shows the strongest binding, consistent with its superior transport performance, while K+ exhibits slightly weaker adsorption energy than Li+ and Na+ in DFT calculations. Its experimental selectivity is comparable to Li+, likely due to its smaller hydrated ion size and weaker interaction with the membrane, resulting in lower migration barriers. These synergetic effects in Q-PEI@ZIF perform the K+ ~ Li+ > Na+ ion transport ability order of the resulting membrane under an applied electric field.
Current-voltage (C-V) characteristics of membranes in 25 mM LiCl, 25 mM NaCl, 25 mM MgCl2, and 25 mM mixed solutions (i.e., LiCl/NaCl and LiCl/MgCl2) are measured under the electric field, respectively, to describe the dynamic behavior of ion conductivities (Fig. 3E). In 25 mM LiCl, all the prepared membranes present different C-V characteristic slopes (Fig. 3F). These varying C-V characteristics are used for mimicking the Li+ transport properties of prepared constructions and composed of different ion conductivities, demonstrating that the Q-PEI@ZIF has high Li+ transport ability. To further demonstrate the electrical field–assisted ion control in synergistic principles on this distinct cation-selective construction, the C-V characteristic of these membranes is evaluated in 25 mM NaCl and 25 mM MgCl2 (figs. S26 and S27). In contrast, all the Q-PEI@ZIF constructions show lower Na+ transport abilities and almost no Mg2+ transport abilities from the C-V conductivity characteristic slope, while the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM shows a much higher Li+ transport ability. Figure 3 (G and H) shows the C-V characteristics of Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM in different solutions. These C-V characteristic slopes in LiCl or binary solutions containing Li+ are much higher than those in unary NaCl or MgCl2 solutions, which indicates that this highly conductive phenomenon is mainly caused by the superior Li+ transport ability.
To investigate the ion transport ability through the Q-PEI@ZIF structure, here, a binary ion solution is chosen to simplify and investigate the ion transport mechanism. The membrane selectivity of Li+/Mg2+ in a mixed aqueous solution is further explored in electrodialysis by using as-prepared membranes, at varying Li+ and Mg2+ initial concentrations and Li+/Mg2+ ratios (Fig. 4A). In electrodialysis (i.e., current density of 5.0 mA·cm−2, 100 ml of 25 mM initial Li+/Mg2+ mixtures in both dilute and concentrated chambers), the concentrations of both Li+ and Mg2+ increases in a similar trend to the concentrate chamber through the pristine CEM (Fig. 4B). In contrast, all ZIF-based CEMs show different trends of Li+ and Mg2+ under the same operating conditions (figs. S28 to S35). The ZIF#CEM, designed only on the basis of size sieving effects, leads to both lower Li+ and Mg2+ transport, although Li+ can be separated from Mg2+ by the ZIFs (fig. S29). In contrast to the ZIF#CEM, the ionized ZIF-based membranes show not only high Li+ concentration in the concentrated chamber but also low Mg2+ in 120 min (figs. S28 to S35). In particular, the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM achieves the same Li+ increased value (20.0 mM) as the pristine CEM (20.5 mM) in 120 min, while almost no increase of Mg2+ in the concentrate chamber was observed (Fig. 4B). Under the same operating conditions, different increasing trends of the Mg2+ and Li+ through these membranes reflect varying ion transport behaviors, which is mainly due to the presence of ZIFs and the Q-PEI fixed in ZIFs.

The ion permeability (Fig. 4C) and Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity (Fig. 4D) of CEM, ZIF#CEM, and Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM were investigated to assess Mg2+ and Li+ transport behavior through membranes. The Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM yields an ion permeation rate of 0.50 mol m−2·hour−1 for Li+ during 120 min of testing, which is comparable to the pristine CEM (i.e., 0.52 mol m−2·hour−1). However, Mg2+ cannot pass into the recovery chamber through Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM, with an ion permeation rate of 0 mol m−2·hour−1. Thus, an extremely high Li+/Mg2+ permselectivtity of 20,000 can be achieved with Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM, while the Li+/Mg2+ permselectivtity of pristine CEM is as low as 0.99. This phenomenon is probably because of the Li+ control of the ionized structure. Because of the positively charged groups fixed within the Li+ controlled structure, the Cl− enters into Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF cavity by electrostatic attraction so that Li+ can easily transport through this structure via the Donnan equilibrium and the electron double layer. The ZIF#CEM demonstrates a decreased Li+ permeation rate, although the Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity is slightly improved compared to pristine CEM. Thus, on the basis of the electric field–assisted assembly strategy and synergistic separation principles of size sieving, electrostatic interaction, and bonding affinity, the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM has a high Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity and a high Li+ permeation rate in an electro-driven process. Accordingly, this high Li+ ion permeation rate together with the high Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity can make the purity of Li+ in the concentrate chamber reach >99.99% in 120 min, while it is only 63.55% of ZIF#CEM and 49.88% for the pristine CEM (Fig. 4E). Compared to the most advanced membranes reported to date, the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM sets a benchmark by achieving unparalleled Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity while maintaining an excellent balance in Li+ permeation rate, thereby overcoming the permeation-selectivity trade-offs (Fig. 4F and table S5) (14, 42–49). Consequently, superior Li+ recovery rate and Li+ purity are achieved by using the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM in electrodialysis compared to other Li+ recovery systems (Fig. 4G and table S6) (50–60).
Considering that different water streams may have different salt concentrations as well as ion ratios, the effect of salt concentration (Mg2+ and Li+) on the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM performance is comprehensively studied. With increasing concentration of the mixed solution from 25 mM to 100 mM, the Li+ permeation rate increases from 0.50 to 0.80 mol m−2·hour−1 (Fig. 4H and fig. S36), while the Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity (in 120 min) decreases from 20,000 to 10 (Fig. 4I). This is because the high ion concentration could increase the current efficiency, enhancing transport of both Mg2+ and Li+. Figure 4J shows the effect of the Li+/Mg2+ ratio of 1:1, 1:10, and 1:100 on Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity, the Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity of Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM decreases from 100 to 60 and 30, respectively. During this process, the Mg2+ permeation rate remains constant because of the fixed concentration, while the Li+ is substantially reduced at a lower concentration (fig. S37). Thus, it consequently causes a decreasing Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity with decreasing Li+/Mg2+ ratios.

## DISCUSSION
On the basis of the hypothesis of electric field–assisted ion control and the synergistic principles of size sieving, electrostatic interaction, and bonding affinity, this work constructs an ionized MOF functionalized layer on a CEM surface. The as-developed membrane shows an ultraefficient transport trend of Li+. In electrodialysis, the resulting membrane has the following order of ion transport ability: K+ ~ Li+ > Na+ > Ca2+ ~ Mg2+. Supported by DFT calculations (based on electrostatic interactions analysis) and ion conductivity testing (based on dynamic experimental analysis), it confirms that the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM membrane has a stronger affinity and enhanced transport ability for Li+ compared to Na+ and Mg2+. In 5 mA·cm-2 electrodialysis, with 25 mM initial Li+/Mg2+ mixture, the Q(5%)-PEI(1.0)@ZIF#CEM membrane exhibits the Li+/Mg2+ permselectivity as high as 20,000. The purity of Li+, 99.99%, is obtained by 120 min of electrodialysis. The design concept, technological principle, and evaluation methods will expand the electric field–assisted Li+ control in the construction of an ionized framework for highly efficient recovery of ion resources.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

## Chemicals and membranes
2-methylimidazole (99%), Zn(NO3)2·6H2O, polyethyleneimine (Mw = 2000), ETAC, DMP-30, lithium chloride (LiCl), sodium chloride (NaCl), sodium chloride (NaCl), potassium chloride (KCl), magnesium chloride (MgCl2), calcium chloride (CaCl2), and sodium sulfate (Na2SO4) were purchased from the Sigma-Aldrich (Germany). All the chemicals were used without any further purification. In this work, the Fujifilm commercial anion exchange membrane (type I) and CEM (type I) were used to support this study (in table S1).

## Preparation of the Q-PEI@ZIF#CEM membranes
In this study, the Q-PEI@ZIF crystals are constructed via the PEI threaded ZIF (PEI@ZIF) through an in situ confinement conversion process and the ETAC quaternized PEI@ZIF (Q-PEI@ZIF) via an alternating current electric field–assisted Li+ control assembly process (27). The commercial CEM (pristine CEM) is immersed in a 2-methylimidazole solution and kept in a horizontal placement state. Different contents of positively charged PEI mixed with the Zn(NO3)2·6H2O are added and added into this 2-methylimidazole solution (volume ratio 1:2). Then, the PEI@ZIF crystals are assembled on the pristine CEM surface, and the first PEI@ZIF layer on CEM surface based on in situ growth and electrostatic bonding interaction is constructed. Then, the defective PEI@ZIF layer is eliminated by repeating the construction of PEI@ZIF crystals. Last, the PEI@ZIF#CEM with ample -NH2 groups is placed perpendicular to a laboratory-designed membrane assembly device (an electric field–assisted ion control membrane assembly device, i.e., eF-IC membrane device). In theory, the -NH2 in PEI@ZIF can react with the epoxy group in ETAC under the catalyst of DMP-30. Thus, the positively charged ETAC mixed with LiCl solution is added into the above eF-IC membrane device and is chemically assembled in the PEI@ZIF structure with activation of the DMP-30 catalyst. In this process, the Li+ can not only transport the PEI@ZIF#CEM but also affect the ETAC monomers assembly on PEI@ZIF architectures, obtaining the Q-PEI@ZIF layer on the pristine CEM surface. In addition, unstable stacked Q-PEI@ZIF crystals can fall from the layers under this alternating-current electric field (10 V, 50 Hz). After that, only the stable and thin Q-PEI@ZIF crystals are constructed on pristine CEM.

## Characterization methods

## Chemical characterization
All the samples were dried thoroughly in a vacuum oven at 45°C for 24 hours. The total reflectance ATR-FTIR (Nicolet 6700, USA) is used to monitor the functional groups of Q-PEI@ZIF crystals and membranes. XPS (Kratos AXIS Ultra DLD, Japan) with a current of 1.8 A, bias 3.3 V and bias 1.0 V is used to analyze the elemental compositions of the synthesized Q-PEI@ZIF crystal samples. The high resolution of Q-PEI@ZIF crystals is also characterized by XPS.

## Structure characterization
Photographs are taken with a digital camera. The surface roughness of the Q-PEI@ZIF crystals (the scanning area is 1 μm by 1 μm of each sample) and membranes (the scanning area is 5 μm by 5 μm of each sample) is evaluated by an AFM (Bruker, USA) under tapping mode at 25°C. ## Ion-selective measurements

## Multi-cation mixed system
The temporal evolution of multi-cations (Li+, Na+, K+, Ca2+, and Mg2+) concentrations in electrodialysis is measured to evaluate the ion-selective performance, including ion permeation rate and Li+/Mz+ permselectivity (fig. ## DFT calculation
The DFT calculations are performed by using the Vienna ab initio simulation package (63). We adopt the generalized gradient approximation implemented by Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof and the projector augmented wave approach (64, 65). The C(2s22p2), N(2s22p3), H(1s1), O(2s22p4), Cl(3s23p5), Li(2s1), Na(3s1), K(3s23p64s1), Mg(3s2), and Ca(3s23p64s2) valence states are used to describe electron-nuclear interact. The plane-wave basis cutoff is set as 400 eV. The energy convergence criterion is set to 10−5 eV, and the residual forces in the converged structures are smaller than 0.01 eV/Å. The Brillouin zone is sampled by a single gamma. The branched PEI polymer is placed in a simulation cell of 50 Å by 50 Å by 30 Å, providing over 15 Å of vacuum spacing in all directions to eliminate spurious interactions between periodic images.
To investigate the intrinsic ion selectivity behavior, we model a positively charged Q+-PEI polymer segment in vacuum, neglecting explicit solvent and background electrolyte.
