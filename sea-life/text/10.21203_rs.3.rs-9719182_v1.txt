applicable potential for future edge-computing deployment in the automated yield measurement of
benthic organisms.

and yield estimation. For decades, sea cucumber growth monitoring has relied on manual diving capture,
with volume quantified via contact-based methods such as water displacement. This approach is not only

labor-intensive and high-risk, but also prone to severe measurement bias: as stress-sensitive soft-bodied
organisms, sea cucumbers frequently undergo body contraction or even evisceration during manual
handling and air exposure (Harper et al., 2020, Liu et al., 2024a). Accordingly, developing non-contact,
low-stress in-situ visual measurement methods has become an urgent demand for automated aquaculture
production (Santos et al., 2025).
Early non-contact measurement studies predominantly used binocular vision to acquire target depth
information, and estimated volume by simplifying aquatic organisms into regular geometric models (e.g.,
ellipsoids, cylinders) (Muñoz-Benavent et al., 2018). However, sea cucumbers exhibit non-convex
geometry with surface papillae, which are difficult to be accurately fitted by regular models, leading to
large measurement errors especially during postural changes (Jongjaraunsuk et al., 2025).
To address the morphological representation limitations of regular models, subsequent studies
adopted deep learning-based instance segmentation to extract organism contours. Networks such as Mask
R-CNN and early YOLO series can acquire target 2D boundaries via mask extraction and map them to
3D point clouds. However, sea cucumbers’ mimicry camouflage makes them visually similar to
background sediment and aquatic weeds; in low-contrast, high-noise underwater environments,
conventional models frequently produce blurred boundaries and incomplete masks. Furthermore, discrete
3D point clouds from mask mapping are difficult to be directly used for closed volume calculation
without surface topology reconstruction.
Recent studies have addressed background interference and closed volume calculation via cascaded
large-model architectures, using the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for complex background
segmentation and Poisson Reconstruction for watertight surface generation from discrete point clouds
(Kazhdan et al., 2006, Kirillov et al., 2023, Zhen et al., 2024). While these schemes achieve high
geometric accuracy in static tests, they face critical limitations for edge deployment in dynamic
underwater video streams (Fan et al., 2024). First, at the perception stage, Transformer-based
architectures like SAM have computational complexity scaling quadratically with image resolution,
leading to notably high inference latency on resource-constrained edge devices (Cai et al., 2025). Second,
at the reconstruction stage, implicit algorithms like Poisson Reconstruction require iterative sparse linear
system solving, and produce two key errors for incomplete underwater point clouds (Ren et al., 2023):
global smoothing erases surface papillae details causing volume underestimation, while implicit surface
closure properties induce surface expansion at point cloud holes, both exacerbating geometric distortion.
Third, most existing schemes rely on single-frame static measurement, lacking temporal constraint
mechanisms to mitigate dynamic interference from occlusion and target curling (Xie et al., 2024).
To mitigate the fundamental trade-off between high-precision in-situ measurement and strict edge
computing resource constraints, this paper proposes a lightweight binocular vision volume estimation
framework optimized for underwater edge deployment. Unlike conventional paradigms that improve
accuracy via increasing model complexity, we conduct targeted optimization of the three core pipeline
stages to reduce full-link computational overhead while preserving geometric topology fidelity:
(1) To address the computational bottleneck of large models and the limited feature extraction
capability of conventional lightweight networks, we design the YOLO11-ED-Head instance
segmentation network with a group convolution front-end backbone and asymmetric decoupled branches.
This design reduces redundant cross-channel computational redundancy, reduces inference latency, and
maintains high geometric consistency mask extraction for non-convex benthic organisms.
(2) To overcome the high latency and detail loss of implicit reconstruction algorithms, we replace
implicit surface fitting with an explicit Alpha Shape topological algorithm. Leveraging local simplex

3D reconstruction is the core approach for spatial geometric information acquisition in plant and
animal phenotypic analysis and volume measurement. Early agricultural measurement studies mostly
used bounding box approximation or voxel grid partitioning, which are suitable for volume estimation
of regular rigid agricultural products (e.g., apples, potatoes) (Gongal et al., 2015). For complex organisms,
recent studies have adopted point cloud-based surface reconstruction, combining instance segmentation
masks and binocular stereo matching to acquire the 3D morphology of underwater organisms (Zhu et al.,
2025). Representative implicit methods include Poisson Surface Reconstruction and the Marching Cubes
algorithm (Lorensen and Cline, 1987), which can generate watertight smooth meshes via global implicit
function solving for rigid structures or smooth-surfaced organisms.

challenges for in-situ 3D mapping of sea cucumbers in turbid water. Sea cucumbers have body surfaces
densely covered with non-convex papillae protrusions, and the acquired point clouds are often

2.3. Target Tracking and Global Temporal Filtering in Dynamic Video Streams
In continuous dynamic visual measurement, single-frame static observations are vulnerable to
ambient noise-induced fluctuations. Traditional temporal processing strategies, including multi-frame
arithmetic mean filtering, Kalman filtering, and optical flow-based inter-frame smoothing, deliver
reliable performance for Gaussian-distributed random noise or continuous trajectory tracking of rigid
targets (Li et al., 2025).
However, in real seabed marine ranch environments, dynamic measurement errors of benthic
organisms exhibit non-Gaussian and non-linear characteristics. During a tracking cycle, sea cucumbers
may remain in a stress-induced curled state or suffer long-term partial occlusion by aquatic weeds for
most of the observation period, generating a large number of continuous underestimated frames and
forming an asymmetric negatively skewed distribution. Conversely, sporadic floating aquatic weed
adhesion may trigger over-segmentation and isolated volume peak values (Hao et al., 2024, Liu et al.,
2024b, Sun et al., 2019). In this scenario, traditional mean filtering is pulled down by long-term
underestimation intervals, while maximum value strategies are misled by sporadic anomalies.
Accordingly, there is an urgent need for a robust temporal mechanism coupling spatial gating constraints
and non-parametric statistical aggregation to stably extract the true physical volume of targets from
continuous data streams with highly dynamic interference and asymmetric deviations (Wang et al., 2025).

To achieve non-contact, high-precision in-situ volume measurement of sea cucumbers in complex

3.2.2. Structural Design of the Efficient-Decoupled Head (ED-Head)
The baseline model’s native multi-task decoupled head suffers from suboptimal computational
resource allocation for our specific task. Its detection head adopts a symmetric convolution structure for
classification, bounding box regression, and mask segmentation branches. However, sea cucumber insitu observation is a single-class detection task with inherently low classification complexity, while mask
segmentation requires high spatial feature resolution to capture non-convex papillae features in turbid
underwater environments. The symmetric structure causes notably parameter redundancy in the
classification branch, and independent standard convolution for feature extraction across all branches
introduces unnecessary cross-channel computational overhead. To resolve this architecture-task
complexity mismatch, we propose the ED-Head via asymmetric reconstruction of the native head, with
two core innovations as follows:
(1)

Group Convolution-based Front-end Feature Fusion Backbone

In the native decoupled head, spatial and channel mapping is performed independently by each
branch after feature splitting, which increases back-end inference computational load. To address this,
we front-load spatial feature extraction and design a front-end fusion stem (GroupConv Stem). As shown
in the dashed box in Fig. 3, the feature map output from the Neck layer first feeds into the Stem module,
which consists of two cascaded 3 × 3 group convolutions. The group number hyperparameter is fixed

module extracts local spatial texture features of sea cucumber body surfaces via group convolution,

3.3. 3D Surface Reconstruction and Volume Estimation for Non-convex Biological

Using the 2D mask output from the ED-Head, the system back-projects the target into 3D space via
the binocular depth camera’s disparity map, generating a discrete point cloud of the sea cucumber’s local
surface. Converting this discrete point cloud into a watertight continuous surface for volume integration
is the final step to complete in-situ measurement.

computational load on resource-constrained edge devices.

4.4. Temporal Volume Filtering and Robustness Analysis
In dynamic underwater measurement streams, single-frame images are frequently affected by
multiple disturbances including targets moving into or out of the field of view, body curling, and local
occlusion. To guarantee the system’s final measurement accuracy in long-duration video streams, this
paper proposes a joint constraint mechanism combining spatial gating and global temporal filtering.
4.4.1. Design Principle and Morphological Integrity Constraint of Spatial Gating Mechanism
The effective perception range of an underwater binocular vision system is constrained by the
camera’s physical FoV. In real measurement scenarios, when a sea cucumber target moves into or out of
the frame, its body structure is inevitably truncated by the image boundary. Without effective spatial
boundary constraints, the system will feed these incomplete edge targets directly into the 3D
reconstruction pipeline, where the Alpha Shape algorithm will forcibly close the surface on incomplete
point clouds, directly causing the output instantaneous volume to be lower than the target’s true physical
volume. In addition, targets at the FoV edge are also vulnerable to optical interference from lens radial
distortion, further aggravating geometric distortion.
To reduce these physical observation defects, this paper designs a region of interest (ROI)-based spatial
gating mechanism at the front end of the measurement pipeline. The system strictly defines the
measurement trigger region as a fixed rectangular bounding box covering 64% of the central FoV. During
continuous observation, only when the 2D mask bounding box of a sea cucumber target is fully contained
within this central ROI will the system mark it as a valid observation frame and activate volume
measurement. This spatial filtering strategy effectively filters the input of truncated and notably distorted
frames, guarantees the morphological integrity of the underlying point cloud fed into the 3D
reconstruction module, and provides a reliable geometric benchmark for subsequent temporal
aggregation.
4.4.2. Global Temporal Filtering Strategy and Asymmetric Noise Suppression
Experimental results show that system performance has obvious sensitivity to the temporal quantile
threshold (Fig. 7), where the primary axis (left) denotes Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE, dark
blue solid line) and the secondary axis (right) denotes Root Mean Square Error (RMSE, orange solid
line). Without temporal aggregation (RAW group), the system’s MAPE reaches 12.8% due to underwater
dynamic noise interference. After introducing quantile filtering, the overall error presents a typical
nonlinear U-shaped trend with increasing threshold: low-to-medium thresholds are easily pulled down
by volume-underestimated frames caused by terrain occlusion or target curling, leading to negative
systematic bias; while high thresholds (e.g., 90th percentile) are easily misled by occasional oversegmentation frames induced by suspended solids or specular highlights, resulting in significant volume
overestimation, with MAPE rising to 19.2% and RMSE reaching 19.5 cm 3.

The baseline cascaded scheme (YOLOv8 + SAM + Poisson Reconstruction) was first tested, with
results showing that it achieved an average processing frame rate of only 0.09 FPS in dynamic underwater
scenarios, accompanied by high overall measurement error due to underwater noise interference. In the
first-stage ablation, replacing the large-scale visual segmentation model SAM with the proposed
YOLO11-ED-Head increased the overall operating frame rate to 1.2 FPS, while maintaining roughly
consistent measurement accuracy (MAPE = 26.8%, RMSE = 19.5 cm 3). This system-level performance
aligns with the model-level evaluation conclusions in Section 4.2, confirming that ED-Head effectively
alleviates the computational bottleneck of edge deployment without significant degradation of mask
extraction quality.
Building on the speed improvement of the 2D perception module, the second-stage ablation replaced
the implicit Poisson reconstruction algorithm with the explicit Alpha Shape algorithm. Results show that
this module delivered a not bad optimization in system accuracy, with MAPE dropping from 26.8% to
12.8% and RMSE optimized from 19.5 cm 3 to 8.5 cm3. This error convergence trend echoes the
theoretical analysis in Section 4.3, as the explicit geometric shrinking property of Alpha Shape suppresses
the "water expansion" phenomenon caused by incomplete underwater point clouds and reduces
morphological distortion. Meanwhile, by alleviating the computational overhead of solving global
implicit functions, the system’s average frame rate was further increased to 9.7 FPS.
Following the lightweight optimization of the perception and reconstruction modules, the spatial
ROI gating mechanism and global 70th percentile temporal filtering were introduced in the final ablation
stage. As shown in Table 4, the full proposed system achieves a stable average frame rate of 16.8 FPS,
with MAPE reduced to 9.8% and RMSE reaching 6.2 cm 3. This result validates the analysis in Section
4.4: the global 70th percentile strategy effectively filters low-value noise caused by aquatic weed
occlusion and sea cucumber curling, while the ROI gating mechanism avoids unnecessary 3D
reconstruction of invalid targets at the FoV edge and cuts unnecessary computational consumption.
In summary, the progressive ablation experiments quantify the independent contribution of each
optimized module to system accuracy and operating efficiency, and verify the effectiveness of the
proposed measurement pipeline in balancing measurement precision and edge computational cost. With
a mean absolute percentage error of 9.8% and a processing speed of 16.8 FPS, the final system provides
a feasible edge-side technical solution for continuous underwater in-situ observation of benthic
organisms in marine ranching.

5. Conclusion
This study developed an in-situ volume estimation system for sea cucumbers, aiming to address the
computational bottleneck between high-fidelity 3D measurement and processing requirements. Instead
of relying on highly complex models, this research achieved an effective system balance through targeted
spatiotemporal constraints and pipeline reconstruction. The systematic evaluation of the proposed
framework yields the following core conclusions: First, the paradigm of asymmetric resource allocation
is well-suited for single-class benthic organism detection. By reducing classification parameter

underwater point clouds. Finally, statistical filtering based on the 70% quantile mechanism provides a
practical mathematical approach to dynamic measurement noise, alleviating the asymmetric errors
caused by biological stress curling and weed occlusion. Running at 16.8 FPS with an overall MAPE of
9.8%, the proposed framework serves as a computationally efficient algorithmic foundation for the
automated yield measurement of benthic organisms. However, a current limitation of this trajectory-level
measurement lies in its dependency on continuous observation; tracking interruptions caused by severe
occlusion in high-density scenarios can still disrupt the temporal sampling distribution. Future work will
explore lightweight multimodal association operators fusing targets' geometric topology and kinematic
features, and introduce multi-view collaboration to improve multi-target identity maintenance and longterm measurement stability in complex seabed environments.
