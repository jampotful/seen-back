Resumen.— Se documenta por primera vez un caso de anoftalmia unilateral en la salamandra Isthmura bellii. Este fenómeno se
caracteriza por la ausencia total del ojo izquierdo sin evidencia de lesiones asociadas a depredación. Esta observación es escasa en
el orden Caudata, por lo que amplía el conocimiento de esta anomalía en anfibios mexicanos.
Palabras clave.— Anfibios, anomalía, ausencia ocular, conservación.
Abstract.— We document for the first time a case of unilateral anophthalmia in the salamander Isthmura bellii. This condition is
characterized by the complete absence of the left eye, with no evidence of injuries associated with predation. This observation is rare
within the order Caudata and therefore expands current knowledge of this anomaly in Mexican amphibians.
Keywords.— Amphibians, anomaly, conservation, ocular absence.

Anophthalmia is a congenital ocular disorder characterized by
the complete absence of ocular tissue within the orbit. There is
no supraocular bulge or eyelids, and it may occur unilaterally or
bilaterally (Verma & FitzPatrick, 2007; Goyal et al., 2025; PonceRosales et al., 2025). This malformation may affect the biological
fitness of the organism, as vision plays a key role in spatial and
depth perception, orientation, and discrimination of barriers
and surfaces. It is especially important for predation detection
and for finding and selecting prey (Ingle, 1976; Ramalho et al.,
2017; Ponce-Rosales et al., 2025).

of this phenomenon and help explain the low incidence of
malformations in Caudata (Ayres et al., 2022).

Records of ocular anomalies in amphibians have been
documented mainly in species of the order Anura (Lannoo,
2008; Ferreira et al., 2019; Bosch et al., 2021; Ayres et al., 2022;
Venerozo-Tlazalo et al., 2022). In contrast, records within the
order Caudata are scarce, and to date, there are no reports for
the order Gymnophiona (Cante-Bazán & Ramírez-Bautista,
2023). In a review, Henle and Dubois (2017) documented 159

Isthmura bellii (Gray, 1850) is one of the largest neotropical
plethodontid salamanders, reaching up to 146 mm in snout
vent-length (SVL) (Parra-Olea et al., 2005; Ponce-Rosales et
al., 2025). It has a robust tail shorter than the body, a gular
fold, and 13 costal grooves between the axilla and the groin. Its
dorsal coloration consists of paired reddish blotches on a dark
background covering the body (Canseco-Márquez & GutiérrezMayen, 2010; Ponce-Rosales et al., 2025).

The causes of anophthalmia are diverse and include genetic
factors, alterations during embryonic development, and
environmental agents such as exposure to ultraviolet radiation,
parasitism, as well as contaminants such as heavy metals and
agrochemicals, all of which may interfere with the normal
development of ocular tissues (Hayes, 2005; Bacon et al., 2013;
Castro-Torreblanca & Blancas-Calva, 2021).
Cases of anophthalmia in the order Caudata are limited
to only three salamander species, all belonging to the family
Plethodontidae. Although the information was searched for Latin
America, the records are exclusively from Mexico: anophthalmia
in Bolitoglossa platydactyla from Bambuver, Huatusco, Veracruz
(Venerozo Tlazalo et al., 2022); Pseudoeurycea leprosa from La
Malinche, Tlaxcala (Cante‑Bazán & Ramírez‑Bautista, 2023).
Additionally, a case of microphthalmia has been reported in
Aquiloeurycea cafetalera from Las Cañadas, Huatusco, Veracruz
(Díaz-García et al., 2019). In this context, the present note
documents the first record of unilateral anophthalmia in I. bellii
from a population in Mexico City, contributing to the knowledge
of morphological anomalies in salamanders.
On October 18, 2025, at 16:54 h, during a hike in the locality
known as “Valle de Tezontle” in the Ajusco region, Mexico City
(19.217967° N, 99.283317° W, 3444 m a.s.l.), we observed an
individual of I. bellii in an area composed of pine forest with
abundant grassland and rocks. Likewise, free-range grazing and
recreational off-road motorcycling activities are present in the
area, and agricultural fields are located nearby. The individual
was photographed using a two-peso Mexican coin (23 mm in
diameter) as a scale reference, and its size was estimated using
ImageJ software. The organism presented a total length of 51 mm
and a snout-vent length of 34 mm (Fig. 1a). We observed that the
salamander exhibited complete absence of the left eye, with no
supraocular bulge or functional eyelid present, showing only an
imperforate palpebral fissure and a small remnant of tissue in
the orbital region. Although slight irregular folds were present
in the supraocular area, no evidence of active or scar tissue,
tissue regeneration or traumatic deformation was observed.
Likewise, the absence of the eyeball appeared to affect the
development of the orbital cavity, which exhibited a collapsed
appearance and a clear loss of its normal morphology (Fig. 1b).
No scars or lesions attributable to predation were detected;
supporting its interpretation as case of unilateral anophthalmia.
The salamander continued its movement and took refuge under
a rock.

activities, and the presence of urban solid waste is common,

The documentation of morphological anomalies such as
anophthalmia in wild amphibian populations is relevant for
conservation, as these abnormalities may reflect environmental
stressors acting during early developmental stages. Salamanders
of the family Plethodontidae have been widely recognized as
sensitive organisms and potential bioindicators of environmental
quality due to their permeable skin, low dispersal capacity, and
strong dependence on stable microhabitat conditions (Welsh
& Ollivier, 1998). In this context, I. bellii, which is associated
with humid montane environments and stable microhabitats
and is highly sensitive to environmental disturbances (Rovito
et al., 2009), may serve as an indicator of ecosystem integrity
in high-altitude landscapes. In areas surrounding Mexico City,
amphibian populations are increasingly exposed to habitat
fragmentation, pollution, and alterations in microclimates.
Therefore, continued monitoring and the systematic reporting of
developmental abnormalities are essential to better understand
their prevalence, potential causes, and implications for the
conservation of Mexican amphibian populations.
