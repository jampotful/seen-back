Morphological identification of stingless bees from Belitung
M G Azizi, W Priawandiputra, and R Raffiudin*
Department of Biology, Faculty of Mathematics and Natural Sciences, IPB University,
Jl Agatis Kampus IPB Dramaga, Bogor, 16680, Indonesia
*Corresponding author: rika.raffiudin@ipb.ac.id
Abstract. Stingless bees, the smallest honey-producing bees, have high diversity with over 600
species in the world. Exploration at Bukit Barisan Sumatra found 23 species of stingless bees.
However, stingless bee diversity in Belitung has not been explored and identified. This study
aims to identify stingless bees based on morphology from Belitung. The methods used
included the preservation of dry samples, characterization and identification of morphology
based on: hind tibia, hind basitarsus, malar space, mandible, head, thorax, abdomen, and fore
wing. A total of 12 stingless bee colonies were collected in Belitung. The study found three
identified species of stingless bees in Belitung: Tetragonula laeviceps (colonies 1, 4, 5, 7, 8),
Tetragonula aff. fuscobalteata (colonies 6, 11, 12), and Heterotrigona itama (colonies 2, 3, 9,
10). Body length, body color, eye color, abdominal color, mesoscutum, teeth in the mandible,
malar space, wing length, wing venation and wing color are morphological characters that
differentiate between the three species. Three morphometric parameters also differentiate
between the three species, those are the width of hind basitarsus, malar space, and wing length.
We also observed that T. aff. fuscobalteata show variations in the color of the eye and
abdomen in all three colonies.
Keywords: Belitung, Heterotrigona itama, morphological identification, stingless bee,
Tetragonula fuscobalteata

1. Introduction
Stingless bees in the world have more than 600 species [1-8]. The distribution of bees in the world is
divided into 3, namely Neotropic, Afrotropic, and Indo-Malayan / Australasian [9]. The distribution of
stingless bees in the Indo-Malayan / Australasian region stretches from India to the Solomon Islands
and from China to Australia [8]. Stingless bees in Indo-Malaya / Australasian have been recorded as
many as 89 species [8].
Stingless bees in Indonesia have been reported as many as 46 species in 10 genera [5]. Twentythree species of several stingless bee genera such as Tetragonula, Trigonella, Lepidotrigona,
Homotrigona, Heterotrigona, Lophotrigona, Geniotrigona, and Tetrigona have been found in Bukit
Barisan, Sumatra [11]. However, stingless bee’s diversity in Belitung has not been studied yet.
Therefore, it is necessary to observe the identification of the morphology of stingless bee’s diversity in
Belitung.
The morphological identification of stingless bee is difficult because of the cryptic species
phenomenon, which has morphological similarities between species. The cryptic species phenomenon
is found in the genus Tetragonula [10]. Stingless bees have morphological characteristics that are
difficult to distinguish between species, so differences between species can be indicated as

2.2. Dry Preservation of Stingless Bee
The stingless bee were submerged in hot water for several minutes to relaxing the bodies. It will make
easier to organize the bee's body parts. Then, the stingless bee were pinned with 00 insect size needles
(0.30 mm diameter). The needle was placed in the dorsal or lateral thorax [14]. Therefore, the pinning
process was aimed to facilitate observation under the microscope.

2.3. Identification and Characterization of Stingless Bee Morphology
Morphological characters were observed and photographed using stereo microscope and Optilab 2.2.
Morphological characters that were observed is: hind tibia, hind basitarsus, malar space, mandible,
head, clypeus, propodeum, mesoscutum, mesoscutellum, antennae, eyes, gena, forewing, wing
venation, hamuli, and body color (head, clypeus, thorax, abdomen, tegula, wings) [11] (figure 2).
Morphological observations were carried out on 6 individuals from each collonies. The character were
measured and scaled using Image Raster 3.0 to obtained the average value and standard deviation.
Morphological characters that were measured is: body length (BL), forewing length (WL), malar
space (MS), the width of hind tibia (HTW) and hind basitarsus (HBW), the width of eye (EW), gena
(GW), and flagellomere F2 (F2) (figure 2) (11, 13). Identification was based on Sakagami et al. [11]
and Smith [13].

3. Result and discussion
3.1 Morphological identification of stingless bee
Based on the morphological identification of 12 stingless bee colonies, 3 species are identified, the
three species being T. laeviceps, H. itama, and T. aff. fuscobalteata. Tetragonula laeviceps were
identified from colonies 1, 4, 5, 7, and 8 (5 colonies), H. itama was identified from colonies 2, 3, 9,
and 10 (4 colonies), and T. aff. fuscobalteata were identified from colonies 6, 11 and 12 (3 colonies).
The stingless bee morphology is reduced wing venation and sting [7]. The three species that have
been identified, consisting of the genera Tetragonula and Heterotrigona. Morphological characters of
the genus Tetragonula are having two small teeth, short space malar, transparent wings,
mesoscutellum distinctly projecting backward, exceeding posterior slope of propodeum, and including
small bees [11-13]. Morphological characters of the genus Heterotrigona are having one small tooth,
long space malar, and the width of hind basitarsus about 2/3 the width of hind tibia [11-13]. The three
species in this study belong to the species that have been studied by Kahono et al. [5]. In Indonesia
especially in Bukit Barisan (Sumatra) which has been studied by Sakagami et al. [11].

3.2 Comparison between species
The three species in this study have similarities in morphological characteristics, including the
posterior fringe of hind tibia mostly consisting of plumose hairs, having an elliptical disc on hind
basitarsus, and propodeum medially glabrous and shiny. Apart from similarities, the three species
differ in morphology and morphometrics. This difference can be seen from several morphological
characteristics, namely, H. itama's body length has a larger size compared to T. laeviceps and T. aff.
fuscobalteata, mesoscutum hair pattern of the three species in this study have their respective
characteristics, teeth in mandibular H. itama only have 1 weak tooth and T. laeviceps and T. aff.
fuscobalteata has 2 weak teeth, malar space H. itama is longer than T. laeviceps and T. aff.
fuscobalteata, fore wing venation and H. itama wing color differ from T. laeviceps and T. aff.
fuscobalteata (table 2).

4. Conclusion
The results of the identification of 12 stingless bee colonies in Belitung consisted of three species,
namely T. laeviceps, T. aff. fuscobalteata, and H. itama. Body length and hair pattern on mesoscutum
is a distinguishing character between the three species. Malar space and teeth on mandible is a
character that can distinguish H. itama from T. laeviceps and T. aff. fuscobalteata. There are
morphological variations in the three colonies identified by T. aff. fuscobalteata, which is in the eye
color and abdominal color.
