# Assessment of the feed additive consisting of taurine (3a370) for Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish for the renewal of its authorisation and the request for a new use in all poultry and all porcine species (Regal B. V.)

## Abstract
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of taurine as a nutritional feed additive (functional group: vitamins, pro‐vitamins and chemically well‐defined substances having similar effect) for the renewal of its authorisation for use in Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish and for the new use in the same category (functional group: amino acids, their salts and analogues), in all poultry and all porcine species. The FEEDAP Panel concludes that taurine remains safe under the current conditions of authorisation for Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish, consumer and environment. The Panel also concludes that levels up to 0.2%, with no margin of safety, are tolerated in all poultry and all porcine species, for which the new use of taurine as an amino acid is requested. The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additive is a skin and eye irritant, and a skin and respiratory sensitiser and that any exposure is considered a risk. There is no need for the assessment of the efficacy of the additive in Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish in the context of the renewal of the authorisation. The Panel concludes that taurine cannot be considered a nutritional additive for all poultry and porcine species.

## INTRODUCTION

## Background and Terms of Reference
Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 4(1) of that Regulation lays down that any person seeking authorisation for a feed additive or for a new use of a feed additive shall submit an application in accordance with Article 7 and Article 14(1) of that Regulation lays down that an application for renewal shall be sent to the Commission at the latest 1 year before the expiry date of the authorisation.
The European Commission received a request from Regal B. V. 2 for the renewal of the authorisation of the additive consisting of taurine, when used as a feed additive for Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish (category: nutritional; functional group: vitamins, pro‐vitamins and chemically well‐defined substances having similar effect) and for the authorisation for a new use in all animal species (category: nutritional additive; functional group: amino acids, their salts and analogues). During the assessment, the applicant requested a change in the target species from all animal species to all poultry and porcine species for the new use under the functional group amino acids, their salts and analogues.
According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 4(1) (authorisation of a feed additive or new use of a feed additive) and under Article 14(1) (renewal of the authorisation). The dossier was received on 12 June 2024 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2024‐00394. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 4 October 2024.
According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of taurine when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.2 ).

## Additional information
The additive is currently authorised as a nutritional additive (functional group: vitamins, pro‐vitamins and chemically well‐defined substances having similar effect) for use in feed for Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish (3a370). 3
The additive is also authorised as a sensory additive (functional group: flavouring compounds) in all animal species (2b16056). 4
EFSA issued an opinion on the safety and efficacy of this product when used in feed for all animal species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012).

## DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

## Data
The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 5 in support of the renewal of the authorisation request and for the new use of taurine as a feed additive in all animal species.
In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 6 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 7 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.
According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 23 January 2025 to 13 February 2025 for which no comments were received.
The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 11 October 2024 to 11 January 2025; the comments received were considered for the assessment.
The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' knowledge, to deliver the present output.
The European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) considered that the conclusions and recommendations reached in the previous assessment regarding the methods used for the control of taurine in animal feed are valid and applicable for the current application. 8

## Methodologies
The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and efficacy of taurine is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 9 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the consumer (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017a), Guidance on the identity, characterisation and conditions of use of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017b), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the target species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017c), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019), Guidance on the renewal of the authorisation of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the users (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023) and Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024).

## ASSESSMENT
Taurine is currently authorised for use as a nutritional additive (functional group: vitamins, pro‐vitamins and chemically well‐defined substances having similar effect) in feed for Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish. The applicant requests a renewal of the authorisation and a new use for all poultry and all porcine species as a nutritional additive, functional group: amino acids, their salts and analogues.

## Characterisation

## Characterisation of the additive
Taurine (IUPAC name: 2‐amino‐ethanesulfonic acid; synonyms: beta‐aminoethylsulfonic acid, tauric acid) is identified by Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS) number 107‐35‐7 and European Inventory of Existing Chemical Substances (EINECS) number 203‐483‐8. The molecular formula of taurine is C2H7NO3S and its molecular weight is 125.15 g/mol.
The active substance taurine is currently authorised in solid form, produced by chemical synthesis and with a minimum purity of 98% taurine for use as a feed additive. The applicant states that no modifications to the manufacturing process and composition of the additive have been introduced since the original authorisation. 10
The authorising regulation states that taurine may be placed on the market and used as an additive consisting of a preparation. The applicant stated that the additive taurine is placed in the market not as the pure active substance but only in the form of a preparation with a minimum of 98% of the amino acid taurine. The applicant provided an example of a preparation specified to contain a minimum of 98% taurine mixed with silicon dioxide (amorphous) at a level of 0.5%–1.0%.

The data provided showed that the batches analysed complied with the specifications set in the authorising regulation.
The FEEDAP Panel considers that the microbial contamination and the amounts of the detected impurities do not raise safety concerns, with the exception of nickel, which is addressed in the user safety section (see Section 3.2.3 ).

## Safety

## Safety for the target species, consumer and environment
In its previous assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012), based on data available from the literature, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that taurine at levels 0.05%–0.25% in complete feed (on a dry matter (DM) basis) is safe for cats with a margin of safety between 4 and 20. Based on the limited data set available from the literature on carnivorous fish, it was considered that levels between 2.0% and 2.5% are safe. For other target species, no data were provided or found which would allow firm conclusions on the safety of certain levels of taurine. However, the historical use of diets containing up to 20% feedingstuffs of animal origin led the Panel to conclude that levels up to 0.2% taurine in complete feed are tolerated, but a margin of safety could not be provided. The Panel also concluded that the use of taurine as a feed additive for all animal species would not raise concerns for the safety for consumers or the environment.
In the same opinion, the Panel recommended that since synthetic taurine is not effective in poultry, pigs or ruminants and has an adverse effect on egg production, it should no longer be authorised for use in these animal species.
The applicant states that no incidents or safety issues have been documented or reported since the previous authorisation. 16
In the context of the current application, the applicant provided an extensive literature search to support that the additive remains safe under the approved conditions for Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish, consumers, users and the environment. 17 The search covered the period 2014–2025 and included the following databases: Scopus, Web of Science and the meta database of Wageningen University. The search protocol described the inclusion and exclusion criteria applied for the screening process. In total, 248 articles were considered for the screening and after applying the exclusion criteria, a total of 12 publications were retrieved and considered eligible by the applicant. The Panel assessed all the papers and considered that none of them provided relevant information of the safety for the target animals, consumer and environment that would lead the Panel to modify its previous conclusions.

## Safety aspects related to the new use of the additive
The applicant requested a new use of the additive in feed for all poultry and all porcine species as a nutritional additive (functional group: amino acids, their salts and analogues).
No data were provided to support the safety of taurine for all poultry and porcine species.
The FEEDAP Panel considered some publications identified through the literature search submitted to support the efficacy of the additive for the proposed new use. 18 None of these studies fully complied with the requirements for tolerance trials defined in the Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the target species (e.g. short duration, lack of relevant safety endpoints, no analytical confirmation of the use levels tested, poor reporting of husbandry conditions). Therefore, the Panel reiterates its previous conclusions regarding the safety of taurine for poultry and pigs, for which a supplementation level of up to 0.2% was considered tolerated. As already observed in the previous opinion, in laying hens, dietary supplementation with 0.25%–0.5% taurine was associated with an adverse effect, in particular a reduction in egg weight (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012).

## Conclusions on the safety for the target species, consumer and environment
The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additive is considered safe for cats at levels of 0.05%–0.25%, with a margin of safety ranging from 4 to 20. For carnivorous fish, it is safe at levels of 2.0%–2.5%. For other target species, including all poultry and all porcine species, the Panel concludes that levels of up to 0.2% taurine in complete feed are tolerated; however, a margin of safety cannot be established. The Panel also concludes that the use of taurine as a nutritional feed additive, in both vitamins and amino acids functional groups, would not raise any safety concerns for the consumers or the environment.

## Safety for the user
In the previous opinion, due to the lack of data, taurine was considered to be a skin and eye irritant and skin sensitiser, and to be hazardous if inhaled (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012).
No new information regarding the safety for the user was submitted for the current application, and no relevant papers were identified in the ELS.
Based on the highest dusting potential measured value (see Section 3.1.1 ), the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of users through inhalation is likely.
The FEEDAP Panel notes that the additive contains nickel (see Section 3.1.1 ). The European Directive 2022/431 19 set an occupational exposure limit (OEL) of 0.01 and 0.05 mg/m3 for both respirable and inhalable fraction, respectively, as nickel meets the criteria for classification as carcinogenic (category 1A). Therefore, to reduce the risk, the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of the users should be minimised.
Due to the presence of nickel, the additive is considered a skin and respiratory sensitiser, and based on the information present in the safety data sheet, taurine is a skin and an eye irritant. 20
The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additive is a skin and eye irritant, and a skin and respiratory sensitiser. Any exposure is considered a risk.

## Efficacy
The present application for renewal of the authorisation of taurine as a nutritional additive (functional group: vitamins, provitamins and chemically well‐defined substances having similar effect) for Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish does not include a proposal for amending or supplementing the conditions of the original authorisation that would have an impact on the efficacy of the additive. Therefore, there is no need to assess the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.
The applicant is also requesting the authorisation for a new use of taurine as a nutritional additive (functional group: amino acids, their salts and analogues) for all poultry and porcine species.
In the previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012), the FEEDAP Panel concluded that: ‘In the case of poultry, pigs and ruminants, no studies demonstrating beneficial effects of taurine supplementation on performance, health or product quality were found. In laying hens, dietary supplementation with 0.25%–0.5% taurine has been shown to have an adverse effect (reduced egg weight)’.
To support the efficacy of taurine for its new use under the functional group of amino acids, their salts and analogues, the applicant submitted a series of published studies retrieved from the literature. 21
None of the papers reviewed provided evidence of the efficacy of the additive in meeting the nutritional needs of poultry and porcine species. The FEEDAP Panel considers that taurine supplementation is unnecessary in healthy animals receiving well‐balanced diets. Therefore, the additive cannot be considered a nutritional additive for all poultry and porcine species.

## Post‐market monitoring
The FEEDAP Panel considers that there is no need for specific requirements for a post‐market monitoring plan other than those established in the Feed Hygiene Regulation 22 and good manufacturing practice.

## CONCLUSIONS
The applicant demonstrated that the additive currently in the market complies with the conditions of authorisation.
The FEEDAP Panel concludes that taurine remains safe under the current conditions of authorisation for Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish, consumer and environment. The Panel also concludes that levels up to 0.2% are tolerated in all poultry and all porcine species for which the new use of taurine as an amino acid is requested.
The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additive is a skin and eye irritant, and a skin and respiratory sensitiser. Any exposure is considered a risk.
There is no need to assess the efficacy of the additive in Canidae, Felidae, Mustelidae and carnivorous fish in the context of the renewal of the authorisation as a nutritional additive (functional group: vitamins, provitamins and chemically well‐defined substances having a similar effect).
The Panel concludes that taurine cannot be considered a nutritional additive for all poultry and porcine species.
