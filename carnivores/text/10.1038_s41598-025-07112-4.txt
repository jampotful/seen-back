# A refined lion optimizer for deep learning

## Abstract
Optimization algorithms play a fundamental role in training neural networks. The optimizer focuses on the updating weights of momentum and velocity on learning rates and losses, furthermore the complexity of the optimizer and the quantity of updated parameters are considered. The Lion optimizer, proposed by Google, is an excellent optimizer known for its faster training speed and more efficient memory usage. However, due to the discreteness of the sign function, the optimizer’s parameter updates may fail to adapt dynamically with momentum in some models, leading to non-convergence issues. In this paper, a Refined Lion Optimizer(RLion) introduce a novel update rule for the optimizer by leveraging a non-linear continuous bounded function that maps the product of momentum and a scaling factor. This design enables the optimization parameters to adaptively adjust according to both the magnitude of momentum and the scaling factor. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that the RLion is able to smooth out the fluctuations, converge faster and more reliable. The FasterNet, EfficientNetV2 and the YOLOV8 on ImageNet1k dataset are trained without warm up for classification by leveraging the RLion optimizer. The YOLOV8, YOLOV11 on VOC2012 data and Object detection with Vision Transformers on Caltech 101 dataset are trained for Object detection. The DeepLabV3+ on instance-level human parsing dataset, TwinLiteNet on BDD100K, UNet on part of the CARLA self-driving dataset are trained for semantic segmentation. Compared to the AdamW and Lion optimizer, the loss and accuracy present the RLion can promote the classification validation accuracy about \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0 \sim +20\%$$\end{document} higher than AdamW on many models even the learning rate is as high as AdamW. For object detection and semantic segmentation, RLion achieves or approaches the performance of the adamw, but overcomes the Lion gradient’s explosive or disappear susceptibility. The RLion has better convergence performance and versatility.

## Introduction
Neural networks are composed of layers of neurons that process inputs and produce outputs1. Each neuron has a set of weights and biases that determine how it responds to the inputs. The weights and biases are the parameters of the neural network that need to be learned from the training data. To learn the optimal values of the parameters that minimize the error or maximize the performance, optimization algorithms play a fundamental role in training neural networks2. There are a large number of handcrafted optimizers introduced in recent years. Especially, many optimizers such as the Adam3, Adfactor4, AdamW5, Lion6 etc., are still the standard optimizers for training most deep neural networks. Grouping optimization algorithms is based on the amount of information available about the target function that is being optimized that, in turn, can be used and harnessed by the optimization algorithm. The more information that is available about the target function, the easier the function is to optimize if the information can effectively be used in the search. The major division in optimization algorithms is whether the objective function can be differentiated at a point or not. That is, whether the first derivative (gradient or slope) of the function can be calculated for a given candidate solution or not. Many recent first-order optimization algorithms such as the AdaBelief7 and SAdam8 have been developed based on the Adam optimizer for practical training9. Some optimizers use the second optimizer based on adam optimizer for improving the optimization performance10,11. This partitions algorithms into those that can make use of the calculated gradient information and those that do not12. Sophisticated Search9 is a direction to automatically discover such optimization algorithms aims at discovering effective optimization algorithms that can improve the state-of-the-art benchmarks. The optimizer focuses on the updating weights of momentum and velocity on learning rates and losses13. The complexity of the optimizer and the quantity of updated parameters are also focus.
Lion (EvoLved Sign Momentum) optimizer6 is a new type of first-order optimization algorithm based on momentum proposed by Google, which has the advantages of relatively high computational efficiency and low memory usage. Since it directly use the discrete sign function to simplify the parameter update, in practical applications, it is likely to cause gradient explosion or gradient disappearance.
In this paper, a Refined Lion optimizer(RLion) is presented using a continous function arctan as the weight of the learning rate with a scaleable factor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document}, also the moment bias correction is used as the part input of the arctan operation. The FasterNet14, EfficientNetV215, YOLOV816 on ImageNet1k17 are trained for classfication, Vision Transformers18 on Caltech 101 dataset19, YOLOV8, YOLOV11 on VOC2012 data are trained for object detection, TwinLiteNet20 on BDD100K, UNet on part of the CARLA self-driving dataset and the DeepLabV3+21 on camera data are trained for semantic segmentation. The Convergence and accuracy exhibit the Refined Lion optimizer can trace the gradient even when the learning rate is large, and fine tunning according to the bias to achieve better performance and more stable. ## Lion optimizer introduction
Lion optimizer is a more memory-efficient than Adam as it only keeps track of the momentum6. Different from adaptive optimizers, its update has the same magnitude for each parameter calculated through the sign operation. For autoregressive, masked language modeling, and fine-tuning, Lion exhibits a similar or better performance compared to Adam. It also requires a smaller learning rate than Adam due to the larger norm of the update produced by the sign function. The formula for Lion is exhibited as follows:1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} {\textbf{u}}_t = \text {sign}\big (\beta _1 {\textbf{m}}_{t-1} + (1 - \beta _1) {\textbf{g}}_t\big ) { + \lambda _t \mathbf {\theta }_{t-1}} \\ \mathbf {\theta }_t = \mathbf {\theta }_{t-1} - \eta _t {\textbf{u}}_t \\ {\textbf{m}}_t = \beta _2 {\textbf{m}}_{t-1} + (1 - \beta _2) {\textbf{g}}_t \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$\end{document}In Lion optimizer, the EMA(exponential moving average) is still leveraged to calculate momentum. The hyper-paramete \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _1,\beta _2 \in [0,1]$$\end{document} control the exponential decay rates of the moving average.

## Refined lion optimizer
Lion is simple and has fewer hyperparameters than AdamW and Adafactor. However, in the trainning process, Lion would cause gradient vanishing or gradient explosion on some deep models. Also, in refined Lion optimizer, momentum \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{m_t}=c_t$$\end{document} or moment bias correction \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{m_t} = c_t/(1-\beta _1^t)$$\end{document} is used to update the weight. ## Theoretical analysis
Sign-based Stochastic Gradient Descents(Sign-based SGDs) are proved based on uniformly bounded gradient gradient assumption for finite sum optimization22. When the objective is general convex, removing the gradient bounded assumption of SGD by only leveraging the smooth property is also sufficient to guarantee the convergence of nonconvex SGD23.

## Disscussion
In optimizer, when the distribution of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{m_t}$$\end{document} is far from uniform or symmetric, the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta$$\end{document} will deviate substantially from the direction of momentum motion which can cause that loss unable to track the gradient. It is the reason that the gradient may explode with the Lion optimizer. In RLion optimizer, especially when \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} is small, the fluctuation range is also small, which may cause the tracking may fall into local optimal and fail to reach global optimal. When \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} is large, the fluctuation is close to Lion, which may lead to the same phenomenon as Lion, which is not easy to converge and causes a gradient to explode or disappear. In the RLion, the default \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} is set as 50 for classification but 30 in YOLOV8 and YOLOV11 for object detection. Compared to the AdamW and Lion, RLion is slightly less versatile.
Some models are trained for classification, object detection, segmentation. The experimental results show that RLion is applicable to classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation tasks. Especially in classification, the accuracy of most models using the RLion optimizer is better than that of models using AdamW optimizers. The convergence performance of the RLion is closer to the Lion and the RLion can adjust fluctuation range according to the momentum, so the RLion versatility is better. On object detection and semantic segmentation, RLion can achieve or approache the performance of the adamw, but overcomes the Lion gradient’s explosive susceptibility. RLion’s scale factor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} has great influence on target detection and segmentation, so it is very important to select the appropriate \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document}. In RLion, the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} can be set as constant without adding additional storage.
Compared with the learning rate decay schedule, the RLion also can achieve good performance. The learning rate decay schedule applies a decay function to an optimizer step, given a provided initial learning rate. The decay function and decay steps are critical to the learning rate decay schedule. In each train step, the learning rate is fixed. In RLion, the scale factor does not act directly on learning rate but on momentum. When converged, the momentum is close to 0, the scale factor can help the optimizer more easily to trace the changes in momentum. Therefore, RLion can achieve superior performance. In the learning rate decay schedule, a judicious selection of the decay function and decay steps can lead to superior model performance. On the contrary, an improper choice will result in a decline in performance. Parameter selection for the learning rate decay schedule poses a greater challenge. Additionally, RLion can be combined with the decay strategy to enhance the model’s performance.

## Conclusion
In this paper, RLion optimizer based on the Lion optimizer is denoted with a scaleable factor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} and arctan operation to denote the update rule \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta _t=\theta _{t-1}-\eta _t(\frac{2}{\pi }arctan(\alpha *\hat{m_t})+\lambda \theta _{t-1})$$\end{document}. It is proved in theory that the expentation and variance of the arctan function are less than that of the sign function, the fluctuation of RLion is less than that of Lion. The scale factor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} can adjust the fluctuation range of the convergence. The FasterNet, EfficientNetV2, YOLOV8 on ImageNet1k are trained for classfication, the YOLOV8 , YOLOV11 on VOC2012 data and Object detection with Vision Transformers on Caltech 101 dataset are trained for Object detection and the DeepLabV3+ on instance-level_human_parsing dataset, TwinLiteNet on BDD100K, UNet on part of the CARLA self-driving dataset are trained for semantic segmentation by using AdamW, Lion and RLion with different learning rate, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} and the moment bias correction. Compared to the loss, accuracy, Iou of the AdamW, Lion optimizers, the RLion can promote the classfication accuracy about \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0 \sim +20\%$$\end{document} higher than AdamW on many models. For object detection and semantic segmentation, RLion achieves or approaches the performance of the adamw, but overcomes the Lion gradient’s explosive or disappear susceptibility. Most experiments demonstrates that the RLion optimizer has better convergence performance more stable, robust and versatility.
