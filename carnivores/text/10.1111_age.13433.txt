A PAX3 insertion in the Celestial breed and certain feline breeding
lines with dominant blue eyes
Abstract
During the last 60 years many inherited traits in
domestic outbred cats were selected and retained
giving birth to new breeds characterised by singular
coat or morphological phenotypes. Among them,
minimal white spotting associated with blue eyes
was selected by feline breeders to create the Altai,
Topaz, and Celestial breeds. Various established
breeds also introduced this trait in their lineages. The
trait, that was confirmed as autosomal dominant by
breeding data, was first described in domestic cats
from Kazakhstan and Russia, in British shorthair and
British longhair from Russia, and in Maine Coon cats
from the Netherlands, suggesting different founding
effects. Using a genome-­
w ide association study we
identified a single region on chromosome C1 that
was associated with the minimal white spotting and
blue eyes phenotype (also called DBE by breeders for
dominant blue eyes) in the French Celestial breed.
Within that region we identified Paired Box 3 (PAX3)
as the strongest candidate gene, since PAX3 is a key
regulator of MITF (Melanocyte-­Inducing Transcription
Factor) and PAX3 variants have been previously
identified in various species showing white spotting
with or without blue eyes including the mouse and the
horse. Whole genome sequencing of a Celestial cat
revealed an endogenous retrovirus LTR (long terminal
repeat) insertion within PAX3 intron 4 known to
contain regulatory sequences (conserved non-­coding
element [CNE]) involved in PAX3 expression. The
insertion is in the vicinity of CNE2 and CNE3. All
52 Celestial and Celestial-­
m ixed cats with a DBE
phenotype presented the insertion, that was absent in
their 22 non-­DBE littermates and in 87 non-­DBE cats
from various breeds. The outbred Celestial founder
was also heterozygous for the insertion. Additionally,
the variant was found in nine DBE Maine Coon
cats related to the Celestial founder and four DBE
Siberian cats with an uncertain origin. Segregation
of the variant in the Celestial breed is consistent with
dominant inheritance and does not appear to be
associated with deafness. We propose that this NC

During the last 60 years many inherited traits in domestic
outbred cats were selected and retained giving birth to
new breeds characterised by singular coat or morphological phenotypes. Among them, minimal white spotting
associated with blue eyes was selected by feline breeders
to create the Altai, Topaz, and Celestial breeds (http://​
messy​beast.​com/​blue-­​eye-­​breeds.​htm; https://​loof.​asso.​
fr). Various established breeds also introduced this trait
in their lineages. The trait, which was confirmed as autosomal dominant by breeding data, was first described
in domestic cats from Kazakhstan and Russia, in British
shorthair and British longhair from Russia, and in Maine
Coon cats from the Netherlands, suggesting different
founding effects. It is admitted that the Altai breed resulted from a single founder whereas the Topaz breed was
created by mixing two blue-­eyed lineages, one of them
being the Altai lineage (http://​messy​beast.​com/​blue-­​eye-­​
breeds.​htm). In France, the Celestial breed was founded
in 2016 using an outbred male showing minimal white
spotting and a blue eye (Figure 1a), that was bought in
Ukraine, and a purebred British longhair female with
no white-­spotting and copper eyes (https://​loof.​asso.​fr).
Both cats were tested wild-­type for gloving (Montague
et al., 2014), white spotting (WS), and dominant white (W)
alleles (David et al., 2014). Systematic breeding of blue-­
eyed descents with purebred British longhair and British
shorthair cats with no white and copper or green eyes
revealed an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern for
the phenotype that was called DBE by breeders (dominant blue eyes). DBE with DBE breeding was avoided
as a result to the warning issued by Altai and Topaz
breeders who reported white and deaf cats born to two
DBE parents. These cats were suspected to be homozygous for DBE (http://​messy​beast.​com/​blue-­​eye-­​breeds.​
htm). Rapidly, breeders also reported kittens with minimal white spotting and no blue eye but with a red-­eye

kittens with blue eyes but no visible white spotting. They
suspected latent cats to be carrier of DBE without expressing the blue eye phenotype. In this context, the Livre
Officiel des Origines Felines (the French feline studbook:
https://​loof.​asso.​fr, LOOF) accepted the Celestial breeding program with important restrictions: all the British
shorthair and British longhair cats that were introduced
must have been DNA tested wild-­type for gloving, white
spotting, and dominant white alleles. White, white spotted, colourpoint and silver shaded coated cats are not allowed to breed. All cats must have a DNA profile and
parentage must be confirmed for each kitten. These strict
rules are applied to insure traceability and healthy breeding in the absence of a DBE DNA test. Finally, since the
beginning of the Celestial breeding program in 2016,
cats were tested for hearing using the brainstem auditory
evocated response (BAER) test. Among 47 tested DBE-­
Celestial cats, three cats were diagnosed unilaterally
deaf, and none was bilaterally deaf. These three cats had
history of otitis, vestibular syndrome and falling with ear
cartilage lesion before BAER testing. A single Celestial
DBE × DBE mating was performed, giving birth to three
healthy kittens and a white kitten that died from cleft palate just after birth. Eye colour and hearing status were
therefore unrecordable. In summary, the DBE trait in the
Celestial breed was characterised by an autosomal dominant inheritance with variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance for both white spotting and blue eyes,
and pleiotropy (Figure 1).
To initiate the search for the Celestial DBE variant,
we collected DNA and phenotypic data from 130 DBE
Celestial, Celestial-­
mixed, Altai, Maine Coon, British
shorthair, British longhair, Chinese Tank (Munchkin–
British shorthair/longhair mix), and Siberian cats, and
from 101 control cats from the Celestial breed and from
various breeds (Appendix S1). Among DBE cats, the
white kitten that was born to two DBE Celestial parents
was supposed to be homozygous for the DBE variant.
All other DBE cats were supposed to be heterozygous.
Forty-­four Celestial or Celestial-­mixed cats (including 23
DBE and 21 controls) were genotyped using the Illumina
Feline 63k SNP array (Appendix S1, Figure 1g), among
which 60 599 SNPs yielded usable results (minor allele
frequency MAF >5%, genotyping rate >95%). All 44
cats had genotyping rates >95%, and all were conserved
for the analysis. Following basic case–control analysis,
the genomic inflation factor was 1.29 and the 20 highest significant associations were identified for 20 SNPs,
among which 14 markers were located on chromosome
C1 (Appendix S1, Figure 1e, Table S1). After Bonferroni
correction of the praw values for multiple tests, a single
SNP located at position 208 399 153 on chromosome C1
had significant pBonferroni value (Figure 1f, Table S1). The
14 SNPs from chromosome C1 were located between position 202 883 881 bp and position 210 126 247 bp (Table S1)

gene, Paired Box 3 (PAX3, chromosome C1: 206 906 912–
207 004 190 bp, Felis_catus 9.0), that was shown in several
vertebrate species to encode a transcription factor regulating various pathways including muscle, neural and melanocyte development and chromatin structure (Boudjadi
et al., 2018; Monsoro-­Burq, 2015). PAX3 variants have
been identified in mice and horses with white spotting

pigmentary syndrome characterised by a white forelock
and premature greying, heterochromia or brilliant blue
eyes, and deafness (www.​omim.​org, OMIM: #193500).
Exons and exon–intron boundaries from PAX3 were
sequenced in two DBE cats and a non-­DBE cat and
compared to the reference feline sequence (transcript
ENSFCAT00000018878.5, Appendix S1, Table S2). Five
synonymous variants were identified (Table S3).
We thus analysed the whole genome sequence of a DBE
cat to screen for non-­coding variants in PAX3 regulatory
sequences and across the candidate region (Table S4).
We specifically searched for insertions and deletions
(structural variants) because: (i) the 1.6-­kb upstream region of PAX3 was shown to contain two evolutionarily
conserved elements that are critical for its expression; (ii)
an upstream enhancer that mediates hypaxial somite expression has been identified more than 6 kb upstream of
the transcription start site; (iii) conserved non-­coding elements were identified within the fourth intron of PAX3
(Degenhardt et al., 2010; Moore et al., 2013).
Paired-­
end reads (2 × 150 bp) were collected from
a shotgun DNA fragment library from a DBE cat
achieving genome-­w ide coverage of 30×. Variants were
called against the Felis_catus 9.0 reference genome
(Appendix S1). A single variant from the candidate region drew our attention, namely NC_018730.3:g.2069740
29_206974030insN[395] located in the intronic region of
PAX3 (Table S4). This variant consisted in a 395-­bp insertion at position 206,974,029 in PAX3 intron 4.
We then genotyped 87 non-­DBE control cats from
various breeds (British shorthair and British longhair, Bengal, Birman, Chartreux, Celestial-­
m ix,
Devon Rex, Donskoy, Maine Coon, Siamese, Siberian,
Sphynx, Turkish Angora, Domestic shorthair) for
the
NC_018730.3:g.206974029_206974030insN[395]
variant (Appendix S1). None of them carried the insertion (Table 1). We genotyped 61 purebred Celestial
cats for this variant. All 45 DBE cats and the latent
cat that were born to a DBE parent and a non-­DBE
parent were heterozygous for the insertion. The 14
non-­DBE Celestial cats were wild-­type. The putative
homozygous-­
DBE white kitten, that died at birth
from cleft palate, was homozygous for the insertion.

The NC_018730.3:g.206974029_206974030insN[395]
variant consisted of an element that demonstrated
the highest level of identity to a long terminal repeat
(LTR) from a FERV1 (feline endogenous retrovirus
1; Appendix S1). Alignment of feline PAX3 intron 4
sequence with human, mouse and zebrafish intron 4
sequences was performed (Appendix S1) and showed
that five sequences were conserved between cat,
human, mouse, and zebrafish (Figure S1). Using conserved non-­
c oding element (CNE) sequences from
the fourth intron of PAX3 that were published by
Moore et al. (2013) for the zebrafish, blast (https://​
blast.​n cbi.​n lm.​n ih.​gov/​Blast.​cgi) and the Felis_catus
9.0 reference sequence, we identified that the conserved sequences found in feline PAX3 intron 4 were
homologous to the CNE sequences reported for the
zebrafish (Moore et al., 2013; Figure S2). The LTR
insertion at position 206,974,029 on chromosome C1
lied in the vicinity of these CNEs, especially CNE2
and CNE3 (Figure S2) that were shown to be regulators of PAX3 expression (Moore et al., 2013). PAX3
is transcription factor shown to be a key regulator of
MITF (Melanocyte-­I nducing Transcription Factor), a
protein that regulates melanocyte development and is
responsible for pigment cell-­s pecific transcription of
the melanogenesis enzyme genes (Cui & Man, 2023).
As previously mentioned, variants in both PAX3 and
MITF have been associated with pigmentary phenotypes such as white spotting and blue eyes in animal
species (www.​i nfor​m atics.​jax.​org/​a llele/​MGI:​1856173;
https://​omia.​org/​home, OMIA:001688-­
9796; Epstein
et al., 1993; Hauswirth et al., 2012, 2013; McFadden
et al., 2023).
In conclusion, we identified a non-­coding PAX3 variant associated with the DBE phenotype of Celestial cats
and certain Maine Coon and Siberian cats. This variant

is located within the fourth intron of PAX3, in the vicinity of CNEs involved in PAX3 expression. We propose
that this NC_018730.3:g.206974029_206974030insN[395]
variant represents the DBECEL (Celestial dominant blue
eye) allele in the domestic cat, which further enlarges the
panel of PAX3 alleles described in animals. Additional
research is needed to identify the other feline DBE variants that have spread among breeds, especially the original variant that defines the Altai breed.
