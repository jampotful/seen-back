Dominant blue eyes in Maine Coon cats: New PAX3 variant and updated phenotypic data
Marie Abitbol, Ana Cloquell, Adriana Kaczmarska, Karen Holmes, Gesine Lühken, Kristina Macaulay (2025). Animal Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1111/age.70020. doi:10.1111/age.70020. Licence: cc-by-nc-nd. Text from europepmc-xml.

# Dominant blue eyes in Maine Coon cats: New PAX3 variant and updated phenotypic data

## Abstract
AbstractFeline minimal white spotting associated with blue eyes, a trait called DBE by breeders for dominant blue eyes, was selected recently to create various lineages of cats and the Celestial breed. Previously, we identified three Paired Box 3 (PAX3) gene variants that account for some of the DBE phenotypes in cats, confirming different founding effects. Using a candidate gene approach, we identified a fourth PAX3 variant in a purebred Maine Coon lineage segregating for DBE and lacking a previously identified genomic variant. Segregation of the variant was consistent with the dominant inheritance pattern observed in this DBE lineage called the “Agostino line” by breeders. This PAX3:c.160del frameshift variant, located in PAX3 exon 2, was predicted to produce a truncated PAX3 protein: PAX3:p.(His54ThrTer108) that would lack 78% of the amino acids of the wildtype protein. All 13 DBE Maine Coon cats from the line were heterozygous for the variant, that was absent in all non‐DBE Maine Coon cats from the line and in all control cat genomes (n > 300). We propose that this PAX3:c.160del variant represents the DBE AGO (Agostino Dominant Blue Eyes) allele in the domestic cat. In addition, we improved the phenotypic description of DBE in Maine Coon cats, highlighting the need to reassess the prevalence of deafness and revealing the existence of latent cats in the previously described DBERE (Rociri Elvis Dominant Blue Eyes) Maine Coon lineage.
Minimal white spotting associated with blue eyes was selected by feline breeders to create the Celestial breed and develop various lines of established purebred breed or crossbred lines of cats (http://messybeast.com/blue‐eye‐breeds.htm; https://loof.asso.fr). In all lines, the trait was confirmed as autosomal dominant by breeding data, but different founding effects were confirmed by molecular analyses (Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024). The trait was therefore called DBE (dominant blue eyes) by breeders. We previously identified three Paired Box 3 (PAX3) gene variants accounting for some DBE feline phenotypes, but many DBE lines still lack a molecular aetiology (Abitbol, Couronné, et al., 2024; Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024; Rudd Garces et al., 2024). In the Maine Coon breed, we first identified four distinct DBE lines (Rociri Elvis, Pillowtalk‐Foreigner, Topaz, and Nahal lines; Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024) and the presence of two of the three PAX3 variants (Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024; Rudd Garces et al., 2024). The NC_018730.3:g.206974029_206974030insN[395] (DBE CEL for Celestial Dominant Blue Eyes) variant was introduced by crossbreeding in the Topaz line and the PAX3:c.937C>T (DBE RE for Rociri Elvis Dominant Blue Eyes) variant arose spontaneously in the purebred Duch Rociri Elvis line (Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024; Rudd Garces et al., 2024). The DBE CEL variant was not associated with deafness, in contrast to the DBE RE variant (Abitbol, Couronné, et al., 2024; Rudd Garces et al., 2024). To date, we have not identified any Maine Coon cats carrying the NC_018730.3:g.206975776_206975777insN[433] variant (DBE ALT for Altai Dominant Blue Eyes; Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024) and the variants segregating in the Pillowtalk‐Foreigner and Nahal lines are not yet known.
Recently, cat breeders reported a fifth DBE lineage in the Maine Coon breed called the Agostino line. To characterise the DBE trait in this line, we collected pedigree data, cheek‐swab samples and pictures directly from feline breeders. A total of 91 cats were included in the study. They included cats from previously described DBE lines: two cats from the Topaz line, two cats from the Celestial (DBECEL) line, three cats from the Altai (DBEALT) line and 84 Maine Coon cats among which 27 cats were from the Agostino line, 26 cats were from the previously described Rociri Elvis (DBERE) line and one cat was from the Pillowtalk‐Foreigner line. We also included 30 purebred Maine Coon cats that were not related to any DBE line (Table 1, Tables S2 and S3; for the lines of cats please refer to Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024).

DBE cats from the Agostino line exhibited one or two blue eyes associated with minimal white spotting. In some cats, more extended white spotting (bicolour, harlequin, or van phenotypes with white areas representing 25%–50%, 50%–75%, and >75% of the coat respectively) was present because of the W S white spotting allele (David et al., 2014; Figure 1). Segregation of DBE in the Agostino line was consistent with the dominant inheritance pattern observed in other DBE lineages (Figure 1; Abitbol, Couronné, et al., 2024; Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024; Rudd Garces et al., 2024).

DNA was extracted from buccal swabs using a Maxwell® 16 Instrument (Promega Corporation, Madison, USA), according to the manufacturers' protocols. We genotyped two DBE cats from the Agostino line for the known PAX3 variants, according to Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al. (2024). The two cats were wildtype for the three DBE CEL , DBE ALT , and DBE RE variants. Thus, we performed a candidate gene approach focusing on PAX3, the strongest candidate gene for DBE in cats. We recruited both DBE and non‐DBE individuals from a Maine Coon family belonging to the Agostino line (Figure 1). Subsequently, we performed PCR amplification and Sanger sequencing of PAX3 exons and intron–exon boundaries in three Maine Coon cats including two cats from the Agostino line (Table S1), as previously described (Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024). We compared sequences to the reference feline sequence (transcript ENSFCAT00000018878.5; Felis_catus 9.0 reference genome) and identified four synonymous variants and a non‐synonymous PAX3:c.592G>T variant that was predicted benign by PolyPhen‐2 (http://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2/) and MutPred2 (http://mutpred.mutdb.org). We also identified PAX3:c.160del frameshift variant in PAX3 exon 2 (Table S1, Figure S1). This PAX3:c.160del variant was predicted to produce a truncated PAX3 protein: PAX3:p.(His54ThrTer108) that would lack 78% of the amino acids of the wildtype protein (Figure S1). We then genotyped 36 Maine Coon cats from the family for this PAX3:c.160del variant using PCR amplification and Sanger sequencing of PAX3 exon 2 (as described in Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024). All 12 DBE cats that were born to a DBE parent and a non‐DBE parent were heterozygous for the variant. The 24 non‐DBE Maine Coon cats from the family (including 14 cats born from a parent belonging to the Agostino line and 10 unrelated breeding stocks) and 20 Maine Coon controls were wildtype (Table 1, Figure 1). The variant was also found in one additional DBE Maine Coon cat from the Agostino line that was recruited separately but revealed linked to the family (Table 1, Figure 1). We searched for the PAX3:c.160del variant in the genomes of the 99 Lives cat whole genome sequencing project (Buckley et al., 2020; Lyons et al., 2021) but did not detect the deletion (Table 1). Finally, DBE cats (n = 11) from the DBEALT, Topaz, and DBECEL lines, and Maine Coon cats from the DBERE and Topaz lines were wildtype (Table 1). We observed a perfect genotype–phenotype correlation for PAX3:c.160del in cats from the Agostino line (Figure 1). Thus, we have identified a fourth PAX3 variant associated with the DBE phenotype in cats, that was classified as pathogenic according to the Animal Variant Classification Guidelines (Table S2, Boeykens et al., 2024). We therefore propose that this PAX3:c.160del variant represents the DBE AGO (Agostino Dominant Blue Eyes) allele in the domestic cat.
In the Agostino (DBEAGO) Maine Coon line, no deafness was reported by breeders, but no brainstem auditory evoked response (BAER) testing was performed. In contrast, in the DBERE Maine Coon line where the PAX3:c.937C>T variant segregates, deafness was associated with DBE in the eight DBE cats that were BAER tested (Rudd Garces et al., 2024). This high level of deafness in the DBERE family of Maine Coon cats studied was in contrast with data reported by breeders for this line and for other DBE lines (Abitbol, Couronné, et al., 2024; Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024). We thus conducted BAER test (TruTrace® EMG, Deymed Diagnostic, Hronov, Czech Republic) on 10 additional DBE and non‐DBE cats from the DBERE line. The cats underwent this examination at the request of their owners, as part of a screening for deafness in DBE cats. Testing was performed on awake animals without any sedation. None of the six DBE or four non‐DBE Maine Coon exhibited deafness (Table 2, Figure S2). This result contrasted with the result of the previous study which tested 10 Maine Coon cats, eight of which were DBE. These eight DBE cats were deaf while the two non‐DBE cats were hearing. Additionally, among the eight DBE deaf cats, five cats had less than 25% of white in their coat. Thus, deafness in these cats was not associated with coat colour, amount of white in the coat or the presence of the W S allele (Rudd Garces et al., 2024). As all the cats were from the same family, it is possible that the deafness in this pedigree was due to modifying alleles from their genetic background. A similar situation has been described in white spotted dogs due to Melanocyte Inducing Transcription Factor (MITF) variants and which are predisposed to deafness (Kluth & Distl, 2013; Strain, 2015). Finally, in DBERE cats from the previous study (Rudd Garces et al., 2024) and from our study, a small amount of white in the coat (less than 25%) was observed in both deaf (Rudd Garces et al., 2024; n = 5) and hearing (our study, n = 6) cats.

In conclusion regarding deafness in DBE cats, it is associated with the DBE RE variant, but further data are needed to determine its prevalence. Deafness has not been associated with the DBE CEL and DBE ALT variants in heterozygous cats but in compound DBE CEL /DBE ALT heterozygous and in DBE ALT /DBE ALT homozygous cats (Abitbol, Couronné, et al., 2024; Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024). Data are lacking for the new DBE AGO variant, due to the recent history of this lineage.
In the DBECEL and DBEALT lines, breeders reported kittens with minimal to no white spotting and no blue eye but with a red‐eye effect during infancy (they called them latent cats). These latent cats were proven to be carrier of DBE by genotyping, but without expressing the blue eye phenotype (Abitbol, Couronné, et al., 2024; Abitbol, Dufaure de Citres, et al., 2024). In the DBERE line, no latent cat was reported to date (Rudd Garces et al., 2024). By genotyping Maine Coon cats from the DBERE and Agostino lines for both PAX3:c.937C>T and PAX3:c.160del variants, we identified no latent cat in the Agostino line (Table 1) but two latent cats in the DBERE line (Table S3, Figure S3).
In conclusion, our study identified a fourth PAX3 variant for DBE in cats and improved the phenotypic description of DBE in Maine Coon cats, highlighting the need to reassess the prevalence of deafness and revealing the existence of latent cats in the DBERE Maine Coon lineage.
