As artificial intelligence (AI) moves from digital interpretation to embodied
action, robotic perception must move beyond visible-scene description towards
physical representations. Inspired by dragonfly compound eyes, DragonflyTeraVision is proposed as a task-driven heterogeneous sensing architecture
for physically interpretable environment reconstruction. The system integrates
panoramic vision for dense semantics, Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR)
for metric line-of-sight (LoS) geometry and monostatic terahertz (THz) sensing for propagation-aware structure and material-sensitive reflection responses.
A task-driven agent decomposes each sensing objective, selects modalityspecific processing modules and associates their outputs through physics-guided
multi-modal constraints. As a demonstration, Dragonfly-TeraVision reconstructs
semantic three-dimensional (3D) geometry in an indoor environment, by detecting THz-derived hidden structural evidence beyond optical/LiDAR visibility
and associating semantic surfaces with material-sensitive THz responses. This
work establishes a route towards environment reconstruction for embodied
sensing systems that links visual semantics, metric geometry and materialsensitive THz propagation and reflection responses within a unified physical scene
representation.

As artificial intelligence (AI) moves from digital interpretation towards physical
action, perception becomes a central interface between computation and the world.
For embodied AI, autonomous robots and physical agents, sensing must support
action, interaction and decision-making in changing real-world scenes, rather than only
recognizing object categories or estimating depth [1–6]. Visual recognition, semantic mapping and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) have substantially

these advances primarily recover image appearance and line-of-sight (LoS) geometry,
referring to surfaces that are directly visible. In reality, physical environments also
contain occluded structures, reflective surfaces and material-dependent responses that
are not directly captured by appearance or LoS geometry. A sensing system designed
to “see the world” therefore requires a representation that combines semantic identity,
metric geometry, hidden or non-line-of-sight (NLoS) content and material-sensitive
physical attributes.
Biological perception offers a design reference for converting wide-field, incomplete
sensory inputs into behaviorally relevant representations. Dragonflies provide a particularly relevant example: their compound eyes provide broad visual coverage, while
specialized visual neurons selectively encode target features in complex scenes [12, 13].
During prey interception, their steering behavior is consistent with internal models
that predict target and body motion, rather than a simple reaction to instantaneous
visual input [14]. This combination of wide-field observation, task-relevant feature
selection and predictive perceptual organization suggests an architectural principle
for engineered sensing. Rather than treating sensor measurements as raw signals for
uniform fusion, a multi-modal sensing system can use heterogeneous sensing modalities to capture complementary aspects of a scene. A central agent, analogous to a
biological brain, then selects and associates task-relevant observations into a common
physical representation. Similar abstractions from animal perception and navigation
have recently enabled efficient robotic systems [15–17].
The engineering counterpart of this principle is not to add sensors indiscriminately,
but to combine sensing modalities with complementary physical contrast mechanisms.
For physical-world perception, Dragonfly-TeraVision follows this principle by combining panoramic vision, Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) and terahertz (THz)
sensing. Panoramic vision observes dense texture, color and object-level context, making it suitable for semantic interpretation, but images alone are ambiguous in metric
scale and provide little direct access to hidden structures or material-dependent electromagnetic (EM) properties [18, 19]. LiDAR converts visible surfaces into an accurate
three-dimensional (3D) geometric scaffold and has become central to robotic mapping,
ranging and camera–LiDAR perception [20–26]. However, LiDAR is fundamentally
constrained to LoS measurement, which measures exposed surfaces, while semantic
and material attributes must be inferred from additional information. Different from
vision and LiDAR, THz sensing introduces another physical contrast thanks to the
large bandwidth, which supports delay-resolved propagation measurements, and interaction with matter enables material-sensitive reflection responses and, for selected
non-metallic occluders, penetration or NLoS observations [27–34]. These properties
make THz sensing a natural complement to vision and LiDAR for hidden-object
and material-aware perception, although THz measurements are sparse, directiondependent and difficult to interpret without semantic and geometric context. Thus,
the three modalities should not be viewed as interchangeable sensors, but as heterogeneous perceptual modalities that contribute different parts of a physical scene
representation, analogous to compound eyes of a dragonfly.

Multi-modal sensing and fusion have been widely studied in autonomous driving,
semantic mapping, wearable sensing and robotic perception, where cameras, LiDAR,
inertial sensors, radar, tactile sensors and soft sensors are combined to improve robustness or accuracy [22, 35–41]. These studies show the value of complementary sensing,
but most fusion pipelines are optimized for predefined outputs such as object detection, depth completion, localization, kinematics estimation or semantic maps. In
parallel, agentic frameworks are emerging for robotic mapping, embodied language
models and embodied decision-making, suggesting that task-level reasoning can help
coordinate perception, simulation and action [2, 42–44]. However, task-driven agentic integration of vision, LiDAR and THz sensing for semantic, geometric, NLoS and
material-aware perception remains largely unexplored. In particular, propagation- and
material-sensitive THz observations are rarely connected to semantic and geometric
scene representations through an explicit sensing workflow.
In this work, we propose Dragonfly-TeraVision, a dragonfly-inspired heterogeneous
sensing system for task-driven multi-modal perception, as illustrated in Fig. 1. The
system integrates panoramic optical vision, LiDAR and monostatic THz sensing as
complementary perceptual modalities, enabling a physical scene representation that
jointly describes visible semantics, metric geometry, hidden structures and materialsensitive attributes. A task-driven agent coordinates the sensing workflow according

Dragonfly-TeraVision architecture
At the architectural level, the dragonfly analogy is used to motivate distributed observation followed by selective perceptual organization. Compound eyes collect many
visual samples, whereas the nervous system integrates distributed signals to support navigation, target tracking and environmental awareness. The relevant principle
is therefore not a simple addition of independent views. Instead, multiple perceptual observations of objects, spatial layout, motion, occlusion, and surface properties
are selected and interpreted according to the behavioral task. Future embodied AI
and robotic sensing systems face an analogous requirement. To construct a physical understanding of the surrounding world, they must organize semantic identity,

Dragonfly-TeraVision follows this principle at the system level. As shown in Fig. 2a,
heterogeneous sensing “eyes” are coupled to a task-driven “brain”, so that visible
semantics, metric geometry, hidden structures and material-sensitive responses can
be treated as complementary descriptions of the same physical scene. The integrated
sensing platform is shown in Fig. 2b and c, where a panoramic fisheye camera, LiDAR
and a monostatic THz transceiver (TRx) are co-located as artificial sensing eyes with
distinct physical contrast mechanisms. The panoramic fisheye camera records dense
appearance and semantic context over a wide field of view. LiDAR measures LoS metric
geometry and provides the spatial scaffold of the visible environment. THz sensing captures propagation-resolved EM responses, including scattering, penetration-enabled
hidden-object cues and material-sensitive reflection characteristics.
The agent provides the corresponding computational organization. Rather than
treating the three modalities as independent pipelines, it selects task-relevant observations and processing modules according to the sensing objective. The input consists
of a task objective and an extensible data manifest. The task objective specifies the
desired inference, such as semantic geometry, hidden-object sensing or material-aware
interpretation. The data manifest records the available sensing modalities, calibration
information, coordinate frames and processing status. Although the present implementation focuses on three functions of panoramic vision, LiDAR and THz sensing,
the manifest is not restricted to these modalities and can be extended to additional
sensors once their data formats and calibration information are defined.
Given the task objective and data manifest, relevant sensing skills are selected from
a modular skill library and assembled into an ordered workflow. In the demonstrations
reported here, the skill library contains representative modules for vision-based semantic segmentation, LiDAR point-cloud registration, THz multipath-component (MPC)
extraction, spatial mapping, NLoS scattering extraction and material association.
Other sensing or fusion algorithms can be incorporated through the same input–
output interface. The selected skills are then executed in sequence, and their outputs
are routed into a common task-specific representation. A web-based implementation
of the task-driven agent is demonstrated in Supplementary Video 1.

Heterogeneous sensing modalities
The experiment is conducted in an indoor L-shaped hallway to instantiate the heterogeneous sensing modality inputs before task-driven integration. The scene contains
representative structural and material elements, including walls, floor, ceiling, doors,
corridor corners and a curtain. Sixteen sensing positions are deployed along the hallway with an inter-position spacing of 1.5 m, and the sensor platform is positioned
at a height of 2 m, as shown in Fig. 3a and b. The co-located platform integrates a
QooCam 8K fisheye panoramic camera, a Livox HAP (TX) LiDAR, and a monostatic
THz transceiver (TRx) on the same rotator platform. At each sensing position, the
three sensing modalities produce complementary raw measurements: i) red–green–blue
(RGB) equirectangular panoramic images with a resolution of 7680 × 3840 pixels, ii)

Modality-specific processing converts these observations into interpretable evidence for later task-driven association. For the vision modality, semantic segmentation
is performed using ADE20K-pretrained Mask2Former [45, 46], yielding a dense
semantic map with labels for ceiling, wall, floor, door, curtain and other visible
structures (Fig. 3d). For the LiDAR modality, point clouds from the 16 sensing
positions are aligned by iterative closest point (ICP) registration [47], after which
orthogonality-constrained random sample consensus (RANSAC) is used to extract
dominant LoS planar structures [48], providing a metrically grounded geometric scaffold of the visible hallway (Fig. 3f). For the THz modality, calibrated angular frequency
responses are processed using space-alternating generalized expectation-maximization
(SAGE)-based MPC extraction [33, 49] to estimate the delay, angle and power of
dominant propagation components. These components are then spatially mapped into
the 3D coordinate system as sparse, power-coded scattering points that represent
propagation-derived structural evidence (Fig. 3h). These outputs therefore constitute
modality-specific descriptors of semantic labels, geometry and propagation responses,
rather than a unified scene representation.
The complementarity of these modality-level observations defines the input space
for the task-driven multi-modal agent. Together, these modalities provide heterogeneous physical contrast, with optical appearance supporting semantic cues, LiDAR
ranging constraining LoS geometry and THz propagation responses carrying hiddenstructure and material-sensitive evidence. The agentic integration framework is
therefore required to select and associate complementary evidence according to the
sensing objective, rather than treating the inputs as interchangeable data streams.

Task-driven multi-modal perception
Task-driven multi-modal perception is formulated as a scene-construction problem
rather than a set of pairwise fusion tasks. By analogy with biological perception,
a physical scene can be organized as a set of attributes, including object identity,
appearance, shape, location, occlusion and material properties. Dragonfly-TeraVision
follows this principle by keeping the modality-level descriptors from all heterogeneous sensing eyes available to the task-driven agent. Given a sensing objective, the
agent decomposes the objective into required scene attributes and routes the relevant
evidence from vision, LiDAR and THz sensing into a common physical representation. In the L-shaped hallway, this organization produces semantic metric geometry,
THz-assisted hidden-structure evidence, semantic-material association and a unified
semantic–geometric–material scene model, as shown in Fig. 4.
Physically interpretable reconstruction first requires establishing what visible
structures are present and where they are located. This function is mainly supported
by panoramic vision and LiDAR. Vision-derived semantic labels identify scene categories such as walls, floor, ceiling, doors, windows, lights, signs, banisters and the
curtain region, whereas LiDAR provides the metric scaffold of exposed LoS surfaces.

The final tri-modal representation integrates these task-specific interpretations
within a common scene model (Fig. 4d). Vision contributes semantic categories,
LiDAR anchors these categories to metric LoS geometry, and THz sensing adds
propagation-derived hidden-structure evidence and material-sensitive THz reflection

where they are located, whether additional structural evidence exists beyond the visible occluder, and which material-related THz responses are associated with selected
semantic regions. This result illustrates the role of the task-driven multi-modal agent
in coordinating heterogeneous observations. Instead of simply accumulating sensor
outputs, the agent organizes them according to the perceptual function required for
physically interpretable environment reconstruction. This representation provides the
basis for the capability comparison and controlled hidden-object sensing analysis
below.

evidence according to the sensing objective.

The individual modalities exhibit distinct capability profiles. Panoramic vision
provides dense appearance and semantic context, with its physical access constrained to optically visible surfaces and image-domain evidence. LiDAR supplies
metrically accurate LoS geometry and forms a reliable spatial scaffold, while semantic labels, material response and concealed structure require additional evidence
beyond geometric returns. THz sensing contributes propagation-sensitive information,
including penetration-enabled hidden-object cues and material-sensitive reflection
responses, although the evidence is sparse, directional and dependent on scan geometry. Dragonfly-TeraVision improves scene representation completeness by preserving
these different physical meanings and associating them through the task-driven sensing
agent, rather than treating the modalities as interchangeable data streams.
Hidden-object and material sensing form a stringent test of this capability profile.
In this task, the relevant scene content lies beyond the visible boundary accessible to
camera and LiDAR. To isolate this condition, an opaque-cup benchmark is conducted
with one empty cup and three cups containing steel, glass and wood balls (Fig. 5b).
The cups are visually opaque and geometrically similar from the outside, restricting
vision and LiDAR to external appearance and surface geometry. THz measurements
exploit partial penetration through the paper cup and resolved delayed reflections
from the concealed object.
The THz power delay profiles (PDPs) separate the common cup-wall response
from hidden-object responses (Fig. 5c). The first peak appears in all four cases and
corresponds to reflection from the paper-cup wall, confirming that the same visible
boundary is present across the measurements. A second delayed peak appears near
3.28 ns for the steel, glass and wood cases, corresponding under the monostatic roundtrip model to an approximately 0.49 m one-way propagation distance between the THz

Conclusions
As embodied AI moves into physical environments, robotic perception is increasingly
expected to move beyond visible layout towards representations that capture how
a scene is structured, occluded and physically constituted. Such perception requires
semantic identity, metric location, hidden structure and material-sensitive THz
response to be organized as coupled attributes of the same environment. DragonflyTeraVision addresses this requirement through a heterogeneous sensing architecture
inspired by dragonfly compound eyes in which panoramic vision, LiDAR and monostatic THz sensing act as complementary sensing eyes, while an agentic integration
framework functions as the task-driven agent. Rather than accumulating independent
sensor outputs, the framework decomposes each sensing objective and assembles the
relevant semantic, geometric, propagation-derived and material-sensitive evidence into
a common physical scene representation.
The indoor hallway experiment demonstrates this principle at the scene level.
Dragonfly-TeraVision anchors vision-derived semantic labels to metric LoS geometry,
uses THz-derived scattering evidence to supplement the visible LiDAR scaffold near
a curtain-occluded region and associates selected semantic surfaces with materialsensitive THz reflection responses. The opaque-cup benchmark further isolates the
hidden-object capability under controlled occlusion. When camera and LiDAR observations are restricted to external cup surfaces, delayed THz reflections reveal concealed
balls, and the compensated reflection-loss estimates are consistent with steel, glass
and wood. Together, these results establish Dragonfly-TeraVision as an experimental
route towards embodied perception systems that organize what an environment looks
like, where its structures are, what remains hidden and how selected materials respond
to THz sensing within one physically interpretable representation.

THz measurements are processed to extract propagation-derived structural evidence
and material-sensitive reflection-loss estimates from calibrated angular frequency
responses. The overall procedure follows signal-processing approaches used in previous THz sensing and propagation-analysis studies [27–29, 50]. Calibrated frequency
responses are transformed into the delay domain to obtain impulse responses. For
each azimuth-elevation pointing direction, the response contains a superposition
of propagation components caused by reflections and scattering from surrounding
structures.
Candidate MPC regions are first identified using connected-component analysis in
the delay-azimuth-elevation domain. The angle-delay power distribution is thresholded
and represented as a 3D connectivity graph, where neighboring cells with similar delay
and angular positions are grouped into a connected domain. Within each connected
domain, an element-wise SAGE algorithm is applied to estimate the delay and complex
amplitude of local MPCs. A 3D MPC tracking procedure is then performed across
adjacent azimuth-elevation cells, and the direction of each tracked MPC is refined by
maximum-likelihood angle estimation using the antenna radiation pattern.

Vision–LiDAR association anchors visual semantic labels to metric 3D structures. For
each sensing position, the LiDAR point cloud is projected onto the corresponding
equirectangular panoramic image using the calibrated camera–LiDAR pose. Each projected LiDAR point is matched with the pixel at the same viewing direction, and the
semantic label of this pixel is obtained from the Mask2Former segmentation result.
After semantic label assignment, local semantic–geometric observations from different
sensing positions are transformed into the common global coordinate system using the
LiDAR registration results.
LiDAR–THz integration combines visible LoS geometry with THz-derived structural information from occluded regions. The globally registered LiDAR point cloud is
retained as the geometric scaffold for directly observable surfaces. THz-derived scattering points are transformed into the same global coordinate frame and interpreted
as propagation-derived structural evidence. This integration allows visible LiDAR
geometry and THz-inferred hidden structures to be represented within a unified
reconstruction.
Vision–THz semantic-material integration associates THz reflection responses
with semantic regions. Selected near-normal-incidence specular components are used
because they provide more stable reflection-loss estimates than weak diffuse or grazingangle components. For each selected THz path, the estimated azimuth and elevation
angles are projected onto the panoramic semantic mask. The semantic label at the

The task-driven agent is formulated as an extensible organizer for sensing data and
processing units. The task specification defines the sensing objective, such as semantic
geometry construction, hidden-object sensing, NLoS structural sensing or materialaware interpretation. The data manifest describes the available sensing modalities, file
locations, calibration information, coordinate frames and processing status. In this
work, the manifest includes panoramic vision images and semantic masks, LiDAR
point clouds and LoS geometric maps, THz MPC estimates and THz material reference
measurements.
