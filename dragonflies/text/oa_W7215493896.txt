Abstract
The rapid expansion of Internet of Things (IoT) deployments has intensified the demand for secure and
imperceptible communication methods capable of protecting sensitive information on lightweight devices.
Conventional cryptographic techniques, while important, cannot independently provide the level of
concealment and reliability required in constrained IoT environments. Many existing steganography and
deep learning-based techniques offer partial solutions but still struggle with visual distortion, low
embedding efficiency, and limited resilience to attacks. To address these limitations, this work introduces
DLSIS, a deep learning-enabled steganography model enhanced with an Optimized Dragonfly Algorithm
(ODA) for adaptive pixel selection. The framework employs a convolutional encoder–decoder and 2-bit
LSB substitution to embed information effectively while preserving image quality. Experimental evaluation
using standard image datasets shows that DLSIS achieves a PSNR of 55.3 dB and consistently surpasses
prior methods. These results establish DLSIS as a reliable and efficient approach for secure data
transmission and authentication in IoT systems.
Keywords: DLSIS, IoT security, image steganography, deep learning, Dragonfly Algorithm, PSNR

1. Introduction
The rapid expansion of the Internet of Things (IoT) has transformed how modern systems sense,
communicate, and make autonomous decisions across sectors such as healthcare, industrial automation,
transportation, and smart cities [1], [2]. As billions of IoT devices continuously capture and transmit
sensitive information—including authentication credentials, physiological readings, and user identifiers—
the security of these communications has become a critical concern [3], [4]. Although cryptographic
methods provide strong protection, they often introduce computational overhead and reveal the presence of
encrypted data, which is unsuitable for resource-constrained IoT devices [5]. These limitations have
increased interest in image steganography, where confidential information is concealed within visual media
to ensure security through imperceptibility [6].
Artificial Intelligence (AI), especially Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL), has further
influenced steganographic research by enabling adaptive embedding and resistance to steganalysis attacks
[7], [8]. ML-based methods optimize embedding rules and improve detection resistance, while DL-based
models—particularly convolutional architectures—enable feature-aware embedding and high-quality
reconstruction [9]. Despite these advantages, many existing approaches struggle to strike a balance between
embedding capacity, imperceptibility, robustness, and computational efficiency, all of which are critical for
IoT deployment [10]. Methods that increase payload size tend to create visible distortions, whereas
techniques that prioritize visual quality often reduce payload capacity [11], [12]. In addition, embedding
processes based on sequential or predictable pixel patterns generate detectable artifacts that weaken system
security against modern steganalysis tools [13].
Recent studies highlight both the progress and limitations of current AI-driven steganography[5]-[14].
Coverless approaches, including generative methods that synthesize camouflage images from secret data,
demonstrate high imperceptibility but require large datasets and heavy computation, limiting their
practicality in IoT settings [14]. Autoencoder-based steganography improves reconstruction fidelity but

often suffers from restricted payload size and vulnerability under channel noise [12], [13], [14]. Lightweight
adversarial embedding techniques support identity-based authentication and tamper detection but may lack
sufficient randomness to resist advanced attacks [7], [10]. Reversible or invertible models improve
recoverability and noise suppression, though their multi-stage architectures increase complexity and reduce
scalability for low-power IoT nodes [12], [13]. Medical imaging steganography frameworks have shown
strong robustness within IoMT applications; however, their specialized designs limit applicability in
broader IoT environments [1], [14].
Other hybrid models focus on maximizing security by integrating compression, encryption, QR coding, or
multi-layer embedding [13], [14]. While these techniques enhance robustness, they often introduce
significant computational cost. Spatial–frequency fusion approaches and edge-driven embedding strategies
achieve better PSNR and SSIM values but still lack intelligent pixel selection mechanisms required to
counter adaptive steganalysis [12], [14]. Evolutionary algorithms such as Adaptive Particle Swarm
Optimization (APSO) have been used for pixel optimization, yet they frequently converge prematurely and
yield suboptimal embedding distributions [13], [14].
The limitations in existing literature indicate that current AI-based steganographic systems cannot
simultaneously achieve high capacity, strong imperceptibility, and resilience under noisy IoT channels
while maintaining low computational complexity [14]. Moreover, many deep-learning methods do not
incorporate global optimization strategies, resulting in redundant embedding and weaker resistance to
detection [12]-[14].
To address these challenges, this study proposes the Dragonfly-based Deep Learning Steganography for
IoT Security (DLSIS) framework, which integrates CNN-based encoder–decoder learning with an
Optimized Dragonfly Algorithm (ODA) for adaptive pixel selection [26]. The ODA module performs multiobjective optimization to maximize payload capacity, enhance visual fidelity, and maintain randomness in
embedding patterns, while the CNN encoder–decoder ensures reliable feature extraction and robust data
recovery even under compression, noise, or adversarial distortions [14]. A 2-bit Least Significant Bit (2LSB) substitution mechanism is adopted to retain high payload size without compromising image quality
[13], [14]. Through the coordinated use of DL-driven learning and swarm-based optimization, the proposed
approach achieves near-lossless imperceptibility, high extraction accuracy, scalability across IoT
environments, and improved resistance to statistical and deep-learning-based steganalysis attacks [14].
The remainder of the paper presents the technical depth and experimental validation of DLSIS. Section II
provides a detailed explanation of the proposed methodology. Section III presents performance evaluation
and comparative analysis, followed by conclusions and future enhancements in Section IV.

2. Methodology
The rapid expansion of the Internet of Things (IoT) has intensified the need for lightweight security
mechanisms that ensure authentication, integrity, and privacy during device-to-device communication. As
billions of IoT nodes continuously transmit sensitive information, these exchanges become susceptible to
interception, manipulation, and other forms of malicious interference. Traditional cryptographic techniques,
although essential, are often insufficient by themselves because they do not guarantee imperceptibility and
may impose computational burdens that exceed the capabilities of constrained IoT hardware. This has led
to growing interest in steganography as a complementary method for concealing confidential data within
innocuous media.
In this work, we introduce DLSIS, an enhanced steganographic framework that integrates an Optimized
Dragonfly Algorithm (ODA) to determine suitable pixel positions within a cover image for data embedding.
Unlike conventional strategies that rely on sequential or random pixel selection, the ODA module performs
adaptive and intelligent positioning based on a multi-objective fitness evaluation, ensuring both security
and robustness. To maintain visual quality and minimize noticeable distortions, the model employs a 2-LSB
substitution strategy for embedding the secret information. The proposed DLSIS framework is further
reinforced with a deep-learning-based authentication component, enabling secure and reliable operation
within IoT environments.
The working of Deep learning-based secured image steganography (DLSIS) Scheme is presented in
Algorithm 1. The DLSIS framework integrates an optimized dragonfly-based search mechanism with
convolution-driven feature learning to achieve secure and imperceptible image steganography for IoT
environments. The unified algorithm combines both embedding and extraction processes, enabling a

complete end-to-end model for hiding and retrieving confidential information. The workflow begins by
transforming the secret message into an 8-bit binary format so that each character can be efficiently mapped
into the image’s least significant bits. The cover image is reshaped into a single-dimensional form, which
simplifies pixel-level manipulation during optimization.
The core of DLSIS is the Optimized Dragonfly Algorithm (ODA), which intelligently selects pixel positions
for embedding. Instead of relying on random or sequential placement, ODA simulates swarm intelligence
behaviors—separation, alignment, cohesion, attraction to optimal regions, and avoidance of poor search
areas. These forces collectively balance exploration and exploitation, preventing premature convergence
while steadily guiding the search toward pixel locations that minimize perceptual distortion. Each candidate
solution (dragonfly) is evaluated using a multi-objective fitness function that considers embedding capacity,
structural similarity (SSIM), and entropy-based randomness. This ensures that the chosen embedding
pattern simultaneously supports high payload, strong visual quality, and resistance against statistical attacks.

Once ODA identifies the optimal embedding indices, the algorithm performs 2-LSB substitution on selected
pixels. This lightweight modification method provides a high-capacity channel for inserting secret bits with
minimal effect on image appearance. The modified pixel array is then reconstructed into a stego image,
which preserves the original structure and visual characteristics of the cover image. The extraction phase
mirrors the embedding process. Using the same global best positions obtained during optimization, the
algorithm isolates the two least significant bits from each chosen pixel. These retrieved bits are reassembled
and converted back into text, enabling accurate message recovery even in noisy IoT communication
scenarios. Because the embedding positions are optimally chosen and structurally robust, the hidden

message can be reconstructed with high reliability. Overall, DLSIS provides an efficient, secure, and IoTcompatible solution for concealing authentication tokens or sensitive sensor data within images, offering a
practical method for enhancing confidentiality and integrity in distributed networks.

3. Results and Discussion
The experimental assessment of the proposed DLSIS was carried out on a system powered by an Intel Core
i7 processor with Intel Iris Xe graphics and 16 GB RAM, using Python 3.11 and standard libraries for deep
learning and image processing. To evaluate embedding quality and robustness, the USC-SIPI image dataset
[15] was selected due to its broad variety of grayscale and color images. Following earlier studies such as
ParticleStego [14], color images of 512×512 pixels were chosen and uniformly resized to 256×256×3 to
align with the encoder–decoder structure while reducing computational load. Figure 1 shows representative
samples—including Airplane, Mandrill, and Peppers —which offer diverse textures and color
characteristics suitable for thorough performance validation.

C. Comparative Study
A comparative assessment was conducted to evaluate how DLSIS performs relative to earlier steganography
approaches. Table 1 compares DLSIS with the APSO-based method in [14] under different payload
conditions. Across images such as Peppers, Mandrill, and Airplane, DLSIS consistently records higher
PSNR values. For example, for the Peppers image with a payload of 81584 bits, DLSIS achieves 57.2 dB,
significantly higher than the 51.14 dB reported in [14]. Similar improvements—typically in the range of 5–
12 dB—were observed for the other images. These gains indicate that the proposed method produces stego
images with substantially lower visible distortion.

Overall, the comparative study demonstrates that DLSIS provides substantially higher PSNR than existing
steganography approaches, ensuring minimal embedding distortion while supporting large payloads. These

concealment of sensitive information must occur without compromising visual quality or drawing attention

4. Conclusion
This study introduced DLSIS, a deep learning-driven steganography and authentication model developed
to enhance secure communication in IoT environments. The work emphasized the growing need for reliable
embedding methods due to rising threats such as forgery and steganalysis. Many existing schemes struggle
to balance capacity, imperceptibility, and security, leaving a clear gap in achieving all three simultaneously.
DLSIS was designed to overcome these limitations by enabling high-quality embedding and accurate
recovery using a DL-based encoder–decoder framework. Experiments conducted on the USC-SIPI dataset
show that the model attains a PSNR of 55.3 dB, surpassing earlier methods and offering stronger robustness.
Future enhancements may incorporate integrity-verification components to ensure tamper resistance in
dynamic IoT communication settings.
