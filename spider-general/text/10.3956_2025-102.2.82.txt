Abstract. Guangdong Province, China, possesses an abundant number of karst caves.
Cave-dwelling spiders typically exhibit distinct troglomorphic characteristics, such as depigmented bodies and reduced or absent eyes. In this paper, we describe a new species of the genus
Alistra Thorell, 1894, Alistra jinlong sp. nov. (male and female), discovered in a karst cave in
Guangdong. Notably, this new species possesses six eyes, a feature that distinguishes it from
all known congeners. Detailed illustrations of the habitus and copulatory organs, along with a
distribution map, are provided.
Keywords. cave-dwelling, comb-tailed spiders, karst cave, taxonomy

Introduction
Karst caves are unique subterranean ecosystems characterized by stable temperatures, high humidity, and a permanent lack of light. These isolated environments often
act as islands, harboring highly specialized organisms that have adapted to life in the
dark (Culver & Pipan 2019). Guangdong Province, China possesses an extensive and
complex karst topography that remains a hotspot for cave biodiversity. Spiders in the
family Hahniidae have a small to medium body size and are characterized by a single transverse row of comblike spinnerets (Lehtinen 1967; Zhang et al. 2011, 2013).
While hahniids occur in leaf litter or forest canopies, several lineages have successfully
colonized the dark zones of caves, showing varying degrees of morphological modification.
During recent surveys of the karst caves in Guangdong, we collected a distinct
cave-dwelling hahniid species belonging to the genus Alistra Thorell, 1894. Morphologically, the species shows close affinities to Alistra pikachu Lin & Li, 2021, particularly in the genital structures. Herein, we treat it as a new species, Alistra jinlong sp.
nov. The new species displays more troglomorphic features than its known congeners.
Notably, the new species possesses only six eyes, whereas other Alistra species are characterized by having eight eyes. Herein we describe this new Alistra species and provide
detailed photos of the habitus and copulatory organs, as well as a distribution map.

Diagnosis. The new species resembles A. pikachu in having an RTA that is long,
narrow and serrated, a copulatory opening located in the anterior part of the epigyne,
and ducts following the same path (Fig. 2C–E; fig. 20B, C, 21 in Lin et al. 2021). However, A. jinlong can be distinguished from the latter by having six eyes versus eight,
the embolus encircling the bulb more than 360° (Fig. 2A, B) versus ~300° (see Lin
et al. 2021: fig. 20B, C), the spermathecae not touching (Fig. 2E) versus touching (see
Lin et al. 2021: fig. 21B), and the copulatory openings separated from each other by
approximately one diameter (Fig. 2D) versus closely situated and nearly touching (see
Lin et al. 2021: fig. 21A).

Discussion
As mentioned above, A. jinlong exhibits typical morphological adaptations to cave
environments. Morphological comparisons with other members of hahniid spiders in
China reveal a remarkably close affinity to A. pikachu, especially in the structures of
the male palp and female epigyne. Based on the morphological similarity, we assign
the new species to the genus Alistra. However, it is noteworthy that A. jinlong possesses
only six eyes, a troglomorphic condition that is not present in any other known Alistra
species, which are typically characterized by having eight eyes. Interestingly, A. jinlong
and A. pikachu are distributed closely within the karst regions of Guangdong, China.
While A. jinlong is a highly specialized cave-dweller with only six eyes, A. pikachu
retains a fully developed set of eight eyes. The transition from surface to subterranean life is widely recognized as a multi-stage process facilitated by ‘exaptations’ that
originally evolved in surface or shallow subterranean microhabitats and subsequently
proved advantageous in deep cave environments (Mammola & Isaia 2017). Given the
extreme morphological similarity and geographical proximity between A. pikachu
and A. jinlong sp. nov., we propose that these species provide a local case study of
this evolutionary gradient, where A. pikachu may occupy an ancestral-like niche, and
A. jinlong reflects a more specialized, cave-adapted lineage.

described based on juvenile females (Thorell 1894), and to our knowledge, no formal
diagnosis has ever been established. Consequently, there are no clear diagnostic features to identify the genus, and most species currently assigned to Alistra lack clear
defined synapomorphies, resulting in a heterogeneous assemblage of species that do
not appear to be closely related.
Given these circumstances, the placement of our new species in Alistra is provisional. We suggest that A. jinlong and A. pikachu might represent a distinct, unrecognized genus-level lineage, separate from the poorly defined Alistra. However, we
refrain from erecting a new genus at this stage. Establishing a new generic concept
requires a comprehensive re-evaluation of the entire family and robust support from
molecular phylogenetic analyses, which are beyond the scope of this study. Therefore,
we focus on the formal description of the new species, acknowledging that a broader
taxonomic revision is essential to resolve the phylogenetic placement of Alistra and its
disparate members.
