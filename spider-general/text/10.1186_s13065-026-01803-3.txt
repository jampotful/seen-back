# Novel eco-friendly spectrophotometric determination of potential combination silodosin and darifenacin hydrobromide in bulk and pharmaceutical formulations

## Abstract
AbstractSilodosin and darifenacin hydrobromide have lately been administered to males with overactive bladder for the management of benign prostatic hyperplasia. The present work details the development and validation of spectrophotometric methods that demonstrate reliability, accuracy, and selectivity for quantifying two medications in bulk and pharmaceutical formulations. Both drugs are quantified from the recorded spectra using different mathematical manipulations. Direct measurement of silodosin concentration was performed at a wavelength of 328.8 nm. The methods for determining darifenacin include Method I, which employs fourier self-deconvolution; Method II, which utilizes ratio difference analysis; and Method III, which applies first derivative ratio. The procedure parameters were validated following ICH recommendations. The methodologies included the direct quantification of silodosin within the range of 10–45 µg/mL with LOD and LOQ of 0.942 and 2.857 µg/mL respectively, as well as Fourier self-deconvolution, ratio difference, and first derivative ratio spectrophotometric techniques for the assessment of darifenacin hydrobromide within the range of 5–70 µg/mL with LOD and LOQ for each method of (0.484, 1.468 µg/mL), (1.353, 4.100 µg/mL), (0.794, 2.408 µg/mL), respectively. Recovery studies validated the accuracy of the methods in pharmaceutical formulations, with an average recovery of 100.37% for silodosin (direct method) and 100.96%, 99.87%, and 100.69% for darifenacin hydrobromide using fourier self-deconvolution, Ratio difference, and first derivative ratio methods, respectively. The procedures demonstrate a high level of environmental sustainability, as evidenced by Analytical GREEnness calculator (a comprehensive, flexible, and straightforward assessment approach that provides an easily interpretable and informative result for evaluating greenness), Modified Green Analytical Procedure Index (which merges the advantages of the visual impact of Green Analytical Procedure Index with the precise total score of the analytical Eco-Scale and provides an overall assessment of the method greenness by calculating a total score), spider chart, green solvents selection tool (spider chart and green solvent selection tools were applied to select and evaluate solvents used during the experiment) and Blue applicability grade index (evaluating the practicality of an analytical techniques).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13065-026-01803-3.Keywords: Darifenacin, Silodosin, Spectrophotometric methods, Fourier self-deconvolution, Ratio difference, Ratio derivative

## Introduction
Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms (LUTS) represent a significant clinical concern, particularly within aging populations. Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is a condition commonly encountered in older men and is a frequent cause of these symptoms. The conditions in question reduce the quality of life for individuals impacted by them and create a significant economic strain on healthcare systems [1–4].
Silodosin (SLD) functions as a selective antagonist of the alpha-1A adrenergic receptor and is utilized in the management of symptoms associated with benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) [5].The molecular structure of SLD is presented as follows: :1-(3-hydroxypropyl)-5-[(2R)-2-[2-[2-(2,2,2-trifluoroethoxy)phenoxy]ethylamino]propyl]-2,3dihydroindole-7-carboxamide (Fig. 1a). Silodosin is an official drug as it was determined in Japanese pharmacopeia [6].SLD has an aromatic benzene ring, an amide group (-CONH-), an indoline moiety, an ether linkage (-O-), and a trifluoromethyl group (-CF₃). As the main chromophore, the aromatic benzene ring is in charge of the π → π* electrical transitions. By means of n → π* transitions, the amide group (C = O) makes participation.The indoline ring structure improves conjugation, which raises the intensity of absorbance. The ether and amino groups work as auxochromes, increase intensity (hyperchromic action) and moving absorption toward longer wavelengths (bathochromic shift). This wavelength primarily correlates to π → π* transitions of the aromatic chromophore, making it appropriate for quantitative UV analysis [7].
Chemical structure of drugs a Silodosin, b Darifenacin Hydrobromide
Darifenacin Hydrobromide (DFH) acts as a selective antagonist of the M3 muscarinic receptor. It prevents involuntary bladder contractions by obstructing M3 receptors in the detrusor muscle of the bladder [8]The Molecular structure of DFH is -1-[2-(2,3-Dihydrobenzofuran-5-yl) ethyl]-3-pyrrolidinyl}-2,2-diphenylacetamide hydrobromide (Fig. 1b). DFH is not official drug in any pharmacopeia. DFH has numerous aromatic rings that function as main chromophores, with strong π → π* transitions in the UV area. The amide carbonyl group causes n → π* transitions, while methoxy and tertiary amine groups act as auxochromes, resulting in bathochromic and hyperchromic effects. These structural characteristics support wavelength selection in the 270–285 nm region for UV spectrophotometry [9].
Several studies have explored the combination of an alpha-blocker with an antimuscarinic agent in men with BPH and concomitant overactive bladder symptoms, providing relevant insights. A study examining the effects of silodosin, darifenacin, and their combination in patients with ureteral stent-related discomfort, which can mimic lower urinary tract symptoms, found that the combination therapy improved both irritative and obstructive symptoms more effectively than either drug alone. This suggests a potential for the combination to address different aspects of lower urinary tract symptoms in a urological context [10, 11].
Many chromatographic studies have been performed for SLD detection [12–19]and estimation of DFH [20–23] and spectroscopic methods including both spectrophotometric and spectrofluorometric approaches for determination of SLD [24] and DFH [25–29], also electrochemical method used for determination of SLD [30–32] and DFH [33, 34].
SLD and its metabolites in plasma can be quantified using highly sensitive LC–MS/MS techniques [14, 15]. These techniques offer great selectivity but necessitate expensive equipment, intensive sample preparation, and specialist knowledge. HPLC-based techniques [16–18, 21–25] offer accuracy and resilience but frequently concentrate on single-drug analysis, use a lot of organic solvents, and need complicated procedures. Sustainable HPTLC techniques allow for the simultaneous measurement of solifenacin and silodosin employing smartphone-aided detection and ecologic-focused methodologies [19, 20]. HPTLC techniques may exhibit reduced sensitivity, poor automation, and manual handling variability, despite being economical and ecologically benign. Although spectrotometric methods, such as spectrofluorimetric, derivative UV, and mathematically filtered techniques [26–30], are straightforward and inexpensive, they may have the drawback of not being able to determine SLD and DFH simultaneously. The sensitivity of electrochemical sensors, especially those based on molecularly imprinted polymers or nanocomposite electrodes, for silodosin and darifenacin is high [32–36]. However, their manufacture is complicated, they may have reproducibility problems, and they can only apply many analytes at once. In general, described methods show trade-offs between cost, greenness, simplicity, and sensitivity. Most are limited in their regular applicability in pharmaceutical quality control because they either need complex instrumentation or concentrate on single-analyte determination. The current study fills these gaps by providing a straightforward, economical, and environmentally friendly method that can accurately determine SLD and DFH simultaneously. It offers a workable substitute appropriate for regular analysis while preserving strong analytical performance by minimizing solvent use, lowering computing complexity, and avoiding specialist instrumentation.
Comprehensive overview of the literature found that while a number of analytical techniques have been documented for the individual measurement of silodosin and darifenacin, no validated spectrophotometric methods has been reported for their simultaneous measurement in pharmaceutical formulations [24–29].
Therefore, we tested more than one spectroscopic method for drug determination, each in the presence of the other drug. The methods were carefully selected after confirming their suitability for identifying the aforementioned drugs using green solvent for preparation of drugs. Direct measurement of silodosin concentration was performed. Even while traditional spectrophotometric techniques like Q-analysis and the simultaneous equation approach are frequently used, their effective use rely on the existence of clear isoabsorptive points or unambiguous absorption maxima. The UV spectra of silodosin and darifenacin in the current investigation show high overlap with no stable isoabsorptive point or suitably selected wavelengths, making these traditional methods inappropriate and prone to substantial analytical error. Thus, sophisticated spectrophotometric methods such as Ratio Difference (RD), Fourier Self-Deconvolution (FSD), and First Derivative of Ratio Spectra (DD¹) were utilized. Despite the significant overlap, these techniques improve spectral resolution and selectivity, allowing for precise simultaneous assessment of both medications.

## Experimental

## Instruments
The UV spectrophotometer (model V-630, Jasco, Japan) was utilized including Jasco spectrum management software. Absorption spectra for both reference and test solutions were recorded in the wavelength range of 200–400 nm utilizing 1 cm path length quartz cell. The scanning speed may be set up to 1000 nm/min, with a spectral slit width of 2 nm. The spectra were processed with a Δλ of 5 nm and a suitable scaling factor of 3 for the derivative approach.

## Materials, reagents and solvents
SLD and DFH reference standards were purchased from (sigma Aldrich- Germany) their potency 99.8% and 99.5% respectively. The pharmaceutical dosage form (Flopadex 8 mg® and Daricont 7.5 mg® manufactured by Eva pharm company, 6th of October city Egypt) were bought from a local pharmacy. Ethanol was purchased from Misr company- Egypt and distilled water from milli Q apparatus.

## Standard stock solution preparation
SLD and DFH standard stock solutions (100 µg/mL) were prepared by precisely weighing and transferring 10 mg of each standard powder into a 100 mL volumetric flask. Following their dissolution and stirring in 20 mL of ethanol, the volume was raised to 100 mL using distilled water. Refrigerated at 4 °C, the solution was stable for 14 days.

## Laboratory-prepared mixtures preparation
A precise transfer of SLD and DFH quantities were executed from their standard stock solutions (100 µg/mL) into a series of 10 mL volumetric flasks. Finally, the volumes were filled with 2mL ethanol then complete to mark using distilled water to prepare solutions with varying ratios of SLD and DFH (16:15, 20:20, 10:30, 15:30, 20:60). Ratio 16:15 is done to represent the ratio in the pharmaceutical dosage form and all the ratios were done with variation in concentrations to ensure the selectivity of each drug.

## Creation of calibration curves
Following the analysis of SLD within the 200–400 nm wavelength spectrum, the absorbance at 328.8 nm was assessed directly, without any interference from DFH. The regression equation was derived by establishing a relationship between absorbance at 328.8 nm and SLD concentrations ranging from (10–45 µg /mL), and the subsequent procedures include the methods used for determination of DFH.

## Fourier self-deconvolution spectrophotometric method (FSD)
Deconvoluted from the stored zero-order spectra using the Fourier deconvolution algorithm included in the spectrophotometer software with an FWHM of 60 was used to compute DFH when SLD was present. Then, the bands of DFH produced at 283.4 nm were plotted against their concentrations.

## Ratio difference spectrophotometric method (RD)
Spectra of DFH at concentrations range from 5 to 70 µg/mL have been divided by the spectrum of 10 µg/mL of SLD, which served as a divisor, to identify DFH in the presence of SLD. The resulting spectra’s amplitudes were determined at 280 and 290.4 nm, and the calibration curve was created by calculating the differences.

## First derivative ratio spectrophotometric method (DD1)
The measured absorbance of DFH was divided by 10 µg/mL of SLD to get the ratio spectra (Δ λ = 5 nm), scaling factor = 3. Additionally, the ratio spectra’s first derivative had to be generated. The resulting derivative spectra of DFH have amplitudes obtained at 295.6 nm.

## Quantification of pharmaceutical dosages
Ten tablets of Flopadex (8 mg SLD/tablet) and ten tablets of Daricont (7.5 mg DFH/tablet) were weighed, and the average weight was computed. The flopadex and daricont tablets were powdered and mixed well to produce laboratory prepared mixture. The equivalent weight of 8 mg SLD and 7.5 mg DFH was accurately weighed and transferred to the same volumetric flask (100 mL), 20 mL of ethanol was added to the flask to dissolve the powder. The flask was subjected to ultrasonic agitation for a duration of 15 min. The content of the flask was diluted using distilled water following filtration through a 0.45 μm membrane filter. To prepare the final concentrations of SLD and DFH, 2 ml of the solution was added to a 10 ml volumetric flask and then diluted with distilled water. The measurement of the final concentrations was repeated five times.

## Results and discussion
Accurate results for competent analytical techniques were ensured by developing direct UV-spectrophotometric method for measuring SLD and FSD, RD and DD1 for DFH concurrently in synthetic mixtures and medicinal formulations. As seen in (Fig. 2), SLD can be measured directly in mixture of ethanol and water (20:80) at 328.8 nm. To determine DFH, the spectra shown in (Fig. 3) show a lot of overlap. Therefore, to assess DFH in a manner that is sensitive, quick, inexpensive, and free of SLD interference, three separate UV-spectrophotometric techniques were created. The employment of different spectrophotometric techniques was deliberate, with the goal of providing complementary analytical choices and ensuring cross-validation. Direct UV analysis is simple and fast, although derivative and ratio-based approaches (FSD, RD, and DD¹) improve spectral resolution and selectivity in overlapping spectra Table S1. The agreement between various methods confirms reliability while reducing method-specific bias. As a result, the proposed methodologies function as complementary analytical tools rather than redundant techniques.

## Fourier self-deconvolution spectrophotometric method
Spectrophotometric analysis of binary mixtures is made possible by the novel FSD method. This simple mathematical method uses the Fourier deconvolution feature of the spectrophotometer program to reduce the bandwidth of zero-order spectra that are heavily overlapped. (Fig. 4) shows the results of determining the DFH concentrations by measuring the spectra at 283.4 nm. To achieve stable and noise-free FWHM, many FWHM were tested, FWHM at 60 achieved optimal spectral smoothness, signal-to-noise ratio, and linear amplitude response for FSD method. It produced good repeatability and excellent linearity across the concentration range under consideration.
Amplitude of Fourier self-deconvolution spectra of darifenacin (5–70 µg/mL) at 283.4 nm

## Ratio difference spectrophotometric method
The amount of the intended drug is proportionate to the difference between any two points on the previous resulting ratio spectrum (DFH/SLD). The greatest outcomes, as seen in (Fig. 5), were derived from the differences in the ratio spectra’s peak amplitudes (∆P) at 280 and 290.4 nm for DFH. SLD at 10 µg/mL achieved optimal spectral smoothness, signal-to-noise ratio, and linear amplitude response for RD. It produced well-defined ratio spectra with increased repeatability and good linearity across the concentration range under consideration.

(5–70 µg/mL) using Silodosin (10 µg/mL) as a divisor at 280 nm and 290.4 nm

## First derivative ratio spectrophotometric method
The DD1 technique was determined by dividing the medicine’s absorption spectra by the spectrum of other medication. Afterwards, the ratio spectrum (DFH/SLD) first derivative was calculated. Figure 6 shows that at 295.6, the DD1 of each DFH concentration was recorded. To achieve stable and noise-free ratio spectra, various concentrations of SLD were used as divisors. SLD at 10 µg/mL achieved optimal spectral smoothness, signal-to-noise ratio, and linear amplitude response for DD1. It produced well-defined ratio spectra with increased repeatability and good linearity across the concentration range under consideration. Many trials were done using different Δ λ and scaling factor and it was found that the spectra (Δ λ = 5 nm), scaling factor = 3 is the optimum condition. Higher-order derivative methods can improve spectral resolution, but also augment instrumental noise by nature. Because it offered acceptable peak resolution with little noise amplification, the first derivative of ratio spectra (DD¹) was chosen as the best method in this investigation. While higher derivatives demonstrated increased noise and decreased precision, Δλ and scaling factor optimization further enhanced signal smoothness and reproducibility.

## Accuracy
The proposed procedures were evaluated using three concentrations of each medication (12, 27, 40 µg/mL) and (20, 45, 60 µg/mL) for SLD and DFH respectively, each concentration was examined in triplicate. The mean percentage of computed recoveries is shown in (Table 1).

## Precision
Three distinct analyte solutions were analyzed on the same day to assess the intraday precision of each proposed method. The inter-day precision was assessed over three distinct days. The examined concentrations for all methods were 12, 27, 40 µg/mL and 20, 45, 60 µg/mL for SLD and DFH respectively, each concentration was examined in triplicate. The findings were presented as a percentage relative standard deviation (%RSD). The computed %RSD values ranged from 0.424 to 1.111 for intra-day precision and from 0.677 to 1.181 for inter-day precision. All results were found to be less than 2.0%, indicating that the suggested procedures are precise and meet the acceptance requirements specified by the International Council for Harmonisation (Table 1).

## Assessment of the greenness
The assessment of the environmental impacts associated with different analytical techniques is crucial, especially in terms of their compliance with green chemistry principles. The assessment of the eco-friendliness of the methods was conducted using the following tools: the spider chart, green solvent selection tool, AGREE, MO-GAPI, and blue applicability grade index.

## Greenness index utilizing the spider chart metric
The assessment of the chemicals involved in the proposed spectrophotometric techniques was conducted using a spider diagram to ascertain the greenness index [37, 38]. The information utilized in this tool is derived from solvent safety data sheets, which offer comprehensive details regarding the characteristics of a reagent, including its impact on safety, health, and the environment throughout each phase of the process. (Table 4) is a hierarchical spider diagram that was created to show how the essential chemicals’ entire sustainability is represented. The results demonstrate that the proposed analytical methods exhibit a favorable environmental footprint. This is mainly attributed to the use of water and ethanol as green solvents, which have high health impact, stability, and fire safety and are odorless for water and have a moderate odor for ethanol.

GlaxoSmithKline (GSK) has implemented a program designed to evaluate the benefits and drawbacks of different solvents by utilizing information obtained from solvent safety data sheets. A variety of factors, such as hydrogen bonding, polarity, and dispersion, are utilized to evaluate solvents on a scale from zero to ten, as illustrated in (Table 4) It is also possible to find the most eco-friendly solvents by using a chemometric equation, denoted by G [40].

## Analytical greenness metric-based evaluation of environmental friendliness
AGREE® program comprises 12 essential principles of green chemistry, illustrated by a clock-shaped graph divided into 12 divisions [41, 42]. The degree to which the analytical procedure follows the green analytical chemistry principle is assessed by each section using a three-point scale ranging from red to green. At its core, the AGREE graph displays a color and an overall evaluation number on a scale from 0 to 1. (Table 4) displays the final result (0.75) that stands for the method’s eco-friendliness; this value was arrived at by giving each of the analytical parameters a separate score. Certain zones showed rather lower ratings. Specifically, zone 1 and 3 (the sampling process and in-situ measurements) due to offline sampling and transfer of sample. The amount of garbage and samples used is comparatively considerable, according to zone 2 and 7.

## Greenness assessment using modified green analytical procedure index
All aspects of an analytical process from sample preparation and volume to solvent and reagent health concerns, instrumentation, waste quantity and handling can be measured and evaluated using GAPI’s fifteen components. The color green denotes a low environmental impact, whereas yellow and red denote a medium and significant impact, respectively. On the other hand, when comparing several approaches, the GAPI statistic does not provide a complete score. The MoGAPI application and tool integrates the precise total score of the analytical Eco Scale with the advantages of GAPI’s visual impact [43]. The application was utilized to assess the environmental sustainability of the proposed analytical methods as shown in (Table 4) The total score was 82, indicating an exemplary green methodology. MOGAPI products include the following: offline sample collection (red), transportation from the source, storage under normal conditions, and a straightforward process for tablet preparation and microextraction utilizing green solvent with waste of around 3 mL and waste degradation (yellow).

## Assessment of the proposed techniques’ appropriateness
The blue applicability grade index (BAGI) functions as a comprehensive framework for assessing the effectiveness of current methodologies. This instrument evaluates ten essential factors: analysis category, sample productivity rate, analytical methodology, reagent quantities, required devices, simultaneous sample handling capacity, preconcentration requirements, level of process automation, sample construction technique, sample volume, and the ability to estimate analytes concurrently. Various degrees of acceptance are represented by different shades of blue: white for complete rejection, light blue for minimum acceptance, blue for fair acceptance, and dark blue for optimum acceptance [44]. To be considered “acceptable,” the approach has to get at least 60 points. (Table 4) shows that the suggested strategy received an 82.5 on the BAGI scale, indicating that the methodology is highly applicable. The suggested methods demonstrate quantitative analysis with proving the lambda max of the two drugs of different classes using a basic instrument available at the QC labs. Simultaneous sample preparation of the two drugs in lab mix and dosage forms and samples about six samples per hour can be analyzed; the solvent used for preparation is commercially available and does not require preconcentration; the device used is semi-automated; and the amount of sample used is less than 100 mg.

## Limitations of applied methods
While Fourier self-deconvolution improves spectral resolution and allows for the separation of overlapping bands, it is intrinsically susceptible to noise amplification. Improper bandwidth and enhancement factor selection might result in false peak distortion or loss of spectral information. The ratio difference approach is straightforward and selective, but the right divisor concentration selection is crucial to its accuracy. Increased noise or decreased sensitivity could result from an improper divisor. Additionally, the method’s global applicability is limited because it depends on the selection of appropriate wavelength pairs, which may not always be available in circumstances of excessively overlapped spectra. When compared to zero-order approaches, the first derivative of ratio spectra increases noise but improves resolution and selectivity. This means that derivative parameters like scaling factor and Δλ must be carefully optimized.

## Conclusion
A direct spectrophotometric approach was used to determine SLD, whereas DFH was quantified using Fourier self-deconvolution, ratio difference, and first derivative ratio. These approaches allowed for efficient resolution of overlapping spectra without prior separation. The methods revealed great sensitivity (LOD: 0.484–1.353 µg/mL), excellent precision (%RSD < 2%) in accordance with the International Council for Harmonization, and high accuracy (recovery: 99.799–101.060%). Their successful use to laboratory-prepared mixes and pharmaceutical dosage forms demonstrates that they are suitable for routine quality control. Furthermore, a greenness assessment utilizing a variety of methodologies confirmed their environmental sustainability profile.
