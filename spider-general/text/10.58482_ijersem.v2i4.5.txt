Abstract: Natural and man-made disasters such as earthquakes, landslides, fires, and structural collapses create hazardous environments that
pose significant risks to human rescuers. To address these challenges, this paper presents the design and development of a spider-inspired hexapod
robot for disaster search and rescue operations. The proposed system utilizes a six-legged bio-inspired structure that enables stable locomotion
over uneven terrain, rubble, and confined spaces. The robot is equipped with an embedded control system based on an ESP32 microcontroller,
which coordinates servo-driven leg movements using a tripod gait mechanism to ensure continuous stability. Additionally, a vision-based human
detection system is implemented using a camera module integrated with YOLO (You Only Look Once) and Open CV for real-time object
detection. The system communicates detection results via UART to enable responsive robot behavior such as stopping and alert generation.
Power management is enhanced through the use of a buck converter and battery management system (BMS), ensuring stable and efficient
operation. The proposed robot significantly improves terrain adaptability, fault tolerance, and operational safety compared to traditional wheeled
and tracked robots. Experimental results demonstrate reliable locomotion, accurate human detection, and efficient power utilization. This work
contributes to reducing human exposure to dangerous environments and enhances the effectiveness of disaster response operations.
Keywords: Hexapod robot, disaster response, bio-inspired robotics, ESP32, YOLO.

1 INTRODUCTION
Natural and man-made disasters such as earthquakes, landslides, industrial accidents, fires, and building collapses often lead
to severe destruction of infrastructure and pose a significant threat to human life. In such critical situations, rapid and efficient
search and rescue operations are essential to locate survivors, assess environmental conditions, and minimize casualties [1].
However, disaster environments are highly unpredictable and hazardous, characterized by uneven terrain, unstable debris, narrow
passages, toxic gases, and poor visibility. These factors make it extremely difficult and dangerous for human rescuers to operate
effectively [2]. Traditional rescue systems rely heavily on human intervention supported by conventional robotic platforms such
as wheeled and tracked robots.
Although these systems are widely used due to their simplicity and cost-effectiveness, they suffer from major limitations in
complex disaster scenarios [3]. Wheeled robots require relatively smooth surfaces and often fail in rubble-filled environments due
to slippage, obstacles, and gaps. Similarly, tracked robots provide better traction but consume high power, exhibit limited
maneuverability in confined spaces, and struggle to navigate irregular terrain [4]. To overcome these limitations, bio-inspired
robotics has emerged as a promising approach for designing efficient and adaptable robotic systems. In particular, hexapod robots
inspired by insects and spiders have gained attention due to their superior stability, mobility, and fault tolerance [5]. A six-legged
configuration ensures that multiple legs remain in contact with the ground at any time, allowing the robot to maintain balance even
when one or more legs lose support. This makes hexapod robots highly suitable for navigating uneven and unpredictable terrains
commonly found in disaster zones [6].
This paper presents the design and development of a spider-inspired hexapod robot for disaster search and rescue operations.
The proposed system integrates an ESP32-based embedded control unit for real-time gait coordination, along with servo-driven
leg mechanisms that implement a tripod gait pattern for enhanced stability [7]. Furthermore, the robot incorporates a vision-based
human detection system using a camera module, OpenCV, and YOLO (You Only Look Once) algorithm to identify survivors in
real time [8]. The primary objective of this work is to reduce human exposure to dangerous environments while improving the
efficiency, reliability, and responsiveness of disaster rescue operations [9].

2 RELATED WORK
The field of bio-inspired robotics has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the development of hexapod
robots for navigation in complex environments [1]. Early research on insect-inspired hexapod robots (2018–2019) focused on
implementing basic locomotion strategies such as tripod gait and wave gait patterns to achieve stable walking. These systems
demonstrated the feasibility of multi-legged locomotion; however, they primarily relied on open-loop control mechanisms without
real-time feedback [2]. As a result, their performance was limited in unpredictable terrains, often leading to instability and
inefficient movement. Subsequent developments in servo-based spider robot prototypes introduced cost-effective designs using
multiple servo motors for leg actuation [3]. While these systems enabled basic walking and maneuvering capabilities, they faced
challenges related to servo synchronization, high current consumption, and frequent system resets due to inadequate power
management.
These limitations highlighted the need for improved control architectures and efficient power regulation techniques [4]. In
parallel, disaster response robotics research explored the use of wheeled and tracked robots for inspection and rescue tasks. Tracked
robots, introduced around 2020, offered improved traction and were capable of operating in rough terrains [5]. However, they
suffered from high power consumption, limited maneuverability in confined spaces, and difficulty in overcoming large obstacles
or debris. Similarly, quadruped robots developed in 2021 provided better mobility than wheeled systems but lacked the stability
and fault tolerance required for reliable operation in disaster scenarios [6]. The failure of a single leg could significantly affect
their balance and performance [7]. Recent advancements in hexapod robotics (2022–2023) have focused on improving fault
tolerance and adaptability [8].
Fault-tolerant hexapod systems introduced mechanisms to maintain locomotion even when one or more legs malfunction.
Additionally, sensor-fusion approaches incorporated inertial measurement units (IMU) and force sensors to enhance terrain
adaptability and stability [9]. However, these systems often involved high computational complexity and lacked efficient real-time
perception capabilities for tasks such as human detection [10]. To address these challenges, the proposed system integrates adaptive
gait control with efficient embedded processing and AI-based vision capabilities [11]. Unlike earlier approaches, this work
combines stable tripod gait locomotion, optimized power management using a servo driver (PCA9685), buck converter, and battery
management system (BMS), along with real-time human detection using YOLO and OpenCV [12]. This integrated approach
enhances stability, reduces power consumption, and enables intelligent decision-making, making the system more suitable for
practical disaster search and rescue applications.
3 METHODOLOGY
The proposed spider-inspired hexapod robot is designed by integrating mechanical structure, embedded control, vision-based
detection, communication, and power management systems into a unified framework suitable for disaster search and rescue
operations. The system is divided into two primary subsystems: the locomotion and control unit based on an ESP32
microcontroller, and the vision processing unit responsible for human detection. The ESP32 acts as the central controller,
generating real-time control signals for servo motors and coordinating gait movement, while the vision system processes
environmental data and communicates detection results to the controller through a serial interface. Fig. 1 shows the block diagram.

The mechanical design of the robot follows a bio-inspired hexapod structure, where six legs provide enhanced stability and
adaptability compared to conventional wheeled or tracked robots. Each leg consists of three joints—coxa, femur, and tibia—driven
by high-torque MG995 servo motors, enabling multi-degree-of-freedom movement. The robot utilizes a tripod gait locomotion
strategy in which three alternate legs remain in contact with the ground while the other three move forward. This gait ensures
continuous stability, balanced weight distribution, and efficient traversal over uneven terrain such as rubble and debris commonly
found in disaster environments . The embedded control system is implemented using the ESP32 microcontroller, which offers high
processing capability along with integrated Wi-Fi and Bluetooth features. The ESP32 generates Pulse Width Modulation (PWM)
signals required to control multiple servo motors simultaneously. To improve signal stability and reduce computational load, a
PCA9685-based servo driver is employed, which ensures synchronized operation of all servos and eliminates jitter issues observed
in earlier designs.
The controller executes predefined gait algorithms and responds dynamically to inputs received from the vision system,
enabling real-time decision-making during navigation [2]. For intelligent perception, the system incorporates a vision-based human
detection module using a camera interfaced with a processing unit. The captured video frames are processed using OpenCV for
preprocessing tasks such as resizing and filtering, followed by the application of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) algorithm for
real-time object detection. YOLO identifies human presence by generating bounding boxes around detected individuals in each
frame. This approach ensures high detection accuracy and low latency, making it suitable for time-critical rescue operations. Upon
detection, the system sends corresponding signals to the ESP32 controller via UART communication, enabling responsive actions
such as stopping the robot or triggering alerts [3].
The communication between the vision system and the control unit is established using UART (Universal Asynchronous
Receiver-Transmitter), a reliable and low-latency serial communication protocol. This interface allows efficient transmission of
detection signals, positional data, and control commands, ensuring seamless integration between perception and motion
subsystems. The low complexity and robustness of UART make it suitable for embedded robotic applications where real-time
performance is essential [4]. To ensure stable and efficient operation, a dedicated power management system is implemented. The
robot is powered by a 12V lithium-ion battery, which supplies energy to all components.
A buck converter is used to step down the voltage to required levels (5V, 6V, and 3.3V) for different modules, while a Battery
Management System (BMS) provides protection against overcharging, over-discharging, and short circuits. This arrangement
prevents voltage fluctuations, enhances battery life, and ensures reliable system performance during prolonged operations in
disaster environments [5]. Additionally, an alert mechanism using buzzers is incorporated to provide immediate feedback during
operation. When a human is detected, the buzzer generates an audible signal, assisting rescue teams in identifying the location of
survivors [6]. The overall workflow of the system begins with locomotion using tripod gait, followed by continuous environment
monitoring through the vision system. Fig. 2 shows the flowchart of the methodology.

4 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The performance of the proposed spider-inspired hexapod robot was evaluated under simulated disaster-like conditions to
analyse its effectiveness in navigation, stability, human detection, and overall system reliability. Experimental observations
indicate that the robot achieves stable locomotion using the tripod gait mechanism, where three legs maintain continuous ground
contact while the remaining legs move forward. This configuration ensures improved balance and minimizes the risk of instability
when traversing uneven surfaces such as rubble, debris, and inclined terrain. Compared to conventional wheeled and tracked
robots, the hexapod structure demonstrated superior adaptability by stepping over obstacles rather than being obstructed by them,
thereby enhancing mobility in complex environments. Fig. 3 shows the hexabot robot.

Fig. 3. Hexapod Robot
The embedded control system, based on the ESP32 microcontroller and PCA9685 servo driver, provided efficient and
synchronized control of all servo motors. The use of the servo driver significantly reduced timing issues and eliminated servo
jitter, which were common limitations in earlier designs. Additionally, the integration of a buck converter and battery management
system (BMS) ensured a stable power supply, preventing voltage fluctuations and unexpected system resets. This resulted in
improved power efficiency and extended operational time, which are critical for real-time rescue missions where uninterrupted
operation is required. The vision-based human detection module, implemented using OpenCV and the YOLO algorithm,
demonstrated reliable performance in identifying human presence within the robot’s field of view. The system was capable of
detecting humans in real time with acceptable accuracy and low latency, even under moderate variations in lighting and
environmental conditions.
Detected individuals were highlighted using bounding boxes, enabling clear identification. The communication between the
vision processing unit and the ESP32 controller via UART was observed to be stable and responsive, allowing immediate execution
of actions such as stopping the robot and activating alert mechanisms upon detection. The system performance was further analysed
using key performance metrics, including locomotion stability, terrain adaptability, servo synchronization, motion accuracy, power
efficiency, human detection accuracy, communication reliability, and response time. The results indicate that the proposed system
achieves a balanced trade-off between computational efficiency and functional performance.
While more advanced systems incorporating sensor fusion and high-end processors may provide higher precision, they often
come at the cost of increased complexity and power consumption. In contrast, the proposed approach utilizes a lightweight
embedded system with sufficient accuracy and responsiveness, making it more practical for real-world deployment in disaster
scenarios. Overall, the experimental results validate that the spider-inspired hexapod robot provides a stable, efficient, and
intelligent solution for disaster search and rescue operations. The integration of bio-inspired locomotion, embedded control, and
AI-based vision enhances the robot’s capability to navigate hazardous environments and detect human presence effectively,
thereby reducing risks to human rescuers and improving the efficiency of rescue missions.

5 CONCLUSION
This paper presented the design and development of a spider-inspired hexapod robot for disaster search and rescue operations.
The proposed system successfully integrates bio-inspired mechanical design, embedded control using ESP32, and AI-based vision
techniques to address the limitations of traditional rescue robots. The hexapod structure, combined with tripod gait locomotion,
provides enhanced stability, fault tolerance, and adaptability to uneven terrains, making it highly suitable for navigating complex
disaster environments. The implementation of a real-time human detection system using YOLO and OpenCV enables the robot to
identify survivors efficiently and respond through alert mechanisms. Additionally, the use of a servo driver, buck converter, and
battery management system ensures stable operation, reduced power consumption, and improved system reliability. Experimental
results demonstrate that the proposed robot can effectively traverse rough terrains, maintain balance, and perform accurate human
detection with minimal latency. Overall, the system reduces the risk to human rescuers by enabling remote exploration of
hazardous zones and improves the efficiency of rescue operations. The project validates the feasibility of hexapod robots as
intelligent assistive tools in disaster response scenarios and provides a strong foundation for future advancements in bio-inspired
robotics and autonomous systems.
