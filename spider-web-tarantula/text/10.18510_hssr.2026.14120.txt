Abstract
Research objective: This literature review provides a critical evaluation and
functional classification of a new generation of bioactive, naturally derived
biomaterials that serve as alternatives to complication-prone synthetic polymers and
scarce autologous grafts, assessing their potential in advanced surgery and tissue
engineering.
Methodology: The study is based on a comprehensive search of the PubMed
database, through which three primary technological axes were identified and
analyzed — namely marine, arthropod, and botanical substrates.
Main conclusions: The synthesis of data demonstrated that marine resources, owing
to their unique catechol-driven chemistry and high omega-3 fatty acid content in fishderived matrices, establish a new standard for wet-field tissue adhesion and nonpharmacological chronic wound management, while concurrently eliminating the risk
of mammalian zoonotic transmission. In the domain of reconstructive microsurgery
and neurosurgery, the high clinical efficacy of living biotherapies in macrovascular
decongestion and biofilm eradication (including MRSA and P. aeruginosa strains)
was confirmed, alongside the revolutionary potential of Nephila edulis spider silk
constructs to guide peripheral nerve regeneration across anatomical gaps exceeding
20 cm. Furthermore, the integration of terrestrial flora assets, specifically plant
polyphenols and cannabinoids, demonstrated the capacity to actively modulate the
immune microenvironment via macrophage polarization toward a pro-healing M2
phenotype and the stimulation of osteogenesis. Crucially, the synthesized evidence
highlights a major translational bottleneck: the majority of these next-generation
technologies (e.g., Bone-O2, bacterial nanocellulose conduits) still rely heavily on
preliminary preclinical data, media releases, or small pilot cohorts.
Application of the study: The findings may support clinicians and tissue engineers in
selecting and implementing naturally derived biomaterials for advanced surgical
reconstruction, chronic wound management, and peripheral nerve regeneration,
guiding more individualized material choices in regenerative medicine.
Originality/Novelty of the study: This review integrates and functionally classifies
recent advances across marine, arthropod, and botanical biomaterials, highlighting
bioinspired approaches such as catechol-driven wet-field adhesion, spider silk nerve
conduits, and immunomodulatory plant-derived compounds, while explicitly
identifying the translational gap that must be addressed through large-scale,
multicenter randomized controlled trials before routine clinical adoption.

INTRODUCTION
Present-day surgical approaches and restorative medicine continuously strive to engineer novel therapeutic modalities, a
shift necessitated by demographic aging and a worldwide surge in chronic ailments linked to modern living, such as
diabetes, obesity, and cardiovascular pathologies (Bazaliński et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Bao et al., 2022). Properly
managing extensive tissue damage, rapidly arresting hemorrhages, and mitigating the threat of microbial infections are of
paramount importance to lower perioperative complications and fatal outcomes (Bazaliński et al., 2023; Daud et al., 2020;
Ibrahim et al., 2023). Traditional interventional protocols, however, suffer from notable drawbacks; widely utilized tissue
sealants dependent on fibrin and thrombin yield minor clinical improvements while posing risks of clot formation or
immunological responses, whereas the classically favored paradigm—autologous transplantation—induces donor-site
morbidity and struggles with severe shortages of viable biological material (Liu et al., 2023; Daud et al., 2020; Kornfeld et
al., 2016). Furthermore, conventional synthetic materials underperform within reduced-caliber vasculature, while
commercially available neural conduits fail when addressing extensive anatomical gaps (Liu et al., 2023; Bao et al., 2022;

Kornfeld et al., 2016). Driven by these hurdles, the scientific community is shifting its focus toward utilizing nonconventional raw matrices of natural origin and sophisticated, highly engineered biomaterial architectures (Liu et al., 2023;
Ibrahim et al., 2023; Kornfeld et al., 2016; Lee et al., 2011; Alsaei et al., 2026; Balu et al., 2021).
Marine mussels serve as an exceptional blueprint for designing adhesive systems capable of enduringly binding tissue
components in humid environments, as they synthesize distinct proteins heavily enriched with the amino acid Dopa and its
catechol groups (Lee et al., 2011). A comparable capability is found in alginate—a polysaccharide extracted from brown
seaweeds that crosslinks into a hydrogel state within the body, maintaining appropriate humidity at the injury site while
simultaneously serving as a drug-free intervention for obesity and diabetes by physically inducing fullness and hindering
the passive assimilation of nutrients (Alsaei et al., 2026). Substantial progress in treating severe thermal injuries and
ulcerative lesions has been achieved through acellular xenografts derived from North Sea fish skins; this strategy
circumvents the hazard of transmitting mammalian-specific infections, and its high concentration of omega-3 fatty acids
triggers robust anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial actions, accelerating vascular network formation and wound closure
(Daud et al., 2020; Ibrahim et al., 2023). Contemporary clinical practices also successfully integrate biotherapies
employing living organisms; for instance, medicinal leeches are used to alleviate peripheral blood congestion and prevent
the death of tissue flaps during reconstructive surgeries, a mechanism driven by hirudin—a natural thrombin blocker found
in their secretions (Foppiani et al., 2024). Meanwhile, for the localized biological debridement of stubborn, non-healing
ulcers, larval therapy utilizing Lucilia sericata is deployed, where digestive enzymes efficiently dissolve degraded tissue
matrices and disrupt biofilm architectures, eradicating highly drug-resistant pathogens, including MRSA strains and
Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Bazaliński et al., 2023).
The current era of tissue engineering relies on fabricating highly tolerated matrices that mirror the configuration of the
native extracellular matrix, allowing precise modulation of cellular processes within the wound environment (Liu et al.,
2023; Kornfeld et al., 2016; Balu et al., 2021). This trend is exemplified by manufacturing biomimetic vascular prostheses
from bacterial cellulose modified with fish-derived gelatin, yielding mechanically resilient constructs with anticoagulant
properties that successfully encourage the attachment and proliferation of essential endothelial and smooth muscle cell
types (Bao et al., 2022). Similar principles of structural biomimicry are reshaping neurosurgery, where natural spider silk
implants are replacing inadequate conventional techniques (Kornfeld et al., 2016). The extraordinary mechanical
endurance and excellent safety profile of this protein minimize inflammatory foci, creating a secure environment for the
survival and migration of cells crucial for reconstructing nervous structures over substantial distances (Kornfeld et al.,
2016). The spectrum of modern reconstructive methodologies is completed by advanced modifications of intrinsically
disordered proteins, enabling the synthesis of modular, resilin-like polypeptides (Balu et al., 2021). The resulting hydrogel
matrices with programmable traits combine extreme elasticity with dynamic responsiveness to environmental cues,
charting unprecedented directions for controlled pharmaceutical transport, diagnostic imaging, and state-of-the-art
restorative medicine (Balu et al., 2021).
METHODS
A comprehensive literature search was conducted to evaluate the clinical efficacy and biomechanical properties of naturederived biomaterials—specifically marine, arthropod, and botanical substrates—in modern regenerative medicine and
surgical reconstruction. The primary database utilized for this review was PubMed. The inclusion criteria were strictly
limited to English-language publications. The selected literature primarily consisted of systematic reviews, meta-analyses,
narrative reviews, in vitro and in vivo preclinical studies, and early-stage clinical trials.
RESULTS
Biomimetic Marine-Inspired Adhesives in Surgery: Current Advances and Future Perspectives
Biomimetic approaches inspired by marine organisms have recently attracted considerable attention in the development of
novel surgical adhesives for orthopedic applications. Mussels and oysters possess a remarkable ability to adhere firmly to
wet and dynamic surfaces under extreme marine conditions through adhesive proteins rich in catechol-containing amino
acids, particularly L-3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine (L-DOPA) (Lee et al., 2011). These catechol groups enable the formation
of strong covalent and non-covalent interactions even in aqueous environments, making them an attractive model for the
design of next-generation bioadhesives intended for use in blood-covered surgical fields (Lee et al., 2011; Ahn, 2017).
Consequently, synthetic mussel-inspired hydrogels and adhesive polymers have emerged as promising materials capable of
maintaining adhesion under physiologic wet conditions while simultaneously demonstrating biocompatibility and
biodegradability (Ahn, 2017).
In orthopedic surgery, conventional fracture fixation still relies predominantly on metallic implants such as plates, screws,
and intramedullary devices. Although effective, these approaches are associated with several disadvantages, including
prolonged operative time, periosteal stripping, soft tissue trauma, postoperative infection risk, implant corrosion, and the
frequent necessity for secondary surgery for hardware removal (Bone-O2 bio-adhesive, 2026). In contrast, bone adhesives
offer several theoretical and practical advantages. Biodegradable adhesives may eliminate the need for reoperation, reduce
implant-associated complications, and potentially improve osteointegration and bone healing by providing more
physiological stabilization (Bone-O2 bio-adhesive, 2026; Mehdizadeh & Yang, 2013). However, an important limitation
remains their mechanical performance, particularly in load-bearing bones such as the femur, where high compressive and
torsional forces may exceed the adhesive's capacity. For this reason, current bioadhesive technologies appear most suitable

for small bone fractures, craniofacial surgery, or as adjunctive stabilization methods rather than complete replacements for

The concept of surgical tissue adhesion is not entirely new, as adhesive materials are already widely used in several
medical specialties, particularly cardiovascular and vascular surgery. Fibrin and thrombin sealants mimic the physiologic
coagulation cascade through the interaction of fibrinogen, thrombin, factor XIII, calcium, and antifibrinolytic agents,
resulting in the formation of a stable fibrin clot (Daud et al., 2020). These products are primarily used for hemostasis when
conventional methods fail to adequately control bleeding. A systematic review and meta-analysis by Daud et al.
demonstrated that fibrin and thrombin sealants significantly reduce time to hemostasis and intraoperative blood loss in
vascular and cardiac surgery, although their impact on major clinical outcomes such as mortality or reoperation rates
remains limited (Daud et al., 2020). Importantly, despite favorable biocompatibility, fibrin-based adhesives possess
relatively low mechanical strength, restricting their use in tissues exposed to substantial biomechanical loading such as
bone (Daud et al., 2020; Nanomedia Medica, 2026). Other adhesive technologies, including cyanoacrylate-based systems
and collagen-thrombin hemostatic patches, have also shown efficacy in wound closure and vascular procedures but remain
limited by inflammatory reactions, thrombogenic potential, or insufficient load-bearing capacity (Nanomedia Medica,
2026).
Recent reports from China have described the development of a novel oyster-inspired bioadhesive known as Bone-O2,
designed specifically for fracture fixation under wet physiologic conditions (Bone-O2 bio-adhesive, 2026). Structurally,
Bone-O2 represents an organic–inorganic hybrid composed of calcium carbonate crystals integrated with a cross-linked
organic matrix rich in adhesive proteins (Bone-O2 bio-adhesive, 2026). Experimental studies demonstrated substantial
mechanical properties, including tensile resistance exceeding 400 pounds, compressive strength approaching 10 MPa, and
shear strength of approximately 0.5 MPa (Bone-O2 bio-adhesive, 2026). According to preliminary clinical reports
involving approximately 150 patients, Bone-O2 enabled fracture fixation within 2–3 minutes through minimally invasive
incisions and demonstrated promising early outcomes, including reduced postoperative pain and accelerated recovery
(Bone-O2 bio-adhesive, 2026; Nanomedia Medica, 2026). Furthermore, the material undergoes gradual biodegradation
during bone healing, potentially eliminating the need for implant removal procedures (Bone-O2 bio-adhesive, 2026).
Despite these encouraging findings, the current evidence surrounding Bone-O2 remains preliminary. Most available data
originate from media reports and early observational studies, while peer-reviewed randomized controlled trials directly
comparing Bone-O2 with standard fixation methods are still lacking (Nanomedia Medica, 2026). In addition, existing
studies have primarily involved relatively simple fractures, particularly distal radius fractures, whereas its efficacy in
highly comminuted fractures, intra-articular injuries, osteoporotic bone, or high-load-bearing long bones remains uncertain
(Bone-O2 bio-adhesive, 2026). Therefore, although biomimetic bone adhesives represent an exciting and rapidly evolving
field, further large-scale multicenter clinical trials with long-term follow-up are essential before these materials can be
integrated into routine orthopedic practice.
Throughout history of medicine, wound healing and its management has always constituted a major challenge, driving the
continual development of new therapies. Fish skin grafts represent one of these novel approach. Sourced primarily from
two species: Atlantic Cod and Nile Tilapia, the harvested skin undergoes a multi-step decellularization process involving
usage of chemical, enzymatic and physical agents, which eliminates antigenic particles while preserving the collagen-based
intact extracellular matrix and lipid components, that can be safely applied on human wounds (Ibrahim et al., 2023;
Esmaeili et al., 2023; Luze et al., 2022; Di Mitri et al., 2023; Reda et al., 2023).
Acellular fish skin (AFS), composed primarily of Type 1 collagen, retains its porous structural framework highly
analogous to that of human dermis (Ibrahim et al., 2023; Esmaeili et al., 2023; Luze et al., 2022). This structural and
compositional mimicry creates a template for hosts' cells allowing them to migrate into graft and begin the process of
proliferation and revascularization (Di Mitri et al., 2023; Reda et al., 2023).
Significant advantage of AFS grafts in comparison with mammalian-derived biological dressings is absence of zoonotic
disease transmission risk. Unlike porcine or bovine grafts which carry a theoretical risk of transmitting mammalianspecific diseases such as Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease and bovine spongiform encephalopathy, AFS grafts are free of this risk
(Ibrahim et al., 2023; Luze et al., 2022; Di Mitri et al., 2023; Zhao & Shen, 2025). Furthermore, negligible risk of disease
transmission allows the manufacturing process to be less harsh and successfully preserve the three-dimensional
architecture, ensuring a safe and highly compatible framework for wound healing process.
AFS grafts pose intrinsic antibacterial activity that distinguishes them from synthetic dressings. It stems from the grafts'
lipid profile that is retained during the decellularization process. The high content of numerous omega-3 fatty acids such as
eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) minimises the acute inflammatory response and has antibacterial properties (Ibrahim et al., 2023; Esmaeili et al., 2023; Reda et al., 2023). Consequently, AFS grafts represent the
viable option for challenging wounds such as chronic diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs), burns, necrotic angiodermatitis and can
be even used in reconstructive procedures such as neovaginoplasty for Mayer-Rokitansky-Kuster-Hauser syndrome.
(Esmaeili et al., 2023; Di Mitri et al., 2023; Pinto Medeiros Dias et al., 2019).

The Therapeutic Potential of Hirudotherapy and Larval Debridement in Reconstructive Surgery and Chronic
Wound Management
Hirudotherapy, involving the clinical use of medicinal leeches (primarily Hirudo medicinalis and Hirudo verbana), has
been an FDA-cleared treatment modality since 2004 (Foppiani et al., 2024; Daye et al., 2021; Sarikan & Savas, 2024;
Bilden et al., 2026). Its application has significantly evolved from ancient blood-letting practices to becoming a
sophisticated procedure in modern microsurgery. In contemporary plastic surgery, leeches serve as a vital strategy for
salvaging replanted structures, such as noses, fingers, or ears, by ensuring the necessary perfusion and nutrition of
transferred tissue flaps (Daye et al., 2021; Sarikan & Savas, 2024; Bilden et al., 2026; Vargas et al., 2023). The therapeutic
efficacy of this method relies on the synergy of two distinct mechanisms: the physical decongestion of tissues through
active blood suction and the pharmacological impact of salivary secretions, which introduce a complex cocktail of over 20
bioactive molecules into the host (Daye et al., 2021; Bilden et al., 2026; Vargas et al., 2023). The primary anticoagulant is
hirudin—a potent thrombin inhibitor that directly arrests the coagulation cascade. However, the biochemical spectrum is
much broader; hyaluronidase enhances interstitial permeability, eglins and bdellins provide robust anti-inflammatory
effects, and specific analgesic components effectively alleviate localized pain (Sarikan & Savas, 2024; Bilden et al., 2026).
From a reconstructive perspective, hirudotherapy is considered a recognized standard for managing critical venous
congestion. Leeches temporarily assume the role of a drainage system, maintaining flap viability until the patient's own
venous vessels regain patency (Foppiani et al., 2024; Vargas et al., 2023). Clinical data underscores this effectiveness; in
breast reconstructions involving TRAM and DIEP flaps, the use of leeches resulted in a 73.5% tissue salvage rate
(Foppiani et al., 2024). Furthermore, hirudotherapy proves indispensable even when microvascular surgery faces technical
limitations. A notable case involves a 38-year-old patient with a subtotal ear avulsion where microvascular repair was
impossible. A five-day course of therapy led to the complete resolution of congestion, full revitalization of the flap, and the
restoration of sensory function (Vargas et al., 2023). The World Health Organization (WHO) supports the integration of
traditional medicine into modern healthcare, which has bolstered interest in hirudotherapy for managing conditions like
osteoarthritis-related pain and stiffness (Daye et al., 2021; Sarikan & Savas, 2024). Recent experimental research has also
explored the potential of leeches in treating Metabolic Syndrome (METS). Animal models have demonstrated significant
hepatoprotective effects, characterized by a marked reduction in AST and ALT enzyme activity (Bilden et al., 2026).
Beyond stabilizing systolic blood pressure, the therapy improved glucose metabolism (as evidenced by OGTT and AUC
parameters), bringing glycemic profiles closer to those of healthy control groups. These biochemical improvements were
corroborated by histopathological analysis of the liver, showing a regression in steatosis and vacuolar degeneration (Bilden
et al., 2026). Parallel human studies suggest that hirudotherapy may mitigate oxidative stress—a key driver of aging and
chronic disease (Sarikan & Savas, 2024). After only two therapeutic sessions, a statistically significant decrease in
disulfide levels was observed (p=0.021), indicating a measurable enhancement of the body's antioxidant capacity (Sarikan
& Savas, 2024). Despite its high success rate, hirudotherapy carries a risk of complications, predominantly infections
caused by Aeromonas species (mainly A. hydrophila). These bacteria are natural symbionts found in the leech's digestive
tract and were responsible for 17.6% of infectious complications in the aforementioned breast reconstruction study
(Foppiani et al., 2024). Consequently, clinical protocols necessitate antibiotic prophylaxis, typically utilizing ciprofloxacin
(Foppiani et al., 2024; Vargas et al., 2023). Additionally, rare dermatological reactions have been documented, such as
Lichen planus. Its emergence at the application sites is attributed to either a localized immune response or the Koebner
phenomenon—an isomorphic skin reaction to trauma (Daye et al., 2021). Alongside hirudotherapy, larval therapy—also
known as biosurgical debridement—represents another fundamental biological method of increasing importance in the
management of chronic wounds. Persistent tissue damage and foot-related complications in diabetic patients are becoming
an increasingly critical health priority. This problem is often described as a widespread but underrecognized medical crisis.
It clearly necessitates a multidisciplinary clinical approach (Bazaliński et al., 2023; Morris et al., 2023). Effective
management depends primarily on the swift elimination of necrotic debris and bacterial layers. That is why larval therapy
is regarded as a highly efficient biological method for wound cleansing (Bazaliński et al., 2023; Morris et al., 2023; Lam et
al., 2025; Dardari, 2026; Herrero-Matías et al., 2024). Pathogens in long-standing wounds develop a resilient shield – the
biofilm. This shield provides robust protection against conventional pharmacological treatments (Bazaliński et al., 2023;
Morris et al., 2023; Dardari, 2026). However, research shows that substances secreted by the larvae effectively break down
this barrier. These include proteolytic enzymes and antimicrobial peptides, such as lucifensin (Bazaliński et al., 2023;
Morris et al., 2023; Dardari, 2026). Thanks to these biochemical agents, the larvae do not only eradicate microorganisms.
They also actively dismantle the architecture of non-viable tissue, converting it into a liquid state (Bazaliński et al., 2023;
Morris et al., 2023; Lam et al., 2025; Dardari, 2026; Herrero-Matías et al., 2024). This mechanism is known as
extracorporeal digestion. It ensures the removal of necrosis without damaging the patient's healthy anatomical structures
(Bazaliński et al., 2023; Morris et al., 2023; Dardari, 2026). Clinical observations indicate a clear effect in diabetic foot
ulcers. A significant reduction in dangerous pathogens – including Staphylococcus aureus and Pseudomonas aeruginosa –
is observable after just 48 to 96 hours of application (Dardari, 2026). Randomized controlled trials demonstrate that the
outcomes of biological debridement using larvae are equivalent to those achieved with surgical interventions (Lam et al.,
2025; Dardari, 2026; Herrero-Matías et al., 2024). The primary benefit of this approach is its remarkable precision. It can
distinguish necrotic matter from living tissue very accurately. This is vital when treating sensitive body areas, where
traditional surgical instruments might pose too great a risk (Bazaliński et al., 2023; Dardari, 2026; Herrero-Matías et al.,
2024). Contrary to common anxieties about physical discomfort, meta-analyses suggest that patients on larval therapy
report pain levels comparable to those receiving standard clinical care (Bazaliński et al., 2023; Lam et al., 2025).
Interestingly, wound debridement results are very good. However, the data on total healing time remain inconclusive. The

Bioactive Alternatives to Synthetic Materials in Tissue Engineering and Regenerative Medicine
Contemporary regenerative medicine and surgery are increasingly moving away from synthetic materials, such as steel or
Teflon, in favor of bioactive biomaterials. These technologies are not merely experimental alternatives, but advanced
engineering subjected to rigorous clinical trials, becoming a new standard of care (He & Yang, 2026).
Alginates, biomaterials derived from brown algae, find application in the treatment of hard-to-heal chronic wounds. Their
unique properties stem from their chemical structure and their ability to cross-link via divalent cations, which in
biomaterials chemistry is referred to as the "egg-box" model (Alsaei et al., 2026). Due to this mechanism, alginate
dressings rapidly gel upon contact with wound exudate under physiological conditions, creating an optimal, moist
microenvironment. The acidic degradation products of alginates not only significantly reduce patient pain but also exhibit
well-documented antibacterial properties, inhibiting wound colonization (Alsaei et al., 2026). As demonstrated in a recent
meta-analysis, the combination of alginates with negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) reduces healing time and
increases the percentage of completely healed wounds. Such an environment stimulates fibroblast activity and accelerates
epithelial cell migration, offering significant advantages over conventional therapies (Meslier et al., 2025).
Cellulose, the main building block of plants, becomes a material with good mechanical properties at the nanoscale, often
referred to as "plant steel". Bacterial nanocellulose (BNC) differs from plant cellulose in its absolute purity—it is
completely free of lignin and hemicellulose, and its three-dimensional nanofiber network closely resembles the human
extracellular matrix (ECM) (Liu et al., 2023). The clinical efficacy of nanocellulose has been well documented; a
comprehensive review of studies involving over 3,800 patients demonstrated its safety profile in the treatment of burns,
ulcers, dural repair, or tympanic membrane reconstruction (He & Yang, 2026). Currently, it is also being used to create
biocompatible "tubes" that serve as artificial blood vessels. Unlike synthetic Teflon (ePTFE) or Dacron, BNC does not
cause rejection and is characterized by a significantly lower thrombogenic potential, which allows for the replacement of
small-diameter vessels (Liu et al., 2023; He & Yang, 2026).
Despite the mechanical strength of bacterial nanocellulose (BNC), its hydrophilic nature hinders the rapid cellular adhesion
and proliferation of patient cells on its surface. To optimize this process, BNC is modified to create modern composites
impregnated with fish gelatin (Bao et al., 2022). The use of fish-derived protein eliminates cultural and religious
restrictions as well as the risk of transmitting viral diseases typical of mammals. Such a biocomposite stimulates
endothelial cells to adhere while maintaining an excellent safety profile, where the red blood cell destruction (hemolysis)
rate falls below 1.0% (Bao et al., 2022).
Plant polyphenols, such as tannic acid or EGCG derived from green tea, are bioactive compounds that are widely used as
coatings modifying traditional implants. They act as a biological buffer at the interface of a foreign material and living
tissue, exhibiting anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. Their molecular mechanism of action relies on a reduction
of free radical (ROS) levels and the stimulation of macrophage polarization from the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype to
the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype. Such reprogramming of the local immune microenvironment modulates the body's
inflammatory response, which effectively supports the collision-free integration of the implant. Polyphenols also inhibit the
activity of bone-resorbing cells (osteoclasts) while simultaneously promoting the differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells
(BMSCs) toward bone-forming osteoblasts. The use of these plant compounds in tissue engineering proves that organic
modifications of synthetic scaffolds and prostheses accelerate bone union (Wang et al., 2026).
Biodegradable scaffolds in tissue engineering the use of cellulose fibers from hemp is an emerging strategy in the
development of tissue engineering materials. Due to high porosity and hydrophilic properties, hemp cellulose constitutes
an ideal base for the production of biodegradable scaffolds supporting cell regeneration (Promdontree et al., 2023).
Composites based on hemp and polymers (e.g., polyvinyl alcohol) are used to produce hydrogel matrices mimicking the
architecture of soft tissues and cancellous bone. Such an implant supports physiological loads, while human cells and
blood vessels freely grow into its pores, leading to the ultimate degradation of the scaffold and its replacement by the
patient's own tissue (Promdontree et al., 2023).
Cannabinoids in skeletal system regeneration The medical potential of hemp in orthopedics is not limited solely to
cellulosic scaffolds but also encompasses the active substances contained within them. Research on the pharmacology of
cannabinoids demonstrates their fundamental role in bone tissue healing processes and in so-called fusion medicine
(Urreola et al., 2025). Endocannabinoid system receptors are widely present in the skeletal system, and the administration
of specific phytocannabinoids significantly stimulates the recruitment of mesenchymal stem cells to the site of a defect or
fracture (Urreola et al., 2025). Modulation of these receptors accelerates cell differentiation toward osteoblasts (boneforming cells), making cannabinoids promising promoters of bone union with a research-proven reparative potential
(Urreola et al., 2025).

Nature-Derived Biomaterials Obtained from Arthropods in Modern Medicine: Applications of Spider Silk,
Chitosan, and Resilin-Like Polypeptides
The dragline silk of Nephila edulis combines mechanical parameters – including a breaking strength of 1.1 GPa and
elongation capacity reaching 35% that remain beyond the reach of currently available synthetic suture materials (Kornfeld
et al., 2016). Chitosan, a deacetylated derivative of crustacean chitin, achieves rapid hemostasis via electrostatic
aggregation of blood cells at the wound site through a mechanism that does not depend on the physiological coagulation
cascade (Gheorghiță et al., 2023). Resilin, whose rubber-like properties in arthropod wing joints and tendons were first
characterized in the 1960s, is distinguished by its combination of minimal stiffness, substantial extensibility, and resistance
to mechanical fatigue across millions of loading cycles (Balu et al., 2021). Collectively, these three materials offer
solutions to distinct unmet needs: peripheral nerve repair, trauma hemorrhage control, and soft tissue engineering,
respectively.
In contrast to conventional suture materials, which have been associated with neuroma formation and impaired axonal
regeneration, spider silk provides a structurally favorable substrate for Schwann cell migration in the peripheral nervous
system (Kuhbier et al., 2011). Preclinical ovine studies demonstrated that silk-based conduits supported both axonal
regrowth and remyelination to a degree comparable with autologous nerve grafts as assessed electrophysiologically
(Radtke et al., 2011). Clinical translation was achieved in 2024, when four patients with peripheral nerve defects greater
than 20 cm received silk-based conduits containing approximately 2,500 Trichonephila dragline fibers within autologous
vein grafts; no adverse tissue reactions were observed in this small pilot cohort (Vogt et al., 2024).
In the domain of traumatic hemorrhage, chitosan gauze products have been integrated into the Tactical Combat Casualty
Care (TCCC) framework and are now standard issue across multiple NATO armed forces and US Special Operations units
(Bennett et al., 2014). Analysis of battlefield usage identified successful hemostatic outcomes or marked bleeding
reduction in 97% of documented applications, with the greatest clinical utility observed in anatomical regions where
tourniquet application is not feasible (Wedmore et al., 2006).
Recombinant engineering of resilin-like polypeptides (RLPs) has enabled the production of materials that reproduce the
viscoelastic profile of native resilin, with promising in vitro performance as scaffolding components in soft tissue
engineering contexts (Balu et al., 2021).
DISCUSSION
The synthesized literature highlights an ongoing paradigm shift from bio-inert synthetic and metallic implants toward
instructively bioactive, nature-derived macromolecular platforms. While conventional materials are frequently
bottlenecked by chronic foreign-body reactions, thrombogenic risks, and mechanical mismatches, biomimetic architectures
derived from marine life, arthropods, and terrestrial flora successfully translate evolutionary adaptations into targeted
clinical countermeasures.
Aquatic and marine resources uniquely address the challenges of moisture-compromised surgical environments. Catecholdriven chemistry from mussels and oysters overcomes hydration barriers through simultaneous covalent and non-covalent
bonding, allowing rapid bone or soft tissue stabilization without the hardware-associated complications of metallic
systems. Concurrently, acellular fish skin and algal alginates accelerate the management of chronic cutaneous defects, such
as diabetic foot ulcers. These substrates provide porous Type 1 collagen frameworks that promote neovascularization,
carry intrinsic antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties, and completely eliminate mammalian zoonotic transmission
risks.
Furthermore, arthropod proteins, botanical agents, and controlled living biotherapies offer exceptional mechanical
durability combined with precise cellular guidance. The clinical renaissance of macroorganism-based interventions-such as
hirudotherapy for salvaging congested tissue flaps and larval debridement for non-invasive biofilm eradication
demonstrates how biological mechanisms can outperform standard pharmacology. This structural orchestration is
complemented by Nephila edulis dragline silk, which guides Schwann cell migration across extensive neural gaps,
alongside chitosan matrices and recombinant resilin-like polypeptides. Locally, this framework is enhanced by
phytochemical synergy: plant polyphenols immunomodulate the microenvironment by polarizing macrophages toward a
pro-healing M2 state, while cannabinoids actively recruit mesenchymal stem cells to accelerate tissue union.
Despite these milestones, critical translational bottlenecks remain. Many next-generation technologies-including hybrid
bone glues, nanocellular vascular conduits, and spider silk scaffolds still rely on preliminary observational data, media
releases, or small pilot cohorts. Robust, multi-center randomized controlled trials (RCTs) directly comparing these natural
matrices against established standards of care are scarce. Systematically resolving long-term degradation kinetics,
mechanical behavior under complex physiological loads, and standardized isolation protocols remains essential before
these materials can be integrated into routine clinical practice.
CONCLUSION
In summary, the transition from bio-inert synthetic implants toward nature-derived macromolecular platforms represents a
major cornerstone of modern restorative medicine. By leveraging evolutionary blueprints, advanced biomaterials do not
merely substitute damaged structures but actively orchestrate physiological healing cascades. Compared to conventional

Within this framework, marine, arthropod, and botanical substrates provide targeted solutions to distinct clinical

and standardized isolation protocols. Ultimately, fusing modern bioengineering with nature's structural design holds the
key to achieving fully predictable, programmable tissue regeneration.
