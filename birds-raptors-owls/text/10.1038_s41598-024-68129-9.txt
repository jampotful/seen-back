# Research on the perception method of tiny objects in low-light and wide-field video

## Abstract
AbstractAt present, many trackers exhibit commendable performance in well-illuminated scenarios but overlook target tracking in low-light environments. As night falls, the tracker's accuracy drops dramatically. Challenges such as high image resolution, intricate backgrounds, uneven illumination, and the resemblance between targets and backgrounds in Hawk-Eye surveillance videos make tracking small objects in low-light and wide-field scenarios exceedingly difficult for previous trackers. To address these challenges, this paper introduces an innovative approach by integrating the difference constraint method into the CF (correlation filters) tracker, which generates a change-aware mask using inter-frame difference information. In addition, a dual regression model and inter-frame difference constraint term are introduced to restrict each other for dual filter learning. In this paper, we construct a new benchmark comprising 41 night surveillance sequences captured by Hawk-Eye cameras. Exhaustive experiments are conducted on this benchmark. The results show that the proposed method maintains superior accuracy, surpasses state-of-the-art trackers in this dataset, and achieves a real-time performance of 27 fps on a single CPU, substantially advancing tiny object tracking on Hawk-Eye surveillance videos in low light and in night scenes.Subject terms: Electrical and electronic engineering, Information theory and computation, Imaging and sensing

## Introduction
Visual object tracking (VOT) is widely used in surveillance video, robot vision, and automation. VOT predicts the location and size of objects, which is an important field of study in computer vision. According to previous research, the discriminant correlation filter-based tracking algorithm has excellent potential in VOT. With the wide application of high-performance surveillance cameras and the increasing demand for multi-scene application of tracking algorithms, tracking in dark scenes requires more research attention. However, most trackers work well during the day when the target is easily distinguishable from the background. Night is also a common application scenario for target tracking, but it is challenging to capture high-quality images at night. Nighttime images are accompanied by a large amount of random noise, which causes adverse interference to target recognition and feature extraction. The contrast degradation of an image leads to more severe background clutter. The uneven illumination distribution in the image (with and without streetlight illumination areas) and the complex sources of light (thermal and non-thermal emitting light sources) make the object's color characteristics deviate from frame to frame. This poses a significant challenge to the trackers and reduces the accuracy of the tracking methods at night.
Now, 24-h uninterrupted video surveillance is ubiquitous. At night, current target tracking research cannot effectively solve the problems of low illumination and wide field of view target tracking. To improve the efficiency of safety protection and reduce the workload of monitoring personnel, it is necessary to establish a low illumination and wide field of view target tracking laboratory. With the popularity of digital and high-definition equipment further promoting the development of the video surveillance field, Hawk-Eye surveillance cameras have also been widely used. Hawk-Eye cameras have a unique visual mechanism, divided structurally into a front central concave area and side central concave areas. The side central concave areas have a larger field of view and, by overlapping the left and right sides, constitute an even larger field of view. Hawk-Eye cameras feature high resolutions, long ranges, and large fields of view, resulting in images with a small percentage of target object pixels, high background-target similarities, and higher requirements for real-time tracker performance.
Object tracking in low-light and wide-field conditions confronts several difficulties as follows: (1) The target size is relatively small in the image and can be regarded as a tiny object. Moreover, the background is more complex than usual, making the target is easily occluded. (2) In the dark, it is difficult to distinguish the tiny target from the background and extract the geometric and color features. (3) Significant noise in the image seriously interferes with the tracking accuracy. (4) Insufficient computing resources hinder real-time tracking in low-light and wide-field conditions.
It is imperative to research tiny object tracking with low-light and wide-field. First, there is an increasing demand for object tracking in low-light scenes in daily life. Second, the current SOTA trackers have poor robustness in tiny object tracking with low-light and wide-field. Therefore, the research results have wide application prospects. Previously, visual tracking algorithms rarely tracked small targets with low-light and wide-field, but this is of great significance for expanding security surveillance applications and reducing labor costs. For visual target tracking at night, many related studies have added the SOTA dark enhancement algorithm into the preprocessing part of the tracking algorithm1–3 to enhance the image. Then, the preprocessed image is used as the input for object feature extraction and matching, to implement the object tracking. However, there are several disadvantages: (1) Existing enhancement algorithms are overwhelmingly slower than 24 fps. Therefore, embedding the enhancement algorithm will cause the tracking algorithm to not meet the real-time performance. (2) The better image produced by the enhancer depends only on human subjective feelings, not on the machine, which means that the enhancer does not have a quantifiable standard against which to improve the tracking accuracy. (3) The enhancer not only enhances the image but also increases the random noise, severely distracting the tracker.
This paper proposes a new tiny target tracking algorithm, CATrack, which is suitable for low light and wide field scenes, which is proven through extensive experiments and theoretical arguments. It ensures the real-time performance and robustness of tracking. Specifically, we propose using a change detection algorithm to detect the image interframe difference information. However, the interframe difference mask is disturbed by lots of random noise, making its effect insignificant. Therefore, we assume that the images obey a normal distribution according to the 3σ criterion, with automatic detection of the actual faintly changed and unchanged areas of the images. This assumption allows us to eliminate the vast majority of the interference noise and achieve excellent extractions of changing regions and implement background recognition. We use the faint change mask to obtain the target region of interest, extract the target information, train the target filter and train the background filter based on the no-change area. The proposed method can maintain real-time performance and excellent tracking accuracy in low-light and wide-fields.
Many researchers use enhancers as the entry point, combining enhancers and CF-based trackers, solving the problem of low tracking accuracy in the dark. We propose for the first time to use the image differences for object tracking. The weakly varying mask is obtained by detecting the weak motion of the tiny target. A dual regression model is introduced, and temporal regularization is added to the model to construct the algorithm, which is more sensitive to small target motion perception and is more generalized in the dark and for wide fields. No previous studies have focused on the low-light, wide-field and the small target perception. Therefore, this paper collected a new night Hawk-Eye camera monitoring dataset containing 41 sequences of tiny targets as the experimental benchmark.
The research contributions of this paper are as follows:
We propose to use the temporal-spatial information of the video. The weak change perception mask of low-light image is acquired and combined with CF structure for real-time tiny target tracking in the low-light and wide-field, without using the dark enhancement model.
Temporal regularization is introduced into the dual regression equation, which improves the tracking performance of the algorithm in the case of fast-moving, deformation, and motion blur.
A new night Hawk-Eye image dataset has been constructed, and the proposed algorithm and the comparison algorithm have been extensively tested on this dataset to demonstrate the efficiency and robustness of CATrack.

## Related works
We use change detection to preprocess images. Change detection is the process of identifying the differences in the state of the same object or phenomenon by observing it at different times and can be universally classified into three categories. The first category is as in Refs.4–6, where deep learning is introduced into the field of change detection, which is the end-to-end method, with the advantage of high accuracy and the disadvantage of high computational resource requirements. The second category is the nondeep learning methods by principal component analysis theory, discrete wavelet change theory and nondownsampled contour wavelet change theory7,8. The third category is the traditional methods, such as logarithmic log ratio and mean ratio. After a substantial amount of comparison work, we finally selected the log ratio for image change detection. Since it converts a large amount of multiplicative random noise generated by the images under dark conditions into additive noise, it alleviates the noise problem. In addition, we find that the change information applies to the target filter, which improves the performance of the proposed method. Related night trackers such as Ref.9 enhance the tracking accuracy of the tracking algorithms in low-light images by processing the images with CE10 and LIME11 enhancement algorithms before tracking; 12 chose to embed a high-speed dark enhancement algorithm13 into the tracking framework, Ref.14 by enhancing low-light images with ME-Net and combining it with a tracking framework.
Tracking approaches are generally divided into two types. The first type is the DL-based (deep learning) trackers, which learn similarities between target images and search regions through end-to-end offline training, which requires large amounts of data for pretraining. However, there are no data-set for low-light and wide-fields. The other type of tracking approach is when the CF-based tracker mainly uses the correlation between the predicted image and the template image to determine the target position during the tracking process. H.K. Galoogahi et al.15 used actual translation to generate negative samples, which expanded the search area and mitigated the boundary effect. F. Li et al.16 introduced a temporal regularization term in the DCF to prevent the filter model from overfitting. Tracking at night places additional demands on the tracker. B. Li et al.12 proposed a dual regression model to enhance the robustness of the tracker in the dark. However, they all ignore small target tracking. However, tracking tiny targets in the dark is a scenario they generally overlook. The proposed method uses a double regression model with a time regularization term. In the tracking process, the information of the previous frame is used to improve the tracking performance.
There is no public datasets for visual tracking of small targets with low-light and wide-fields. In order to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method, our team used the Hawk-Eye camera to capture lots of image sequences and produced a new dataset used in this paper. In the fourth part of this paper, the production of relevant data, the comparison algorithm, and the evaluation method is described in detail.
In this paper, the proposed method differs from the previous methods in the following aspects.
The change detection method is first exploited to CF-based tracker. By change constraint, our method has robust ability in tiny object tracking in low-light and wide-fields.
Different from conventional night tracker, where the preprocessing part selects the low light image enhancement model, the pretreatment part in our method selects the change aware model and can segment the changed and the unchanged area of the image due to the change aware mask. The different information has a good effect on finding tiny targets under low light.
It has no public benchmark and no data available. Hence we constructed newly dataset, which is screened by a Hawk-Eye camera at night.

## Proposed method
The CATrack flow chart is shown in Fig. 1, which is divided into the image preprocessing stage, model training stage, and weighted response fusion stage. As the Hawk-Eye camera screen the f-th frame at night, CATrack conducts change detection at first to achieve area division and mask generation. Specifically, form a difference mask by the logarithmic difference of f-th frame and f-1-th frame. Then we use denoising methods twice. We used median filtering at first and then set a secondary denoising threshold based on the 3σ criterion in statistics to remove the residual noise. Finally, a precise aware mask is generated. Then, the dual regression model is trained by changed regions and unchanged regions according to the mask. The background filter and target filter generate the corresponding response maps in the image, respectively, and the weighted responses are fused to obtain the final response to locate the target position.

## Dataset
To evaluate the tracker's performance, we captured a series of video sequences at night using a Hawkeye camera and selected some typical small target sequences as benchmarks.
Aiming at the problem of insufficient datasets, this paper constructs a large-scale tiny target object tracking dataset by performing video acquisition and data processing on low-illuminance wide-field scenes. The newly produced dataset in this paper differs from the previous datasets in that: 1. It was taken with a fixed Hawk-Eye camera; 2. The dataset was taken under extremely low-light conditions at night; 3. the number of target pixels in this dataset is less than 400, which meets the definition of tiny targets. This dataset contains more than 15,000 pictures, which are divided into 41 manually calibrated video sequences. The new dataset was annotated with attributes, including 41 illumination variation (IV), 10 scale variation (SV), 22 occlusion (OCC), 21 deformation (DEF), 30 motion blur (MB), 11 fast motion (FM), 18 in-plane rotation (IPR) and 39background clutter (BC) sequences. The above attributes were annotated according to the standards of the OTB100 dataset. The datasets used during the current study available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

## Comparison algorithms
The proposed algorithm and 14 SOTA algorithms, including AutoTrack18, ECO19, ADTrack12, STAPLE20, C-COT21, ASRCF22, CF223, SCT424, BACF15, MCCT&MCCT_H25, ARCF_H&ARCF_HC26, GFSDCF27, are comprehensively evaluated in newly sequences. All parameters in the comparison tracker adopted in this paper keep the official initial parameters to ensure that these trackers can obtain more convincing results in the evaluation. The performance of the tracker is judged according to the AUC (area under curve) of the success plots and the DP (distance precision) of the precision plots.

## Evaluation criterion
Success plots and precision plots evaluated all trackers. The success plots reveals the ratio of successful frames to the entire sequence when the IOU score surpasses the predefined threshold. IOU score indicates the intersection and union ratio of the GT and traker predict box in the same frame. The precision plots judge the tracking accuracy by the distance between the target center predicted by the tracker and the actual center manually marked. The higher the ratio of the video frame whose distance is less than the threshold to the whole video sequence, the higher the tracking accuracy. The OPE (one-pass evaluation) method is used for testing. OPE indicates that the tracker predicts target localization for all subsequent frames based on the GT (ground-truth) in the first frame of the sequence.

## Robustness evaluation
In this section, we will discuss the robustness of the tracker. SRE (spatial robustness evaluation) and TRE (temporal robustness evaluation) are commonly used to verify the robustness of trackers, both based on OPE. SRE moved the initial GT by 10% in 12 directions, including 8 spatial directions and 4 scale directions. TRE divides a sequence into 20 segments and takes the statistical average. SRE and TRE respectively add perturbations to the initialization of the tracker in space and time, verifying spatial robustness and temporal robustness respectively.
Figure 5a,b shows the TRE Precision Plots and Success Plots camparing our algorithm with other algorithm. Our algorithm ranks first in DP and second in AUC. This evaluation indicates that, compared to other algorithms, our algorithm is more robust in tracking tiny targets in low-light and wide-field scenarios with different temporal initializations. Figure 5c,d shows the SRE precision plots and success plots comparing our algorithm with other algorithm. It can be seen that our algorithm achieved the best ranking in DP but performed relatively poorly in AUC. Through our analysis, it is mainly since the preprocessing stage of the proposed algorithm is too sensitive to space perturbation, which leads to the instability of the generated masks and finally makes our performance inferior.

## Conclusion
In this study, we introduce a novel change-aware tracker (CATrack) designed to maintain robustness and precision while tracking small objects under low-light and wide-field conditions, all while ensuring real-time performance. Our approach for handling low-light tiny object tracking incorporates inter-frame difference constraints and adds temporal regularization in a dual regression model. CATrack initially obtains a weak change mask of the target based on inter-frame difference information, which is then utilized for training dual filters. In the dual regression model, the target-aware filter and the background-aware filter constrain each other during training iterations and mutually enhance each other during target tracking. Additionally, we integrate temporal information into the dual regression model to prevent model overfitting. The proposed method has so far achieved more robust real-time tracking of tiny targets under low-light and wide-field conditions. Moreover, a newly tiny target dataset for low-light and wide-field has been constructed in this paper. In this dataset, the proposed method outperforms most comparative tracking algorithms, albeit with limitations in spatial robustness. Notably, the algorithm in this paper exhibits challenges in change awareness; it struggles to re-locate the target when it is partially or completely occluded or disappears from the field of view. Nonetheless, this work establishes a research direction for low-light target tracking without relying on image enhancement techniques. The algorithm is straightforward, easily implementable, and has minimal hardware requirements. We aim to provide valuable insights for researchers in related fields and plan to further optimize the algorithm while exploring the integration of deep learning methods into low-light tracking in future work.
