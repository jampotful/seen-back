# Distribution bias embedding tuning of vision transformer for remote sensing object detection

## Abstract
The expanded availability of remote sensing data in recent years has yielded an increasing demand for advanced object detection methods to support Earth observation applications. Vision Transformers (ViTs) have recently emerged as a powerful backbone in the field of visual recognition, achieving exceptional performance in various tasks. However, adapting the pretrained ViTs to specific downstream tasks, particularly in remote sensing, represents a challenge due to the different distribution between the pretraining and downstream task datasets. To address this challenge, this paper introduces the distribution bias embedding-based tuning (DBET) method. DBET integrates a bias embedding token, which dynamically adjusts the feature representations to better align the distribution bias between the pretraining and downstream task datasets. DBET also reduces the number of parameters that require fine-tuning, thus significantly improving computational efficiency and reducing training time while maintaining the original structure unchanged. DBET is verified by experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets, including the DOTA-v1.0, RarePlanes, and EAGLE datasets. The results demonstrate that DBET can not only reduce the number of trainable parameters and accelerate the fine-tuning process but can also significantly improve accuracy, particularly for tasks with a limited number of categories, providing a scalable solution for remote sensing object detection. The DBET method achieved a recognition accuracy of 56.94% on the EAGLE dataset, 80.56% on the five-class RarePlanes dataset, and 70.68% on the DOTA-v1.0 dataset. Moreover, the DBET method reduced the average training time to 58% of full fine-tuning method.

## Introduction
There has been a growing demand for Earth observation applications due to the increased accessibility of various remote sensing data. Among these applications, object detection has become one of the most crucial tasks in reducing the requirements of human activities surveillance on Earth observation1. Recently, Vision Transformers (ViTs)2 have been demonstrated to be a powerful technique for visual recognition, achieving remarkable performance in various visual tasks. Therefore, the idea of applying the ViTs to the field of object detection in remote sensing images (RSIs) has been highly attractive, showing considerable appeal.
To enhance the adaptation ability of ViTs in object detection tasks, recent work has proposed several approaches. For instance, He et al3. constructed a feature pyramid from a single-scale feature map to support multi-scale feature representation during the fine-tuning phase while maintaining the pretrained backbone. The ViTPose4, which employs the ViT and a lightweight decoder, was proposed for pose estimation. Alexey et al5. deployed a dense prediction head for the ViT to pursue fine-grained predictions and reached a new state of the art on several semantic segmentation datasets. Moreover, to address the differences between natural images and RSIs, researchers devised rotated varied-size window attention (RVSA)6to substitute the original full attention in ViTs to investigate the arbitrarily oriented object detection in RSIs. Zhang et al7. developed the angle tokenization technique based on pyramid Transformer architecture to learn the orientation feature efficiently for geospatial objects. Typically, the aforementioned models are pretrained on large-scale datasets, and subsequent fine-tuning is required for specific downstream tasks. In addition, these models focus on the problem of handling the directional variance of objects in RSIs and exhibit promising performance.
The previous research has raised the critical question of how to improve performance in downstream remote sensing tasks by refining the pretrained model. Namely, when the data distribution of a downstream task matches the data distribution of a pretraining dataset, the refining process can be performed by freezing most parameters of the pretrained model and fine-tuning only the classification head. For downstream tasks with different data distribution, to effectively and efficiently adapt large pretrain ViT models to downstream tasks, numerous attempts have been endeavored. Jiaet al8. proposed visual prompt tuning to revise only the prompt part of model parameters in a ViT and achieved significant performance gains. Yanget al9. devised a visual pretraining prompt tuning framework for few-shot image classification. Qiet al10. used the information-preserving normalizing flows to adjust the crucial shortcut layers. The above-mentioned approaches can achieve consistent performance gains by tuning only a small set of parameters. The main insight behind their success is that the distribution biases of downstream tasks can be introduced from the fine-tuning stage11.
However, although the pretrained ViT models can show excellent feature extraction capabilities, adapting them for specific downstream tasks still remains challenging due to the distribution difference between the pretraining and downstream datasets12,13. In addition, fine-tuning typically requires substantial computational resources, extended training times, and the modification of large portions of the pretrained model. This can often involve adding extra modules and the introduction of additional trainable parameters, which can not always be an ideal choice, particularly when computational efficiency is a priority.
To address the aforementioned problems, this paper proposes an innovative tuning method in the ViTs for remote sensing object detection named the distribution bias embedding-based tuning (DBET) method. The proposed DBET method employs a bias token encoding approach that allows a model to align the distribution of downstream tasks with the pre-trained parameters without altering the underlying model architecture. The DBET method focuses on reducing the number of parameters that require adjustment during the fine-tuning process. This enables efficient adaptation of the ViT-based models to remote sensing tasks while maintaining their backbone intact, thus making it easier and faster for these models to adapt to new datasets. Thus, the proposed DBET method can significantly accelerate the fine-tuning process and improve the accuracy of object detection tasks in scenarios with a limited number of categories. The effectiveness of the DBET method is verified on multiple datasets and remote sensing applications, as well as through a comparison with the existing fine-tuning method to highlight its advantages in both performance and efficiency.

## Related work

## Transformer-based visual models
The Transformer architecture18 has achieved remarkable success in the natural language processing (NLP) field, serving as a foundation for the state-of-the-art large language models, such as the ChatGPT and DeepSeek models. The advantages of this architecture have been considered to be transferable to the field of computer vision (CV)19, particularly to remote sensing object detection tasks20–23. The first well-known related method is the detection Transformer (DETR) model developed by Carion et al24. to achieve end-to-end object detection and segmentation using the Transformer encoder-decoder architecture. However, the DETR model’s shortcoming of slow convergence has stimulated further research in this field25. Another well-known related model is the Swin Transformer model26, which employs a pyramid structure to reconfigure the Transformer blocks. Meanwhile, this model uses a shifted windowing scheme to improve the model’s ability to capture the relationships between different regions of an image, which as a result doubles the number of attention windows. The well-known ViT model2 adopts the original Transformer encoder structure with fewer inductive biases for visual tasks, which maximally decouples the pretraining process from the downstream tasks3. Li et al27. have used the ViT model in the remote target recognition task.
Numerous studies have been conducted on the ViT structure to explore its performance in visual tasks3,6,28–32. For instance, Zhang et al28,29. proposed the SegViT model for semantic segmentation tasks. Yao et al30. developed the ViTmatte model for image matting. Ye et al32. introduced the Transgaze model for gaze estimation. Regarding object detection, the ViTDet model3,33 uses the ViT as a backbone of the object detector and adds a simple feature pyramid as a neck, using the Mask R-CNN model34 or the Cascade R-CNN model35as a detector head to detect objects from images of natural scenes. To advance object detection in remote sensing, Zhang et al6. proposed a rotated varied-size attention (RVSA) mechanism to replace the original full attention in the ViT blocks. This modification effectively reduced the computational and memory costs while improving the feature representation learning effect. In this work, the proposed method is integrated into the ViTDet and RVSA frameworks, demonstrating the generalization ability of the proposed approach across different ViT-based schemes.

## Fine-tuning for downstream task
The ViT model has garnered significant research interest in the field of computer vision due to its minimal inductive biases. As a result, numerous pretrained models with unchanged weights have been developed and become publicly available. A natural approach to leverage the weights for downstream tasks is to fine-tune all parameters of a pretrained model on new datasets. However, this approach faces several challenges when a pretrained ViT backbone is applied to downstream tasks, including the need for a large amount of fine-tuning data, an extended fine-tuning time, and high computational demands. To tune a model with as few parameters as possible for downstream tasks, recent research has explored the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) approach8,10,36–43. In the context of the PEFT approach, two distinct research directions can be identified. The first includes using a trainable residual module to project the features of the original dimension into a smaller dimension for a unchanged backbone network; this method has achieved good results in improving the performance of downstream tasks10,36–39. Although these methods can significantly reduce the number of trainable parameters, they alter the original computational pathway of the transformer layers. In this work, the process of projecting task-specific datasets into the dimensionality of the pretraining dataset in the input space is explored, preserving the primary computational framework.
Another research direction refers to the application of a trainable token, which has also achieved promising results in terms of performance and efficiency8,40,41. The visual prompt tuning (VPT)8 first employs a token-based method in visual tasks by injecting the task-specific token into the input space of each transformer layer, aiming to emulate its success in the NLP field. Several improvements to VPT have been developed since then40,41 to obtain higher efficien cy. The visual pretraining prompt tuning (VPPT) framework40aims to tune the pretrained model with few-shot learning settings. Han et al41. introduced a set of learnable key value prompts to visual prompts. Zhu et al. proposed MVP tuning method applied few-shot to VPT for remote sensing tasks9. However, the designs of these tokens are task-specific, which limits their application flexibility to new downstream tasks44.
Unlike the above-mentioned methods, the proposed DBET method aims to improve the pretrained ViT model for the downstream tasks without altering the original computational pathway, achieving flexible applicability to different downstream tasks45.

## Vision transformers in remote sensing and related fields
Vision Transformers (ViTs) have demonstrated strong potential in remote sensing image analysis, particularly in capturing long-range dependencies and global contextual information. However, traditional ViTs face challenges when applied to remote sensing data due to high-resolution inputs, complex backgrounds, and arbitrarily oriented objects, which lead to increased computational costs and limited inductive biases.
To address these issues, Zhao et al46. proposed a Local and Long-Range Collaborative Learning Framework (L2RCF) for remote sensing scene classification, which addresses the issue of CNNs struggling to capture long-range context and VTs overlooking local features, significantly improving remote sensing scene classification performance. Besides, Dang et al.47 proposed the Joint Superpixel and Transformer (JST) framework, which addresses the issues of insufficient context dependency and poor semantic adaptability in existing methods, enhancing the classification performance of high-resolution remote sensing images.Both papers demonstrate the advantages of Transformers in classification tasks,particularly their remarkable performance in addressing the limitations of traditional methods, enhancing feature representation quality, and adapting to diverse and complex scenarios.
In addition, Zhang et al.48 proposed the Efficient Inductive Attention-based Pyramid Vision Transformer (EIA-PVT) model, which enhances the model’s efficiency and detection capability by introducing an adaptive multi-granularity routing moduleand an angular token mechanism. Liu et al.49 proposed a remote sensing image object detection model based on an improved DETR. By enhancing the attention mechanism, optimizing the model architecture, and refining the loss function, the model achieves high detection accuracy. Xu et al.50proposed a low-resolution remote sensing image object detection framework that integrates fine-grained enhancement with the Swin Transformer, effectively improving detection performance under low-resolution conditions. These works demonstrate the strong potential of Transformers in remote sensing object detection.
These studies indicate that incorporating convolutional priors and efficient attention mechanisms has become a key trend in advancing ViT-based approaches for remote sensing tasks. Building on this line of research, the proposed Distribution Bias Embedded Tuning (DBET) method further improves the adaptability of pretrained ViTs for remote sensing object detection, providing a more efficient and flexible solution for remote sensing image analysis.

First, the problem considered in this work is defined; second, the framework architecture of the proposed method is introduced; then two bias token encoding approaches are presented; finally, the loss function and bias calculation mechanism are described in detail.

## Proposed framework architecture
The proposed DBET method is used to implement a mapping function g into Eq. (5), optimizing a learnable component \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{U}$$\end{document} to achieve an effective representation of features in datasets for downstream tasks with a minimal subset of pretrained weights \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{f}$$\end{document}. The framework architecture of the proposed DBET method is shown in Fig. 1, which includes fine-tuning process and inference process.Fig. 1The framework architecture of the proposed DBET method.
The framework architecture of the proposed DBET method.
In the fine-tuning process, the bias token is initialized before the first iteration. In each iteration, the pretrained model inputs a mini batch of images from the datasets for a downstream task and generates the corresponding output by performing the inference process. Next, the DBET method calculates the bias between the output and annotation and updates the bias tokens accordingly. During this process, the backbone of the pretrained model remains unchanged.
In the inference process, the detection model first splits the input image into several fixed-size patches and then embeds image patch tokens, position tokens, and bias tokens jointly. Afterward, the embedded tokens are inputted into the backbone model to obtain a feature vector of the input image. Finally, the detection head outputs the box tokens and class tokens obtained from the feature vector.
Theoretically, the proposed DBET method does not alter the pretrained model’s backbone weights, offering the following advantages. First, the proposed DBET method can be plug-and-play integrated with different pretrained models and backbone architectures. Second, the DBET method does not perform adjustments to the entire model during the training process, but instead tunes only a subset of parameters, thus decreasing the number of training parameters and consequently accelerating the training process.

Considering a new dataset for downstream tasks, the bias tokens are introduced to induce different feature activations, thus fine-tuning the pretrained model. By updating the bias tokens, the pretrained model can match the distribution of the downstream task. During the fine-tuning process, the head part of the pretrained model and the bias tokens are updated, whereas the backbone part is kept unchanged.
Aiming at considering diverse computational environments, this study designs two DBET variants, namely DBET-single and DBET-multi, which employ single- and multi-layer bias token encoding approaches, respectively. The two methods encode bias tokens into different involved layers of a pretrained model, as shown in Fig. 2. Theoretically, compared to the DBET-multi method, the DBET-single method has a simpler architecture with fewer parameters, which results in lower memory requirements and reduced training time. This makes the DBET-single method a more valuable option in scenarios with limited storage and time. ## Loss function and bias calculation
In this paper, distribution bias refers to the bias between the pre-training dataset and the downstream task dataset, which causes the pretrained model to require sufficient fine-tuning in order to achieve satisfactory recognition performance on downstream tasks. In object detection tasks, distribution bias can be divided into two components: class distribution bias and box distribution bias. The difference in categories between the pre-training and downstream datasets leads to class distribution bias, while the discrepancy in object characteristics between them results in box distribution bias.
The proposed DBET aims to train a prompt that enables the pretrained model to directly recognize images fused with this prompt. Essentially, the prompt is designed to adapt to the distribution bias between the pre-training and downstream datasets. To this end, a loss function based on classification loss \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L_{\text {cls}}$$\end{document} and regression loss \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L_{\text {reg}}$$\end{document} is formulated.
The output features of the backbone part of a pretrained model are sent to the detector head for subsequent object location and classification tasks. In the fine-tuning process, a loss function \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal {L}(\cdot )$$\end{document} is used to measure the object recognition effect, and the training purpose is achieved by minimizing the loss function’s value, which can be expressed by:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {L}\left( p_k, p_k^*,\delta _k, t_k^* \right) =\frac{1}{K} \sum _{k=1}^{K} L_{\text {cls}} \left( p_k, p_k^*\right) + \frac{1}{K} \sum _{k=1}^{K} p_k^* L_{\text {reg}} \left( \delta _k, t_k^*\right) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where K is the number of a batch; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_k^*$$\end{document} is the ground-truth label of the kth anchor; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_k$$\end{document} denotes the classification prediction result; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_k^*$$\end{document} is the supervision offset of the ground-truth box relative to the kth anchor; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta _k$$\end{document} is the box regression result; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L_{\text {cls}}(\cdot )$$\end{document} indicates the classification loss function; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L_{\text {reg}}(\cdot )$$\end{document} is the regression loss function of the bounding box.
Unlike the full fine-tuning method that uses loss \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal {L}(\cdot )$$\end{document} to adjust a pretrained model’s weights, the proposed DBET method employs loss \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal {L}(\cdot )$$\end{document} to modify the bias tokens in each training iteration.

## Pretrained models
To assess the generalization ability of the proposed method, the DBET method was applied to two ViT models pretrained on the COCO14 and MillionAID3 datasets respectively. The COCO dataset contained 80 categories, containing samples collected from natural scene images, whereas the MillionAID dataset consisted of 28 categories, including samples gathered from remote sensing imagery. In addition, the DBET method was also applied to the ViT model with the RVSA method6, which was pretrained on the MillionAID3 dataset. The ViT with RVSA could dynamically adjust the size, position, and orientation of the attention window. These three above pretrained models differed significantly in terms of the number of parameters and model architecture. The pretrained ViT model on the COCO dataset was obtained from the paper3, and the pretrained ViT model and ViT model with RVSA on the MillionAID dataset were obtained from the paper6.
To further illustrate the distribution bias between the two pretrain datasets and the three downstream task datasets, we counted the number of instances of various sizes and aspect ratios in each dataset, discarding those instances that appeared infrequently. ## Fine-tuning methods
To prove the accuracy and efficiency of the proposed DBET method, this study compared the DBET-multi and DBET-single methods with other commonly used tuning methods. The specific denotations of the comparison methods were as follows:Full: This method fully updated all parameters, including backbone, neck, and detection head parameters;Linear: This method used only a linear layer as a detection head;MLP-3: This method employed a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with three layers, instead of a linear layer, as a detection head.MVP9: This method denoted a prompt fine-tuning method for ViT models; it was suitable for remote sensing tasks and similar to the proposed DBET method. ## Implementation details
For the pretrained models, the original configurations were adopted by deploying a ViT backbone with the Mask R-CNN detection head (as specified in3) and a ViT+RVSA backbone with the Oriented R-CNN detection head (as described in6). For the proposed DBET method, the core optimizer is defined as SGD with an initial learning rate of 0.005. It incorporates a momentum of 0.9 to accelerate convergence by maintaining directional inertia, reducing oscillations during parameter updates. A weight decay of 0.0001 is applied, acting as L2 regularization to prevent overfitting by penalizing large parameter values. This study initialized the bias token parameters using a standard Gaussian distribution and applied this initialization to both the ViT and ViT+RVSA pretrained models. ## Main results
The performance of DBET-single and DBET-multi methods was evaluated on three remote sensing object detection tasks:Task A: In this task, the detection performance of the ViT model pretrained on the nature sense COCO dataset and fine-tuned using different methods was evaluated;Task B: This task evaluated the detection performance of the ViT model pretrained on the remote sensing MillionAID dataset and fine-tuned using different methods;Task C: This task examined the detection performance of the ViT+RVSA model pretrained on the remote sensing MillionAID dataset and fine-tuned using different methods.The results of the methods on Tasks A, B, and C are respectively presented in Tables 3, 4, and 5, where it can be seen that the proposed DBET method affected the training speed and accuracy of the ViT-based pretrained models. In Tables 3, 4, and 5, ‘time (ms)’ refers to the time required for a single iteration, ‘speedup’ represents the ratio of the time taken by the baseline method (Full) to that of the target method, ‘mAP’ stands for mean average precision and the best performances, except for the Full method, are denoted in bold, and the second-best are underlined. The results demonstrated that the pretrained models tuned by the DBET method achieved a significant improvement in terms of the training speed compared to the Full method. Meanwhile, compared to the Linear, MLP-3, and MVP methods, the proposed DBET method performed better in terms of accuracy. Particularly, the DBET method could improve accuracy on downstream tasks with a limited number of categories (such as EAGLE dataset) compared to full fine-tuning method. Furthermore, the results show that DBET-single method achieves the second-best performances while requiring less trainable parameters and training faster than the DBET-multi method. DBET-single has a shorter iteration time per step, indicating that it generally offers an advantage in inference speed. This makes it suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments. At the same time, on tasks with a small number of categories, its accuracy is very close to that of DBET-multi. ## Efficiency study
The model training time and convergence speed denote key factors in evaluating the effectiveness of tuning methods. For the purpose of a comprehensive efficiency analysis, this study selected the EAGLE dataset for Task A, ensuring consistency across all methods by using the same device. The accuracy results of all the methods are shown in Fig. 4(a), where it can be observed that the proposed DBET-multi method outperformed the other methods, achieving the best performance and requiring only 36 epochs to achieve convergence, which was significantly less than that of the full fine-tuning method. Meanwhile, the proposed DBET-single method performed second best in terms of accuracy, requiring only 24 epochs to achieve the convergence. This indicated that the DBET-single could be a valuable option for applications with strict memory or time constraints. The number of epochs required for model convergence of different methods is presented in Fig. 4(b). The convergence of each model was assessed by evaluating the training time for one epoch, as illustrated in Fig. 4(c). The results clearly indicated that the proposed method not only improved accuracy but also helped the model to converge rapidly.Fig. 4The efficiency results of different tuning methods: (a) the training process accuracy; (b) the convergence epochs; (c) the total training time. ## Ablation and sensitivity study
To verify the effectiveness of the distribution bias token, a set of ablation experiments are conducted on the Rareplanes dataset in this paper, using a ViT detection model pretrained on the COCO dataset. In the ablation study, we compared the number of trainable parameters and accuracy performance of two bias tokens with different dimensions when combined with the four different insert strategies. Specifically, in 12 layers implementation which is identical to DBET-multi, bias tokens are inserted into all 12 layers; in 6 layers implementation, bias tokens are inserted into the first 6 layers; in 1 layer implementation which is identical to DBET-single, bias tokens are only inserted into the first layer; and in 0 layer implementation which is identical to Leaner, no bias token is inserted into any layer. ## Conclusions
This study proposes an innovative DBET method for ViTs in remote sensing object detection. The proposed DBET method performs the PEFT to adapt pretrained ViT models to downstream tasks with minimal computational overhead. In addition, by introducing learnable bias tokens into the input space of the ViT backbone, the proposed method can effectively address the distribution bias between the pretrained and downstream datasets, enabling the model to align better with the specific characteristics of remote sensing imagery.
The proposed DBET method reduces the number of trainable parameters during the fine-tuning process, ensuring a faster training speed while maintaining high accuracy. In addition, the DBET method can be seamlessly integrated with various pretrained models and backbone architectures without altering the original computational pathway, allowing it to be applied to a wide range of downstream tasks. Compared to the full fine-tuning approach, the proposed DBET method can not only achieve competitive performance but also outperform the other parameter-efficient methods in terms of accuracy, even improving the mAP value on datasets with limited target categories.
In conclusion, the proposed DBET method offers a computationally efficient and effective solution for adapting the pretrained ViT models to remote sensing tasks. By reducing the number of trainable parameters while maintaining high accuracy, the proposed DBET method can address the problems in fine-tuning large models in resource-constrained environments, paving the way for more efficient and scalable remote sensing applications.
