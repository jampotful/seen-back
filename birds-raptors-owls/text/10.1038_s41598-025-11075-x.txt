# An ensemble of deep learning models with falcon optimization assisted diabetic retinopathy diagnosis on retinal fundus images

## Abstract
AbstractDiabetic Retinopathy (DR) is a prominent results of diabetes mellitus that causes abnormalities lesions in retina. If not identified at early, it may progress to complete loss of vision. Unfortunately, DR is an irreversible, and treatment only sustains existing vision. Timely detection and accurate treatment of DR can considerably decrease the chance of blindness. Manual diagnosis of DR in retinal fundus images (RFIs) by ophthalmologist is time consuming, costly and laborious tasks with a higher risk of misdiagnosis. Recently, Deep learning (DL) has gained popularity and shown remarkable performance particularly in medical image analysis and classification. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are increasingly being used as a DL approach in medical image analysis, and they are very efficient. This manuscript offers the design of Falcon Optimizer with Ensemble of Deep Learning Algorithm Assisted Diabetic Retinopathy Diagnosis Model (FOEDLA-DRDM) system on RFIs. The FOEDLA-DRDM system employs a Wiener filtering (WF) based preprocessing approach to eliminate noise from images. Following this, FOEDLA-DRDM system leverages the SE-DenseNet method to generate the feature vectors. For DR recognition FOEDLA-DRDM system applies an ensemble approach that combines - AutoEncoder, long short-term memory (LSTM), and deep belief network (DBN). Finally, Falcon Optimizer (FO) adjusts the hyperparameter values of the ensemble approach, giving rise to classification efficiency. The FOEDLA-DRDM system is validated by simulating it on a Kaggle DR dataset, with results being measured according to various criteria. ## Introduction
Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) is a dominant factor of visual deterioration in diabetic patient, resulting from changes in retinal capillary structure1. This process begins with damaging the blood vessels in the photo-sensitive tissues that also grow at the backside of the eyes. Once DR achieves its advanced level, eventually leading to completely lost. On a global scale, the sightlessness level of DR is nearly 2.7%. No symptoms occur until it attains the serious stages2. In the coming years, diabetes patients need routine retina check-ups to detect DR in its initial stage, manage their advancement, and avoid sightlessness threats. The risk components of DR are divided into modifiable factors such as glucose levels, arterial hypertension, and tobacco use, as well as non-modifiable factors namely age, genetic predisposition, and disease duration3. The retina is composed of elements such as macula, fovea, optic disc (OD) and blood vessels, experiences DR in two forms: Non-Proliferative DR (NPDR) and proliferative DR (PDR). Damage to the retinal blood vessels results in NPDR, leading to the liquid leakage that causes the retina to become swollen and moist4. Lesion in NPDR phase may involve Haemorrhages (HMs), Inter retinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA), Micro-aneurysms (MAs), and Exudates (EXs). In PDR, the retina forms new, irregular blood vessels because of low oxygen levels caused by damaged vessels, leading to the severe vision loss5.
The development of computer aided diagnosis (CAD) is prominent area of research in medical imaging, enabling the detection and study of early-stage abnormalities through image analysis6. During CAD processing, low-contrast medical images are enhanced using different imaging methods, highlighting key details crucial for disease detection7. CAD techniques have been quite helpful for the examination of RFIs. Regular examination and promoted treatments have reduced the likelihood of severe eye issues. Hence, misdiagnosis has been avoided, and overall time, expense and efforts have been decreased by employing a CAD diagnostic system8. Considering the previous era, the deep learning (DL) method has developed and accepted in various fields specially in medical image investigation. DL accurately detect features from input information for categorization or partition and usually overtakes all standard image investigation methods9. DL algorithms are not required to remove the handmade features though which need wide information for the training stage. On the other hand, ML methods need to removal of the hand-made features, but they don’t require wide information for the training phase. Consider the DL applications which include the detection, segmentation, retrieval, registration, and classification of the images. Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is a kind of DL technique that is very active, effective, extensive, and applied for image scrutiny10.
The main advancement and noteworthy contribution of this research are listed below.
A unique Falcon Optimizer with Ensemble of Deep Learning Algorithm Assisted Diabetic Retinopathy Diagnosis Model (FOEDLA-DRDM) system is designed.
Weiner filtering (WF)-based image preprocessing approach is performed to eradicate the noise.
FOEDLA-DRDM system exploits the SE-DenseNet approach to derive feature vectors.
For DR recognition, the FOEDLA-DRDM system an Ensemble of three classifiers – AutoEncoder (AE), long-short-term memory (LSTM), and deep belief network (DBN) models.
Falcon Optimizer (FO) model adjusts the hyperparameter values of the ensemble technique effectively and leads to improved categorization performance.
Kaggle dataset is used to test the simulation validation of the FOEDLA-DRDM system, and the results are evaluated using a variety of metrics.

## Literature review
Hemanth et al.11 introduced a new DL technique named the He Weighted Bi-directional Long Short-term Memory (HWBLSTM) method. The gathered fundus imageries primarily undergo pre-processing utilizing a Hybrid Gaussian Filter and probability density Function-based Gamma Correction (HGFPDFGC) method. Later, it divided the retinal lesion of the BV and OD deleted images through the Improved Grasshopper Optimizer-based Region Growing Algorithm (EGORGA). At last, categorization is implemented by using the HWBLSTM method. Fang and Qiao12 developed a new DAG network method for the identification of DR depends on multi-feature fusions of fundus imageries. Primarily, 3 medical signs of DR were mined by various methods: retinal varices, fundus neovascularization, and retinal hemorrhagic plaque. These 3 features sent to the identification method based on a new DAG system for performing feature learning and multi-feature fusions. At last, the enhanced method of classification was employed to classify and recognize DR. Ramesh and Sathiamoorthy13 proposed an Automatic DR recognition technique by applying the Glowworm Swarm Optimizer (GSO) with DL (ADR-GSODL) method. The presented ADR-GSODL technique forced MF as a preprocessing stage. Moreover, this method employed the NASNetLarge technique for obtaining the GSO, and a feature vector was used for parameter fine tuning. The Variational Autoencoder (VAE) method was employed for the DR identification approach.
Kumari and Saxena14 addressed the classifications of retinal imageries (S-0 to S-4) based on the ranges of DR by a CNN made clearly for retinal fundus imageries. To enhance the RINet features performance of classification from intermediated layers were removed. These mined features were presented through layer visualization. For the localization of illness, Grad-CAM was combined at final convolution layers, efficiently emphasizing the important areas for each phase in the image. In15, fundus imageries are employed for the recognition of the developing phases of DR by 2 suggested techniques, each containing 3 methods. The first presented technique utilizes GoogLeNet with ResNet-18 and with SVM. Oulhadj et al.16 introduced a novel automated DR recognition technique that was based on DL. The above-mentioned method comprises 2 major stages: an early pre-processing stage where the deformed register was used by the retina to capture the whole imagery and remove the effects from the background by the classifying method. Another stage was the identification stage in which we trained 4 CNN methods (Xception, Resnet-50, Inception-v3, Densenet-121) to identify the phase of DR. Shoaib et al.17 proposed a new technique that provided more accuracy in DR analysis by using enhanced DL methods. The main concept of this method was transfer learning (TL), which involved the usage of well-established, pre-existing methods, Especially Inceptionv3 and InceptionResNetv2 for extractions. Simultaneously, a novel developed method specifically DiaCNN was also offered, that was personalized for the identification of eye infections. Rachapudi et al.18 presented a method that contains 4 stages. Later histogram equalization, contrast limited adaptive HE (HE and CLAHE), and pre-processing were employed. Later, the phase eliminates the optic disc (OD) and it was completed by Circle Hough Transform (CHT). Next, the Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) was employed for feature extractions. Finally, the feature were categorized by the Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) by a Butterfly Optimizer Algorithm (DNN-BOA) classification which was utilized to classify the numerous phases of DR. To meet the unique requirements of medical image denoising, Annadurai et al.19 created an Efficient Transfer-Learning-Based Fractional Order Image Denoising Approach in Medical Image Analysis (ETLFOD) that combines transfer learning with fractional order techniques. It does this by utilizing pre-trained models like DenseNet121. This method has improved denoising efficiency without sacrificing important picture features. Panneerselvam et al.20 have created a multifaceted method for skin cancer prediction that combines dermoscopic images and clinical information. To extract important visual characteristics from dermoscopic pictures, they have employed transfer learning approaches in conjunction with pre-trained convolutional neural networks, such EfficientNetB3. Additionally, they have used TabNet to handle clinical metadata, such as medical history, gender, and age. To improve forecast accuracy, the characteristics gathered from the two fusion approaches have been combined.
Existing literature limitation in automation and early detection capabilities of current DR diagnostic systems, Ophthalmologists’ manual diagnosing process is expensive, time-consuming, and prone to error. Although deep learning (DL) has demonstrated potential in medical image analysis is efficient methods are required to identify DR from RFIs with high accuracy. This entails tackling the difficulties associated with feature extraction and refining DL models for this particular purpose. By creating a unique deep learning-based method called FOEDLA-DRDM system that combines ensemble learning and optimal feature extraction to enhance DR diagnosis with high accuracy, the work that is being presented seeks to close this gap.

## Materials and methods
In this manuscript, we present the development of FOEDLA-DRDM system on retinal fundus images (RFIs). The RFIs are automatically analysed by the proposed FOEDLA-DRDM system to identify and categorize DR. The FOEDLA-DRDM system comprises WF-based image preprocessing, SE-DenseNet-based feature extractor, the ensemble of three classifiers namely AE, LSTM, and DBN for DR detection and classification, and FO-based hyperparameter tuning. A Falcon Optimizer fine-tunes the setup, and the result is a DR severity prediction.
In our methodology, we deliberately selected a combination of deep learning models for the ensemble to capitalize on their complementary strengths in diagnosing DR. The AE is employed to compress features, which is vital given the high-dimensional nature of RFIs. The LSTM is incorporated to capture subtle patterns over time or across image segments. Lastly, the DBN enables the system to identify complex, nuanced features that may be indicative of early-stage DR. Together, these models form a robust ensemble that addresses various aspects of feature learning, leading to improved diagnostic accuracy when combined with the overall system.

## Feature extraction process
Subsequently, FOEDLA-DRDM system exploits the SE-DenseNet approach to derive feature vectors. Owing to the restrictions on the general performance of ML and rule-based techniques, we have opted to use a DL method. For the extraction of features, we utilize the backbone system of a DenseNetl69 structure, which includes a channel attention mechanism22. The SE block in DenseNet introduces a channel-wise attention mechanism. It first “squeezes” global spatial information into concise channel descriptors using global average pooling, and then “excites” the channels by learning weights that emphasize more informative features. In retinal image analysis, this mechanism helps the network focus on important structures like vessel patterns and lesions while downplaying irrelevant background information, thereby enhancing overall feature extraction and improving classification performance. DenseNet enhances the data flow and gradient among the layers of neural networks by associating feature mapping from every previous layer with a similar dimensional. The SE module‐based channel attention module permits the system to adjust the significance of dissimilar feature channels and permit it to concentrate on distinctive features. ## Ensemble learning
The FOEDLA-DRDM system uses a majority voting ensemble of three classifiers, including AE, LSTM, and DBN models, for DR recognition. By choosing the class that gets the most votes from each classifier, the final prediction is decided. This uncomplicated strategy has been chosen because to its ease of use and ability to effectively utilize the ensemble members’ variety.

## AE classifier
After attaining a huge set of vectors equivalent to sub-images, an AE network is trained on them23. An AE is a kind of NN model that was invented to map the assumed input to its vital feature, which permits the original input to be most precisely reconstructed. The crucial features are generally low‐dimension and denoted as latent variables. An AE contains 3 parts such as encoder, latent space, and decoder. In the latent space, the encoder reduces an assumed input to its latent variables. The decoder rebuilds the input data from the latent variables. As an unsupervised learning method, the training of an AE does not need labels. The loss function usually evaluates the MSE among the reconstructed output and original input. ## Conclusion
We have outlined a FOEDLA-DRDM system to RFIs in this manuscript. The FOEDLA-DRDM system as novelty incorporated Wiener filtering (WF)-based preprocessing, SE-DenseNet feature extraction, and an ensemble of AutoEncoder, LSTM, and DBN classifiers. Falcon Optimization (FO) is used for hyperparameter tuning. The simulation results demonstrate the system’s success, achieving a high average of 99.82%. Notably, the system’s 99.27% it can reduce false negatives, which is essential for early DR diagnosis. Additionally, the system’s low processing time of 2.41 s, when compared to alternative approaches, highlights its efficiency and suggests that it may find practical use in clinical situations. The model may be integrated into medical workflows as a decision-making tool boosting early DR detection and decreasing diagnostic mistakes. Its refined ensemble method enhances accuracy, facilitates quicker screening, and is suitable for deployment even in resource-limited settings. This makes it a workable way to increase accessibility and diagnostic effectiveness in the treatment of DR. There are possible directions for future study, even if the FOEDLA-DRDM system shows encouraging findings in the classification of DR. To increase the system’s effectiveness, methods such as model compression might be used to reduce its computational complexity. To confirm its wider application, future studies will also concentrate on improving system interpretability and expanding the system to additional medical image datasets. looking at ways to improve the interpretability of the system and applying the system to additional medical picture datasets to confirm its wider applicability.
