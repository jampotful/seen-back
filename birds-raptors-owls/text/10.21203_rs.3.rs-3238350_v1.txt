Abstract
The utilization of Hawk-eye technology is a reliable method to ensure the fairness and precise while playing. However, due to the high hardware cost such an
application in amateur badminton matches is nearly impossible. To address this
problem, this study proposes a convolutional neural network (CNN) based on
the feature pyramid structure for badminton place’s detection. The model incorporates the improved MobileNetV3 structure with the CBAM mixed attention
mechanism, the improved CSP structure designed to reduce redundant computation, and reach low memory access through redundant feature information. Its
objective is that we aim to develop a lightweight model. Furthermore, we have
incorporated the NWD loss function and frame difference to improve the accuracy
of the model. Morphological processing is then employed to divide the outline of
the badminton with the boundary line of the field and determine whether it is out
of bounds. The model is experimentally evaluated on the badminton dataset created by us. The results indicate that the precise reaches 82.8%, and the map0.5
reaches 76.8% of the compared YOLOv5 algorithm. Furthermore, the GFLOPs
and parameter quantity of the proposed model are reduced to 34.9% and 50.46%
of the compared YOLOv5 algorithm. Based on which we designed a challenge
system for amateur badminton enthusiasts. Our lightweight Hawk-Eye challenge
system has much lower hardware costs than the existing Hawk-Eye Challenge
system, and is flexible in hardware deployment.
Keywords: Badminton, Hawk-eye, MobileNet, YOLO, Inter-frame Difference

1 Introduction
Hawk-Eye, officially named the ”Instant Replay System” was developed by engineers at Roke Manor Research Limited in Hampshire, England in 2001. Its purpose
was to provide referees with a tool to make accurate decisions during sports matches,
including cricket, tennis, and badminton. The system tracks and records the ball’s trajectory and displays graphic images of its path. The technology is patented by Paul
Hawkins and David Sherry, with ”Hawk-Eye” deriving from Hawkins’ name. The system utilizes 8 to 10 high-speed cameras, four computers, and a large screen to confirm
whether a ball has crossed the boundary line or landed on it. If a player or coach
believes that a decision regarding the ball is incorrect, they can appeal to the referee
and request the use of the Hawk-Eye technology. The application of Hawk-Eye ensures
fairness and precise in sports matches, enhancing their watchability and entertainment
value.

Fig. 1 An overview of our lightweight Hawk-Eye Challenge.

The principle of Hawk-Eye technology is not complex but requires precise calculation and processing. Firstly, the computer divides the three-dimensional space of the
playing field into millimeter-measured units. Then, multiple high-speed cameras are
set up to capture the basic data of the ball’s flight trajectory from different angles
simultaneously. When the player serves or the ball lands, the cameras transmit the
captured images to the computer for analyzing the ball’s trajectory through image
processing algorithms. Next, through 3D reconstruction technology, the player, net,
and field elements are established, and the computer generates a three-dimensional
image show the movement trajectory using the data. Finally, using real-time imaging
technology, the system presents the three-dimensional rendered scene model, motion
path, and landing point to the audience and referees on a large screen. The Hawk-Eye
technology enables referees to judge controversial decisions and make correct decision
during the game.

However, the use of Hawk-Eye technology in amateur badminton matches has
limitations. Not only requires high-quality camera equipment and computer vision
technology, but also accurate data processing and analysis ability [1]. Factors such as
field size and shape, player, and audience position also need to be considered to ensure
the system’s precise and stability. Moreover, professional technicians are required to
operate and maintain the system, increasing the cost of usage.
As the physical fitness and skill level of amateur badminton enthusiasts improve,
the ball’s speed also increases, making it increasingly challenging for referees to make
judgments with the naked eye. Therefore, it is necessary to introduce the substitutions of Hawk-Eye system in amateur matches. On that account, this paper proposes a
convolutional neural network (CNN) based on the feature pyramid structure for badminton detection, by which to build a lightweight Hawk-Eye system for badminton
amateurs. The paper uses an improved MobileNetV3 BNeck structure and partial
convolution to reduce the network’s parameters and memory access. We also design
a loss function that is more suitable for detecting small targets like badminton and
weakens the sensitivity of traditional IoU loss functions to position deviations in small
object detection. We construct a lightweight Hawk-Eye challenge system for amateur
badminton enthusiasts, which achieves badminton detection, landing frame discrimination, badminton contour segmentation, etc. We simplify the research work as a
flowchart shown in Figure 1. The contributions of the paper are as follows:
1.We propose a lightweight CNN for the detection of badminton shuttlecocks
accurately and efficiently.
2.We redesigned the CSP structure based on the idea of redundant information to
accelerate processing speed
3.We integrate frame difference into the neural network for reducing interference
from background environment during detection and redundant computing.
4.We implement a lightweight Hawk-Eye challenge system for amateur badminton
enthusiasts, which integrates badminton detection, falling point frame discrimination,
badminton segmentation, etc.

2 Related Works
Currently, research on the Hawk-Eye system mainly falls into two categories:
traditional tracking algorithms which based methods and deep learning algorithms
methods. However, these studies often start from a single domain, such as target
detection, trajectory tracking, and contour segmentation.
Hawk-Eye systems based on traditional tracking algorithms usually use optical
flow or differential methods to capture the motion trajectory of the ball and judge
whether the ball is out of bounds through target feature extraction. For example, Fan
et al.[2] proposed a method based on an enhanced frame difference method, Sengar
et al.[3] used frame difference method and W4 algorithm to overcome foreground
ghosting problem, Xin et al.[4] proposed based on four inter-frame differential method
and optical flow algorithm to improve the processing speed of optical flow algorithms
and reduce the impact of environmental factors such as lighting. Li et al.[5] proposed
algorithm improves the classic Gaussian mixture background models to remove the

3

undesirable subtraction results due to sudden illumination change. Hui Ma et al.[6]
proposed a robust automatic calibration algorithm for badminton courts.
Hawk-Eye systems based on deep learning algorithms capture the position, tracking trajectory, and instance segmentation of the ball through neural network learning.
Badminton detection is essentially the detection of small targets[7]. With continuous research on deep learning and computer vision, several two-stage algorithms such
as R-CNN[8], Fast R-CNN[9], Faster R-CNN[10], and SPPNet[11] have been developed since 2014. However, two-stage algorithms are relatively inefficient and cannot
meet real-time requirements. In contrast, single-stage algorithms do not require candidate region classification, so they run faster but with slightly lower precise. Common
single-stage object detection algorithms include YOLO[12–15] and SSD[16]. Many
studies have been conducted to comprehensively improve the detection performance
of small targets, including: Modifying the backbone network of the network, Attention
Mechanism, Data Augmentation.
Modifying the backbone network of the network: The backbone network[17–23]
is crucial as a target feature extractor. Depending on the performance of different
backbone networks, corresponding modifications can be made. For example, Li Hang
et al.[24] used skip connections to reduce the number of parameters, accelerate the
propagation of features in the network, and alleviate the problem of gradient disappearance. Zheng Qiumei et al.[25] used 1×1 and 3×3 alternating convolutions to
avoid information loss caused by downsampling operations, focusing on shallow feature
information and removing redundant convolution layers. In addition, WANG[26] proposed an improved feature pyramid model AF-FPN to enhance network information
extraction capabilities.
Attention Mechanism: The introduction of attention mechanism is an effective
method to improve the feature expression ability of small targets. Through channel attention mechanism[27] and spatial attention mechanism[28], neural networks
can focus on channels and regions that are relevant to the task, and then allocate
appropriate weights to improve the model’s attention and recognition ability to small
targets.
Data Augmentation: Data augmentation can also improve the robustness and precise of small target detection. Geometric transformations, color
transformations,Mosaic[15], Mixup[29], Cutout[30], Cutmix[31], Fmix[32], GAN[33],
Autoaugment[34], and other methods can increase the diversity of training data,
thereby improving the model’s generalization ability and recognition precise.
For the Hawk-Eye challenge, it is also necessary to track the trajectory of the ball
and determine the landing point. For example, Huang et al.[35, 36] proposed TrackNet
network, which is trained not only to recognize the ball image from a single frame
but also to learn the flight mode from continuous frames. Wei et al.[37] used dynamic
Bayesian network to predict the position of the tennis ball’s landing point to achieve
the judgment of the landing area. Ahmed et al.[38] established an Hawk-Eye detection
system based on the Mask R-CNN model to achieve segmentation of the target.

3 Methodology
In CNNs, as the network depth increases, the model can acquire richer target feature information, thereby improving the precise of target prediction. However, this
deep network is also prone to losing target location information, especially for small
targets. Therefore, researchers widely adopt the FPN+PAN[15] structure to improve
the performance of convolutional neural networks. FPN[39] is a top-down feature
pyramid that enhances the entire pyramid’s information by passing strong semantic features from higher layers. However, enhancing semantic information alone is
not enough to solve the problem of losing target location information, so PAN[40]
introduces a bottom-up pyramid structure on the basis of FPN to supplement lowlevel localization features. Studies have shown that the FPN+PAN structure performs
extremely well in terms of precise.

Fig. 2 The FPN + PAN structure.

Although FPN+PAN has shown a significant advantage in improving precise, in
practical applications, such as mobile devices and embedded devices, computing power
is often limited, so there is a need to build a small and efficient model. This paper
aims to design a small and fast model based on the MobileNetV3 structure and an
improved CSP structure that reduces redundant memory accesses. Figure 3. shows
our network structure.

3.2 Imporoved CSP Structure
The Cross-Stage Partial (CSP) structure[47] is a common neural network module
designed to enhance the network’s feature extraction and generalization capabilities.
By introducing auxiliary branches, the CSP structure can better capture features of
different scales and perform well on multiple tasks and datasets. Its structure is shown
in Figure 3(b).
However, as the network becomes deeper, the drawbacks of the CSP structure are
also apparent describe as follows:
1.High computational complexity: The CSP structure requires multiple convolution
operations on the feature map, resulting in high computational complexity, requiring
significant computing resources and time.
2.Large memory access cost: The branch structure in the CSP structure requires
more space to store its results, including the weights and biases contained in each
branch, as well as a series of intermediate results stored in memory for updates and
calculations during backpropagation. When the number of branches increases and the
structure becomes more complex, it will lead to an increase in memory access costs.
As more modules similar to the CSP structure are introduced, it increases frequent memory access, and even if the total number of parameters decreases, it may
not accurately translate into a reduction in network latency. In addition, the relationship between network latency and FLOPs (total number of operations) and FLOPS
(operations per second) are not a simple linear relationship.

3.3 LOSS
Small objects are difficult to detect and recognize accurately by models due to their
sizes. The sensitivity to objects of different scales varies greatly based on the Intersection over Union (IoU) loss function. As shown in Figure 7, for small objects, slight
positional deviations lead to a significant decrease in IoU, which causes inaccurate
label assignment.

Fig. 7 The IoU between large targets (left) and small targets (right) at the same deviation.

9

To reduce the sensitivity of IoU to positional deviations of small objects, a loss
function based on Wasserstein distance is devised, namely Normalized Wasserstein
Distance (NWD)[50]. Wasserstein distance is a method for measuring the difference
between two probability distributions. The basic idea of Wasserstein distance is to
view a probability distribution as a collection of points whose mass (i.e., probability)
is distributed over the entire space. It measures the distance between two distributions
by computing the minimum cost of transforming one distribution into another.
For small objects, foreground pixels and background pixels are concentrated at the
center and edges of the BBox, respectively. To give the center pixel of the BBox the
highest importance while gradually decreasing the importance of the outer expansion
area, the weights of different pixels in the bounding box are measured. First, the BBox
is re-modeled as a two-dimensional Gaussian distribution, and the IoU between the
predicted and true boxes is transformed into two Gaussian distributions. Secondly, a
design is proposed that uses Wasserstein distance to measure the distribution distance
between the two Gaussian distributions.
For the shape of a badminton, an ellipse is more suitable for the two-dimensional
Gaussian distribution projection of the BBox, and the center has the highest weight.
As the weight gradually decreases from the center to the edge, the distribution function
is best set as an ellipse. Assuming that the rectangular BBox is (x0 , y0 , w, h),(x0 , y0 ) is
the center of the rectangle, (w, h) is the length and width of the rectangle, the formula
for constructing the inscribed ellipse of the rectangle is:

3.4 Inter-frame Difference
Inter-frame difference[51] is a motion detection method based on pixel changes.
It can detect moving objects by comparing pixel value differences between adjacent
frames. When the pixel difference between two frames exceeds the set threshold, it can
be considered as object motion at that pixel. By inter-frame difference of all pixels,
the contour and position information of the moving object can be obtained. However,
it is susceptible to threshold setting, changes in lighting, and motion speed.

To train the badminton detection algorithm, we created a badminton dataset
named badminton-dataset, which includes 6,500 images with high diversity. Figure 10.
shows some sample images from the badminton-dataset. The size of the dataset has

4.3.1 The Lightweight Hawk-Eye Challenge System for Amateur
Badminton Players
We have devised a Hawk-Eye Challenge System for amateur badminton enthusiasts, which consists of an acquisition terminal, a control terminal and a service
processing terminal. The service processing terminal is used for processing the video
captured by the acquisition terminal. By sending control commands through the control terminal, the video captured by the acquisition terminal is transmitted to the
service processing terminal for frame-by-frame video processing, including badminton
detection, trajectory analysis, and judgment of whether the shuttlecock is out of
bounds. The final landing point of the shuttlecock is determined and the result is
returned to the referee. The acquisition terminal can be divided into two parts, as
shown in Figure 12: (a) is a Raspberry Pi collection node equipped with a high-speed
camera and (b) is a smartphone running a WeChat mini-program. A smartphone can
be placed at the back boundary line of the badminton court as the shuttlecock moves
relatively slowly at this position. A Raspberry Pi collection node with a high-speed
camera can be placed at the sideline of the badminton court as the shuttlecock moves

4.3.2 Comparison of Common Convolution Methods
We compared five types of convolutions, including Conv, Depth-Conv, GSConv,
PConv, and DCNConv, by conducting 4000 computational tests on them with a batch
size of 8, an image size of 2562 , input and output channels of 128, and a convolution
kernel size of 32. The Cp channel of the partial convolution was set to one-quarter of
the channel to obtain the total time consumption, average time consumption, FLOPs,
and parameter count of each convolution type.Assuming the convolution kernel size is
K, Cin is the number of input channels, and Cout is the number of output channels,
the computational complexity of a regular convolution for an output feature map of
size Wout ×Hout ×Cout can be expressed as follows:
Wout × Hout × Cout × Cin × K2 .

(20)

For deep convolution, it only performs convolution operations on each channel of the
input feature map separately, which means each channel has a different convolution
kernel. The computational complexity of each deep convolution can be expressed as
follows:
Wout × Hout × Cin × K2

(21)

For partial convolution, it only performs convolution operations on a portion of the
input feature map’s Cp channels, and the computational complexity of each partial
convolution can be expressed as follows:
Wout × Hout × Cp2 × K2

(22)

From Table 2, it can be seen that both partial convolution and deep convolution
significantly reduce the computational and parameter complexity compared to traditional convolution, by a factor of 1/16 and 1/8, respectively. Specifically, both partial
convolution and deep convolution adopt a local perception approach that only considers effective pixels in the input data, enabling the network to greatly reduce its
parameter count and achieve lightweight network design. Meanwhile, we replace the
C3 module in the 17th, 20th, and 23rd layers of the network structure with CSPFast
based on YOLOv5s, YOLOv5m, and YOLOv5l to verify the improvement of CSPFast
in terms of parameter count, GFLOPs, and computation time (in milliseconds), as
shown in Table 3, which demonstrates that our CSPFast module can save a significant
amount of GFLOPs and parameters, especially when the network is deeper and wider.

The trajectory of a shuttlecock falling to the ground is relatively simple and fixed.
Figure 16. shows the annotated trajectory position of the detected shuttlecock center.
We can use the change in slope of the shuttlecock’s falling trajectory to determine
the landing frame. During the shuttlecock’s descent, the slope of its falling trajectory
changes with its state. Therefore, when the shuttlecock is about to land, the slope of
its falling trajectory will change dramatically. By detecting the slope change, we can
determine the landing frame of the shuttlecock. Then, we can perform morphological
processing on the shuttlecock in that frame to determine if it is out of bounds. By
dividing the shuttlecock’s contour with the boundary line of the field, we can determine
if the shuttlecock is out of bounds.

5 Conclusion
This paper proposes a convolutional neural network (CNN) designed with feature pyramid structure for badminton detection. The model combines MobileNetV3
architecture and an improved CSP structure that reduces redundant computation and
memory access to create a small and fast model. The main part of the network introduces the BNeck structure with CBAM attention mechanism to compensate for the
traditional BNeck’s lack of spatial information. To address the problem of high similarity and redundancy in feature map information, the CSPFast structure based on
the partial convolution idea is proposed to replace the traditional CSP structure. Furthermore, the loss function based on the combination of NWD loss and IoU loss is
redesigned to improve detection performance for small badminton targets.
Experimental results show that our model achieves comparable precise, recall,
mAP0.5, and mAP0.5:0.95 with the original YOLOv5s model, reaching 82.8%, 68.2%,
75.8%, and 48.4%, respectively, while reducing GFLOPs and parameter count to 34.9%
and 50.46%, meeting the requirement for lightweight network structure. Moreover,
badminton detection, trajectory analysis, and landing point judgment are integrated
into a single system to meet the needs of amateur badminton players. Our lightweight
Hawk-Eye challenge system has much lower hardware costs than the existing HawkEye challenge system, and our lightweght Hawk-Eye challenge system is flexible

proposed model.
