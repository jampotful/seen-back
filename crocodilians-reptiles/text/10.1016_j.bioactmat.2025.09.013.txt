# Snake-fangs-bioinspired intelligently-responsive two-stage AIE gen-containing microneedle-based platform for integrated photodynamic infected-wound management and visual therapeutic diagnosis

## Abstract
The efficient treatment of prevalent drug-resistant (DR) bacteria-infected wounds caused by the clinical use of conventional antibiotics remains a significant challenge. Here, a well-integrated intelligent drug-releasing, photodynamic therapy, and visual therapeutic diagnosis strategy combining burgeoning aggregation-induced emission luminogen (AIE-gen) and microneedling technology is innovatively proposed for advanced infected-wound management. This strategy is based on an intelligently responsive two-stage AIE-gen-containing microneedle-based platform (AIE-film/TS-MN) with a double-layer structure. The uppermost layer comprises an on-demand pH-responsive AIE-photosensitizer (AFPP)-based film (AIE-film). This layer is designed to achieve the precise release of reactive oxygen species (ROS) or nitrocefin, which responds to β-lactamase in DR bacteria. The basal layer is a two-stage microneedle (TS-MN) patch with a “core-shell” structure bio-inspired by snake fangs, providing outstanding anti-migratory origins and acid-responsive drug-releasing properties through crustaceous gelatin loaded with Fe-carbenicillin frameworks (Fe-MOF). AFPP and nitrocefin in AIE-film accurately respond to acidic wound-microenvironment and β-lactamase, colorfully changing from yellow and green to orange and red, respectively. This indicates that AIE-film/TS-MN exhibits significant potential as a novel biosensor to record the color-change semiquantitatively at wound-sites through smartphone analysis, visually achieving significantly infected wound-diagnosis/-assessment to facilitate the therapeutic efficacy. Additionally, the smart multiple drug-releasing synergistically eliminates DR-bacteria, including carbenicillin from Fe-MOF and AFPP-activating ROS. Comprehensive in vitro and in vivo experiments confirm that the telemedicine AIE-film/TS-MN offers advanced integrated therapeutic effects for active infected wound management. Additionally, it reduces antibiotic abuse and provides an innovative and promising strategy for visual medical diagnostics and therapeutic interventions.

## Introduction
The skin is an excellent barrier against bacterial infection, however, minor disruptions, including abrasions, punctures, surgical wounds, or pre-existing lesions, can facilitate bacterial entry, resulting in skin infections [1,2]. Bacterial infections in skin wounds can result in localized tissue damage and systemic infection [[3], [4], [5], [6]]. Antibiotics are typically used to eliminate bacteria, however, the widespread clinical overuse of antibiotics has resulted in drug-resistant (DR) bacteria [[7], [8], [9]]. Unlike drug-sensitive (DS) bacteria, DR bacteria produce β-lactamase, which hydrolyzes and breaks the four-membered β-lactam ring in antibiotics, making the drug ineffective [[10], [11], [12]]. This resistance significantly complicates treatment, prevents wound healing, and increases the risk of further complications. Consequently, early detection and timely treatment of bacterial infection can significantly reduce the risk of subsequent deterioration. However, the treatment of many bacterial infections still has limitations, including systemic contact with antibiotics and frequent administration. This complicates the treatment of bacterial infection wounds, reduces the patient's treatment experience, and prolongs wound healing time.
Emerging microneedle (MN) provide a promising transdermal drug delivery system that can create small pores in the skin to facilitate drug absorption [[13], [14], [15], [16]]. MN is minimally invasive, easy to use, and painless, offering distinct advantages for therapeutic delivery [[17], [18], [19], [20]]. Additionally, MN can be customized in number and length (hundreds to thousands of microns [[21], [22], [23], [24]]) based on specific needs. They can be designed to be long enough to penetrate the dermis or short and narrow to avoid stimulating nerves or piercing blood vessels within the skin [[25], [26], [27], [28]]. However, significant scientific challenges arise when conventional MN-based patches are pressed into the skin, and part of the MN usually pops out, reducing drug delivery efficiency. Therefore, designing intelligent drug delivery MN has attracted significant attention [[29], [30], [31]]. Currently, researchers have turned to natural evolution to address this issue by designing MN structures biomimetically. This next generation of MN significantly improves performance, providing a promising new direction for advancements in the field [32,33].
In the initial stages of infection, the pH at wound sites typically decreases. Accordingly, developing a pH-sensitive drug-delivery system has always been the focus of research for infected wound therapy. Iron-metal-organic frameworks (Fe-MOF) have been used to develop drug-release systems for treating DS bacterial infections due to their acid-base responsiveness [34,35]. A significant scientific challenge in treating wounds infected with DR bacteria is that they are highly resistant to multiple broad-spectrum antibiotics, making them significantly difficult to treat. Compared with traditional antibiotics, photodynamic therapy (PDT) has emerged as an effective alternative for DR bacteria. This is attributed to its distinctive ability to penetrate biofilms and bacterial cell walls, lack of resistance development, inherent optical properties, and anti-inflammatory effects [36,37]. A key component of PDT is the photosensitizer; however, conventional photosensitizers face limitations due to aggregate-induced quenching [38]. In 2001, Benzhong Tang et al. first discovered aggregation-induced emission (AIE) photosensitizers, which exhibited minimal luminescence in dilute solutions and significantly increased emission in the aggregated state [[39], [40], [41]]. AIE photosensitizers efficiently produce reactive oxygen species (ROS) during aggregation or intramolecular rotation, improving their therapeutic efficacy against DR bacteria [[42], [43], [44]].
MN patches have been instrumental in developing antibacterial drugs or nano-killers. However, it is important to acknowledge that integrating real-time diagnostic functions into MN devices is a relatively new development. Integrating diagnosis and treatment in MN has significant potential to create multifunctional tools that can simultaneously detect infection, assess its severity, and effectively eradicate infection. This opens the door to timely and personalized infection management [45]. Herein, a double-layer platform (AIE-film/TS-MN) was developed by integrating an emerging AIE gen-containing film with a two-stage "core-shell" MN for advanced photodynamic infected wound management and visual therapeutic diagnosis (Fig. 1A). The upper layer comprises polyvinyl alcohol (PVA)-based film (AIE-film) containing on-demand pH-responsive AIE-photosensitizer (AFPP) and nitrocefin. This composition provides AIE-film with a precise response to acidic microenvironments and β-lactamase. This enables differentiated monitoring of DS and DR bacteria as they change from yellow-green to orange and red. The basal layer is a TS-MN patch (TS-MN) based on polylactic acid (PLA) with a “core-shell” structure bio-inspired by the venom-injection mechanism of snake fangs. This structure provides exceptional anti-migratory origins and acid-responsive drug-releasing properties through crustaceous gelatin loaded with Fe-carbenicillin frameworks (Fe-MOF) in the MN tips. Due to the AIE-film design, AIE-film/TS-MN achieves precise monitoring of DS and DR bacteria and the wound microenvironments by analyzing RGB values through a smartphone-based color recognition application. This enables remote patient-provider interactions for timely treatment decisions (Fig. 1B). From a therapeutic perspective, AIE-film/TS-MN releases carbenicillin (CARB) from Fe-MOF and ROS from white light-activated AFPP, achieving synergistic antibacterial effects and promoting photodynamic wound healing (Fig. 1C). This intelligent visual medical diagnostics and photodynamic-antibiotic treatment strategy provides a promising avenue for advancing next-generation clinical medicine.Fig. 1(A) The schematic illustrations of AIE-film/TS-MN construction. ## Results and discussion
Design and integration of the intelligent system (AIE-film/TS-MN). The PVA-based AIE-film integrates multifunctional components, specifically, the AIE luminogen AFPP, chosen for its unique aggregation-induced emission properties that overcome conventional fluorophores' ACQ limitation to ensure sustained fluorescence and ROS generation in aggregated states, and Fe-MOF-encapsulated nitrocefin utilizing Fe-MOF's established pH-responsive release characteristics. In order to improve the mechanical properties of MN patch, polylactic acid (PLA) with excellent mechanical properties (Table S1) was selected as the needle body in this study. This system monitors bacterial infection types via dual-response mechanisms: wound acidification triggers AFPP's chromism shifting the film from yellow-green to orange under ambient light with concurrent fluorescence attenuation under UV, while drug-resistant bacteria (e.g., MRSA) induce β-lactamase-mediated nitrocefin release causing a distinct red-shift. Crucially, AFPP maintains efficient ROS production under white light irradiation, enabling targeted MRSA eradication through synergistic photodynamic-antibacterial action.

## Fabrication and characterization of the TS-MN patch
MN was first prepared by a thermoforming process using a polydimethoxysilane (PDMS) template. Subsequently, MN was removed, and a gelatin solution containing Fe-MOF was poured into the mold and pressed towards the needle tips under a vacuum. A 100 μm-thick gasket was placed in the mold, and MN was reinserted. After natural drying, TS-MN was obtained (10 × 10 conical MN arrays were formed on 13 × 13 mm patches) (Fig. 2A). By improving the method reported in the literature [46], an AFPP was successfully synthesized (Fig. 3A) and confirmed by proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR), 13C NMR, and Electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (ESI-MS) (Fig. S1-3). The AIE properties of AFPP were investigated through a fluorescence spectrometer. As shown in Fig. 3B, the fluorescent spectra revealed that in a mixed solution of acetonitrile and water, AFPP exhibited an emission peak near 525 nm when the water fraction (fw) ranged from 0 % to 80 %. However, as the fw increased to 90 %, the emission peak red-shifts to approximately 557 nm, accompanied by a significant increase in fluorescence intensity. Furthermore, with the fw subsequently increasing to 100 %, the emission peak of AFPP red-shifts to around 570 nm, while the fluorescence intensity decreases. A trend chart depicting the relationship between fluorescence intensity and fw is presented in Fig. 3C. AFPP exhibited its maximum fluorescence intensity at fw = 90 %. The insert exhibited the bright orange fluorescence of AFPP under UV light at fw = 90 %. As illustrated in Fig. 3D, the dynamic light scattering indicated that the average diameters (D) of nanoaggregates were 801.9 nm. Additionally, the aggregation process of AFPP was observed through SEM (Fig. 3E). AFPP appeared as highly dispersed fine particles in pure acetonitrile, which gradually aggregated into flakes, and the size increased significantly as the fw continued to increase. These results indicate the typical AIE properties of AFPP, and the proposed mechanism is illustrated in Fig. 3F. In pure acetonitrile, the highly dispersed state of AFPP facilitated significant intramolecular rotation, which dissipated the energy of the excited state, reducing the molecule's non-luminescence. However, as the aggregation state increased, molecular stacking was restricted, and steric interactions with neighboring molecules hindered intramolecular rotation. The fluorescent spectra of AFPP were measured in buffer solutions across a pH range of 3.4–6.8. The emission peak of AFPP at 562 nm gradually increased with an increase in pH from 3.4 to 6.8 (Fig. 4B). A significant linear relationship (y = 41.707x – 133.251, R2 = 0.974) was observed between the fluorescence intensity of the AFPP solution and the pH values. Additionally, as the pH increased from 3.4 to 6.8, the fluorescence color of AFPP solution shifted from colorless to yellow and subsequently to orange (Fig. 4C). Consequently, AFPP holds significant potential for monitoring pH in wounds. To further validate this, AFPP was incubated with E.coli for 4 h, and the fluorescence intensity of the system was continuously monitored. The results indicated that the fluorescence intensity gradually decreased as the incubation time increased from 0 to 4 h (Fig. 4D). A significant trend of decreasing fluorescence intensity at 562 nm with extended incubation time was observed (Fig. 4E). Besides, compared to the initial 0 h time point, the fluorescence color of the system shifted from yellow-green to colorless after 4 h of incubation, and this change was readily visible to the naked eye (Insert in Fig. 4E). AIE-film undergoes a visible color change from yellow-green to orange within 5 min in a buffer solution with a pH of 5.5 (Fig. 4F). Under ultraviolet light, it was observed that as the pH decreased from 6.8 to 3.4, the fluorescence of AIE-film was gradually reduced, transitioning from yellow-green to colorless (Fig. 4G). The recorded fluorescence spectra indicate that AIE-film exhibits a strong emission band around 505 nm at pH 6.8. However, this emission band red-shifts to approximately 532 nm as the pH decreases to 3.4, accompanied by a corresponding decrease in fluorescence intensity (Fig. 4H). Furthermore, analysis of the RGB values revealed a good linear relationship between the G value and pH (y = – 21.444x + 38.407, R2 = 0.998) (Fig. 4I). Additionally, the color of the nitrocefin solution significantly changed from yellow to red as the concentration of MRSA increased, and the absorbance at 486 nm gradually increased (Fig. 4J). Various concentrations of MRSA (0-102 cfu/mL) were dripped into AIE-film, and the change was continuously observed. AIE-film changed its color shift from orange to red with the incubation time from 0 h to 12 h. Besides, the red color intensity deepened significantly as the MRSA concentration increased (Fig. 4K). A significant linear correlation was observed between the G value of AIE-film and MRSA concentration (y = −10.307x + 93.261, R2 = 0.987) (Fig. 4L). Therefore, AIE-film holds significant potential for application in visually monitoring DS and DR bacterial infection types in wounds.
The biomimetic design endows the TS-MN with both superior mechanical strength and exceptional drug delivery efficiency. Upon penetrating the skin, the Fe-MOF embedded in the gelatin layer begins to release CARB precisely at the targeted site. Additionally, even after the patch is removed, the drug-carrying layer remains at the wound site, ensuring sustained drug release (Fig. 5Ai). TS-MN with Rh B as the model drug was pressed into the skin of SD rats and subsequently removed to evaluate the performance. Fig. S8 indicates pinhole marks on the rat dorsum. The skin tissues were then harvested for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining and confocal laser microscopy analysis. TS-MN tips successfully penetrated the skin, with the red model drug Rh B visible, demonstrating that the needle length of TS-MN is appropriately designed to deliver drugs to the dermal layer (Fig. 5Aii and iii). These results further demonstrate that AIE-film/TS-MN possesses sufficient mechanical strength and structural integrity, making it suitable for wound dressing applications. Furthermore, AIE-film/TS-MN provides a selective therapeutic strategy for DS and DR bacteria. The environmental pH value decreases in the early stage of wound tissue infection with bacteria (Fig. 5B). The acid-responsive Fe-MOF was pre-loaded in the Gel@Fe-MOF layer, which penetrated the skin, decomposed and released CARB, thereby binding penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) on bacterial membranes to block cell wall synthesis and induce osmotic lysis, ultimately eliminating E. coli. This suggests that the therapeutic effect is significantly influenced by the successful load of CARB and the MN's ability to penetrate the skin. As shown in Fig. 5C, FT-IR spectrum revealed that compared to CRAB, O–H stretching vibration at 3410 cm−1, C=O stretching vibration at 1765 cm−1, C–O stretching vibration at 1327 cm−1, and C=O bending vibration at 879 cm−1 in Fe-MOF were significantly reduced. This result confirms the successful coordination of Fe3+ with the carboxyl groups in CARB. Furthermore, the loading efficiency of CARB on Fe-MOF, determined through UV–vis spectrometry, was found to be 66.03 %. The regular spherical morphology of Fe-MOF was observed through SEM images (Fig. S9). Besides, the mechanical properties of MN are essential to adapt well to soft and deformable human skin. The conical MN has a suitable geometry (height, base width, and array density) and sufficient mechanical strength to pierce the skin. The transdermal ability of TS-MN primarily depends on the two-stage drug-loaded MN located at the tip of the needle. Considering the pH-dependent degradation performance of iron-based metal-organic frameworks, the decomposition of Fe-MOF under acidic conditions aligns with expectations. The CARB release kinetics of Gel@Fe-MOF at different pH values were studied to evaluate the release efficiency of acid-responsive drugs. Compared with the neutral environment, the release rate of CARB under weakly acidic conditions was faster, indicating that the coordination bond between CARB and Fe3+ is acidic at lower pH, which is beneficial for biological applications (Fig. 5E). This rapid release is particularly advantageous given CARB's relatively short half-life of approximately 1.5 h, as reported in previous pharmacokinetic studies [47]. The pH-responsive release profile of our system, therefore, ensures timely drug availability to match the antibiotic's pharmacokinetic characteristics. Additionally, the drug release kinetics were analyzed through the Korsmeyer-Peppas model, exhibiting a 28 % burst release within 2 h due to rapid coordination bond dissociation at pH 5.5. Korsmeyer-Peppas fitting results indicated that t50 = 9.5 h and n = 0.27, which indicates the diffusion-dominated release mechanism of Fe-MOF (Fig. S10). The images of Fe-MOF solution at different pH values are presented in Fig. 5F. Compared with the colorless Fe-MOF in a neutral solution (pH 7.4), the color at pH 5.5 becomes light yellow, further supporting the coordination of Fe3+ and CARB to decompose under weak acid conditions. The concentration of Fe-MOF in Gel@Fe-MOF was optimized according to the bactericidal activity against E. coli. The bactericidal efficiency increased linearly to 99 % in the range of Fe-MOF concentration of 0.25-2.5 μg (Fig. S11 and 5G). However, the increased dependence remained unchanged when the concentration was higher than 2.5 μg. As a consequence, the optimal doping concentration of Fe-MOF is 2.5 μg.
The therapeutic mechanism of AIE-film/TS-MN in cases where the wound becomes further infected with DR bacteria is illustrated in Fig. 5H, white light irradiation activates AFPP molecules within the AIE-film, generating ROS that eliminate MRSA by disrupting membrane integrity and suppressing ATP synthesis [48]. Accordingly, the high-efficiency ROS generation observed in AFPP aligns well with expectations. The commercial indicator 2,7-dichlorodihydrofluorescein (DCFH) was employed to detect ROS production (Fig. 5I). Under white light irradiation (5 mW/cm2), the fluorescence intensity at 525 nm (I525 nm) in the AFPP/DCFH co-incubation system significantly exceeded that of DCFH alone, demonstrating the superior ROS-generation capability of AFPP. This indicates AFPP's therapeutic potential for drug-resistant bacterial infections. Furthermore, the I525 nm progressively increased with prolonged illumination time and essentially plateaued at 6 min, which is selected for subsequent experiments. Moreover, analyzing the swelling rate of biomedical films is essential for determining material performance in both in vitro and in vivo environments. The introduction of AFPP improved the antibacterial activity of the system to a certain extent. When Fe-MOF loading was 2.5 μg, the system exhibited a more significant antibacterial effect in the presence of AFPP than in Fe-MOF (Fig. S12). Furthermore, after the introduction of AFPP, the colonies exhibited purple under natural light and fluorescence quenching under ultraviolet light, which further proved the color development ability of AFPP to DS bacteria. AIE-film exhibited a swelling rate of up to 204 % (Fig. 5J), with a significant increase in volume post-swelling (Insert in Fig. 5J). This excellent swelling capacity ensures effective adsorption in wound tissue and enhances drug release. Furthermore, the stress-strain curve indicates that AIE-film has an elastic modulus of 2.008 MPa, which is sufficient for practical applications (Fig. 5K). In conclusion, AIE-film/TS-MN possesses superior advantages over existing MN and photodynamic therapy approaches as a result of these demonstrated properties, which uniquely integrate deep tissue penetration, real-time theragnostics, and on-demand drug release within an equipment-independent platform (Table S3).

## In vitro cell proliferation and migration behavior evaluation of the AIE-film/TS-MN
To determine the potential of AIE-film/TS-MN for in vivo research, its biocompatibility was evaluated through cell compatibility, flow cytometry, scratch assays, and blood compatibility tests. Live/dead cell staining and the CCK-8 assay were utilized to determine the materials' cell viability and proliferation potential at each step. CHL cells were co-cultured for five days with MN, TS-MN, PVA@AFPP, AIE-film, and AIE-film/TS-MN, respectively. Live/dead staining images revealed that the four groups exhibited almost no dead cells except PVA@AFPP (Fig. 6A). Additionally, CCK-8 results indicated that cell viability increased significantly when nitrocefin and TS-MN were loaded onto PVA@AFPP, likely due to the partial hydrolysis of gelatin into amino acids and proteins, which provided additional nutrients for cell growth. OD values indicate no significant difference from that of the control group (Fig. 6Bi), with the calculated cell viability for each group exceeding 90 % (Fig. 6Bii), indicating that AIE-film/TS-MN construct developed in this study exhibits strong biocompatibility. Moreover, the impact of materials prepared at each stage on cell migration was evaluated using a scratch assay (Fig. 6C). After 24 h of incubation, scratch closure of all sample groups exhibited no significant difference from the control group. The blood compatibility of materials at each step was evaluated through hemolysis assays, using PBS and Triton as negative and positive controls, respectively. MN, TS-MN, PVA@AFPP, AIE-film, and AIE-film/TS-MN supernatants appeared light red, with the precipitate at the bottom of the tubes, comparable to the negative control (Fig. 6D). In the Q4 quadrant of all groups, AIE-film/TS-MN maintained high cell viability, with 100 % of cells remaining non-apoptotic, further confirming its excellent biocompatibility. In summary, AIE-film/TS-MN developed in this study demonstrated a significant ability to enhance cell proliferation and migration, highlighting its potential to accelerate wound healing. Potential biotoxicity risks were evaluated through histopathological analysis and biochemical parameter measurements of hepatic, renal, and splenic tissues. The wound images, profiles, and wound areas indicated that in E. coli-infected and MRSA-infected rats, the AIE-film/TS-MN treatment group exhibited significantly smaller wound areas (Fig. 7D-E). In the E. coli-infected group, the wound healing rate with AIE-film/TS-MN reached 99.69 % by day 10, compared to only 63.53 % with PBS treatment. In the MRSA-infected group, the wound healing rate with AIE-film/TS-MN was as high as 99.80 % by day 10, whereas PBS treatment exhibited a rate of only 69.20 % (Fig. S14). Additionally, wound tissue samples surrounding the infection sites were collected for quantitative bacterial enumeration. Both E. coli and MRSA-infected rat models exhibited significantly reduced bacterial counts in the AIE-film and AIE-film/TS-MN treatment groups compared to the controls, confirming the potent antibacterial efficacy of our integrated platform (Fig. S15). These findings confirm that AIE-film/TS-MN treatment exhibits strong therapeutic efficacy against wounds infected with E. coli and MRSA, highlighting its significant potential for clinical application.

Anti-inflammation property and promoted angiogenesis of the AIE-film/TS-MN in vivo. A short-term inflammatory response is conducive to the healing of the infected wound. However, the long-term inflammatory response will cause tissue damage; accordingly, the healing process stagnates in the inflammatory period, delaying wound healing. Additionally, angiogenesis is a key activity in the wound healing process. New blood vessels deliver nutrients and oxygen to the wound surface and remove waste products and carbon dioxide, expediting wound healing. Consequently, the ideal wound dressing should have anti-inflammatory and angiogenic properties. To evaluate the anti-inflammatory and angiogenic properties of AIE-film/TS-MN, on day 10, immunofluorescence staining on platelet endothelial cell adhesion molecule-1 (PECAM-1/CD31), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin 10 (IL-10) were used to regenerate skin tissue to evaluate angiogenesis efficiency and inflammatory response (Fig. 9A–B). For E. coli and MRSA infection group, the green fluorescence of CD31 in the AIE-film/TS-MN group was the strongest (Fig. 9Ai and ii), while the green fluorescence of TNF-α was the weakest (Fig. 9Bi and ii). The semi-quantitative analysis results indicated that CD31 and IL-10 expression in the AIE-film/TS-MN group was significantly higher than in other groups (Fig. 9Aiii, iv, and S16). However, TNF-α expression was significantly lower than in other groups (Fig. 9Biii and iv), suggesting that AIE-film/TS-MN could significantly promote angiogenesis in regenerated skin tissue and shorten the inflammatory response time. Limitations and Prospects. While the technology of photodynamic therapy utilizing 5 mW/cm2 white light irradiation is well-established, several limitations persist. First, the penetration depth of broad-spectrum white light is constrained due to strong scattering and absorption effects at shorter wavelengths, potentially compromising therapeutic efficacy for deeper lesions. Second, although the low power density minimizes thermal damage, it introduces notable trade-offs: inadequate singlet oxygen generation, extended irradiation durations required to achieve therapeutic outcomes, and the potential induction of tumor cell sublethal resistance. Additionally, the broad-spectrum nature of white light may also non-specifically activate endogenous chromophores. These limitations serve as a critical reference for clinical transformation and also direct our current research endeavors, which involve the development of narrow-spectrum illumination systems and combined treatment strategies to enhance the profundity and specificity of their application. Furthermore, RGB analysis chromaticity standardization of smartphones is susceptible to ambient light interference. In the subsequent work, a standard colorimetric strip is required to be embedded in the AIE-film to standardize the imaging conditions.

## Conclusions
In summary, a double-layer system consisting of an AIE-film and a two-stage "core-shell" MN achieved the highly complex differential monitoring and on-demand treatment of DS and DR bacteria. AFPP and nitrocefin loaded in the upper film respond to DS and DR bacteria with orange and red colors, respectively, enabling real-time visual monitoring of these two bacteria. By analyzing the RGB value of the film using a smartphone, patients at home can communicate remotely with doctors, who can then analyze the type and degree of infection and provide tailored solutions for subsequent bacterial inactivation. Fe-MOF in the MN shell decomposes under acidic conditions to release CARB, which inactivates DS bacteria with more than 94 % antibacterial activity. The upper film can release ROS under white light irradiation to inactivate DR bacteria. Furthermore, the device's biocompatibility, antibacterial effect, and reactive oxygen production effect have been verified with satisfactory results. In vivo experiments indicated that the device can heal wounds within 10 days. This telemedicine device alleviates the emergence of antibiotic abuse in clinical practice, saves medical resources, and provides innovative strategies for contactless communication between doctors and patients.
