# Voice samples, round 2

Revised 13 October 2026 at 22:00 after the owner's notes on round 1, then put into US English. The owner approved this voice for now at 22:13 and will revisit it; its rules are `docs/views/VOICE.md` on `main`. The same five views and three rows each, rewritten. The grade marks are the sheet's own: `m` known in this animal, `g` known in a relative, `i` speculative, `x` left out. No row in `views/` has changed.

## Jumping spider (`jumper`)

- `m` **Eight eyes.** You have eight eyes. Six of them watch for movement all around you. When they see something move, you turn to face it so your two big front eyes can take a close look.
- `m` **The front eyes.** Each of your big front eyes sees sharply only within a narrow upright strip, about as wide as a human thumb held at arm's length and ten times as tall. The strips of your two front eyes cross in an X. If you are a Phidippus, you see about a sixteenth of the detail a human sees through them. If you are a Portia, you see about a fifth.
- `i` **Putting it together.** Nobody yet knows how your brain combines what your eight eyes see.

## Dog (`dog`)

- `m` **Sharpness.** Your eyesight is much blurrier than a human's. You see a quarter or less of the fine detail a human sees.
- `m` **Sharpest spot.** If you have a long nose, like a grayhound, you see most sharply along a band level with the horizon. If you have a short nose, like a pug, you see most sharply in one spot in the middle.
- `m` **Color.** You have two kinds of color-sensing cell, where humans have three. A blue-green that looks like a clear color to a human looks gray to you.

## Bee (`bee`)

- `m` **Red.** You have no color-sensing cell for red, so red flowers and other red things look dark to you.
- `m` **Flicker.** If you are a honeybee, you can see a light flicker even when it flashes 200 times a second. Humans stop seeing flicker at about 60 flashes a second, so a lamp that looks steady to a human can look like it is flashing to you.
- `m` **Colors.** Like a human, you have three kinds of color-sensing cell. Yours pick up ultraviolet, blue and green, where a human's pick up blue, green and red. Ultraviolet is light that humans cannot see at all.

## Owl (`owl`)

- `m` **Sharpness.** You see much less fine detail than a human does. If you are a barn owl, you see less than a tenth as much. The other owls measured so far see about a third as much.
- `m` **Hearing.** You can find and catch prey by sound alone. If you are a barn owl, you can hear sounds about a hundred times fainter than other birds can.
- `x` **Where sounds come from.** If you are a barn owl, you can tell where a sound comes from to within a few degrees, about the width of a human thumb held at arm's length. You are less exact about whether a sound is above or below you.

## Fungus (`fungus`)

- `g` **No eyes.** You have no eyes. In two molds that have been studied closely, the red bread mold and a pin mold, cells sense how much light there is, above all blue light, and the pin mold's stalk bends toward it. You very likely sense light in much the same way.
- `m` **Up and down.** Your stem grows upward and your gills hang straight down, so your spores fall clear. If something knocks you over, your stem bends until its top points straight up again within a few hours, and your gills turn back toward straight down. In the gray shag, the stem starts to bend within half an hour.
- `g` **Pace.** The stalk of a pin mold starts to bend toward a light within about five minutes. You very likely respond to light over minutes as well, where an animal with eyes responds in a fraction of a second.

## What changed

- **Rows describe the animal and nothing else.** Every sentence about the app, the phone, the screen or the drawing is gone, since the grade mark beside each row already tells the reader what is speculative or left out. Two rows existed only to explain the drawing. The bee's color row now states the bee's three colors, a fact measured in honeybees (346, 430 and 540 nm in the bee log), so it is graded `m`. The fungus's color-and-time row had nothing left about the fungus once the app's settings went, so it is cut, and the sheet's Pace row takes its place.
- **The dog's color row is `m`.** Round 1 called its count unchecked; the cat-dog log has it from Neitz, Geist and Jacobs, 1989, which also places the gray blue-green near 480 nm.
- **Humans are "humans" or "a human".** "Person" and "people" are gone, since the app does not assume that only humans are persons.
- **Literal verbs and plain nouns.** Eyes see, cells sense, stems bend. Nothing "answers" light, eyes do not "report", and adjectives are not turned into nouns ("the surround").
- **Numbers have a job.** A number stays when it lets the reader compare (200 flashes a second against 60), picture something (a thumb at arm's length for 2 or 3 degrees) or be impressed (half an hour for a mushroom), and it is rounded to what a reader can hold. Otherwise it goes: the barn owl's 10,000 vibrations a second told the reader nothing without a comparison, and its 20 decibels became "a hundred times fainter", which is the same figure in sound energy. Common names come first (gray shag for *Coprinopsis cinerea*, red bread mold for *Neurospora*), and a figure stays with the species it was measured in.
- **One correction.** The jumper's front eyes do not swing toward a target. They are fixed in the head, the retinas move inside them, and the spider turns its body to face what moved.

## The voice for the pass over every row

Every row is about the animal and speaks to it as "you". Humans are "humans" or "a human", never "people" or "a person". A row never mentions the app, the phone, the screen or how the picture is drawn; the grade mark carries that. Verbs are literal and sentences are complete and direct, at the level of a museum panel read by all ages: plain words, real names for body parts, nothing dumbed down. A row opens with something the reader can picture and runs one to three sentences. A number stays only when it helps the reader compare, picture or be impressed, rounded to what a reader can hold, and a figure measured in one species arrives as "If you are a barn owl". An `m` row states its claim flatly. A `g` row names the relatives and says "you very likely". An `i` row says plainly what nobody knows. An `x` row states the ability. Rows never praise, hedge or exclaim.

## Open for the owner

- **The closing sentence.** Round 1 let a row end with one sentence on what the view does ("This view is blurred to match"). Round 2 drops it everywhere, reading "meta statements" to include it. If only the disclaimers were meant (the app's choices, the screen's limits), each row can end again with one plain sentence on what the picture shows.
- **The false-color key.** With the bee's color row about the bee, nothing in the rows tells a reader that the bee's green shows as red on the screen. A small legend on the picture would carry it outside the rows.
