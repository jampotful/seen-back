The DOI did not resolve through doi.org in this session, but the Lund record lists it.
- **Petie, Garm and Hall 2016**, *Frontiers in Zoology*, doi:10.1186/s12983-016-0174-9. **Corrected.** The record exists (https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/en/publications/crown-of-thorns-starfish-have-true-image-forming-vision gives volume 13, article 41; https://bmc.altmetric.com/details/11872531 gives the DOI; doi.org redirects it to link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12983-016-0174-9). - **Petie, Hall, Hyldahl and Garm 2016** (added), "Visual orientation by the crown-of-thorns starfish (*Acanthaster planci*)", *Coral Reefs* 35(4): 1139 to 1150. **Confirmed** (abstract, https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/en/publications/visual-orientation-by-the-crown-of-thorns-starfish-iacanthaster-p): "approximately 250 ommatidia in each adult compound eye", "average interommatidial angles of 8°", "a narrow, horizontal visual field of approximately 100° wide and 30° high", only animals with intact vision oriented to "a nearby coral reef" and blinded animals walked in random directions. No distance is given. The page shows no DOI. Kirwan and colleagues (2018) cite this paper for starfish vision.
- **Live Science, 8 January 2014.** **Confirmed** (https://www.livescience.com/42393-starfish-eyes-vision.html). The author is Denise Chow. Garm is quoted: about 200 pixels; "With these starfish, we're talking about one to two hertz"; blinded animals walked "at the same pace but in random directions"; *Linckia laevigata*; grayscale vision. The same article quotes Garm giving 30 to 40 hertz for people, while the row uses the app's 60.
- **Yerramilli and Johnsen 2010**, *J. Exp. Biol.* 213(2): 249 to 255, doi:10.1242/jeb.033159. **Confirmed** (https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/734169; doi.org redirects to the JEB article 213/2/249): 39 urchins; no apparent response to the 6.5° target; strong axial orientation to the 10° target; spine density predicts 8.3 ± 0.5° (interambulacrum) and 11 ± 0.54° (ambulacrum).
- **Ullrich-Lüter and colleagues 2011**, *PNAS* 108(20): 8367 to 8372, doi:10.1073/pnas.1018495108. **Confirmed** (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3100952): up to 140 photoreceptor cells per tube foot and up to 200,000 per animal; rhabdomeric opsin Sp-opsin4; the calcite skeleton shields one side; adults show negative phototaxis with the maximum at 450 nm; the whole animal "rather like a huge compound eye" is offered as a model.
- **Kirwan and colleagues 2018**, *J. Exp. Biol.* 221(14): jeb176271, doi:10.1242/jeb.176271. **Confirmed** (https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/ws/files/163630297/jeb176271.full.pdf). Its introduction reports starfish vision through "optic cushions" at the arm tips, and its discussion calls the starfish vision "coarse spatial vision". It cites the *Coral Reefs* paper of Petie and colleagues, not the *Frontiers* one.
- **Palmer and colleagues 2017**, *Science* 358(6367): 1172 to 1175, doi:10.1126/science.aam9506. **Confirmed** (https://weizmann.ac.il/molgen/gur/sites/molgen.gur/files/2024-10/Palmer%20et%20al%202017.pdf): *Pecten* "possesses up to 200 minute eyes lining the mantle tissue"; mirror of 20 to 30 layers of crystals; images on both retinas; angular resolutions of about 5° on both.
- **Speiser and Johnsen 2008**, *J. Exp. Biol.* 211(13): 2066 to 2070, doi:10.1242/jeb.017038. **Confirmed** (https://fds.duke.edu/db/aas/CMAC/faculty/sjohnsen/publications/228576): *Argopecten irradians*; about 2°; 1.5 mm (3.4°) particles gave more gaping than 0.6 mm (1.4°) or none; 2.5 and 5 cm/s gave more than 10 cm/s.
- **Land 2003**, *Proc. R. Soc. - **Grawunder and colleagues 2015**, *Sci. - Section 4 says the rhopalium of *Aurelia* carries a gravity-sensing organ (Nakanishi and colleagues, 2009). The audit could not open that abstract, and the checked sheet says so.
- Live Science's author was not recorded and is Denise Chow.
- The rows' wording passes the brief's rules: Canadian spelling, no em dashes, no "not X but Y", no fragments, nothing said to matter, no praise. "Color" appears only inside quoted titles.
