# Audit of the bat sensing sheet

Sheet audited: `bat.md` (views `bat` and `flyingfox`). Corrected sheet: `bat.checked.md`. The audit ran from 04:51 to 05:20 Eastern on 12 October 2026, against `BRIEF.md` and `AUDIT.md`.

How the pages were read. Shell access to frontiersin.org was refused by the proxy (HTTP 403), so every page was read through WebFetch, which returns a summary with short verbatim quotes. Each figure below was matched against a verbatim fragment of its source unless the line says otherwise.

Pages refused during the audit: https://doi.org/10.1159/000508863 (Karger, HTTP 429, rate limited); https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00359-011-0639-4 (HTTP 429); https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1001150 (HTTP 429); the Europe PMC search service (HTTP 429); https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14660703/ and the Semantic Scholar search service (permission requests went unanswered). Other copies were found for the first two and the last; the PLoS Biology paper and the abstract of Müller and colleagues 2007 stay unread.

## Rows

`bat`

1. Calls (`m`). **Kept.** The three big brown bat rates and the "snapshots" phrase match Moss and Surlykke. A hold of 110 ms and a thrust of 30 ms make 140 ms, about 7.1 snapshots a second, inside 5 to 10. Wilson's figures match.
2. Distance (`m`). **Kept.** Figures and species match. The 15 cm is the paper's own rounding of 800 μs, and "or worse" fits "more than".
3. Reach (`g`). **Reworded.** The grade stands, since the ranges were calculated for two other calling bats from measured inputs. The text gave the higher-threshold insect ranges (2 to 6.5 m) and then said nothing is heard beyond them; at the paper's lower threshold the ranges reach 14 m. The new text gives about 2 to 14 m, and the source line names both thresholds and credits the 21 m to Holderied and colleagues 2005.
4. Direction (`m`). **Kept.** Both figures are for the big brown bat, and the 70 degrees is in `docs/SOURCES.md` line 77.
5. Sharpness (`g`). **Kept.** 0.6 cycles per degree in Daubenton's bat, and a hundredth is right. Daubenton's bat is a vespertilionid, so `m` could be argued; `g` claims less and is left.
6. Dim light (`m`). **Kept.** Big brown bat; 0.00079 rounds to 0.0008. It rests on Boonman's report of Ellins and Masterson 1974, which neither the sheet nor the audit opened.
7. Colour (`g`). **Kept.** The cone share and the ultraviolet cones match Müller 2009, and the losses match Simões and Brokaw. "No phone records ultraviolet" rests on `docs/SOURCES.md` (Vasas and colleagues 2024), which the source line did not name; it is added.
8. Echoes (`x`). **Kept**, with the source line corrected (the Bristol post) and the Digital Biology page added. The ecoObs and Bristol statements concern iPhones and iPads, and the Android page covers the rest; no source opened covers every phone model.
9. Magnetic compass (`x`). **Kept.** The Nature abstract matches.

## The app claim

The claim holds. In `src/cam.r.js`, the table is read top to bottom and the first match wins (lines 3 and 4: "First match wins, so the order settles "fox squirrel", ..."; line 27: `for (var i = 0; i < LEX.length; i++) if (LEX[i][1].test(t)) return LEX[i][0];`). No entry before `dog` matches "flying fox", and line 20 does:

```
    ['dog', /\b(dogs?|pupp(y|ies)|fox(es)?|coyotes?|wol(f|ves))\b/],
```

Line 8 sends "bat" to `other` through `\bbats?`. I copied lines 6 to 29 into a scratch file and ran names through the same loop: "flying fox", "flying foxes", "grey-headed flying fox", "Indian flying fox", "spectacled flying fox" and "flying-fox" all return `dog`; "bat", "fruit bat", "Jamaican fruit bat" and "straw-coloured fruit bat" return `other`; "megabat" and "Pteropus poliocephalus" return nothing.

The typed-name path is in `src/cam.c2.js` (read only): line 325 takes a species from the region's list when the typed text equals a listed English or scientific name exactly (`src/cam.rec.js` line 610), line 326 otherwise calls `kindOfName(t)`, and line 331 reveals any key other than `other`. The Alachua list holds no pteropodid, so a typed "flying fox" is shown with the dog's eyes. The sheet's fix, a new entry placed before `dog`, is right.

Two things the sheet's plan leaves out. The `other` entry on line 8 comes before every animal entry and holds `\bbats?`, so it would catch "bat" and "fruit bat" unless that word is taken out of it or the new entries go above it. And `KINDS` on line 53 decides whether a meeting counts as a new kind and how many kinds a person has met (lines 100 and 190 to 194); a `bat` or `flyingfox` key left out of it would not count. `src/cam.rec.js` line 15 keeps a parallel `KIND_ORDER`, said to follow `tools/fusion/fuse.py`.
