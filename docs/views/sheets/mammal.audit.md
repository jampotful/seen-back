Audited on 12 October 2026, from 14:51 to 15:34 EDT. Every source below was opened in this session unless the line says otherwise.

## Sources

- Jacobs and Deegan, 1992. **Confirmed.** Springer page (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00223965): title, authors, *J Comp Physiol A* 171(3): 351 to 358, ERG flicker photometry, "the raccoon and kinkajou are monochromatic", the coati a dichromat with an added cone class "close to 433 nm". No peak is given for any species' middle-to-long cone, as the sheet says.
- Jacobs and Williams, 2010. **Confirmed.** Springer page (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00359-010-0519-3) and eScholarship (https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5141d6jc): 196(5): 379 to 384, DOI right, "a single cone pigment having peak absorption at 561–562 nm", no evidence of a short-wavelength pigment in the ERG, two South American marsupials with two cone opsin classes. The rod value of about 493 nm is in the Discussion on the Springer page, not in the abstract and not on the eScholarship record.
- Neitz and Jacobs, 1989. **Confirmed.** Cambridge page (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/visual-neuroscience/article/spectral-sensitivity-of-cones-in-an-ungulate/1BC07A10BDF59B9D630C7A73B5837CAB): *Visual Neuroscience* 2(2): 97 to 100, DOI right, domestic pig, "two classes of cones" at 439 nm and 556 nm.
- Heyward and colleagues, 2021. **Confirmed** at the University of Wisconsin page the sheet names: all six authors, *Anat Rec* 304(3): 662 to 672, DOI right, "L/M cones outnumber S cones by at least 3:1", a cone-dense area centralis dorsotemporal to the optic nerve, dichromatic vision predicted. The publisher's page was not opened by me either.
- Levenson and colleagues, 2006. **Confirmed** at the openpolar record the sheet names (https://openpolar.no/Record/ftcdlib:qt8sg757mn): six authors right, S cones of the polar bear and otter inferred near 440 nm, M/L cones inferred at 545 to 560 nm, inferred from opsin sequences. The record names no journal, as the sheet says.
- Ebeling, Natoli and Hemmi, 2010. **Confirmed, with one addition.** ANU record (https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/05feeda9-948e-49bd-b553-d9873a67ffb5): "confirmed that the wallaby has only two cone types", 20 to 25% of dunnart cones unlabelled, the quokka, quenda, honey possum and fat-tailed dunnart earlier described as trichromatic. The Curtin record (https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/12990?show=full) gives *PLoS ONE* volume 5, issue 12, now added. I did not confirm a DOI.
- Hemmi and Mark, 1998. **Confirmed.** Springer page (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s003590050264): 183(3): 379 to 387, DOI right, behavioural acuity "about 4.8 cycles/deg", anatomical upper limit "about 6 cycles/deg".
- Veilleux and Kirk, 2014. **Confirmed.** Karger PDF (https://www.karger.com/bbe/article-pdf/83/1/43/3925878/000357830.pdf): 83(1): 43 to 53, DOI right, western grey kangaroo 11.2 cycles per degree, an anatomical estimate; *Didelphis virginiana*, *Sus scrofa* and *Choloepus didactylus* in the anatomical list with no value in the text.
- Shyan-Norwalt and colleagues, 2010. **Confirmed.** Butler record (https://butler.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/initial-findings-on-visual-acuity-thresholds-in-an-african-elepha/): five authors right, *Zoo Biology* 29(1): 30 to 35, DOI right, one African elephant cow at 48 minutes of arc (2.75 cm at 196 cm), Asian elephants 8 to 11 minutes of arc "near the tip of the trunk" at about 2 m from earlier unnamed studies.
- Emerling and Springer, 2015. **Confirmed.** eScholarship PDF (https://escholarship.org/content/qt8w19d5kp/qt8w19d5kp.pdf): *Proc R Soc B* 282(1800): 20142192, DOI right, *D. novemcinctus* a rod monochromat, PDE6C inactivated in both *Choloepus* species and *B. tridactylus*, anteater SWS1 pseudogenic with rod monochromacy still to be tested, *Manis pentadactyla* SWS1 inactivated "but all other cone phototransduction genes are functional".
- Jacobs, 2013. **Confirmed.** Cambridge page (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/visual-neuroscience/article/losses-of-functional-opsin-genes-shortwavelength-cone-photopigments-and-color-visiona-significant-trend-in-the-evolution-of-mammalian-vision/B1DF60C9E19B91EF3DC2BBB09E04F95F): 30(1 to 2): 39 to 53, DOI right, "SWS2 and LWS in monotremes", "the modal pattern for mammals is to have two types of cone photopigment", SWS1 lost in "several dozen species from five mammalian orders".
- *Comparative morphology of the tapetum lucidum (among selected species)*, 2004. **Confirmed except the authors.** PubMed (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14738502/): *Vet Ophthalmol* 7(1): 11 to 22, DOI right, retinal tapetum in marsupials, choroidal tapetum cellulosum in carnivores, no tapetum in the red kangaroo and the pig. **Authors not confirmed.** The PubMed page as fetched lists none, and the Wiley page, the Crossref record and the PubMed summary service were all refused.
