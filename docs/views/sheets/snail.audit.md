## Sources

- Irwin, Williams, Speiser and Roberts, 2022: **confirmed, with a correction**. Opened the Scholar Commons record (https://scholarcommons.sc.edu/biol_facpub/319) and the full text PDF (https://scholarcommons.sc.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1316&context=biol_facpub). The paper gives a behavioural minimum resolvable angle of 1.06 degrees, defined as twice the width of the narrowest checks that drew a response (0.53 degrees), an anatomical 2Δφ of 1.04 degrees (Δφ 0.52), an eye 1.2 mm across and a Michelson contrast threshold of 0.07. It gives 52 degrees (behaviour) for *Arion rufus*, 3.6 degrees (anatomy) for *Littorina littorea* and 0.94 degrees (2Δφ, anatomy) for *Lobatus raninus*. The published version cites these by author and year: Zieger, Vakoliuk, Tuchina, Zhukov and Meyer-Rochow (2009, *Acta Zoologica* 90: 189 to 204), Seyer (1992, *J. Exp. Biol.* 170: 57 to 69) and Seyer (1994, *J. Exp. Zool.* 268: 200 to 207). The sheet's reference numbers 69, 56 and 57 are not in the version I read, and I could not open the bioRxiv preprint they probably come from. The red slug's primary paper is now named. The journal PDF (https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-pdf/225/16/jeb243927/2159231/jeb243927.pdf) repeats the definition of αmin as "twice the angular width of the smallest check to which animals responded", states 2Δφ only for *Lobatus*, and labels the periwinkle's 3.6 degrees "anatomical data" without saying whether it is Δφ or 2Δφ.
- Serb and Eernisse, 2008: **confirmed, with a problem in the review itself**. Opened the Springer page (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12052-008-0084-1) and the PDF (https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s12052-008-0084-1.pdf). Volume 1, issue 4, pages 439 to 447. Table 1's column is "Resolution/inter-receptor angle (Δ, °)" and gives *Helix aspersa* 14.5, "*Cepaea nemorais*" 8.0, *Littorina littorea* 4.5 and *Strombus luhuanus* 0.23, with the references the sheet names. The only footnote marks the planarian value as an acceptance angle, and the text never says which measure the other values are. The review's reference list gives Hamilton and Winter (1982, *Animal Behaviour* 30: 752 to 760) the title "Behavoural responses to visual stimuli by the snail, Littorina irrorata", so the source it names for the garden snail's 14.5 degrees is a study of a different snail. The four eyed classes are named on the Springer page as Gastropoda, Bivalvia, Polyplacophora and Cephalopoda, so tusk shells are not among them. The ocellus count for *Onithochiton neglectus* (411 to 1,472, citing Boyle, 1969) and the aesthete sentence are confirmed.
- Speiser, Eernisse and Johnsen, 2011: **confirmed**. Opened https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/778613. The abstract gives "about 9°-12°" and a response to black overhead circles of 9 degrees with no response to an equal uniform dimming, in *Acanthopleura granulata*.
- Duke Today Staff, 2011: **confirmed**. Opened https://today.duke.edu/2011/04/chiton.html. It says "Hundreds of tiny lenses", disks of 3 cm or larger 20 cm above the animals, and "West Indian fuzzy chitons".
- Kingston, Chappell and Speiser, 2018: **confirmed**. Opened the JEB PDF (https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-pdf/221/19/jeb183632/1903976/jeb183632.pdf). *Chiton tuberculatus* oriented to static lateral targets of 10 degrees (only 10 and 30 degrees were offered), and the Discussion gives "≤10 deg". It responded to a 27 degree target in 81% of trials and to a 17% dimming in 94%, with no difference between them (P = 0.25). The taxa, the aesthetes and the *A. granulata* eye (about 80 µm, about 100 photoreceptors) are as the sheet says.
- Liu, Sigwart and Sun, 2022: **confirmed**. Opened https://publikationen.ub.uni-frankfurt.de/files/83424/2022.12.12.520088v1.full.pdf. "restricted to only a few genera, in the family Schizochitonidae and the family Chitonidae", with Acanthopleurinae and Toniciinae named.
- Matsuo and colleagues, 2023: **confirmed, with a caution**. Opened https://bmcbiol.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12915-023-01789-7.pdf. Electroretinogram peaks of 480 nm (dark-adapted) and 420 nm (light-adapted) in *Limax valentianus*, opsin maxima near 456, 475, 500 and 470 nm, and ultraviolet sensitivity in Opn5A and Opn5B are confirmed. The paper places all four opsins in the rhabdomere of the "Type-I photoreceptors". That name implies at least one other type, so the sheet's step from "co-expressed" to "no second cell type" is weaker than it reads. I did not check what the other types do.
- Nishiyama, Nagata, Matsuo and Matsuo, 2019: **corrected**. Opened the JEB PDF (https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-pdf/222/14/jeb208595/1909494/jeb208595.pdf). The lens eyes on the superior tentacles and the short-wavelength escape at 10 μW cm−2 are confirmed. The paper locates the light sense of blinded slugs in the brain: head illumination with blue light worked, tail illumination did not, opsin mRNAs were found in the brain, and the isolated suboesophageal ganglia responded to 400 to 480 nm. It treats the skin as less likely. The sheet's "senses through its body" and "sense light through the skin" are wrong for this paper. Intact slugs were tested at both 100 and 10 μW cm−2, so "ten times the light used for intact slugs" is also wrong. Blinded slugs entered the dark in the group test under white light near 503 μW cm−2, and at 100 μW cm−2 their avoidance showed only by head position.
- Shepeleva, 2023: **confirmed**. Opened https://www.ruthenica.net/sites/default/files/2023-06/Vol33_95-104_Shepeleva.pdf. Seven species, eyes 147 to 270 µm, *Cepaea hortensis* 233 µm (from Shepeleva 2006c), relative pupil size 0.5 in six species and 0.6 in *Succinea putris*, defined as pupil diameter over transverse eye diameter. The paper also describes the pupil as round in all seven, which the sheet called its own step.

## Other problems

- The conch's conversion is wrong by a factor of two. Irwin's 1.06 degrees is already a full cycle (two checks of 0.53), so it gives 1 / 1.06 = 0.94 cycles per degree, and 2Δφ = 1.04 gives 0.96. The sheet's "0.5 to 1", "cpd about .5", "15 to 30 times finer" and "blurs over 4%" become about 0.95, about 30 times, and 0.6 / 34 / 0.95 = 1.9% of the long side.
- The periwinkle's upper bound of 0.28 assumes Seyer's 3.6 degrees is a 2Δφ. The text of Irwin I read calls it anatomical without saying which, so the bound stands as an assumption.
- The nudibranch's 0.033 cycles per degree came from the unopened preprint and is removed from the scope table and from the `cpd` row.
- All other conversions recompute: 1 / 29 = 0.034, 1 / 16 = 0.063, 1 / 104 = 0.0096, 1 / 18 to 1 / 24 = 0.056 to 0.042, 1 / 20 = 0.05, 1 / 9 = 0.11, 1 / 0.94 = 1.06. The blur radii (0.50 of the long side at `VIEW_DEG` 34 and 0.71 at 24 for cpd .035; 0.29 at 0.06; 1.76 at 0.01) also recompute. The four parts sum to 2,085,365 and the shares are right.
- The scope bullet on land snails, the picture's "skin's light sense" and the first effect in section 4 repeated the skin error and are corrected.
- Wording: no em dashes, no "not X but Y" and no American spellings outside quoted titles. I found no breach of the other wording rules.
- Not reached: the primary papers behind Table 1, Seyer (1992) for whether 3.6 degrees is Δφ or 2Δφ, Zieger and colleagues (2009) for the slugs' task, a second GBIF count, and the bioRxiv text of Irwin and colleagues. The audit stopped at 15:30 EDT.
