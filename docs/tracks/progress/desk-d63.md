## Advice on yield
1. The fetch helper returns the DOI, authors and character counts reliably from journal pages (Springer, Frontiers, Inter-Research, CSIRO, SciELO, MDPI abstracts when the abstract prints), but never shows search snippets, so every candidate costs a fetch. About one search in four produced a record.
2. Seagrass and submerged-macrophyte shading papers (Inter-Research, Springer Estuaries/Hydrobiologia, Aquatic Ecology) were the best new vein: short sentences, set shade levels, and most species are absent from main. Untried: Halophila stipulacea, Zostera japonica, Ruppia (needs a DOI source), Najas, Potamogeton shading, Ottelia, Stratiotes, Nuphar.
3. Parasitic-plant germination abstracts are mostly long. The short ones come from host-exudate and GR24 species-comparison papers (Weed Research, Journal of Central European Agriculture) and from hormone or GA3 papers on hemiparasites (Turkish Journal of Botany). Orobanche and Phelipanche names split between POWO (Orobanche) and papers (Phelipanche), so confirm the accepted name on POWO before reading the paper.
4. Cactaceae light-germination papers in SciELO often print the key sentence in under 100 characters, but the species is usually on main already through oa-10 compilation rows; search the species on main first.
5. Mangrove and halophyte abstracts usually either lack a DOI (Pakistan Journal of Botany, Gayana Botanica, Anales de Biologia) or pool several species; the Frontiers and MDPI ones are worth a second pass with the full text.
