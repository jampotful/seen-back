## The Ciona species fault (lines 1 to 3)

Every Ciona note says "Animals were Ciona intestinalis type B from the Marine Biological Laboratory (Woods Hole), per the Methods." The Methods name two species. "Ciona robusta (a.k.a., Ciona intestinalis type A) were collected from the Santa Barbara Yacht harbor or were obtained from M-REP (Carlsbad). Ciona intestinalis (type B) were obtained from Marine Biological Laboratory (Woods Hole)." Type B was used for the in situ (HCR) maps, "staged to match the animals used in the connectome study (Ryan et al., 2016)", and for microinjection of one-cell embryos. "Unfertilized Ciona robusta eggs" were electroporated with the reporter constructs (pVACHT>CFP, pVGAT>H2B::RFP), and the key resources table lists the pVGAT>kaede stable line as C. robusta; these carry the coexpression figures (Fig 2a opsin and VGAT reporters in the ocellus; Fig 5a AMPA-receptor and VACHT constructs in the relay neurons). The frimousse mutant and the stable line were cultured at the UC Santa Barbara Marine Lab. The Methods do not say which species the behavioural larvae (phototaxis, dimming, spontaneous swims) were. Line 1's `conditions` "~18 hpf embryos for expression work" is in fact the electroporation step ("Embryos were cultured at 18°C ... until 18 hpf"), that is C. robusta.

So the notes misstate the provenance of a species-level grade-m record whose evidence spans two accepted species, the split the unit was asked to carry. For lines 1 and 2 the glutamatergic and GABAergic identities rest on the type B in situ maps and the connectome (whose reference title names Ciona intestinalis), so the taxon can stand with a corrected note. For line 3 the measured larvae are of unstated species, so a grade-m C. intestinalis record is not supported; it belongs at genus.

Line 3 has two further faults. `conditions` says "light intensity and assay temperature not reported"; the Methods give both ("The 505 nm LED light was mounted to one side to the petri dish at approximately 3000 lux."; "All larvae used were approximately 25 hpf (18˚C)."). And `eye` says "perampanel blocks AMPA receptors in the relay neurons" without the paper's own limit: the AMPA receptor "is expressed in larvae in the two antenna cells, and in a small cluster of neurons in the pBV", and perampanel is given in the sea water, so the block is not confined to the visual relay; the paper's controls (treated larvae still swam; the touch response and the PR-II dimming response were not inhibited) belong beside it.
