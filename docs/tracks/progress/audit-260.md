## Rulings asked for

**UV-B under sense_light_nonvisual (lines 1 and 2).** The owner's ruling that UV counts under sense_light_nonvisual stands, and audit 217 applied it without counting any line on that ground. Under audit 217's reading, regulated responses such as flavonoid accumulation are the best fit for a signal reading of UV, and damage readouts (ROS, membrane peroxidation, Fv/Fm) would be the first to fall if the owner narrowed the ruling. Line 1 (flavonol glycosides and hydroxycinnamic acids up 1.2- to 2-fold) is a regulated response at a dose the paper calls "moderate UV-B, below ecologically summer day doses"; no damage measure is reported. Line 2 (singlet oxygen scavenging of extracts) is neither a damage readout nor a regulated response itself: it is a property of an extract that follows from the flavonoid change. The extract is the assay sample, not the stimulus, so the sense_chemical exclusion of extracts does not apply. Existing rulings admit both lines; not counted. Owner question only if the UV ruling is narrowed to responses that read UV as a signal. In that case line 1 stays and line 2 is the doubtful one, because it is a biochemical property of extracts once removed from the plant's response.

**Cultivar and accession (lines 1, 2 and 6).** Neugart: "rocket salad (Diplotaxis tenuifolia), were cultivated in a controlled environment, in a greenhouse", with no cultivar or seed supplier named; the record says so. An unnamed cultivar is not by itself an error (audit 214). Kale and kohlrabi were correctly not logged. Kusuma: 'Rex' (main, replicated three times in time), 'Green Salad Bowl' (three times) and 'Waldmann's Dark Green' (once, "no statistical analysis was performed"). The paper presents the finding for lettuce in the abstract ("in three cultivars of lettuce"), and the record names the cultivars and their replication in `conditions`, so audit 204's rule is met and the species-level result is what is logged. Not errors.

**Hormone on living seeds under sense_chemical (line 4).** GA3 solutions (0, 0.01, 0.1, 0.5 mmol/L) added to fresh, dormant, living seeds in Petri dishes and incubated in light at 20/30 °C. This is a hormone response of a living organism, the 10(d) case audit 254 confirmed. The seeds are not on a culture medium whose composition is the variable (audit 220's reading of the culture-medium clause), and no extract or cut tissue is involved. Not an owner question; not an error.

**Wind-inferred field comparison under sense_touch (line 5).** Existing rulings point against. Every plant sense_touch line on main is an applied mechanical stimulus with a control: Mimosa pudica touched with forceps (desk-d120), Brachypodium roots probed (desk-d128), Arabidopsis given fan-forced agitation against boxed controls (desk-d157, confirmed in audit 242). The desk itself held wind-loading and static-bending papers out of sense_touch (Populus nigra, Pinus pinaster; audit 216's held-out list). Shiba et al. apply no stimulus. They compare two wild populations collected in the field (Kyonan, coastal; Futako, inland) and do not measure wind at either site. Wind is taken from 2023 annual averages at AMeDAS stations elsewhere (Tateyama 3.40, Kisarazu 2.88, Tokyo 2.75, Fuchu 1.71 m s-1), and Kyonan "is assumed to have higher wind speed values". The sites also differ in soil (Table 1: "coastal humus" and "inland black soil"). Salt spray, light and common-garden growth are not addressed. For genotype the authors cite Nomura et al. (2010), "there had no genetic differentiation among populations", which they did not test. So the stimulus that would make this a touch record is never shown to differ, and the response cannot be separated from other site differences. **Owner question:** whether a field comparison along an inferred wind gradient (with no applied stimulus) may stand as sense_touch. Until the owner says so, line 5 should be dropped. It is counted below on the existing grounds (value outruns the quote; causal reading).

**Which scape number is right (line 5).** The paper cannot settle it. The Results text gives "(SL; p< 0.001, mean; coastal = 434.54, inland = 572.64: Figure 4A, Table 2)", and the Figure 4 caption gives "ncoastal = 21, ninland =30" (tool summary for the caption). Table 2 gives "419.12 ± 19.53 (n = 30) b" and "571.06 ± 17.71 (n = 32) a". Petiole length disagrees the same way: text 320.85 and 362.28, Table 2 332.67 ± 20.43 (n = 22) and 358.89 ± 15.47 (n = 30). The two pairs come with different n. A plausible reading is that the text and Figure 4 use a subset of the plants in Table 2. I have no source for this; it's my own construction, and the paper does not say. A more ordinary explanation fits equally well: one of the two was not updated after a re-analysis, with no subset involved. The corrigendum does not address it. Neither pair can be preferred from the paper. A record should give no means in `value` and carry both pairs, each with its n, in `conditions`. The record picks the Table 2 pair in `value`, and its note gives the text pair without the n mismatch.
