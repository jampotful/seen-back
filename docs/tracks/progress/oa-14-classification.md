## Source inspection

Read Linatoc, Owen and Dalimin (2023), DOI [10.30880/jsunr.2023.04.02.002](https://doi.org/10.30880/jsunr.2023.04.02.002), all 13 PDF pages: introduction, Methods 2.1-2.3, Tables 1-3, figures/captions, discussion, conclusion and references. Inspected rendered PDF pages 4 and 6 to verify table column positions and representative names. Mechanically compared all 165 extracted VOC/EFN pairs to the recovered candidate rows: no missing rows or mismatches. This verifies transcription, not identification or scientific admission. No fresh full-file `quotecheck.py` run is claimed; the full text was extracted with PDFKit rather than the original OA job's layout extractor.

The [publisher PDF](https://journal.uthm.edu.my/index.php/jsunr/article/download/15676/6299/72052) and [NERC repository PDF](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/536623/1/N536623JA.pdf) are byte-identical, 557,930 bytes, SHA-256 `6350b11e15dfbdefb51e20255c39ac29ddada8e456b84bbe287dbe34000e3f31`. The PDF and [publisher landing page](https://journal.uthm.edu.my/index.php/jsunr/article/view/15676) explicitly state CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. No login or proxy was used; no paper text/PDF is committed.

Methods describe plants chosen for dominance at Danum Valley and identification assisted by a resident botanist, followed by name checks using World Flora Online and Tree Flora of Sabah and Sarawak. Attached healthy, expanded leaves were enclosed in a Li-COR chamber; air was sampled for 20 minutes at 150 mL/min onto sorbent tubes and analysed chromatographically, with blanks. Only isoprene and monoterpenes were targeted. The methods report instrumental detection limits but no row-specific replicate counts, sampling dates/times or specimen/voucher identifiers sufficient to resolve the flagged identities. Recorded environmental parameters are not supplied row by row. There is no controlled chemical exposure or receiver assay. Prefer baseline emission with induction untested over claiming experimentally established constitutive regulation.

The introduction/discussion cite other species' defensive and signalling functions; they do not measure those functions for these 165 samples. The discussion explicitly cautions that non-detection does not establish inability to produce VOCs. Table 3 gives aggregate compound-class counts, so its totals cannot assign isoprene versus monoterpenes to an individual positive Table 2 row.

Further internal discrepancies reinforce the need for a source reconciliation: the abstract reports 12 dipterocarps, Table 2 contains 11 (rows 35-45), and the discussion uses 11. Table 3's EFN-bearing VOC totals sum to 33, while Tables 1-2 report 34. Do not silently choose or repair a denominator. No supplementary dataset, correction notice or row-level provenance link was found in the inspected article, publisher landing page or NERC deposit; this does not prove that unpublished data or later clarification cannot exist.

## Taxonomic investigation

[oa-14-taxonomy-check.jsonl](oa-14-taxonomy-check.jsonl) records all 40 original flags, exact GBIF queries/accepted keys, match status, WCVP source taxon keys, deduplicated checklist localities, introduced-range flags and explicit limitations. Kew's site returned HTTP 403; its WCVP distribution records were instead read through GBIF's public distribution API, with `limit=1000` and `endOfRecords=true` checked for every response. This is an independent geographic screening, not a substitute for the project's admission-level name authorities or physical specimen identification.

- Thirty-nine names returned WCVP range records; none included Borneo. The fortieth, Guatteria dumetorum, returned a Colombian regional-checklist locality but no WCVP range entry. Treat that as incomplete range verification, not global absence.
- Nomenclature does not resolve the pattern: GBIF maps Acacia nigrescens to Senegalia nigrescens, Acacia tortilis to Vachellia tortilis, and Nectandra purpurea to Damburneya purpurea. Their retrieved ranges still do not include Borneo. Ficus nymphaefolia receives a **fuzzy**, not exact, match to Ficus nymphaeifolia; retain the source spelling and uncertainty rather than silently correcting it.
- The matching queries use binomials, not vouchers or full author strings. For example, the paper prints Pera arborea with authority Baill., while the returned name has authority Mutis. A binomial match does not resolve that name-use discrepancy. API pagination completeness does not mean a globally exhaustive range inventory.
- The old flag's native-range argument is insufficient. [World Agroforestry's Carapa profile](https://apps.worldagroforestry.org/treedb/AFTPDFS/Carapa_guianensis.PDF), p. 2, explicitly lists Malaysia, Indonesia and Singapore as exotic/planted range. The [Annona profile](https://apps.worldagroforestry.org/treedb/AFTPDFS/Annona_senegalensis.PDF), p. 2, gives African native countries and India as exotic range, without establishing Danum occurrence. WCVP also reports Miconia introductions in Malaya and elsewhere in Asia; Malaya is not the TDWG region Borneo, and an unidentified Miconia cannot be resolved to a species from this fact.
- A checklist's omission of cultivated occurrences can coexist with documented planting. These results support a provenance hold, not a declaration that all 40 identifications are false. The original list is not exhaustive clearance for the other 125 rows.

The source explicitly presents a single-site study. Its citation of Owen et al. (1998) concerns an adapted analytical method, not an attributed multi-region species dataset. The inspected material provides no basis to reclassify Table 2 as a compilation or assert a particular copying source. Misidentification, misapplied names, table assembly and undocumented cultivation/sampling remain possible explanations; their cause cannot be decided from the published record. The necessary resolution is a specimen/sample-to-row crosswalk, vouchers or other auditable identification records and sampling locations, or a publisher/author correction identifying imported data and original sources. No author contact was made.
