- Quotes: line 1 MATCH (Abstract, whole sentence; the Results restate it as "exhibited significantly greater germination (30.7%) compared to the SW (17.2%) and control (14.7%)"); line 2 MATCH (Abstract, whole sentence, follows line 1's sentence); line 3 MATCH (Abstract, whole sentence, follows line 2's). - Ruling, sense_chemical: germination of living seeds in response to karrikinolide, smoke-water and applied GA3 is a hormone/smoke/karrikin response of a living organism, counted by the owner's plant sense_chemical reading (10(d), 10(e); audits 100 and 101). Filing right.
- Conditions confirmed (tool summary unless quoted): "Four replicates with 50 seeds each"; "for 21 d with 10 h light and 14 h dark conditions", with the Figure 1 caption giving "14 h light and 10 h dark", as line 1 says; the Results do not test SW against the control, so line 1's "SW was not distinguished from the control" is right.
- **Counted, lines 1, 2 and 3 (scientific meaning, `conditions`/`note`; cultivar rule).** The abstract refers only to "a celery cultivar"; the Methods name it: "Celery (Apium graveolens L.) seeds (cultivar Oderdorfer, accession No. 09H1000051, variety rapaceum)", from the Czech national genebank at the Crop Research Institute, Praha-Ruzyně. Variety rapaceum is the celeriac group, not leaf or stalk celery. Each record says "One celery cultivar was tested (cultivar not named in the abstract)" although its note says the Methods were read. Under audit 204's standing rule (applied in audit 218), a single-cultivar result is accepted at species rank only when the paper presents the finding for the species (it does: the title and abstract speak of Apium graveolens seeds) and the record names the cultivar in `conditions`. These records do neither: the cultivar, the variety and the single accession are missing, which changes applicability (one celeriac genebank accession, not celery in general). Species rank and grade m stand once fixed. Counted once per record.
- Line 2 context (uncounted): the Results give the 3 h values, "59.5%, 72.0%, and 67.5% germination were achieved in seeds treated with SW, KAR1, and GA3" against 47.5% in the water control; at 6 h "a slight decline" with the control lowest, no figures. "the abstract gives no percentages" is true but the paper does give the 3 h figures, which belong in `conditions`. The 47.5% soaked control against 14.7% unsoaked at 21 d suggests a different scoring period or protocol in the soaking experiment; I could not read the soaking methods, so the record's "same ... germination-table conditions as the 21 d trial" is unverified.
- Line 3 context (uncounted): the Results say germination "reduced dramatically in all treatments" at 12 h, with no figures and no comparison with the unsoaked 14.7%; the record's wording does not overstate this.

### Zhang et al. 2026 (lines 5 and 6), Artemisia vestita and Rumex hastatus, sense_thermal

- Quote: MATCH in Section 4.1 ("Ecological meaning of semilethal temperature and cold-tolerance classes"), whole sentence, values contained, with one typographic difference: the PDF prints "(− 26.87°C)<Rumex" with no space after "<", the record "(− 26.87°C)< Rumex". That adds a whitespace word for check.py (33 words either way under 40). Uncounted, as audit 39 treated a typographic difference; replacement below restores the printed form.
- Methods confirmed (tool summary): 7-day preacclimation at 25 °C/18 °C day/night, 60 to 70% RH, 14 h light/10 h dark; cooled at 5 °C per hour to −5, −15, −25 and −35 °C, 12 h hold, 25 °C control; three plants per species, three biological replicates per treatment; seedling age not stated. Vitex negundo has no LT50 in the main text (Supplementary Table S1, not read) and the integrated ranking in the abstract is "A. vestita > A. lanceolatus > V. negundo > R. hastatus > S. davidii", as the records say. Section 3.1: "Since semilethal temperatures serve as an inverse indicator of cold tolerance".
- **Counted, lines 5 and 6 (trait filing; causal interpretation). Ruling question: is a freezing-tolerance LT50 a sense_thermal record?** On the existing rulings, no. (1) Main's plant sense_thermal is a living plant's response to temperature: phyB thermal reversion, cold acclimation, vernalization, germination at set temperatures, leaf and flower movements as temperature changes (audit 212's survey; desk-d124, d131 and d140 counts). The one LT50 line on main, Kim 2014 (oa-13b:141, confirmed in audit-oa-13 as oa-13:750), records a *change* in LT50 caused by low-temperature acclimation, i.e. the plant sensed cold and hardened; audit 212 likewise ruled that a thermal output fits sense_thermal "only when the record states the response". (2) Here there is no temperature response to log: the only pre-treatment is a warm 25/18 °C preacclimation, the paper reports no acclimated-versus-non-acclimated comparison, and LT50 is the temperature at which half the membrane integrity is lost in a forced freeze, which the authors themselves use as an inverse index of tolerance. It is tissue injury, the analogue of an animal's lethal limit, not a threshold of perception. (3) The records' own notes concede this ("a damage threshold and not a sensing threshold"), so filing under a sensing trait misstates the causal reading a user of the key will make. Audit-oa-13 listed a freezing-tolerance line under sense_thermal as "trait keys used loosely" and left it uncounted under audit 31's practice; the 29 October oa-audit.md rule now counts misstated causal interpretation, so I count both. **Owner question:** the plant rulings name no home for a bare freezing-injury threshold, and the owner may choose to admit cold-hardiness LT50 values as a tolerance figure under sense_thermal (the key's description is "temperature sensing; string with figures"). Until the owner says so, lines 5 and 6 should be dropped; if the owner admits them, they need only the quote-spacing fix and the value wording below.
- Grade m right for both values as measurements; "partial": false right.
- Dropped species (Sophora davidii, Arthraxon lanceolatus) are not logged, so no name fault arises; I did not check them.
