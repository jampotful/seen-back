## Checks per paper

Chandel et al. 2024 (lines 1 to 4). All four quotes are verbatim whole sentences in the eScholarship PDF ("trpA11" is the flattened superscript trpA1^1; typographic, not counted). Values match the printed numbers. Context read: Fig. 2e "Female An. stephensi were exposed to two zones with human odour and 5% CO2"; Fig. 2j legend "Mosquitoes exposed to CO2, human odour and 34 °C IR in one zone, and 29.5 °C in the other zone"; Fig. 3 legend "Females in both zones were exposed to 5% CO2 and human odour" and "Zone 2 was exposed to an IR source at 34 °C (a,d,f)"; 80 females per trial; 1 to 3 weeks; females only; 5 min; "n = 6 biological replicates for each group" (Fig. 2 legend for a-e, g, h and j; Fig. 3 legend for a, d-f and h); each cage "tested a minimum of three times".

Shared fault, all four records (scientific-meaning: causal interpretation). Every `conditions` ends its arena description with "so convective heat and infrared are both present at short range." The paper says the reverse: "We devised our behavioural assay to transmit only thermal IR to the mosquito cage from the heated Peltier plate"; "To prevent thermal conduction, we placed the mosquito cage 4 cm away from the arena wall that housed the Peltier plates"; "To block convection from the warm surface from reaching the mosquitoes ... we positioned the Peltier plates an additional ~0.5 cm away" (pieces). In the distance test a further "convective barrier, which consisted of high-IR-transmitting PE film 1 cm away from the cage" was used. The records therefore tell a reader the response may be to convective heat, which is the confound the design removes and the paper's central claim depends on. Counted in each of lines 1 to 4.

Line 1 (distance). In addition, the source for this test is misdescribed by the shared text ("a heated Peltier plate ... behind the mesh"): the authors "heated a 0.44 m2 plate (91 cm × 49 cm) to 34 °C", "covered half of the plate with a 0.15-cm-thick clear acrylic panel to block IR transmission from that side", so the IR source was "0.22 m2 (45.5 cm × 49 cm)", and "We then placed the cage at different distances from the IR source". The 70 cm range depends on that source area and the 34 versus 29.5 C contrast, and none of it is in line 1 (it appears, wrongly sized, in line 2). Changes the practical scale of the 70 cm figure; counted with the shared fault (one error). The 80 and 90 cm sentence in the note is verbatim.

Line 2 (34 C peak). In addition, `conditions` says "the source was a 0.44 m2 plate (91 cm by 49 cm) in the mid-range test": the plate was 0.44 m2 but half was masked, so the IR source was 0.22 m2, and the mid-range test is not the Fig. 2h experiment. The peak is shallow (33 C PI 0.45 +/- 0.04; 37 C 0.53 +/- 0.05; 40 C 0.44 +/- 0.07); the record's "highest among the temperatures tested, not a threshold" is fair. The note's twofold-HSI and single-cue statements are the paper's ("highly significant, twofold increase in the HSI", "Any single cue alone (CO2, IR and human odour) evoked either no HSI or a very weak response").

Line 3 (trpA1 null). In addition, the record omits the two results that bound the null: trpA1 mutants "were as effective as the wild type at navigating to a zone with human odour and CO2", and in the set-up with all three heat-transfer modes "both the wild-type and the trpA11 mutant showed the same preference for the 34 °C zone". TRPA1 is needed for the IR response, not for heat seeking or host seeking as such; the note's "a mechanism needed for the behaviour" leaves that open. A limiting result omitted; counted with the shared fault (one error).

Line 4 (An. stephensi). In addition, two faults. (a) Ambient is wrong: the paper "performed IR-preference experiments at an ambient temperature of 27 °C" for this species (Results; Fig. 2e legend "The zones were at 27 °C ambient temperature or exposed to 34 °C IR"), not 29.5 C, and "the same arena and 34 C source as for Aedes" hides the changed conditions; the IR contrast is 7 C, not 4.5 C. (b) The note says the contrary point "is stated in the value"; it is not. The paper continues "after landing on the IR zone, An. stephensi rarely walked around probing, and would quickly resume their flight" and "they did not exhibit the same type of host-seeking behaviour" (pieces). A landing bias without probing is a qualification that limits the finding; it belongs in `value`. One error.
