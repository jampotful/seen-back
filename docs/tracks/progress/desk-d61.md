## Advice on yield
1. Ask the fetch for a numbered list of every abstract sentence with its character count, as a first step. The default "quote the result sentences" prompt returns fragments that pass as sentences (Ephedra, Cedrus, Bletilla and Elmlinger's "(iii)" all came out wrong that way until re-listed).
2. Fern spore papers were the best new vein: Brazilian groups publishing in Acta Botanica Brasilica, Revista Brasileira de Botanica and Ambiencia (Randi, Droste) report set shade-net levels or photoperiods in short sentences, and SciELO, core.ac.uk and Springer pages print authors and DOI. Most species are absent from main. Search "<fern genus> spore germination light" per genus (Alsophila, Lygodium, Pteris, Nephrolepis, Microgramma, Asplenium are untried).
3. Old Planta abstracts (1960s to 1990s, Mohr group on Pinus sylvestris, Funaria, Dryopteris) are short, print DOI and authors on the Springer page, and give red/far-red results; Mohr-group conifer and moss papers are not exhausted.
4. Silva Fennica pages print DOI and keep abstract sentences short; the keyword listings (light, germinatio, dormancy, chilling) surface candidates without a search. Page fetches of URLs that no search result showed are refused, so run one search naming the paper's title first.
5. Orchid yield fell after d60: the remaining abstracts either run past 125 characters (Himantoglossum, Cymbidium, Phalaenopsis) or sit behind PMC. Orchid diel papers (Annals of Botany scent and anthesis rhythms) are worth one search per genus; the Bonatea one was readable through a Pherobase literature record when PMC was blocked.
6. Bryophyte names cost searches: the WFO page rarely surfaces for a moss, and TAXREF or worldfloradb pages are not authorities. Spend the name search before reading the paper.
