### Line 1, Xenopus laevis, attracted to newt chemical stimuli

Abstract, verbatim, whole, one piece: "However, our short-duration experiments found that X. laevis were attracted to newt chemical stimuli rather than deterred." **Quote MATCH**, 17 words (the note says 18).

Design, verbatim: "We studied feral X. laevis captured from stormwater ponds in Lacey and Issaquah, WA and housed in a" "captive facility at the Saint Martin's University campus in Lacey."; "Mesh pouches made of black window screening were placed inside of the aquarium"; "One pouch was empty (control) and the other contained a newt (treatment)."; "we manually agitated each newt for 1 min, by gently stroking the anterior and posterior sides"; "An X. laevis was placed in the center of the tank, parallel to the mesh pouches and facing out of the aquarium."; "We performed a total of 50 X. laevis behavioral choice tests: 25 with the newt on the southwest side" "of the aquarium and 25 with the newt on the northeast side of the aquarium."; "For 10 min post-release, we observed X. laevis behavior"; "Each choice test was conducted in 2 L of aged tap water inside of a rectangular 38 L aquarium."; zones: Newt = "the section with the newt or the section adjacent to the newt", "Center" = "the middle fifth section", Away = "the section with the empty mesh bag or the section adjacent to the empty mesh bag"; "We used trial day as a random effect". Number of frogs, reuse, fasting, size and sex not stated (tool summary). Results, verbatim: model with Choice "(Fig. 5; p = 7.99 e -14)"; "Newt vs Away p = 0.003"; Away "mean = 210.6 s, ± 4.9 s SE", newt "mean = 368.5 s, ± 5.1 s SE", Center 8.9 s (and the Results call it the "Center third"). Discussion, verbatim: "Finally, X. laevis may be attracted to visual cues more so than chemical ones."; "this result, in tandem with our findings, suggests that X. laevis may generally respond to visual cues like movement."; "Future studies may benefit from testing the response of X. laevis strictly to chemical cues."; "We did not detect TTX in the sample solutions."; "Longer trials may reveal different patterns if aqueous TTX takes longer than 10 min".

Ruling on the sensing claim: the stimulus is a live, freshly agitated newt in a mesh pouch, so visual, mechanical (movement, water disturbance) and chemical cues are all present, and the authors themselves say the frogs may be responding to visual cues and recommend a strictly chemical test; TTX, the chemical the design meant to release, was not detected. The design does not separate the chemical sense from vision or lateral-line detection, which is the same defect for which audits 317 and 344 held playback reactions out of sense_hearing. The abstract's "newt chemical stimuli" is the authors' label for the treatment, not a demonstrated chemical response.

**Error, HOLD OUT (trait not supported by the design; design omitted; false note claim).** (a) sense_chemical is not separable from visual and mechanical cues of a live agitated newt, and the authors' own visual-cue reading and TTX-not-detected result are omitted. (b) The conditions say zone areas are unequal; Newt and Away are two sections each and Center one, so the two compared zones are equal in area (the Results' "Center third" conflicts with the Methods' "middle fifth"). (c) Black window-screen pouches, the frog's release position, trial day as the only random effect (no frog identity) and the authors' short-duration caveat are omitted. (d) The note's "peerj.17307 absent from the whole docs/ tree" is false (desk-d222.md line 18). No replacement proposed; a re-file would need a trait that does not name the modality (or an owner ruling that a "chemical stimuli" label with a live animal suffices).
