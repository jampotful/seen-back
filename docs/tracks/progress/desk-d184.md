## Records
- Zebedin and Ladich 2013, PLoS ONE 8(6): e67049, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0067049 (Crossref CC BY 4.0; journals.plos.org read twice): Amblydoras affinis lowest AEP thresholds at 0.5 kHz and lowest threshold of the study 59.6 dB re 1 µPa; Megalodoras uranoscopus best hearing at 1 kHz. Fish immobilized with Flaxedil; per-species hearing n (3 to 9) not read. This is the held d183 lead; the DOI was found by WebSearch and Crossref title query.
- Maiditsch and Ladich 2014, PLoS ONE 9(9): e108583, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0108583 (Crossref CC BY 4.0; journals.plos.org read three times): Silurus glanis x3 (best 0.3 to 1 kHz; sensitivity significantly lower at the lower temperatures, F2,176 = 346.6; maximum gain 10.3 dB at 0.5 kHz) and Cyprinus carpio x2 (best 0.3 to 1 kHz; lower at 15 C, F2,168 = 36.9). Catfish n = 8, carp n = 9. The S. glanis and C. carpio best-hearing sentences sit under species headings and do not name the species; the notes say so.
- Rößler, May and Dähne 2024, Sci Rep 14(1): 30592, DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-82942-2 (Crossref CC BY 4.0; nature.com read twice, no licence line on the page): Spheniscus humboldti in-air audiogram, 50% hit rate below 76 dB between 0.25 and 10 kHz, best 2 kHz mean 15.3 dB; the authors call the 0.25 kHz sensitivity (36.8 dB against 64.1 dB at 0.5 kHz) unexpected and possibly an artefact. n = 4.
- Names: Amblydoras affinis (Catalog of Fishes spid 5640, valid), Megalodoras uranoscopus (spid 5889, valid), Silurus glanis (gbif.org/uk/species/2337607, Accepted), Cyprinus carpio and Spheniscus humboldti (ACCEPTED in docs/views/gaps/gbif-names.tsv).
