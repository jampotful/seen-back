The authors say ABR estimates commonly underestimate hearing sensitivity, "particularly at the low and high ends", which the note's "ABR thresholds usually sit above behavioural ones" conveys. The ear-insert explanation is in line 3, where it applies (the low-frequency decline); lines 1 and 2 concern 4 kHz and above, where it does not apply.

Rößler et al. 2024 (line 4). Quote MATCH (Abstract). Confirmed: four captive penguins (two males, two females) at the Ozeaneum Stralsund; go/no-go with a pointing response, fish reinforcement, double-blind by video; 50% hit-rate criterion (d′ and probit fits also used); frequencies 125 Hz to 10 kHz; anechoic pentagonal foam-lined box; 2 kHz "15 dB rms re 20 µPa (SD ± 10.6 dB, n = 4)"; 0.250 kHz 37 dB, 0.500 kHz 64 dB; the authors find it "difficult to interpret whether it is an artefact of the methods or a biologically relevant finding". Egri et al. 2025 (lines 5 to 8). Quotes: lines 5, 6 and 7 MATCH (Abstract), line 8 MATCH (Results). Two scientific-meaning faults run through all four lines.
- Preparation. Every line's `method` says "excised-eye preparations". The Methods: "In the case of adults, the abdomen and partly the thorax with the wings were fastened to the plexiglas with adhesive tape," "while the head and the forelegs were fixed to the plexiglas using melted paraffin wax." and "The recording and reference electrodes were inserted to the right eye and the abdomen, respectively." These are intact animals; the records' own "reference in the abdomen" is impossible in an excised eye. An excised-eye ERG and an intact-animal ERG differ in viability and response speed, which bears directly on flicker fusion (line 8) and on how the sensitivities compare with other species' in vivo ERG.
- Template fits (lines 5 to 7). The `method` says "the peaks are peaks of the whole-eye ERG spectral sensitivity, not measured pigment lambda max". The Results: "The sum of two visual pigment templates was fitted to the spectral sensitivity of each preparation, providing an estimation for the sensitivity peak of hypothetical green and UV photoreceptors." and "after pooling together the male ventral and female sensitivity peaks, the sensitivity maxima of the UV and green receptors were finally determined at wavelengths of 366 nm ± 12 nm and 536 nm ± 5 nm"; the male dorsal value comes from "a single template" fit, "with an average sensitivity peak at 353 nm ± 2 nm (mean ± SD)". Methods: response amplitudes converted with a Naka-Rushton function; curves normalised to 516 nm (346 nm for male dorsal); 12 wavelengths, 346 to 641 nm (tool summary). So 536, 366 and 353 nm are mean Govardovskii-template peaks of hypothetical receptors, not peaks read off the measured ERG curve; the measured curves' own peaks are not stated. The record says the opposite of what the values are. It also contradicts the desk's own held-out list, which excluded the larval peaks (375, 544 nm) as "template fits of hypothetical receptors": the adult peaks are the same kind of number. Whether template-estimated receptor peaks belong under `lambda_max_nm` at all, or need their own flag, is an owner question; the corrected `method` below files them as what they are.
- Line 8 conditions. "light level and temperature not stated": the Methods give "Mean light intensity of the UV and green flickering stimuli were 1.2 × 10^15 and 2.0 × 10^15" photons cm-2 s-1, and "FFF measurements were made at two wavelengths, 520 nm (green) and 372 nm (UV)." Flicker fusion rises with intensity, so a stated stimulus intensity reported as absent is a consequential misstatement. Temperature is indeed not stated. The dorsal-ventral comparison is pairwise Wilcoxon rank-sum with Bonferroni correction, "dorsal vs ventral (green): p = 0.017; dorsal vs ventral (UV): p = 0.032" (tool summary of the test, p values verbatim).
- Dark adaptation (at least 40 min; 40 min before each flicker recording), chromatic adaptation 15 min with 562 nm or 377 nm LEDs, male dorsal dark-adapted only, and the 6/9/9 flicker preparations are as recorded. The tool reports the pooled 366 and 536 nm peaks as from dark-adapted curves (tool summary; Table 1 body not in the page text).
- Uncounted: the female and male ventral flicker values were retrievable as sentences on the page ("we can consider 104.5 Hz ± 12.2 Hz as the flicker fusion frequency of dark-adapted female eyes"; "we consider the mean ± SD of FFF values of the green- and UV–stimulated ventral eyes, being 104.1 Hz ± 23.2 Hz"; both are fragments as the tool returned them, so whole-sentence form not confirmed). Holding them out is not an error, but line 8 alone presents the dorsal value without the comparison values; the note gives them. SDs of the three peaks are not carried.
