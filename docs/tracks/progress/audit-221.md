Trait question (the owner's sense_chemical reading, which I apply and do not override). Line 1: one branch of each intact wild plant in the field was bagged for 24 h with a filter paper square carrying a pure compound, and damage was scored on that branch at season's end; this is airborne-signal reception by an intact plant, inside the reading. Lines 2 and 3: the saplings were intact and potted, and four living weevils were caged on the trunk of the treatment plant ("Four pine weevils were placed in a plastic foam enclosure (120 × 150 mm) with mesh sides" ... "secured around the trunk of the treatment plant with clips"), with an empty cage on the control; emissions were measured from the living plant in an approximately 70 L Tedlar enclosure "secured to the trunk", normalised "to total needle surface area". So the plant is intact, the feeding is by living insects on the plant, and the finding is emission, not a measurement on cut material. The problem is what the emission is. The authors attribute it to wounding, not to an induced response: "In contrast, the bark-boring pine weevils used in this study mechanically damaged the plant bark which exposed terpene pools to the atmosphere." "This mechanical damage likely led to a larger increase in emissions sourced from terpene pools than from de novo emissions." "An alternate explanation could be that MT-rich resin was conducted from xylem and needles to the wounds for healing." Release of stored terpenes from bark the weevils have opened is volatilisation from damaged tissue, which the owner's reading places outside sense_chemical as a living response (as with processed or cut tissue); the resin-flow alternative is a wound response but the paper does not test it. Neither record states this, and both notes call the finding "Herbivore-induced volatile emission of intact living saplings", which is the causal reading the paper declines to give.
