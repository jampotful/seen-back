Cut traps (line 1). The traps were "cut off under water with micro-scissors" and isolated in cuvettes, and the Discussion says the authors "cannot exclude that cutting the traps off the respective plants leads to unwanted (and unnoticed) side-effects". The owner's exclusion of cut tissue is written for sense_chemical (cut-flower and cut-branch sampling), not sense_touch. The response measured is the closure of a living trap within the recording session, which is what the snap-trap does on the plant; the record says plainly in `note` that these are "Isolated, cut trap leaves in a laboratory chamber, not intact plants". The limitation is already conveyed unambiguously, so it is not counted; the authors' own caveat should be added.

Transgenic line (line 3). The plants are an early-flowering WASE accession (Sakata Seed) of M. pudica carrying the reporter LjUBQ1p::GCaMP6f; the paper says the GCaMP6f imaging works "without affecting the physiological response of leaf movement" (Supplementary Fig. 2, not opened) and presents the result for M. pudica. A transgenic reporter line is a genotype of the species, not a cultivar or hybrid; under audit 204's rule (a line or cultivar of the species is filed at species rank when the paper presents the finding for the species and the record names the line in conditions) species rank and grade m hold. The record names the construct but not the WASE accession; added below, uncounted, because nothing in the paper ties propagation speed to the accession.
