| 1 | Populus balsamifera / sense_light_nonvisual (bud set Julian day 217 White Horse, 60.70 N, against 247 Fredericton, 46.40 N, 2007) | ERROR (value says more than the quote; no light contrast, so the record is not a measured response to photoperiod) | Two grounds, one error. (a) The value says bud set "ranged from Julian day 217 ... to Julian day 247". The quote opens "For example" and gives two population means; the paper does not tabulate the other three populations' dates, so it does not say these are the extremes of the range. White Horse and Fredericton are the northernmost and southernmost origins and the HED cline is near-linear (r = -0.989), so the range reading is likely, but it is the desk's inference, and the quote rule bars a value that says more than the quote. (b) Trait fit. Every tree in the garden grew under the same natural day length at one site in one year. What the record measures is a heritable difference in bud-set date between two origins under a common photoperiod. Reading that difference as a photoperiodic response depends on the Discussion's "fall bud set is primarily controlled by day length" and on earlier studies (Pauley and Perry 1954; the authors' 2009 greenhouse work), none of which is this paper's measurement; this paper never varies, sets or measures light against the response, and nothing in it separates day length from the other seasonal cues the trees met in the same weeks. The owner's sense_light_nonvisual cell is for responses to light (photoperiod, shade, UV, night light, light quality, germination light/dark), and the one other photoperiod line my duplicate grep surfaced (sweep-28 line 3, Charrette 1990) carries a light contrast (set daylengths of 6, 10, 14 and 18 h). The record states its weakness honestly, which is why this is one error and not a misstatement, but a population-origin contrast in a single garden is evidence of latitudinal ecotypes, and the photoperiod reading is the authors' cited inference rather than a result of this study. Otherwise correct: the two dates, the two latitudes (Table 1), the year, the garden, its coordinates, five populations, 15 genotypes, three replicates, twice-weekly scoring, the near-simultaneous bud flush, the "not shown" consistency across years and gardens, and the separate aspen garden. Each value is one named provenance's mean over its genotypes and replicates, not a pool across provenances, so the pooled-provenance rule is not breached, though the record should say the values are population means |

Faults noted, not counted:
- Quote: the record inserts a comma after "217)" that none of the three reads shows. Copy the sentence as printed: "(X = Julian day 217) whereas".
- Conditions call the Sävar aspen garden "a separate unreplicated garden". The Methods state no design for Sävar beyond 116 genotypes from 12 localities and three ramets per genotype for leaf sampling; "unreplicated" comes from the Abstract and Conclusions, which speak of unreplicated outdoor common gardens in general. Say "the Sävar aspen garden (116 genotypes, 12 Swedish localities) is a separate garden, not logged here".
- Conditions paraphrase the Abstract as "photoperiod strongly limits height growth in unreplicated outdoor common gardens". The Abstract says "We highlight the shortcoming of unreplicated outdoor common gardens for tree improvement and the crucial role of photoperiod in limiting height growth"; the two clauses are separate claims. Paraphrase them apart.
- Conditions omit the 0 to 10 phenology scale, that the trees were rooted cuttings raised under a 19-h greenhouse photoperiod before planting out, that the values are population means (x̄) over 15 genotypes and three replicates, that no test is given for the two-population contrast itself, and the population-level statistic the paper does give (HED against latitude r = -0.989, p < 0.01, Table 2; Pearson with Bonferroni correction).
- The Indian Head longitude conflict in the paper (103.39 W in Methods, 105.73 W in the Figure 4 caption) is the paper's; the record follows Methods, which is fine, but could note it.
