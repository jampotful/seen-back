## Paper facts checked

Line 1 (Götze et al. 2016):
- Value sentence verbatim: "The calculated average species-specific terminal frequency of signals with duration of 5.5 ms was 46.5 kHz." Section heading verbatim "Echolocation behavior and transmission channels of single flying bats". Whole sentence, 16 words, contains the value; `where` is right.
- Next sentence (reassembled): "Individual frequencies related to signal durations of 5.5 ms were distributed over a range from 43.5–49.1 kHz," + "indicating that recordings were taken from multiple individuals." The record's conditions carry this range. OK.
- How the value was made: Methods "we correlated terminal frequency with call duration" and regressed (tool paraphrase: regression then residuals). Results: "The species-specific transmission channel of single flying P. pipistrellus was calculated from 43 sound sequences" ... "with 2147 echolocation calls (Fig. 1A)." Methods: the channel used "all search signals from all analyzed sound sequences recorded in single flights"; "Calls with durations of 3.5 ms or greater were counted as search calls". So 46.5 kHz is a regression estimate at 5.5 ms from search calls of single flying bats. The record says "calculated" in method and note but does not say search calls, single flights or regression.
- Design caveat omitted: Methods/Results state "pseudoreplication cannot be completely excluded, as some sequences had similar terminal frequencies." The record says only that the number of bats is unknown. Under rules.md (sample composition, calculated vs measured) this caveat must be carried. Error.
- Peak frequency: listed as measured ("Signal parameters including duration, pulse interval, peak frequency, and terminal frequency were measured.") but the tool found no numerical peak-frequency value in kHz anywhere in the article (tool report). Nothing contradicts or qualifies 46.5 kHz as terminal frequency. The record's note ("gives no peak-frequency value in the sections read") stands.
- Contrary/null results: Abstract "All observed frequency changes were predicted by the attention reaction alone" and "do not support the JAR hypothesis of increased frequency separation"; in reactions to conspecifics terminal frequency rose as duration fell (tool paraphrase: about +0.95 kHz, about -1.27 ms). The record's note carries the null result for jamming avoidance and states the value is baseline single-bat call structure, which is right since the value comes from single flights. OK.
- Site/date verbatim: "Bebenhausen and Ofterdingen, Baden-Württemberg, south-western Germany"; "were observed from July to October 2010". Sex/age not stated. Bats unknown in number: "The number of recorded bats remains unknown as we conducted this study with free flying bats in the field." OK.
- Trait and kind: sense_echolocation with physiological_response matches 4 existing desk records on main. Grade m: one named species, measured and calculated by the authors from their own recordings. OK.
