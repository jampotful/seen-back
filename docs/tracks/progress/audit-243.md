Shared fault, lines 1 and 2 (scientific-meaning: method). The shared `conditions` say "each stimulus starting at 25 °C and holding a 500 s steady-state plateau". That is the general protocol, but the ST-neuron values were not measured that way: in the Methods section on the ST-neuron the authors say they could not test different steady-state temperatures within its narrow working range, so they "tested the neurons response following very slow temperature increases" from 25 °C; the working range comes from sigmoid fits to that slow-ramp activity, and the resolving power (mean 1.58 °C, range 0.31 to 2.90 °C, n = 7) was computed by linear regression over ±1 °C around the fit's turning point (PDF, tool summary). Line 2's 1.6 °C is therefore a value derived from a fitted ramp response, not a step discrimination, and the record says it was measured under plateau steps. This changes how the working range and resolution can be applied (ramp approximation of steady state, untested steady states inside the range) and is counted in lines 1 and 2. The working range is a neuronal one; the authors note it matches the brood-care temperature preference of C. rufipes but did not test behaviour (tool summary). The records say "not behavioural thresholds" and "Resolution is neuronal", which is right.

Line 3 (scientific-meaning: direction). The value [21, 35] °C is filed under sense_thermal beside line 1's working range [26.9, 33.2] and reads the same way, as a range of temperature sensing. The paper's finding is the opposite: the TT-neuron is a "thermal flux-detector" for temperature transients, its steady-state activity is the same from 21 to 35 °C, and the authors state that its adaptation to steady-state temperatures "does not allow the encoding of absolute temperature" (pieces). The note says the range is where median nIF "stayed similar", but not that this means no absolute-temperature coding, and the bare numeric value invites the reverse reading. Counted. The 500 s plateau in the shared conditions plausibly applies to the TT steady-state values (general protocol), though the text read does not say the values were taken at plateau ends; the n behind the TT steady-state analysis is not stated (n = 47 measurements of resting activity, tool summary), so the conditions' neuron counts are detection-threshold n's.
