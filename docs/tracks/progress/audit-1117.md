## Verdicts

1. Natrix natrix, Spaseni et al. 2024, PeerJ 12:e17168 (10.7717/peerj.17168). ERROR, HELD OUT (drop). Read peerj.com/articles/17168 and the PDF. Byline (9 authors, printed order), title, 12:e17168, 2024, DOI match. Licence on the PDF 'Distributed under Creative Commons CC-BY 4.0' (publisher free full text); the article page states a CC Attribution License but the summariser would not return that sentence verbatim, so the note's long page sentence is unconfirmed (not counted). Quote matches the Results exactly in three joined pieces (35 words). Taxon: the paper says 'Our study concerns the nominate species' (N. natrix sensu stricto, with N. helvetica and N. astreptophora separate); Reptile Database page: Natrix natrix (LINNAEUS, 1758), valid, Colubridae (Natricinae). Not on main by species or DOI (DOI appears only in desk-d562.md's not-filed list, which called it 'citizen-science night records, no category'). THE ERROR: the 127 records are the night-time subset of 9,745 iNaturalist records plus 22 personal and 12 Observation.org records, gathered (the paper's words) 'in a non-standardized manner'. Selected night observations cannot assign the species a diel category; they show that night activity occurs. The filed value 'nocturnal (94 of 127 ..., 74.02%) and crepuscular' reads as a category and says more than the data support, even with the disclosure in conditions; a reader of the diel cell sees 'nocturnal' for a species the same paper calls 'primarily diurnal' (Conclusions: 'Although a primarily diurnal species, we suggest extending search efforts through the night, ...'). That Conclusions characterisation is not measured in the paper, so it cannot be filed as grade m either. Unfixable as a diel value: dropped. The Replacement line restates the value as a presence report and carries the hold-out reason, for the record only. Faults: Higher Taxa line abbreviated in the note (page prints Colubridae (Natricinae), Colubroidea, Caenophidia, Alethinophidia, Serpentes, Squamata); page licence sentence not confirmable verbatim.

2. Sciurus vulgaris, Drenske et al. 2024, Front Ecol Evol 12:1455142 (10.3389/fevo.2024.1455142). PASS with faults. Read the frontiersin.org full-text page and PDF. Byline (6 authors, printed order), title, 12:1455142, 2024, DOI match. Licence on the PDF: 'This is an openaccess [line-break hyphen] article distributed under the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution License (CC BY).'; the HTML page prints none; publisher's own copy, accepted. Quote matches the PDF exactly in two pieces ('Squirrels are diurnal, while cats are active throughout the day (Figure 2D) and martens are primarily crepuscular and nocturnal (Figure 2E).'), in Results 3.1.1, the paper's own result with figure refs; the squirrel clause is the value, other taxa disclosed. A separate Introduction sentence 'Squirrels are diurnal and adapt their activity patterns to local conditions' is background and correctly not used. Counts confirmed: 1335 squirrel pictures; 'collected via 346 stations' for all three taxa (with 14087 cat and 715 marten pictures); Section 2.3 '346 out of 516 (67%) stations did not detect any squirrels' (so 170 detected); 3.2.1 'We used all 1335 pictures of squirrels from 170 stations'. The inconsistency is real and the 170 figure is the right one for squirrels. 'Active between 5am and 5pm' confirmed. Methods (50 cm, 4 consecutive weeks, five phases, first phase omitted, volunteers) confirmed. Urban garden squirrels are free-ranging wild animals (no captivity, no handling); the Discussion says residents installed squirrel feeders during lockdowns, which belongs in conditions. MDD: Sciurus vulgaris Linnaeus, 1758, Sciuridae, Rodentia, LC, Palearctic range; species status field blank (no living flag printed). Not on main by DOI; the species is on main only under colour_vision, receptor_classes and fovea from other papers, no diel row. Faults: (a) 'where' should be the printed path 'Results > 3.1 Seasonal activity > 3.1.1 Activity overlap'; (b) feeder context missing from conditions; (c) byline as printed not given in the note.
