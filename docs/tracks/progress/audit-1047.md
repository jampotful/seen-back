## Checks against the paper

- Rows (printable PDF text layer, which renders ± as "6", as the unit said; the HTML tables are images): N. macrotis "Low/(fm) qcf down 28.864.0/16.761.0/12.065.1 13.361.9/... 11/2"; C. greenhalli "Low/qcf down 25.261.3/17.463.6/7.863.1 15.962.1/... 71/14" and "High/qcf down 29.061.5/21.164.6/7.964.1 14.862.4/... 40/14"; E. dabbenei "Low/fm qcf down 21.361.2/13.760.5/7.661.1 28.362.8/... 22/6". Every logged value matches digit for digit. SF > EF in every row (downward sweeps) and SF - EF = BW to rounding (12.1/12.0, 7.8, 7.9, 7.6). No physical inconsistency.
- Captions: Table 2 "Search flight call parameters for the genera Cynomops, Promops, Nyctinomops, and Tadarida."; Table 3 "Search flight call parameters for the genus Eumops." Line 16's `where` gives Table 3 the Table 2 wording ("for the genera ..."): fault.
- Footnotes: Tables 1 and 2 print "For abbreviations and statistical results, see footnote in Table 3."; Table 3 footnote ends "Values correspond to the mean 61 standard deviation, and to the numbers of calls (NC) and sequences (NS) included." (text layer; ±1). It also defines "Call type ( = call frequency alternation) refers to changes in frequency." Low/High appear only as row labels, so the unit's "labels not separately defined" is right.
- Search flight: Methods "we focused our analysis on interspecific differences in search flight echolocation call design" and exclude "faint calls (signal to noise ratio ,10 dB), as well as approach and terminal phase calls." Right.
- Sites: Methods print "Recordings of molossid echolocation calls were obtained in Costa Rica, Panama, Venezuela, Bolivia, and Brazil over a period of 10 years."; reference recordings in "large clearings (20–30 m width)" with released animals "directed towards open space"; high-flying individuals "(up to 60–80 m above ground)"; free-flying recordings in Bolivia, Brazil and Panama ("high flying molossid bats in open space" in Panama). No species is named in Methods and no per-species site or recording type is printed. The `method` field lists only Bolivia, Brazil and Panama and does not say per-species sites are unprinted: fault (en dashes also restored).
- Individuals: Methods and tables give none, but the Figure 3 caption prints "Symbols represent sequences (individuals) per species." The `conditions` say flatly that the paper states no number of individuals and omit this, which bears on sample size (N. macrotis NS = 2): fault.
- `conditions` in all eight lines say "the same species is also printed with a second call type that is not filed here". False for each: N. macrotis has one printed call type only; both C. greenhalli call types (lines 10 to 15) and both E. dabbenei call types (lines 16 to 21) are filed: fault.
- Quote: the unit kept the text-layer "6" for ±. The page prints ±; the replacement normalises to ± and says so (precedent desk-d145, "Quote normalised from the PDF text layer"). Counted as a quote fault, fixable. Quotes 14 to 15 words.
- Discussion (N. macrotis): links its "low calling frequencies" to feeding "primarily on large moths"; added to line 9 conditions as context, not counted.
