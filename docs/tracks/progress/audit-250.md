## Checks per paper

Claes et al. 2014 (lines 1 to 3). Quote MATCH on all three: one whole sentence in Results, Visual pigments, preceded by the sentence ending "likely contain only the vitamin A1 chromophore (rhodopsin)." and followed by the Figure 5 heading. 25 words, each record's value is in it and the value says no more than it.
- Values. The Results give one value per species: 485 nm E. splendidus, 487.5 nm E. spinax, 491 nm S. aliae. "484–491 nm" appears in the Abstract ("Peak spectral sensitivities of the rod visual pigments (λmax) fall within the range 484–491 nm.") and in the Discussion, never against a species; the Figure 5 caption carries no numbers (tool summary; panel images not read). The filed values are the right ones, and the notes state the discrepancy.
- Methods per species, confirmed. "Technique 2 (for E. spinax)": "Transverse absorbance spectra (330–800 nm) of individual photoreceptor outer segments were measured using a single-beam wavelength-scanning microspectrophotometer", retina dissected under dim red light and fixed in 2% glutaraldehyde for 5 minutes, analysis after McNichol and Govardovskii et al.; number of outer segments not stated. Technique 1 (E. splendidus, S. aliae): eyes "removed in darkness, placed in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C until dissection", extracted in n-dodecyl β-D-maltoside under dim far-red light, hydroxylamine added, "Absorbance spectra were calculated as the difference spectra between sequential monochromatic partial bleaches." Line 1 `method` (MSP of individual outer segments) and lines 2 and 3 `method` (detergent extract, difference spectra, an average over the eye) carry this correctly and differently, as they should: an MSP value is a single-cell absorbance peak, an extract value an average of all pigment in the extract, and the records do not present them as the same measure.
- Mixture question. The authors state "The retina of each of these sharks appears to have only one spectrally distinct visual pigment", "confirmed by partial bleaching in E. splendidus and S. aliae (data not shown)", and infer an A1 chromophore from template fits. So the extract values are not presented as mixtures. The records do not carry this; it strengthens the values rather than limiting them, so it is uncounted (recommended addition below).
- n. No number of animals, eyes or outer segments is given for the pigment work (confirmed); the records say so.
- Specimen provenance (uncounted fault, lines 1 to 3). The by-catch sentence ("Specimens from four species were obtained as freshly moribund by-catch") lists S. aliae, T. kabeyai, E. splendidus and E. lucifer. "Etmopterus spinax specimens were collected in the Norwegian Raunefjord (bottom longlines at 180–200 m" and were "humanely euthanized by a blunt trauma to the chondrocranium". Line 1 `conditions` calls E. spinax "by-catch", and the shared `note` on all three lines says "Specimens were moribund by-catch, not dark-adapted live animals", which is wrong for E. spinax. Uncounted because the pigment's absorbance peak in fixed outer segments, and in extracts, does not depend on whether the animal was moribund or euthanized; the dark handling of the eyes, which is what could bleach pigment, is stated correctly on each line. - Ratio units. A diameter ratio of 0.5 is an area fraction of one fourth, which the quote itself states; "ratio of diameters" is right.
- Time scale of "following days". The Results do not give a day count for reaching 0.9; the nearest figure is "In both eye pairs 95% of the asymptotic values" reached "after roughly 210 h" (about 8.75 days), and the Abstract says the pupil "reaches its final size after approximately ten days". Line 5's "(the abstract gives about ten days to final size)" is right; the 210 h figure is the measured one and is recommended (uncounted).
- Mechanism (the question whether the ratio reflects a pupil mechanism). It does not reflect an adjustable pupil. "We will refer to the opening left by the pigment ring as 'pupil'"; the authors propose that "the decrease of the pigment ring is linked to the growth of the corneal lens after ecdysis" (MicroCT), and contrast spider eyes with insect ocelli "that change size as a response to light stimuli". The ratio is therefore a moult-cycle measure of how much of the lens a pigment ring leaves uncovered, which the growing lens corrects over about nine days; it is not a light response and not an iris. The records do not say this in `method` or `note`. Left uncounted because the limitation is already carried in the record: line 4's quote names "the pigment shield" and "shortly after ecdysis", both lines' `conditions` tie the values to time since moulting, and both lines' `source.title` is "Pupil Size in Spider Eyes Is Linked to Post-Ecdysal Lens Growth". No field claims light-driven change. A one-sentence `method` addition (below) is strongly recommended, since a row writer reading only `trait` and `value` could take 0.5 as a constricted pupil.
- Grade `m` right on both (one named species measured). Line 4 marks "in the order of 0.5" and line 5 "approximately 0.9" in `conditions`.
