## CC0 ruling (desk-d233 owner question 0)

Ruling: a CC0 public-domain dedication printed on the exact copy read counts as an open licence statement for a repository copy, and line 10 is accepted on it.

Evidence on this copy. The PLOS article page prints, verbatim: "This is an open-access article, free of all copyright, and may be freely reproduced, distributed," "transmitted, modified, built upon, or otherwise used by anyone for any lawful purpose." "The work is made available under the Creative Commons CC0 public domain dedication." Crossref's single-DOI record carries one licence entry, https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/, content-version "unspecified", start 2011-06-10. No other licence tag appears, so there is no conflicting CC tag.

Reasoning. The licence rule exists so that the repository's quoted sentence rests on a stated permission on the copy read. CC0 waives copyright to the extent the law allows and, where a waiver fails, grants an unconditional fallback licence; it permits strictly more than CC BY, which the rule already accepts. The page sentence names the instrument, and the Crossref tag names the same instrument. The case maps onto the accepted pairing "Crossref 'unspecified' + page-printed Creative Commons Attribution License", with CC0 in place of CC BY: Crossref unspecified, page-printed CC licence, same licence on both. Conditions for future records: (1) the CC0 sentence must be on the publisher's own copy read, not inferred from the journal; (2) Crossref must not carry a conflicting licence for the vor; (3) a Crossref "unspecified" CC0 tag alone, with no page sentence, is not enough, matching the CC BY rule. I have no source for why PLOS issued this paper under CC0 (commonly a US-government-author arrangement); it plays no part in the ruling.
