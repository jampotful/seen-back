## Records
- Malewski et al. 2018, PeerJ 6: e5819, DOI 10.7717/peerj.5819 (Crossref CC BY 4.0; page states CC BY): Spalax galili, no prevailing magnetic direction of burrow axes, Upper Galilee Mountains (Israel), n = 9 burrow systems (Table 1). Sentence, Table 1 row and locality seen on three fetches of peerj.com/articles/5819. Confound kept in conditions: all 9 systems were individually oriented (tool reading of the Results), so the null is that they share no common direction; basaltic soils and regional magnetic anomalies named in the Discussion. Grade g (magnetoreception inferred). Name: BioLib taxon 302672 lists Spalax galili Nevo, Ivanitskaya & Beiles 2001 and cites MSW3, but states no status; mammaldiversity.org and Catalogue of Life were not tried successfully. Main holds the Heliophobius and Ctenomys rows from this paper, none for Spalax galili.
- Lunn et al. 2023, PeerJ 11: e16379, DOI 10.7717/peerj.16379 (Crossref CC BY 4.0): Branta canadensis (ACCEPTED in gbif-names.tsv). Two records: blue 483 nm avoided more than red 631 nm (49% increase in avoidance probability, P = 0.012), with the exposure-order interaction, unequal candela (80 versus 120 cd) and uncontrolled ambient light in conditions; and the null for 2 Hz pulsing versus steady (0.24 versus 0.19, P = 0.522), with the ambient-light interaction noted.
- Goller et al. 2018, PeerJ 6: e5404, DOI 10.7717/peerj.5404 (Crossref CC BY 4.0): Molothrus ater (ACCEPTED in gbif-names.tsv). Two records: avoidance of the 470 nm (16/17) and 630 nm (13/17) LED sides; and the null for 380 nm, 525 nm and white. The LED panels were not equated in radiance and the UV panel was the brightest; trials with no choice dropped without a stated count.
- Muñoz-Galicia et al. 2021, PeerJ 9: e12567, DOI 10.7717/peerj.12567 (Crossref CC BY 4.0): Leptophobia aripa (Boisduval, 1836), valid in ITIS TSN 777775. Two records: innate red preference among four artificial flower colours (G = 29.19, p = 0.0006) and the null among pink, white and yellow. Black priming and red-near-black intensity caveats are the authors' own and are in conditions.
