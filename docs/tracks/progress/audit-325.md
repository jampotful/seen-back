## Per-record findings

### Line 1, Tyto alba, sense_hearing, broadband MAA 4.9 deg

Results, single reference paradigm, verbatim and returned whole: "With BB noise, the MAA further decreased to 4.9° ± 0.3°." MATCH, 11 words. The preceding sentence (two abutting pieces): "In the single reference paradigm, the largest MAA of 13.7° ± 3.2° (mean ± s.d.) was obtained at 500 Hz" ... "MAA decreased to 8.4° ± 1.4° at 8000 Hz" (the tool marked the second piece as the continuation; the middle of the sentence was not returned, so this is not a two-piece quote under audit 324). The ± is mean ± s.d.; Table 1 caption: "mean values and s.d. of three barn owls" (tool summary), so "mean, SD 0.3" is right.

Which number is the measured value. Abstract, verbatim: "The MAA improved with increasing stimulus frequency, from 14° at 500 Hz to 6° at 8000 Hz." and "The smallest MAA of 4° was found for broadband noise stimuli." Multiple-reference Results (tool summary of the sentence): frontal MAAs 14.5°, 10.2°, 6.6° and 3.4° at 500, 2000, 8000 Hz and BB; lateral 19.9°, 13.3°, 11.5° and 8.2°. Discussion, verbatim: "The MAA for BB noise signals presented within frontal space was about 4°." and "The barn owl's MAA was about 14° at 500 Hz, a value that is nearly twice as large as the MAA of 8° obtained at 8000 Hz." The paper does not explain the Abstract figures (tool summary). The record's measured value is the right one: 4.9 ± 0.3° is the single-reference mean for three owls, and the 4° in the Abstract is not printed as a measurement anywhere in Results. My own construction, no source: the Abstract's 14°, 6° and 4° look like rounded figures pooled across both paradigms' frontal values (14° sits between 13.7 and 14.5; 6° near the multiple-reference 6.6; 4° between 3.4 and 4.9). The boring answer: the Abstract was written from an earlier analysis and never updated. Either way, the record's sentence "do not match this paradigm's Results ... so the paper is not fully consistent" is accurate as far as it goes but leaves out the one figure that explains most of the gap, the multiple-reference frontal BB MAA of 3.4°, which is smaller than the filed 4.9° in the same frontal direction.

Methods, verbatim: "The subjects were 3 European barn owls (*Tyto alba*) aged between 2 and 24 years."; "Two of the birds hatched in 2015 at the University of Oldenburg,"; "the third bird hatched in 1993 at the Technical University Munich."; "All birds were hand reared from the age of around 12 days."; "the motivation of the animals was controlled by maintaining a weight of about 10 to 15% below their free feeding weight." The word "imprinted" is not used (tool summary). Stimulus: "presented at a level of 40 dB SPL"; "a level roving of ±3 dB was implemented." Angles: "Overall the angular separation varied between ±1.5° and ±27°."; loudspeakers "arranged in a semicircle with an azimuthal distance of 6° between two adjacent loudspeakers"; positions between loudspeakers by "summing localisation", two adjacent loudspeakers playing identical stimuli "simulating a sound source position between the two loudspeakers" (tool summary around the quoted phrase). Free-flying owls in "a sound-attenuating echo-reduced chamber"; the waiting head orientation toward 0° was checked before each stimulus (tool summary). Session validity: false-alarm rate "≤ 20%" and at least 12 hits (tool summary). ANOVA: "The owls' localisation acuity differed significantly between stimulus types (F = 39.942, p < 0.001)." and "The barn owls' localisation acuity differed significantly between reference positions (F = 26.829, p < 0.001)." "There was no significant difference between the individual owls." (no statistic printed).

**Error (calculated-stimulus confound, sample composition and a contrary same-direction value omitted).** (a) The filed 4.9° lies below the 6° loudspeaker spacing; separations under 6° were phantom sources made by summing localisation across two loudspeakers. That is a simulated, not a physical, source position at the threshold the record files, and the rules list "calculated vs measured stimulus" as a design confound; the record does not mention it. (b) The owls' ages (2 to 24 years; hatched 2015, 2015 and 1993) are omitted; one subject was about 24 years old, which bears on a hearing measure (sample composition). Food restriction to 10 to 15% below free-feeding weight is omitted (minor, included). (c) The multiple-reference paradigm's frontal BB MAA of 3.4° and the significant reference-position effect (F = 26.829) are omitted; the record says only that lateral MAAs were larger. Fixable. Uncounted: the record's wording on the Abstract is replaced with the figures that let a reader see the pattern.

### Line 3, Tipula autumnalis, sense_chemical, nine EAG-active CHCs

Abstract, verbatim and returned whole: "GC-EAD analysis revealed nine EAG-active compounds, which were identified as cuticular hydrocarbons (CHCs)." MATCH, 13 words. Results: "The male cuticular washes contained nine EAG-active compounds," and "the female cuticle contained six EAG-active compounds"; "In female cuticular washes, six EAG-active compounds caused EAG responses in the antennae of males."; "All nine EAG-active compounds of the male cuticle induced EAG responses in male antennae" and "four of them elicited EAG responses in the antennae of females." (Z9-23Hy, Z9-25Hy, Z6E9-25Hy, 27Hy; tool summary). "Eleven male and twelve female antennae were used for the GC-EAD analysis."; criterion "it elicited a response in at least three successful GC-EAD recordings." No normalisation and no mV amplitudes (tool summary). Collection: "Tipula autumnalis adults were collected from September to October 2014–2017 in two natural habitats in Lithuania."; washes from "groups of 30 specimens, either males or females"; only females showing abdominal elevation were washed, because "Under laboratory conditions, T. autumnalis males copulated or attempted to copulate only with females" showing it. Bioassay: "Eight to twelve trials were conducted for each treatment."; "Males were picked up randomly and each specimen was tested only once."; Mann-Whitney U. Female washes: males spent more time in the stimulus zone (Z = 2.1, p = 0.036; tool summary) and "males were significantly faster in choosing the stimulus zone treated with female washes", but "There were no significant differences in the number of visits to the stimulus zone," "total distance moved, or male velocity when female cuticular washes were present or absent (control)." Enantiomers: "Behavioral tests revealed a repellent effect of the (S)-enantiomer and an excitatory effect of the (R)-enantiomer in males." 21Hy, 23Hy and 27Hy were not tested behaviourally and Z7-25Hy "was not selected for behavioral testing" (tool summary).

**Error (unrun pairing, null and contrary behavioural results, and wash-sample selection omitted).** (a) Female washes were run on male antennae only; the female-wash on female-antenna pairing was not run, and female antennae responded to only four of the nine male-derived compounds. The record gives neither, so the sex scope of "nine EAG-active" is unclear (the record says the nine are from male washes but not whose antennae). (b) Behavioural nulls omitted: female washes did not change visits, distance moved or velocity. (c) The (S)-enantiomer of 3-methylheneicosane was repellent; the record calls the enantiomers "novel olfactory and behavioural findings" without the direction. (d) Female washes came only from females showing abdominal elevation (sample composition). Fixable.
