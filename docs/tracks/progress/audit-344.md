### Line 2, Gelasimus dampieri, 75.4% mean escape probability over all treatments

Results, "Response timing and probability", first sentence of the subsection. Returned verbatim, whole, in one piece on both passes: "The crabs reliably responded to all simulated predator treatments with an average response probability of 75.4%." **Quote MATCH**, 16 words (the note says 17); value says no more (units say "mean over all treatments").

Next sentence, verbatim: "In Experiment 1, the crab’s response probability and timing did not differ between treatments," "regardless of whether they were approached by one or two predators," "or whether the paired predators were separated by 90 or 180°" (p = 0.32 probability, p = 0.37 timing, tool summary). Experiment 2, verbatim: "In Experiment 2, the probability of an escape run was also independent of whether the crabs faced" "a low or high contrast, or single or paired stimuli." "(Fig. 2C, Supplementary Material Table S2, p = 0.71)." and "there was a significant effect of predator treatment on response timing, where responses occurred significantly later" "to the low contrast stimulus (Fig. 2D, Supplementary Material Table S2, p = 0.004)." So response probability did not differ in either experiment; the pooled 75.4% hides no probability effect. It pools seven treatments over two experiments (Experiment 1: single, paired 90, paired 180 at −100%; Experiment 2: single −40%, single −100%, and the two mixed-contrast pairs, tool summary); the paper does not give the trial count or the split of crabs between experiments.

Design, verbatim: "In total 50 crabs (25 females, 25 males) and 23 crabs (14 females, 9 males) were used in the behavioural" "and intracellular experiments respectively."; "All stimuli were calculated to mimic an object of 30 mm in diameter approaching with a constant velocity of 200 mm/s" "from a starting distance of 5000 mm." (the record's tool-summary details on size, speed and distance are confirmed); "To avoid habituation, each individual crab was exposed to only two stimuli on any given day and only tested every second day."; "The order of presentation was randomized using a Latin square design, with crabs as columns and treatments as rows."; "We found a small but significant positive relationship between stimulus order and escape probability" "(Supplementary Material Table S2, p = 0.006) as well as escape timing" "(Supplementary Material Table S2, p = 0.001)."

**Error (design misstated; significant order effect and null p values omitted).** (a) The conditions say "each crab saw at most two stimuli, on alternate days"; the paper says two stimuli on any given day, tested every second day, with a Latin square in which crabs are columns and treatments rows, so each crab saw the treatments over several test days (repeated measures). (b) The significant positive effect of stimulus order on escape probability (p = 0.006) and timing (p = 0.001) is omitted; it bears directly on a mean pooled over presentations. (c) The null p values (Experiment 1 probability p = 0.32, timing p = 0.37; Experiment 2 probability p = 0.71) are not given, and the Experiment 2 probability null is not stated at all; the conditions' "did not differ between treatments" covers Experiment 1 only. Fixable.
