- Quote: the sentence prints "96% of jewel beetles were attracted by light at 590 nm and ca. 87% of them still responded to light at 645 nm (C. dimidiata: 100% and 96%; C. favopicta, 93% and 78%; at 590 nm and 645 nm) (Fig. S1)." The records replace "at 590 nm and 645 nm" with " … "; the uncut sentence is 41 words, so the cut is needed, it is marked, and the wavelengths stay in the main clause. MATCH; values contained and the value says no more.
- Methods confirmed (tool summary unless quoted): "In total, 28 individuals from 2 species (16 Castiarina dimidiata and 12 C. favopicta) completed the 6-wavelength trial series"; beetles collected in Victoria, the 28 from Wombargo; LEDs peaking at 590, 645, 680, 700, 720 and 735 nm; responses "attracted/not attracted" fitted by logistic regression over 590 to 735 nm; irradiance not in the main text (Supplementary Information 1, not read), as both records say.
- Limits and contrary results: "The 50% response rate was at ca. 704 nm for C. dimidiata and ca. 685 nm for C. favopicta."; "There was only one C. dimidiata individual that responded to light at 735 nm, which might be random movement."; lit arm reached "19 and 5 times more often, respectively", which the authors use to show choices were not random. Line 1 carries the 735 nm result and the 704 nm point (note). Line 2 does not carry its own 50% point (ca. 685 nm). Uncounted: the value claims only 590 and 645 nm and the conditions give the tested range, so the omission does not change direction, magnitude or applicability of what is logged; but the 685 nm point is the species' long-wavelength limit and belongs in `conditions` (text below). Line 2 also calls the "5 times" comparison a "limiting result"; the paper uses it as evidence that choices were not random, so "comparison" is the accurate word (uncounted). The Discussion's own hedge is that the experiments "suggest" red sensitivity, "possibly extending into the far-red (700–750 nm) in C. dimidiata"; nothing in either record overstates this. No measure of spectral sensitivity is claimed.
- Grade m right for both (one named species each, measured); "partial": false right.

### Nikbakht and Diamond 2021 (lines 3 and 4), Rattus norvegicus, behaviour_vision

- Line 3 quote: sentence verbatim in four consecutive segments, "Average performance was 84 ± 3% under 626 nm illumination (p=0.158 compared to white light; bootstrap tests illustrated in Figure 2—figure supplement 1), 86 ± 2% under 652 nm (p=0.419 compared to white light), 72 ± 3% under 729 nm (p=0.00 compared to white light), 53 ± 2% under 854 nm (p=0.257 compared to chance), and 50 ± 1% under 930 nm average performance (p=0.316 compared to chance)." The record keeps the start and the end and marks two cuts; MATCH. Value contained.
- Line 4 quote: Discussion sentence "Object discrimination began to degrade when illumination wavelength increased from 652 to 729 nm and was almost nil at 854 nm." MATCH; the record drops the final full stop (uncounted).
- Methods confirmed (quoted): "Four male Long–Evans rats (Charles River Laboratories, Calco, Italy) were used."; "Rats were dark-adapted in a light-free environment for 20–30 min prior to each session."; "Average performance was 87 ± 2% correct under white light"; tactile cues blocked by "A transparent panel in front of the object", apparatus in a Faraday cage. The 854 and 930 nm leakage argument is as recorded. The authors state "our study does not include direct measures of photoreceptor sensitivity", as line 3 says.
- **Counted, lines 3 and 4 (scientific meaning, `conditions`; stimulus limits).** The Figure 1 caption gives the integrated irradiance of each array: "while red 626, 652, and 729 nm LEDs emitted 0.12, 0.28, and 0.04 mW/cm2, respectively)" and "infrared 854 nm and 930 nm LEDs emitted 1.95 and 0.48 mW/cm2, respectively," (MATCH asked for the 729 nm figure). The arrays were not matched: the 729 nm array, where accuracy first falls (72%, p=0.00 against white), was the dimmest of all, a third of the 626 nm array and a seventh of the 652 nm array. Wavelength and irradiance therefore change together across the red series, and the drop at 729 nm cannot be read as a wavelength effect alone; no sentence in Results or Discussion addresses the lower 729 nm power (tool summary). Neither record gives any irradiance, and line 4's value states the decline as a function of wavelength. This is the "crossed experimental factors described as confounded" case of oa-audit.md, read the other way (confounded factors described as one), and it changes the causal reading of the 729 nm result and the applicability of all five accuracies (each holds at one stated power). Counted once per record. Fix below.
- Line 4 is the authors' Discussion summary of line 3's data (652 nm 86%, 729 nm 72%, 854 nm 53%) and adds no measurement; BRIEF's "one line per value" gives it no separate value. No ruling counts a within-unit restatement, so it is not counted for that; I recommend dropping line 4 and keeping line 3, which carries every number. If kept, it needs the same irradiance text.
- Grade m right (one named species, measured; values are group means of four rats, stated); "partial": false right.
