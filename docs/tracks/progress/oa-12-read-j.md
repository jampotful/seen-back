- Entry 9, not read in full. The job's text is the PubMed Central landing page: the abstract and a selected reference list. The full text is a 1.5 MB scanned PDF that did not come through. Three abstract values for Sorghum bicolor logged with `partial: true` and `access` "abstract" (the one change to the copied source object): Ma3 is a PHYB gene encoding a 123-kD phytochrome (frameshift in ma3), Ma3 is one of six genes regulating photoperiod sensitivity of flowering, and the 123-kD phytochrome predominates in light-grown plants. No line in `docs/views/sources/oa-12.jsonl`, so the paper stays wanted.
- Entry 17. Ficus carica, own measurements (`m`): leaf FcFT1 expression rises under 16L/8D and under 8L/16D (two records), peaking 12 h after lights on, and stays low in continuous darkness; expression rises immediately with light and is higher with more light. `g`: light-responsive promoter elements, predicted from sequence. Cited in the Discussion, `m` and `partial`: shading (Matsuura and Araki 1995) and day-length and light-intensity effects on fruit bearing (Teragishi et al. 1998, two papers). Not logged: seasonal expression (May to October, not a light response as measured), organ and node expression, and the transgenic tobacco flowering times (Table 1), which describe tobacco carrying the fig gene.
- Entry 18. Sorghum bicolor, own measurements (`m`): days to flowering of 100 M (PHYB) and 58 M (phyB-1) in long days (126 and 62 days) and short days (59 and 48 days), one record each; the morning and evening peaks of SbPRR37 and SbGHD7 in long days, their persistence in continuous light, PhyB's requirement for the evening peak, the attenuated evening peak in short days, and the de-repression in 58 M of SbEHD1 (about 100-fold), SbCN8 and SbCN12 (more than 100-fold, long days) and SbCN15 (about 60-fold, long and short days, two records). Background or cited, `m` and `partial`: the 12.2 h day length below which energy sorghum flowers, the 55 to 75 days of photoperiod-insensitive grain sorghum, the three phytochrome genes, and the shade-avoidance traits of 58 M. The fetched text has no reference list, so the cited studies are named only by their reference numbers. Not logged: the QTL mapping and the genotype-class flowering times of the F2/F3 population (Table 3, long days only), PHYB and PHYC allele sequences (Tables 1 and 4), and SbCO and clock-gene expression.
- Entry 25. Melia volkensii leaf explants in vitro, own measurements (`m`): callus and adventitious-shoot percentages under white, blue (454 nm), red (660 nm) and far-red (745 nm) light on TDZ and on INCYDE medium (16 records, quoted from the Results sentences, which match Table 3), callus in darkness (Table 3 row), no organized shoots in darkness (two records) and somatic embryo-like structures in darkness on TDZ. Not logged: the white-light comparison of regulators (Table 1) and the cytokinin profiles (Table 2, Figs 3 and 4), which are not responses to light.
- Entry 51. A Viewpoint review on cold-hardiness models, so every record is `partial`. The text never names Ostrya (the reason it was wanted), and Table 1 lists models, not measurements. Logged: the 11 h threshold photoperiod for the first stage of cold acclimation in Pinus radiata (Greer and Warrington 1982, `m`); short days inducing acclimation at 15°C in many species (Plantae, `g`); the effective temperature threshold for stem acclimation, generally below 5 to 10°C, no known specific temperature sensor in plants, and the signals by which freezing can be perceived (Plantae, `m`, statements about plants generally). Not logged: model structures, the walnut acclimation-deacclimation offset (a response curve, not perception) and the phenology and physiology factors.
- Entry 63. A Research Square preprint; its source line had no journal (metadata gap), filled as "Research Square (preprint), posted 27 October 2025" from the first page's posting date, and each record's note says it is a preprint.
