### Line 1, Metellina segmentata, sense_vibration, 256 Hz fork: 58% escape, 36% attack

Quote, verbatim in two abutting pieces in one response, reproduced on this audit's second pass: "The 256 Hz tuning fork largely produces escape behaviours (58% of responses across the two years), " "although there was more variation with this tuning fork (36% showed attack behaviours)." Conditions 1 to 6 met (no dash, degree or minus signs). MATCH, 29 words; value says no more.

Design, verbatim: "We used two tuning forks (256 Hz and 440 Hz) and an 8 cm length of wire"; "the tuning forks would be hit on an object (i.e., a plastic spray water bottle) to generate vibrations"; "the fork would then be brought slowly towards the web to touch its frame"; "The wire would touch the spiders directly on the back of its abdomen."; 2020 order "first the 256 Hz tuning fork" "followed by the 440 Hz tuning fork, and finally the wire"; 2021 "we randomized the order in which the stimuli were presented to each spider"; 50 observed and 38 analysed in 2020, 46 and 40 in 2021; Fisher's exact tests; LMMs for reaction time. Nulls: between years p = 0.81, 0.51, 0.49; order for forks p = 0.584 and 0.333, for wire p = 0.033; spider length and web area all non-significant. Reaction time: "whilst spiders had faster reactions when escaping the 256 Hz tuning fork," " they reacted faster when they attacked the 440 Hz tuning". Discussion: "However, interestingly the 256 Hz tuning fork elicited a number of spiders to react with escape behaviours" " before the tuning fork came into contact with the web, thereby producing a negative reaction time." "This occurred across the 2020 and 2021 methodologies." "This suggests that Metellina spiders are able to detect air-borne movements of predator species" "This phenomenon can be explained by trichobothria, which are cuticular filiform hairs that respond to air movements". No measured amplitude or sound level (tool summary). 440 Hz: "the behavioural responses to this frequency are principally attacking (90% of responses for both years combined)." No-response: "with the highest proportion of 16% found for the wire in 2020".

**Error (design and contrary result omitted).** (a) The method says the fork was "brought slowly toward the web"; the paper says it was brought "to touch its frame", which is what makes it a vibration stimulus. (b) The note says the passage read "does not separate" airborne sound from web vibration, but the paper reports a number of escapes from the 256 Hz fork before contact, in both years, and attributes them to air movement sensed by trichobothria. That bears directly on what the 58% escape figure measures and is omitted. (c) Smaller, carried in the fix: the fork-order and between-year nulls that justify pooling, the opposite reaction-time pattern for the two forks, and the laboratory confirmation of a few individuals. Everything else (dates, n, wild adult females, fixed then randomised order, the wire's order effect, no no-stimulus control, 90% attack at 440 Hz, 16% no-response) is right. Fixable.
