## The fork on line 32

Two light-trap tables list a Lomographa. The canon file holds one of them, and the verdict on line 32 turns on how the other is read.

Table 1 of Nowinszky, Hill, Puskás, Tar and Hufnagel 2022 (10.5772/intechopen.102718; canon line 152, wanted as canon-22) lists Lomographa temerata at row 128: wingspan 24 mm, four trap pairs of the Hungarian network, and "E" in the column for the more efficient light source, meaning equal catches at the black-light and visible-light traps, with no entry for the Nagytétény site. The chapter describes its black light as emitting mainly between 320 and 400 nm. Line 32 names the chapter in `canon` and calls its species list unseen, so under point 8 the row is a lead. It is the kind of row audit 59 set aside for Euproctis similis (equal catches, a weaker case than the Pseudoips row that audit 59 counted).

Appendix 1 of Fayle, Sharp and Majerus 2007 ("The effect of moth trap type on catch size and composition in British Lepidoptera", British Journal of Entomology and Natural History 20: 221-229; BioStor 242149, opened twice) lists the catches of three Robinson traps run side by side at one field centre over six nights in 2001: a standard mercury-vapour bulb, the same bulb behind a Pyrex beaker and a black-light bulb whose spectrum shows only the 366 nm peak. L. temerata has 1, 0 and 0. The paper tests totals, richness, diversity and family composition, and tests no species. This table is outside the canon file and outside the reach of the record's prescribed searches.

I read the Fayle row as no measurement of L. temerata. One moth at a broad-spectrum lamp compares nothing between wavelength bands, and the precedent audit 59 applied rests on a column that states which source caught more over 15 paired traps and in a setting where moths could choose. On that reading line 32 is a confirm. If the desk counts any per-species light-trap table under spectral_range_nm, line 32 becomes an error, the sample holds 1 in 20 (5.0%, 0.1% to 24.9%), which is not under the line, and the 15-record extension listed under "Sample" must run before a verdict; the pool would then stand at 4 in 367 (1.1%) before the extension. I recommend the first reading, and I recommend that the desk decide the Nowinszky "E" rows (L. temerata here, Euproctis similis in audit 59) as one rule.
