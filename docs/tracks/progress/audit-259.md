## Check of the records

### Line 1, Sminthopsis crassicaudata, sense_hearing

Quote MATCH (Results); 25 words; one whole sentence; it holds the value. The next sentence, quoted in `note`, also MATCH: "Currently however, it cannot be established whether 47.5 kHz is the most sensitive frequency or if ABR thresholds decline rapidly at higher frequencies." The abstract agrees: "ABR showed the dunnart is most sensitive to higher frequencies increasing up to 47.5 kHz."

Design (tool summary unless quoted). "Eight fat-tailed dunnarts (Sminthopsis crassicaudata) aged between 12 and 18 months (12–18 g weight)", "of either sex"; origin not stated. "anaesthetised via intraperitoneal injection with ketamine (75 mg/kg) and medetomidine (1 mg/kg)". Body temperature held at 38 °C. Stimuli "pure tone bursts (5 ms duration, 1ms rise-fall-time, rate 10/s)" at "4, 8, 16, 24, 32 and 47.5 kHz", delivered through a plastic cone on a reverse-driven 1/4 inch condenser microphone touching "the lower edge of the left tragus"; calibrated "at the level of the eardrum using a Brüel & Kjær pistonphone (94 dB SPL at 1,000 Hz)". Threshold: "the lowest intensity where peaks I and V could still be identified", 10 dB steps down and 5 dB steps up, three observers within 5 dB. "In view of the sampling rate of our sound card 47.5 kHz was the maximum frequency tested." Threshold values are only in Fig. 2B; the text prints none. The paper gives n = 6 to 8 without saying why n varies, and reports no exclusions.

Against the brief's points: n (8 animals, 6 to 8 per frequency) right; anaesthesia drugs and doses right; the 47.5 kHz range edge is stated in `conditions` and its cause (sound-card sampling rate) is in the paper, so "best frequency not established" is the honest reading and the record makes no upper-limit claim; ABR versus behavioural is stated in `conditions` (the paper makes no behavioural comparison and does not discuss anaesthetic effects); the value ("thresholds fall ... with increasing frequency across the 4 to 47.5 kHz stimulus range; the most sensitive frequency was not established") says no more than the quote and the next sentence. Two small additions are uncounted because neither changes the finding: the six tested frequencies, and the 38 °C body temperature. Omitted contrary or null results: none in the text; the Discussion's comparisons (stripe-faced dunnart best threshold at 10 kHz, northern quoll most sensitive at 10 kHz, short-tailed opossum best at 8 to 12 kHz) are other species and make the dunnart's rising high-frequency sensitivity stand out rather than limiting it. Grade `m` right; full text read, so not partial.

### Line 2, Ischnocnema parva, sense_hearing

Quote MATCH (Results, PDF); 26 words; one whole sentence; holds the value. The pooled Brachycephalus sentence the note cites is verbatim except that the PDF has an en dash ("200–1200 Hz") where the note has a hyphen; uncounted, since it sits in `note` and is not the evidence quote.

Design (tool summary unless quoted). ABR: "three I. parva (all males)" (Fig. 2 caption "Ischnocnema parva (blue, n = 3)"); "Individuals were lightly sedated with a topical application of benzocaine gel" (10% or 20% by size) for the whole experiment; stimuli "A series of pure tone bursts of duration 25 ms and ranging from 200 Hz to 6 kHz", from below threshold to 110 dB SPL in 10 dB steps; loudspeaker 80 cm from the frog, calibrated with a ½-inch G.R.A.S. microphone 1 cm above the head, itself calibrated at 94 dB at 1 kHz with a B&K Type 4231 calibrator; in I. parva the active electrode sat behind the tympanic membrane, the second in the nostril on the same side; "an acoustically isolated chamber". No ABR temperature is given (23 °C housing and 22 to 25 °C for the laser-vibrometry work only), so `conditions` "recording temperature not stated" is right. Thresholds per frequency were "determined as the level at the intercept between a linear regression on the steep part of the curve" and the recording noise, and species curves were drawn from "generalized additive models (GAM) ... fitted to the hearing sensitivity data". Fig. 2 caption gives the I. parva call range as 2.87 to 3.53 kHz.

Band edges (the brief's question). The text gives no list or step of test frequencies, no I. parva threshold in dB, and no statement about 1200 to 2000 Hz; thresholds are in Fig. 2b only. So the edges 200–1200 and 2000–3000 Hz are the authors' round verbal ranges read off GAM-smoothed species curves from three animals, and the lower edge, 200 Hz, is the lowest frequency tested. They are not measured band limits. The value presents them as the paper does ("two ABR sensitivity peaks: 200 to 1200 Hz ... and 2000 to 3000 Hz"), the conditions carry the "presumably corresponding" caveat and n = 3, and the record claims no threshold level and no edge precision beyond the quote. I leave it uncounted for that reason, as harmless compression under oa-audit.md: nothing in the record states a measured edge, and the quote, which is the authors' own wording, is the whole basis of the value. Recommended (uncounted): say in `method` that the curves are GAM fits and that 200 Hz is the floor of the stimulus set.

Other context. Omitted contrary or null results: none for I. parva. Calls (2.87 to 3.53 kHz) overlap the BP peak band only partly, and the paper does not relate I. parva's hearing to its call; worth a note line (uncounted). The note's hold-out of B. ephippium and B. pitanga is correct (pooled sentence). Grade `m` right for the printed name (see Names); full text read, so not partial.
