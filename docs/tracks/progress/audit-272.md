Counted once for line 1 (name fault, above; plus a scientific-context omission, fixable). The Discussion gives more for the null than the record carries: "All analyzed burrow systems originate from a single small area in the Upper Galilee Mountains," "and six of nine examined burrow systems were located in basaltic soils." It names two non-magnetic explanations: "The avoidance of crossing a neighboring burrow system might influence the burrow systems orientation" and "the population density in basaltic soil is five times higher than in rendzina soil (Lövy et al., 2015)." It opens with "The reasons, why no directional digging preference was found in S. galili, can be manifold," and notes that magnetoreception has been shown in Spalax judaei, a close relative (tool summary). The record carries only the basalt-signature and anomaly sentence, without saying that most of the sampled burrows were in basalt, and leaves out the neighbour-avoidance and density explanations and the authors' own reading that the null is unexplained. Filed under sense_magnetic, a null without these reads as evidence that the animal lacks a magnetic heading. That is a causal-interpretation fault under the 29 October rule.

### Goller et al. 2018 (lines 4 and 5)

Quotes MATCH (Results): line 4 "Cowbirds significantly avoided the sides illuminated by the 470-nm LED (16/17, P = 0.000137)" with 630 nm 13/17, P = 0.0245; line 5 380 nm 9/17 P = 0.500, 525 nm 7/16 P = 0.773, white 8/17 P = 0.685 (tool gave the figures, not the sentence verbatim; values agree). Whole sentences; values within the quotes. Trait behaviour_vision fits (colour-dependent avoidance).

Lines 4 and 5 share `conditions` and both are counted (wrong facts and scientific context, fixable).
(a) Wrong fact, design. `conditions` says "each bird tested on one LED type only". The Methods say "We tested five different light wavelengths and an individual cowbird was exposed to each wavelength only once." and "In total, we conducted 20 trials with 20 different cowbirds for each of the five LED panels." With 33 birds and 100 trials, birds were reused across panels, once per wavelength. The per-panel tests are therefore not on independent sets of birds.
(b) Wrong fact, control. `conditions` says "A light-off control (18 birds, pooled across two phases)". The Results give "(in 21 out of 39 instances, individuals chose left, in 18 out of 39 instances, individuals chose right)", pooled across two phases by resampling ("we randomly sampled one of the four trials" "for each of the seven first-phase individuals 1,000 times"). 18 is the count of right choices, not of birds.
(c) Scientific context, causal interpretation. `conditions` says "brightness and contrast are confounded with wavelength; no table of LED radiance was found". The paper modelled each LED's contrast for cowbird vision (Vorobyev-Osorio tetrachromatic model, cowbird sensitivities and cone densities, Weber fraction 0.1, sky background, three simulated intensities; tool summary), and the Discussion says the two avoided LEDs "happened to be the ones" "with the highest chromatic contrast of the ones considered (both >35 JNDs)" and "had much lower levels of achromatic contrast" "than the 380-nm (ultraviolet) lights used (Fig. 3)." So the paper does not leave brightness and colour confounded: the brightest and highest achromatic-contrast panel (UV) was not avoided, and avoidance tracked chromatic contrast. The record leaves out the variable the paper uses to explain its result. The Discussion's own limit, "Our findings are limited only to steady lights under diurnal ambient light conditions and a single avian species", is also missing (uncounted on its own).
