## Desk disposition after audit 237
Audit 237 found 3 of 5 records in error (60%), all fixable, so all 5 were merged with the auditor's corrections. The three Ciona records said every animal was Ciona intestinalis type B per the Methods; the Methods name two species (C. intestinalis type B for the in situ maps and microinjection, C. robusta for the electroporated reporter constructs and the stable line behind the coexpression figures) and never say which species the behavioural larvae were. The two eye_role records stay under C. intestinalis because the identities rest on the type B in situ maps and the connectome, with the note saying why. The phototaxis behaviour_vision record is refiled at genus Ciona, grade g, because the species of the behavioural larvae is unstated; its conditions now carry about 3000 lux, 18 C, about 25 hpf larvae, the perampanel concentration as printed ('5 mm' or '15 mM' by supplier), no named statistical test, and its eye field says the AMPA receptor is also in the two antenna cells and a small cluster of pBV neurons, so the block is not limited to the visual relay; the note adds that perampanel-treated larvae could still swim and that touch and PR-II dimming responses were not inhibited. Uncounted fixes applied: the GCaMP sentence now gives both reasons (spectral overlap and poor visualization of inhibition), and the Lymnaea behaviour note says responses were scored as distance travelled per phase, not movement toward the source. The progress note line that every note carries type B animals is wrong. Lesson: when a paper uses two species, the record's taxon must follow the one the measured animals belong to, and the genus is the right rank when the paper does not say. Held out: Eptatretus stoutii eye (Royal Society page blocked, licence unconfirmed), Hermissenda (Alkon 1973 PMC page CC BY-NC-SA, no spectral values in the abstracts), Pecten irradians (not Placopecten), Pyrrhocoris, and groups never reached (Haliotis, Idiosepia, Placopecten, Euprymna, Chiton, Dolomedes, Tegenaria, Lithobius, Dytiscus).
