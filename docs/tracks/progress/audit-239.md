## Checks per paper

Piniak et al. 2016 (lines 1, 2). Both quotes verbatim, whole Results sentences with the parenthetical table and figure citations and the clause after the semicolon kept. Methods: five juvenile turtles, mean 34 kg (26 to 38 kg), 65 cm curved carapace length; background noise "likely masked low-frequency stimuli". Missing from both records: the signal section says responses were recorded "to frequencies between 50 and 3200 Hz", and Table 2 lists the frequencies presented as 50, 100, 200, 300, 400, 600, 800, 1600 and 3200 Hz. So the lower edge of both ranges, 50 Hz, is the lowest frequency presented, and the true lower limit is unknown. My fetch's reading of Tables 1 and 2 (a tool summary, not quoted) also has only 3 of 5 turtles responding at 1600 Hz underwater, 2 of 5 at 50 Hz in air and 4 of 5 at 800 Hz in air, so the range edges are reached by subsets of the five animals; the records give no per-edge counts. Stimuli were attenuated from "the highest level that could be generated at each frequency", so a "no response" is bounded by that ceiling as well. The brief lists stimulus-set bounds on range edges as required context, and the desk carried it for the Trachemys lines and not here.

Cobo-Cuan et al. 2022 (lines 3, 4). The quote is verbatim, under the heading "Results and discussion", subheading "DPOAEs in O. tormota". The two sentences before it read: "In most of our DPOAE analyses, we refer to the primary frequency f1, which is commonly used in the literature, thus allowing direct comparison with other species. Nevertheless, also revealing the frequencies at which the cubic distortions 2f1–f2 and 2f2–f1 were detected helps to grasp the remarkable extent of the hearing range in O. tormota." So the quoted 2 to 7.4 kHz and 2.5 to 20 kHz are the frequencies of the emitted distortion products (including 2f2–f1, which lies above f2), and not primary frequencies. The records' `method` says "range of primary frequencies at which a DPOAE response appeared", which is wrong. The male note's "the 20 kHz upper figure sits at the edge of the stimulus set" is also wrong: Methods record f2 "from 1 to 18 kHz (in 200-Hz steps)" at 80 dB SPL, and the paper's own highest f1 is 18.19 kHz, so 20 kHz is a distortion frequency above every primary presented. The paper's primary-frequency spans, which it says it uses for comparison with other species, are: females, DP2f1–f2 above noise "when f1 was between 2.27 and 6.73 kHz, except for one individual in which the DPOAE response extended to 8.36 kHz"; males, "from 3.09 to 14.73 kHz", with two males to 18.19 kHz, "the highest f1 frequency used". Presence criterion: at least noise + 2SD. Sample (10 non-gravid females, 10 males), anesthesia, capture dates, Fu Creek locality, peaks and body masses (16.72 and 3.19 g) in the records agree with the paper. A minor inconsistency in the paper (f2 ceiling 18 kHz at f2/f1 = 1.1 against a highest f1 of 18.19 kHz) is the authors', not the desk's.

Wang et al. 2022 (lines 5 to 8). The brief asked for this quote to be checked carefully. Verbatim, confirmed on four passes: "The hearing sensitivity bandwidths were 0.2–0.5, 0.2–1.1, 0.2–1.3, and 0.2–1.5 kHz in the temperature groups of 10, 20, 30, and 40°C, respectively (Figure 2)." En dashes, degree sign and the closing "(Figure 2)." are as printed; the sentence sits in Results under "Hearing Sensitivity Bandwidth and Auditory Brainstem Response Threshold", as `where` says. Each value matches its group. Stimuli: tone bursts "from 0.2 to 1.5 kHz with 100 Hz increments", n = 7, 7, 6 and 12, 32 one-year-old males: as recorded. Thresholds were "defined by the lowest stimulus level confirmed by two trained observers". Not in any of the four records: each turtle was placed in a climate box "to acclimatize for 3 h", with cloacal temperature checked before and after recording, so the groups are acute body-temperature exposures and not seasonal acclimation; the Discussion gives the stimulus ceiling ("the upper sound level limit of stimulus was 85 dB SPL"), so a frequency counts as outside the bandwidth when its threshold is above 85 dB SPL; the Methods do not define "hearing sensitivity bandwidth" (the Introduction defines it as "the range of acoustic frequency that the auditory system of an animal could accept"); and the Discussion says the species "prefers 25–30°C" (citing Ma 2013) while the Introduction frames 40 °C as "close to the upper limit of heat resistance". For line 8 the last point changes how the widest range reads in a species cell (an acute exposure near the heat limit, above the preferred range), so line 8 is counted. For lines 5 to 7 the 3 h and 85 dB points are recommended additions, not counted.
