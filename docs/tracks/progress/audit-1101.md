## Stimulus-level ruling

Methods 2.3, rejoined: "After male Little Torrent Frogs were located, we presented four types of call stimuli stochastically using a small speaker (Clip3, JBL, Northridge, CA, USA) from a distance of ~1 m." | "Sound pressure levels were about 80 dB at such distance, which is approximate to the call intensity of this species in natural environments [30]." For the other two: "The playback distance, however, was longer than the Little Torrent Frogs (~1.5 m)." | "In such distance, four sound stimuli were about 78 dB during playback tests." Ruling: **KEEP**. The level is stated at the speaker-to-frog distance, which is the frog's position, the same form audit 1096 kept (75 dB about 1 m from the speaker where the male sat). Per-animal responses with n and a control stimulus (territory song) are printed. The boring alternative reading: "about" and "~" mark nominal figures, the meter position, dB reference, weighting and stimulus band are unprinted, so the level actually at each frog is approximate; if the owner reads it that way, all four lines fall with the held-out lines of audits 317 to 1099.

Wild status: Section 2.1 places testing at a mountain stream near Shuiman Station and a field near Shuiman Village (Wuzhishan Natural Reserve) and bush forest near Honglin Road (Dongzhaigang Natural Reserve); Section 2.3 says males were located and tested where found; no capture, holding or indoor test is printed. The paper never calls its subjects "wild" (the word appears only in the Introduction and Discussion about frogs in general). Ruling: free-living in place is stated enough for "wild"; the records already flag the inference. Kept as is.
