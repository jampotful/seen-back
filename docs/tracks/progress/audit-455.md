- **W/ and W/o.** The legend defines "FP, facial pit" but not W/ or W/o. The Methods code "(5) the presence of pits (present and absent)", the Results compare "the snakes with and without pit", and Figure 5's caption says "with and without pit"; in d339 the pit viper Deinagkistrodon acutus is "W/". W/o therefore means no facial pit. The records say the legend leaves the codes undefined and repeat them as printed, which is true of the legend and asserts nothing wrong; no record claims a pit effect. Uncounted; optional fix below (applied in the line 3 replacement).
- **Methods, sample size, specimen state.** "We examined the specimens of 33 snake species deposited in the National Museum of Natural Sciences (NMNS) in Taiwan."; "The sample size of each species ranged from 3 to 207 individuals, for a total of 1,176 individuals." The full text has no sentence on preservation, fixation, alcohol, formalin, sex, juveniles or ontogeny; the sea snakes (Hydrophis, Laticauda) are museum specimens like the rest, state unstated. Every record gives N and SD and says sex and fixation are unstated. Small N (P. mucosa 5, B. kraepelini 6, Pareas formosensis 9, Oligodon formosanus 9) is stated in each. Met.
- **Outliers.** Ptyas dhumnades 6.53 and P. mucosa 6.18 mm sit with P. korros 7.13 (d339) in a large-eyed genus; Hydrophis melanocephalus 2.02 mm is the smallest of the ten and is the small-headed sea snake (HW 12.90). The paper names none of these species in its prose, so no author comment was omitted. Ptyas major (not filed here) is the odd congener the desk flagged; not in this unit.
- **Omitted results.** As audit 454 ruled: the abstract's findings (terrestrial and/or diurnal snakes larger relative eye size; no foraging or pit effect) and the negative allometry of ED vs HW are cross-species results on relative eye size. The Results' habitat comparison ("marginally higher compared with the seawater type"; "when comparing marine and freshwater types") concerns relative size by habitat class and does not qualify a raw species mean; no record claims a habitat or activity effect, and HW is in every quote. Uncounted.
- **Tu (2004).** These records say "Habitat, activity and foraging codes come from Tu (2004) as the paper states", matching the legend ("habitat types, diel activity patterns, and foraging strategies were based on Tu (2004)"); the Methods also attribute the presence of pits to Tu. Met (audit 454's pit/foraging slip is absent here).
- **Families.** Table 1 as printed (full text): Colubridae (Ptyas dhumnades, P. mucosa, Boiga kraepelini, Oligodon formosanus, Fowlea piscator), Elapidae (Hydrophis melanocephalus, Laticauda semifasciata, L. colubrina), Pseudaspididae (Psammodynastes pulverulentus), Viperidae (header block that ends with Pareas formosensis). Reptile Database Higher Taxa: Ptyas dhumnades, P. mucosa, Oligodon formosanus, Boiga kraepelini "Colubridae, Colubrinae, ..."; Fowlea "Colubridae (Natricinae), ..."; Hydrophis melanocephalus "Elapidae (Hydrophiinae), ..."; both Laticauda "Elapidae (Laticaudinae), ..."; Pareas formosensis "Pareidae, Caenophidia, ..."; Psammodynastes pulverulentus "Psammodynastidae, Elapoidea, ...". Every record's `family` follows the Reptile Database. Where paper and database agree (eight lines), the record matches both. Where they disagree, the database governs: the paper's Viperidae placement of Pareas is a table-layout or classification slip (Pareas is not a viper and has no pit, "W/o"), and Pseudaspididae for Psammodynastes is an older placement. The records do not mention the paper's headers; harmless (family is a placement field, value and taxon unaffected), optional note below. Psammodynastidae opens a family node no other main row uses; it is the authority's current family, so no fix.
- **Names (Reptile Database, opened).** Ptyas dhumnades (CANTOR, 1842), Taiwan listed; Ptyas mucosa (LINNAEUS, 1758), Taiwan listed; Hydrophis melanocephalus GRAY, 1849, "Coasts of Taiwan and Guangdong ..."; Laticauda semifasciata (REINWARDT, 1837), Taiwan listed; Laticauda colubrina (SCHNEIDER, 1799), "Coasts of Taiwan (China)"; Pareas formosensis (VAN DENBURGH, 1909), Taiwan listed, type locality Kanshirei, Formosa; Psammodynastes pulverulentus (BOIE, 1827), "Taiwan (incl. All accepted header names with Taiwan in range. **Fowlea piscator (SCHNEIDER, 1799)** is a valid header name, but its distribution ends "China (Chekiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, Guangdong, Nan Ao Island, Hainan, Guangxi, Yunnan)" and mentions Taiwan nowhere (distribution, comment or synonyms). **Fowlea flavipunctata (HALLOWELL, 1860)** lists Taiwan in its distribution, carries Natrix piscator synonyms, and cites Vogel & David 2012 ("A revision of the species group of Xenochrophis piscator"). repfocus.dk agrees: Taiwan under F. flavipunctata, not F. piscator.
