### Szabo and Whiting 2022 (lines 4 and 5), behaviour_vision

Both quotes MATCH as whole sentences on the BORIS PDF (line 4 abstract, line 5 Results). Design (BORIS PDF, two passes): pilot ("Pilot test" heading; "The pilot was conducted from the end of April to the beginning of June 2019") with lizards ID8 and ID9; ten trials per session (target training 10; discrimination 1 training plus 9 test trials); criterion "8/9 correct choices or better in each of two consecutive sessions" after at least three sessions; descriptive only; reversal: ID8 0 correct, ID9 did not complete a reversal trial. Pattern test: six naive lizards (IDs differ from the pilot), grey cards with two or eight black 10 by 10 mm squares, three rewarded for each; five procedural changes from session 12; two of six (ID4, ID7, both in the eight-squares group) reached criterion after 53 and 54 sessions, after the last change (incorrect card replaced by a plain grey card); improvement over 51 sessions small and non-significant (tool summary). Authors' main explanation is overlap of the two middle squares prompting generalisation; they set aside low acuity and inattention and hypothesise handling stress. Lizards wild-caught near Fowlers Gap, NSW, March 2018.

- **Line 4, counted (scientific meaning; value misstates the result).** The value says "two lizards learned to choose light blue against dark blue (criterion reached after 6 and 10 sessions)". The paper assigns light blue as correct to ID8 (criterion after 6 sessions) and dark blue as correct to ID9 (after 10 sessions): "One of the two lizards used in the pilot was assigned light blue as the correct stimulus", "Lizard ID8 took six sessions", "Lizard ID9 took 10 sessions" (verbatim excerpts). One lizard learned to choose dark blue. The record's own conditions ("one lizard assigned light blue as correct") contradict its value. Counterbalanced assignment is what makes the result a discrimination rather than a preference for the lighter card, so the misstatement changes the finding's interpretation. Fix below. The brightness caveat in the note stands (the paper gives no luminance or chromatic measurement).
- Line 5: value matches the quote and says no more; it is time-bounded ("in the first 11 sessions") and the conditions state that the procedure was then changed, that some lizards reached criterion and that the first 11 sessions are not a measure of the lizards' limit; the note says a failure to learn is not an acuity limit, which agrees with the authors setting aside low acuity. The limiting context is therefore conveyed in the record, so the omission of the exact later outcome is harmless compression, and the null does not say more than the quote. As a behaviour_vision record it is a fair report of a visual discrimination task outcome under a stated procedure. Confirm. Uncounted: `value` or `conditions` would be clearer with "after five procedural changes, two of six (both rewarded for eight squares) reached criterion at sessions 53 and 54", and "which the authors tie to handling and stimulus processing" should read "which the authors attribute mainly to overlap of the two middle squares (generalisation), with handling stress a further hypothesis".
