## A fault outside the count: Tympanuchus and main's grouse rows

All four Tympanuchus records (60 to 63) say that "Main's grouse eye rows belong to the black grouse (Lyrurus tetrix, Potier et al. 2026)". Line 60 adds, in its reason for Caves et al. 2023, that "main holds rows for 143 of its taxa, and no Tympanuchus is among them", and its note says none of those taxa "is a prairie grouse". Main holds 143 distinct taxa under 10.1242/jeb.246063 (birds.jsonl under the year 2023; canon-5, oa-1 and recheck-27-fixed under 2024), and among them are Tympanuchus phasianellus (acuity_cpd 13 cycles per degree from retinal ganglion cell density, canon-5#175 and oa-1#3748; eye axial length 13.7 mm, oa-1#3749) and Bonasa umbellus, besides Gallus gallus, Alectoris chukar, Coturnix japonica and Phasianus colchicus. This fits the queue listing no acuity_cpd cell for any of the three Tympanuchus species (my inference). The statements are wrong, and the verdicts still stand: the sharp-tailed grouse's value is an acuity, filed under acuity_cpd, and none of the four records concerns that trait. The value comes from Lisney et al. 2012 (Potier et al. 2026 cites it so), a retinal-topography study of seven galliforms, which bears on line 60 only through the fork below.

**The fork on line 60.** If interreceptor_deg means photoreceptor spacing, as main's rows of the trait (spiders, molluscs, crustaceans and one human row) and the oa-1 note separating axial length from "retinal receptor spacing" suggest, Lisney et al. 2012 cannot serve the cell and line 60 stands as written. If it also takes a ganglion-cell sampling angle, the peak ganglion cell density of T. phasianellus in Lisney et al. 2012 would give one (that the paper prints the density is my inference from its acuity estimate, which needs it), and line 60 would hold `found: none` over a work its own note misdescribes. I count under the first sense, which is main's practice. Under the second the 20 sampled records would hold 1 error (5.0%, 0.1% to 24.9%), which is not under the 5% line, and the extension would have to run before a verdict.

## Limits of this audit

WebSearch returned titles and links with no snippets, so I judged results by title and by the pages I opened. The fetch tool reads a page through a summarizer, so a statement that a page "names no Synoeca" rests on the text the summarizer received, and for long PDFs (Ruck 1961, the zebra and polarizer preprints) that text may lack parts of the paper. Pages behind bot checks, login screens, robots rules, time-outs, binary returns and redirects to hosts no search of mine returned stayed unread, and I looked for no other route to them. Statements about main come from the gap queue, the canon file, gbif-names.tsv and both row stores on origin/main, searched by taxon, family, author string, title and DOI; a source cited under another spelling would escape that search.
