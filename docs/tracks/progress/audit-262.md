## Ruling question 1: is a noise-exposure effect a sense_hearing record?

What main does. tools/lit/extract.py defines sense_hearing as "hearing range and best frequency; [low, high] in Hz, best frequency in note", and almost every sense_hearing line on main is a threshold, best frequency or range. Main does also hold behavioural responses to sound under sense_hearing, each worded as a behaviour: Schizocosa ocreata, "airborne noise (white noise 0-4 kHz, bird calls) reaches the leaf litter as vibration and lowers mating success" (audit/corrected/spider.jsonl 200), a noise-disturbance effect of the same kind as this unit; Gryllus bimaculatus, a preceding tone raising the jump ratio, method "not an audiogram" (desk-d155 line 10); sphingid responses to bat sound (oa-9 lines 144 to 146); goldfish startle levels (desk-d168 line 6). Audit 253 held that a startle record is admissible when it is labelled as startle and cannot be read as a hearing threshold, and counted the one record (d168 line 7) whose bare value would be read as a hearing frequency. No sense_hearing line on main covers a decapod, bivalve or copepod.

Which way the rulings point. They point to admitting these records as labelled behavioural responses. None of the eight values can be read as a threshold, best frequency or range, and each `note` already says a behavioural change is not a hearing threshold, so under audit 253 none is counted for labelling. Even so, the label belongs in `value` (prefix text below). It is still an owner question, for three reasons the rulings do not settle. (a) These are disturbance or stress effects of loud playback (crowding away from the speaker, slower feeding, less maternal care, valve closure). Only detection follows from them; they give no sensitivity, and the authors themselves offer stress, distraction or masking (Azarm-Karnagh Discussion; Kühn Discussion; tool summaries). (b) None of the five papers measured particle motion, and Ledoux measured no substrate vibration. Oysters, and probably the copepods, would detect particle motion or vibration (main's Pagurus bernhardus is under sense_vibration), so sense_hearing as against sense_vibration is open, like the airborne-against-substrate question of audits 213, 242 and 258 in reverse. (c) Lines 7 and 8 are nulls of a feeding effect. A null disturbance effect says nothing about detection, as the records' own notes say, and main's sense_hearing nulls are about organs or deafness. My recommendation: admit lines 1 to 6 with the labelled values below if the owner accepts noise-effect records at all; hold lines 7 and 8 until the owner rules, since as sense_hearing evidence they carry no information about hearing.

Sal Moyano et al. 2023 (lines 3, 4). Both quotes MATCH (PDF; abstract). Confirmed: Mar Chiquita, late-stage ovigerous females, carapace 22.5 to 26.5 mm, N = 70 (six sound treatments and a silent control, 10 each), 1.2 m circular PVC tank, Lubell UW30 (100 Hz to 10 kHz), white noise peak 6177 Hz 126 dB, motorboat peak 3442 Hz 130 dB, black drum (Pogonias courbina) chorus low-passed at 1000 Hz, peak 195 Hz 121 dB, re 1 µPa; particle motion not measured. **Contrary result misstated.** Both notes say "Only the high-rate pattern had an effect; low-rate stimuli did not differ from the control". Low-rate white noise was not significant (still position p = 0.06), and neither was low-rate fish chorus (PDF and /full page, two passes). The Results sentence, as read on /full, names "both low and high rate motorboat, and high rate white noise" as raising still position. The paper's own Discussion line, "no negative effects on behavior of the low rate anthropogenic stimuli", conflicts with its Table 1 (tool summary). Also, chelae probing was not analysed alone. Kühn et al. 2023 (line 6). Quote MATCH (PDF, abstract).
