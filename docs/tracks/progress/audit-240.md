Two context faults shared by all five records:
1. Sample size misstated. The `conditions` say "about 260 insects used in total; the paper does not say which of these were in each recording". The paper does say it: the Figure 2 caption gives "The number of tested insects for each data point (N) = 30", and the Methods say "the mean firing rate of 30 test beetles was recorded and analyzed at each temperature"; the Statistical Analyses give N = 17 to 30 per data point for the burst analyses (lower N, 17 and 19, for DHN and CHN bursting below 35 °C; tool summary). The caller listed sample sizes as context to carry; here the record states the paper is silent when it is not.
2. Humidity omitted. Thermal stimulation was "performed at constant absolute humidity (9 g m–3)", so relative humidity fell as temperature rose; the Discussion gives 13.7% RH at 45 °C for that absolute humidity. These are hygro-thermal neurons named moist-hot and dry-hot, so a rate change across 20 to 35 °C in this series is a response to rising temperature at falling RH; the two were not varied independently. No record mentions humidity. The authors argue RH does not drive the bursting (Discussion: "our results show no indications that RH might be involved in spike bursting response"), which line 5's note partly carries; the tool returned no such argument for the mean-firing-rate series of lines 1 to 4. This changes the causal reading of lines 1 to 4 (whether the CHN fall and DHN rise are temperature responses alone), so it is counted there.

Trait fit: firing rates of antennal thermosensitive neurons under steady temperatures fit sense_thermal as physiological records; the MHN null (line 4) is a legitimate null. Line 5's value adds "few neurons burst below 30 C", which is in a separate Results sentence and not in the quote, so the value says more than the quote.
