## Ruling on the sex-count and 248-vs-210 flags
No drop. The row values are printed and internally coherent, and the unit misread the footnote. The Table 1 footnote says 'An equal number of males and females were used to calculate the mean population mass and RF'. In every mixed-sex row the population n is exactly twice the smaller sex count (LS 18 = 2 x 9; SKK 22 = 2 x 11; HDH 12 = 2 x 6; DHC 46 = 2 x 23; KNY 4 = 2 x 2; BAV 20 = 2 x 10; TF 36 = 2 x 18; my arithmetic), so the population mean appears to come from a sex-balanced subset while the sex columns use all bats of each sex. The unit's statements that the sex counts 'do not add to the population n' in contradiction of the footnote and that 'the counts are unequal' contrary to the footnote misstate the paper as inconsistent where it is not. The 248 in the Results against 210 in the population column (about 241 in the sex columns; Sleepy Hollow female n not printed) is a real, unexplained paper-level discrepancy: kept as a note.

## Omitted significant result
The Results report that sex significantly influenced echolocation within populations, 'with females emitting higher frequencies than males' (GLM, P < 0.001), with no significant sex-by-population interaction. None of the five records says so. For the male-only colonies ZPK, DHL and BKL this is a sample-composition confound: the all-male mean is not directly comparable with the sex-balanced means of mixed colonies. For LS and SKK it explains why the footnoted sex balance matters. Error under the rules (omitted significant result / sample composition), fixable.
