## Lines 1 and 2, Cephaloscyllium isabella, saccular hair cells 4,830 (smallest) and 14,492 (largest)

Results: "For example, the smallest C. isabellum had an approximated 4,830 saccular HCs," + "while the largest had a 3 fold increase to approximately 14,492." **Quote MATCH** (two adjoining pieces), 23 words, a whole sentence. Statistics: "HCT measurements from 18 individuals (15 saccules, 16 lagenas, 17 utricles, and 18 macula neglectas)". Methods: about 15% of each otolith macula counted in three regions and "HCT in these maculae was estimated by extrapolating the overall HCD to the total macular area." The paper prints no per-fish or size-class table; the two values are the extremes of a 15-saccule series, filed as smallest and largest, which does not imply a species mean. Area sentence: "...from 792,153 μm2 in a 40 cm juvenile fish" + "to 1,524,930 μm2 in an 81 cm adult fish."

Faults (scientific meaning, counted once per record): (a) conditions assign body sizes to the counted fish ("about 40 cm", "about 81-82 cm"). The paper prints no length for the smallest and largest saccular counts; the 40 and 81 cm lengths belong to the area sentence, and with 15 of 18 saccules counted the smallest counted fish need not be the 40.5 cm one. A per-size reference value carries a size the source does not give. (b) conditions give n as 18 individuals; the saccular series is 15 saccules. (c) method says "hair cell counts", but the totals are extrapolated from about 15% of the macula. Each changes how the number reads as a size-specific count. **Lines 1 and 2 FIXABLE (correct).**
