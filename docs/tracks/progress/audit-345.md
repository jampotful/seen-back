### Line 5, Danaus plexippus, no magnetic-compass orientation northward

Abstract, verbatim, whole, on both passes: "We found that summer monarchs would right themselves, but did not use a magnetic compass to orient northward." **Quote MATCH**, 18 words; value says no more and is stated as the quote's null.

Design, verbatim: "Both sexes of adult Eastern North American summer monarchs" "were collected on the wing from 24 June to 1 September 2025" (University of Cincinnati Center for Field Studies, Harrison, OH; wild; total number not stated); "The coils were used to keep the magnetic field consistent with that of the surrounding area"; "monarchs were clamped head-down and released to initiate a response to gravity"; "a mesh wall (32 cm × 32 cm), perpendicular to the ground to serve as the plane of movement"; "Orientation scores were noted once the monarch in a trial maintained a position for at least 30 s."; "The final orientation of a monarch during a trial was measured with a protractor from the video recording."; 0 degrees is the "expected null orientation of 0 degrees (response to gravity with no orientation in response to the magnetic field)"; "we would expect summer monarchs to instead orient northward (poleward)"; "a source of diffused, non-directional light that contained the relevant UV light stimulus"; "Only two individuals in both the long-term and short-term groups completed the righting behavior across all three trials"; Discussion "Since we used diffused light, we did not test the possibility that they may use directional light cues for orientation in". Results: "there was a significant mean group orientation across all trials, with mean orientations not different than zero"; "There was no difference in individual behavior based on treatment or group; all coefficients overlapped with zero". Table 1 N (tool summary): control 14/13/12, long-term 27/5/12, short-term 5/2/2; the short-term treatment and post-treatment trials (N = 2) have no Rayleigh or V-test (tool summary). The wall's alignment to north is not given on the page (tool summary).

Ruling on the weak point. A righting assay in an unaltered field can register a null "did not orient northward under the local field": the 0 degree null is the gravity response and the alternative, a poleward deviation, is stated in advance. It cannot test whether monarchs sense the field, since nothing was shifted: no rotated-field or reversed-inclination control exists, so a null cannot separate "no magnetic compass" from "the righting response does not express one in summer monarchs under diffused light", and a positive result could not have been tied to the field either. The value repeats the authors' wording and says no more than the quote, and the record's own conditions carry the "does not exclude magnetoreception" caveat; so it is fileable as a behavioural null (owner question 2: yes, with the fixes below). Wild-caught summer adults, not captive-reared; heat treatments in the lab.

**Error (overstatement in conditions; design omitted).** (a) "every group's mean orientation did not differ from 0 degrees" overstates: two trial cells (short-term treatment and post-treatment, N = 2) had no test, and only two individuals in each heat group completed all three trials. (b) The geometry that defines the null (head-down clamp on a vertical mesh wall, 0 degrees = gravity response, northward as the expected magnetic response, wall alignment not stated) and the diffused UV-containing light with directional light untested are omitted; without them "null" cannot be read. Fixable.
