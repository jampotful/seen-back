## Scientific context and counting (29 October 2026)

This clarification governs evidence audits, including evidence filed by desk screening units. It supersedes audit 16's blanket exclusion of `method` and `conditions` changes from the error count; historical reports retain their original decisions.

- Read the whole available abstract and the surrounding result and methods passages when accessible. Judge `value`, `method` and `conditions` together against that context, not only against the selected quote. Do not infer missing context from an inaccessible full text.
- Count `correct` when omitted or misstated context changes the finding's direction, magnitude or practical scale, applicability, or causal interpretation. Examples include a low absolute outcome despite a relative improvement; a null result or exception that limits a claimed effect; and crossed experimental factors described as confounded. A verbatim quote, `and/or`, or `partial: true` does not excuse losing an explicit consequential qualification. Put the qualification in `value` when it limits the result; put the experimental design in `method`/`conditions`, and identify the supporting passage in `where` or `note` without altering an exact quote.
- Harmless compression can remain `confirm` when the omitted detail does not change those interpretations, or the same limitation is already unambiguously conveyed elsewhere in the record. Give the specific reason for leaving each flagged omission uncounted. Do not require every source detail in every field. Bibliographic formatting or metadata faults remain separate only when they do not change source identity or scientific interpretation.
- Count each affected record once, even if several fields need correction. Report the record id, verdict, affected fields, supporting source location, and why the fault is material or harmless. Distinguish quote-form faults from scientific-meaning faults in the reasons; both count under the existing verdict rules. Keep unverifiable claims separate. A flagged scientific-meaning fault must be corrected before acceptance even if a pooled rate passes; the existing sampling and rate thresholds otherwise stay unchanged.

For calibration, see `docs/tracks/progress/reassessment-158-159.md`: both targeted records require `correct`; preserving a printed title typo is harmless. An audit note alone does not repair the accepted record.
