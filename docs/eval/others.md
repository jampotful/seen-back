**How "expected nearby" works and how it is combined with the vision score.**

From the Geomodel post, 21 September 2023 (https://www.inaturalist.org/blog/84677; WebFetch, the first four rechecked in the second sitting):
- "Top 1 suggestion accuracy improved from 75% to 83% (+8%) by weighting the raw Computer Vision scores" (with the old 1-degree grid)
- "Weighting with the Geomodel instead improved Top 1 suggestion accuracy to 87% (+12%)."
- "Version 2.7, released today, is about a 4% improvement over the 1-degree grid approach for Top 1 suggestion accuracy"
- "We use an Unthresholded version of the Expected Nearby Map to weight Computer Vision suggestions."
- "On average, both approaches yielded the same Recall of 0.94" and "The F1 statistic (the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall) improved by 9% for the Geomodel compared to the 1-degree grid." These two describe range maps, measured on "the subset of around 5,000 Taxon Ranges that contained at least 90% of the observations for the taxon."
- Not rechecked (first sitting): the Geomodel takes "a location as input and returns the most likely species at that location as output"; "The Geomodel is trained only on iNaturalist observations and an elevation map."; "You can think of the Expected Nearby Map as an estimate of whether the species is present near the location."
- The post does not describe the test set behind 75%, 83% and 87%.

From the v2.22 post (above): a table compares the new geomodel (SINR) with the old grid. As extracted from the table: SINR 0.794 on an IUCN range comparison, 0.741 on an iNaturalist range evaluation, and "+6.5" on "iNat geo prior improvement"; the grid 0.747, 0.723 and "+9.8". The text read does not give the units or the test set for the last row.

From iNaturalist's open-source code (raw), which is the most exact statement of the method:
- Vision service `inaturalist/inatVisionAPI`, file `lib/inat_inferrer.py`, commit 38b2090 (read 2026-10-11): the code comment says "the combined score is simply the normalized vision score multipliedby the normalized geo score", and the line is `leaf_scores["combined_score"] = leaf_scores["normalized_vision_score"] * leaf_scores[geo_column_for_scoring]`. The geo score is floored: `MINIMUM_GEO_SCORE = 0.005` and `dataframe[column_name].clip(InatInferrer.MINIMUM_GEO_SCORE, None)`. So a species the geomodel considers absent keeps at least 0.005 of its vision score.
- API `inaturalist/iNaturalistAPI`, file `lib/controllers/v1/computervision_controller.js` (read 2026-10-11): "expected nearby" is a flag set per suggestion as `s.frequency_score = s.geo_score >= s.geo_threshold ? 1 : 0;`, where each taxon has its own threshold.
- This is the closest published analogue to our month prior. Ours multiplies by a monthly count prior within a list already cut to the county. Theirs multiplies by a location score over the whole world list, and they do not use the month in what was read.

**What "We're pretty sure this is in the genus" means in numbers.** No blog or help page gives a number. The code does (raw):
- Interface text, `inaturalist/inaturalist` `config/locales/en.yml`, commit b1274a0: "We're pretty sure this is in the genus", with variants for family, order, subfamily and superfamily, and "We're not confident enough to make a recommendation."
- Rule, `lib/inat_inferrer.py` (commit 38b2090): `COMMON_ANCESTOR_WINDOW = 15`, `COMMON_ANCESTOR_CUTOFF_RATIO = 0.01`, and `common_ancestor_query = f"{aggregated_score_to_use} > 0.78 and rank_level >= 20"`, to which the default case adds `" and rank_level <= 33"`. When any taxon is expected nearby, candidates are limited to nearby taxa. Candidates are sorted by rank level and the most specific is taken.
- In plain words (my reading of that code): take the 15 highest-scoring leaf taxa (mostly species) whose combined score is above 1% of the top score; add their combined scores up the tree and divide by the total of those 15; the suggested group is the most specific taxon between genus (rank level 20) and superfamily (rank level 33) that holds more than 0.78 of that total. Because the total covers only those 15 taxa, the 0.78 is a share of the top of the list. Rank levels 20 = genus and 33 = superfamily are from the rank table in `inaturalist/vision-camera-plugin-inatvision` `src/index.tsx`. The 0.78 is a share of the model's own normalized score. iNaturalist has not published how often such a group is correct.

**How the test set is built, as far as published:** research-grade observations, 1,000 drawn at random per group, not used in training, scored by whether the model's suggestion matches the community's species. Not found in anything read: the list of groups, whether one photo or all photos of an observation are scored, how location is supplied in the "+ Geo" or "combined" bars, and whether "average accuracy" is a mean over groups or over observations.

## 2. Seek by iNaturalist

No published accuracy, taxon count or model size was found in any page iNaturalist has posted. What the open-source code says (raw):
- **Which model.** `inaturalist/SeekReactNative`, commit a4e5507 (committed 2026-10-08), `config.example.js`: `IOS_MODEL_FILE_NAME: "optimized_model_v2_30.mlmodel"`, `IOS_GEOMODEL_FILE_NAME: "geomodel_v2_30.mlmodel"`, `IOS_TAXONOMY_FILE_NAME: "taxonomy_v2_30.json"`, and for Android `ANDROID_MODEL_FILE_NAME: "INatVision_Small_2_fact256_8bit.tflite"`. The README tells developers that the sample iOS files "need to be renamed to `optimized_model_v2_30.mlmodel`, `geomodel_v2_30.mlmodel` and `taxonomy_v2_30.json`". My inference: the current Seek build expects a small, 8-bit version of model v2.30 together with a geomodel. The real files are not in the repository, so the shipped model is not confirmed.
- **Size.** The sample vision model the README points to, `INatVision_Small_2_fact256_8bit.tflite` in release v25.01.15 of `inaturalist/model-files`, is 22,197,168 bytes (Content-Length from GitHub, 2026-10-11). Its sample `taxonomy.csv` is only 79,845 bytes, so it is probably a cut-down sample; whether the shipped model has the same size was not established.
- **Which rank it will name.** `components/Camera/ARCamera/ARCamera.tsx`: `const confidenceThresholdNumber = 70;` and `const wantedRanks = ["species", "genus", "family", "order", "class"];` with the comment "not looking at kingdom or phylum as we are currently not displaying results for those ranks". The plugin (`inaturalist/vision-camera-plugin-inatvision`, commit ce77232, 2026-07-09, `src/index.tsx`) scales scores by 100, keeps only the main ranks ("only KPCOFGS ranks qualify as "top" predictions"), and keeps a prediction when `prediction.score > (options.confidenceThreshold || 0)`. In plain words (my reading): Seek shows a species, genus, family, order or class only when the model's score for it, after location weighting when location is on, is above 0.70, and it moves down to the most specific rank that passes.
- **Published words.** A 2019 forum post by a member of the iNaturalist team, "Real-time Computer Vision predictions in Seek by iNaturalist 2.0" (https://forum.inaturalist.org/t/real-time-computer-vision-predictions-in-seek-by-inaturalist-2-0/1855, 5 April 2019; WebFetch): "The model includes species as well as non-species “leaf nodes”" "in cases where individual species do not have enough photos to train on, but their collective genus does."; "We hope this will help Seek identify more organisms, if sometimes to a rank higher than species."; the model is "byte-wise smaller than our online model so it will fit on mobile devices." It gives no accuracy, taxon count or size.

## 4. Independent evaluations of iNaturalist's suggestions, and the research-grade bias

**Tests of the iNaturalist and Seek apps on photos taken for the test** (all WebFetch):
- Campbell, Peacock and Bacon 2023 (above), species right on the first attempt: "Seek correctly identified 52.14% of flowers" (22.86% of leaves from Table 4 as extracted); iNaturalist "3.57% of flowers and 6.79% of leaves successfully identified to a species level". My guess, unverified: the iNaturalist app often offers a genus first ("pretty sure" above), which a species-only first-attempt score counts as wrong.
- Schmidt, Casario, Zipse and Grabosky, Arboriculture & Urban Forestry 48(1): 27–43, January 2022 (https://auf.isa-arbor.com/content/48/1/27): iNaturalist reached "92.27% identification rate to genus and a 69.55% identification rate to species for leaf photos" and "48.18% identification rate to genus and a 31.82% identification rate to species" for bark; "PictureThis was the most accurate, followed by iNaturalist". 440 photos of 55 tree species taken by the authors in New Jersey in 2020.
- Hart, Bosley, Hooper, Perry, Sellors-Moore, Moore and Goodenough, People and Nature 5(3): 929–937, 27 February 2023 (https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/12421): over Google Lens, iNaturalist Seek, LeafSnap, PlantNet and PlantSnap together, "69% were correct with the first suggestion" and "85% of images were identified correctly in the top five suggestions", on "857 professionally identified images of 277 species from 204 genera".

**Tests on iNaturalist's own observations:**
- Ants of the Balearic Islands, bioRxiv preprint 10.1101/2025.02.08.637263, not peer reviewed (https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.08.637263.full.pdf; authors and date not shown in the text read): "Among observations with AI suggestions, taxa were correctly identified at the species level in 54.1% of cases" (196 observations); community identifications were right "69.91% at the species level (216 valid comparisons)" and "90.91% at the genus level (297 comparisons)". Of the 300 observations, "168 are “Research Grade,” 132 are “Needs ID”"; accuracy by grade is not reported.
- Lichens: McMullin and Allen, Botany, 2022 (https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10344707) checked 50 random research-grade observations per species and say "The machine learning for species recognition in iNaturalist will not solve these limitations and it can perpetuate errors." Their numbers were not extracted.
- Forum test, 29 August 2021 (https://forum.inaturalist.org/t/computer-vision-performance-summary-data/25918): the original poster ran the website's suggestion with "'default to locally observed taxa' enabled" on 150 random research-grade records. **A benchmark built from iNaturalist photos.** Van Horn et al., "Benchmarking Representation Learning for Natural World Image Collections", CVPR 2021 (https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Van_Horn_Benchmarking_Representation_Learning_for_Natural_World_Image_Collections_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf; WebFetch). The iNat2021 dataset has 10,000 species, 2,686,843 training images and about 500,000 test images. "we have split the training and testing images in iNat2021 by a specific date", and the paper says it "have allowed a particular photographer to have images in both the train and test splits." A supervised ResNet50: "the full model achieves 76.0%" top-1 on the test split (76.4% on validation). The main text defers the selection rules, including any research-grade filter, to its supplement, which was not read. The authors' affiliations are Cornell, Caltech, Google and Edinburgh, and the paper thanks the iNaturalist team for data access. The setup resembles ours in kind (iNaturalist photos split by date). The differences are the species count, the worldwide list, the absence of any prior, and a 2021 model.

**Accuracy of research-grade labels themselves.** First experiment, "We estimate the accuracy of Research Grade observations to be 95% correct!", posted 31 January 2024 (network copy https://inaturalist.ala.org.au/posts/89255-we-estimate-the-accuracy-of-research-grade-observations-to-be-95-correct; WebFetch): "For the Research Grade subset, 95% were Correct, 3% were Uncertain and 2% were Incorrect."; the "Verifiable subset (Research Grade + Needs ID) had a lower accuracy of 90% Correct"; "we generated a sample of 1,000 observations on January 16."; validators were "1,232 candidate validators who each had at least 3 improving Identifications" and "On average each observation was validated by 4 validators." The post cautions that "these results are drawn from a relatively small sample size." Second experiment (https://www.inaturalist.org/blog/90263, posted 26 February 2024, results added 29 February 2024; WebFetch, rechecked in the second sitting): "In this second experiment, we estimated the accuracy of the iNaturalist Research Grade observation dataset to be 97% correct" and "the accuracy of the Needs ID subset to be 79% correct." ## What we may and may not claim

We may say that BioCLIP 2, choosing among the 5,911 species recorded in Alachua County, named the right species first for about 90 of every 100 research-grade iNaturalist photos taken in the county in 2025 and 2026, and for about 92 of every 100 when it was also told the month, and that none of these photos can have been in its training data, which stops at a GBIF snapshot of 1 May 2024. We may also say that iNaturalist tests its own model in a similar way, on research-grade observations held out from training, and that the latest figure it has stated in words is an average accuracy of 88.7% for model v2.20 in February 2025; its test, however, covers more than 100,000 taxa worldwide, reports an average across taxon groups, and does not say whether that figure includes location, so we may not present our 0.90 or 0.92 as higher than iNaturalist's or as a ranking of any kind. We may not claim that the app will be right about 90 times in 100 in a visitor's hands, because research-grade photos are the ones people managed to agree on, often after seeing iNaturalist's own suggestion, and the independent tests we found on photos taken for the purpose gave lower and widely scattered first-suggestion rates, while nobody we found has measured how much easier research-grade photos are. Our labels also carry the error iNaturalist measured in research-grade data, about 3 to 4 wrong in 100, and 36 of our 4,197 test photos show species outside our list, so a difference of a point or two between our models should be checked against those labels before it is reported.
