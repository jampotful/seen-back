# Sources

Every line the app shows about an animal's sight carries one of four grades: measured in this animal, measured in a relative or a wider group, a guess, or left out. This file lists what stands behind the measured lines, and names the conventions that are this project's own.

## Conventions that are this project's own

- **Sharpness is drawn against the viewer's eye.** The phone's long side is assumed to fill 24 degrees of view, and the picture is blurred to what the animal's eye would keep of that screen. Drawn against the scene, a cat's blur would be about one pixel, because a phone picture of a 70 degree field already discards most of what a human eye resolves.
- **A faster eye is suggested by slowing the picture** to 60 divided by the animal's flicker rate, then jumping to catch up. A higher flicker rate does not establish that time feels slower.
- **Depth is guessed from height in the frame**, and a tap moves the focus. A camera feed carries no distances.
- **The wide field is shown by shrinking the camera's picture** and inventing the rest from mirrored, blurred edges.
- **Ultraviolet is invented**: violet for birds and reptiles, blue for bees. Bees are drawn in false colour.
- **The hexagon mosaic appears only during the change of eyes.** An insect does not see hexagons.
- **The length of a wading bird's hold is a guess.** The thrust length is from pigeons.
