# The species people photograph at home

Burst 13 (`docs/tracks/prompts/burst13-everyday.md`). A first-time user photographs a dog, a cat, a budgerigar, a horse, a cow, a chicken, a pigeon, a pothos, a monstera, a snake plant, a rose, a tulip or a lawn. This file says where a species must be added for each path of the app to know it, what was added, and how it was measured.

## Where a species must be added, path by path

| Path | Files the page reads | Where a species comes from | What it takes to add one |

| The pocket model's kinds head | `models/pocket/kinds.f16.bin` (36 rows), `kinds.bias`, `kinds.county_bias` | `tools/world/head.py` on world photos, the Alachua training photos and COCO (people, pets, livestock, rooms) | Nothing for a species whose kind of eye already exists. |
| The larger model's world rows | `species.parts`, `species.scale`, `species.table` in `models/naturalist/manifest.json` | Every species with a research-grade iNaturalist record through 2024 (`tools/coverage/lists.py`), named by GBIF's backbone (`tools/world/names.py`); one sentence a species, "a photo of <name>.", through BioCLIP 2's text half (`tools/coverage/text.py`), one byte a number (`tools/world/pack.py`) | A row made the same way, at the end of the rows, and a line in the table (name, English name, kind or group). A species GBIF lists as a synonym first looks for the world row of the species GBIF accepts for it, which the world table names; when that row has the same English name, the species takes it and gets no new row (since build 36; below). |

## What was added

- **The list** (`tools/species/everyday_list.csv`, 175 queries; `data/everyday/species.csv`, 174 species). Pets, pet birds and reptiles, aquarium fish, poultry, livestock, the feral pigeon, houseplants, garden and cut flowers, lawn grasses, vegetables and fruit trees. The queries are names to look up. Every GBIF key, scientific name, rank, status and lineage in `species.csv` is the one api.gbif.org returned on a build machine (`tools/species/everyday.py resolve`, runs `runs/everyday/1-resolve` and `3-resolve` on `ci`). One query (`Festuca arundinacea`) matched only a family and was left out. Domestic forms keep the name GBIF matched (`Canis familiaris`, `Gallus gallus domesticus`, `Anas platyrhynchos domesticus`), so that a dog is not named as a wolf. The English names are iNaturalist's, where its taxon is the same name or holds it as a synonym.
- **The photos** (`data/everyday/photos.csv`, 5,209 photos: 1,675 tune, 3,534 test). Up to 30 observations a species marked captive or cultivated on iNaturalist, newest first, each one's first photo under the licence rule `nc`, at most three from one person, split by observation id (one in three to tune). The photos themselves stay on iNaturalist; the manifest holds their addresses.
- **The kind of eye** of each species, from its GBIF lineage by `tools/species/eyes.py`: plant 124, bird 10, deer 10, wader 7, squirrel 6, reptile 5, fish 4, mammal 2, and one each of dog, cat, rabbit, salamander, bee and insect (`data/everyday/summary.json`).
- **The home share.** `table.json` carries `"home": [[row, share], ...]`. A row's share is beta times its iNaturalist observations marked captive or cultivated over all of iNaturalist's observations (394,759,406 when resolved). `placeOf` adds it to the row's share in every cell, so it holds anywhere, and the encounter rule skips such a row, since a dog met says nothing of where the phone is. `tools/world/place.py` and `prior.py` do the same in Python; the world stand-in of `test/rec` carries one home row, so `check_rec.js large` holds the page to the Python numbers.
- **Typed names.** `LEX` in `src/cam.r.js` gains pothos, monstera, philodendron, dracaena, anthurium, hydrangea, geranium, marigold, peony, chrysanthemum, carnation, zinnia, petunia, begonia, dahlia, gardenia, lilac, oleander, bougainvillea, lawn, turf, peppers, cucumber, zucchini, lettuce, cabbage, carrot, eggplant, pumpkin, watermelon, mangoes, avocado, papaya, pomegranate, persimmon and loquat as plants. A lone "pepper" or "mango" is left out, since both are common names for pets.

## How it is measured

`tools/species/everyday_build.py` on a build machine (`.github/workflows/everyday.yml`, `run/everyday/build-*`): the photos' BioCLIP 2 vectors in six shards, the new rows made as the world rows were (three shipped rows are made again as a check of the route), and the page's arithmetic (`fuseWith` with the slot's kinds head, `t_species`, `alpha_prior`, `w_kind`, `kind_at`, `name_at`) in numpy. Beta is chosen on the tune photos alone, from 0, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10 and 30, by the species right in each photo's own time zone. The test photos are read once, at the chosen beta (and at beta 0 for comparison), in each photo's own time zone, in Alachua County's (`America/New_York`) and with no zone. The world test's 3,500 photos are scored before and after, with no prior, with the time zone, and with the time zone and three creatures met, at the shipped weight of the prior.

Pass marks (from the prompt): on the new species, the kind right at least 85% and the species right at least 60% with the larger model, in the photo's own zone and in Alachua's; no world-test score drops by more than 1 point. The pocket model is unchanged, so the marks of `docs/tracks/head.md` hold as they were.

- **Where it is weak.** Eight of the 174 species have the kind right on fewer than 85% of their photos. ## The dog and the home share's weight (18 October 2026, build 36)

The second pass (`agent/everyday2`) took the two weak spots build 34 left: the dog's kind was right on 9 of its 20 test photos, and beta was tried only up to 30 while the tune score still rose.

### Why the dog failed

These numbers were read on the build-34 files with the photos' vectors of `runs/everyday/4-build`, at beta 30, in each photo's own zone.

- **Two rows for one animal.** GBIF lists `Canis familiaris` as a synonym. The name it accepts is `Canis lupus familiaris`, a subspecies of `Canis lupus`. Build 34 looked the list up in the world table by the GBIF key of the name it had matched, and the world table holds only accepted names, so the dog got a new row made from "a photo of Canis familiaris." and its home share went there. The world table already has `Canis lupus`, whose English name there is Domestic Dog, and BioCLIP 2 puts dog photos nearer that row: its cosine was the highest of all 427,187 rows on 15 of the 20 test photos and above the new row's on all 20 (0.701 against 0.645 on average). Without a home share, `Canis lupus` had only its wild records.
- **The split and the cat.** The dog's probability split between the two rows (0.54 and 0.42, 0.55 and 0.42, 0.49 and 0.46 on three photos), so neither reached the naming bar of 0.83. The cat's single row, which holds wild records and a home share, came first on 5 of the 20, at 0.36 to 0.98; its cosine with dog photos (0.650 on average) is as close as the new dog row's. The first rows over the 20 photos were `Canis lupus` 8, `Felis catus` 5, `Canis familiaris` 4 and three others.
- **The kinds head.** The larger model's kind phrases put `other` first on 12 of the 20 dog photos and gave `dog` less than 0.25 on 12 (0.30 on average, against 0.51 for `other`). The kind therefore follows the species list, and a list split between two dog rows and the cat loses it: the kind was dog on 9, other on 5, cat on 4, and plant and bird on one each. The page named a species on one photo, and that was the cat.
- **A larger beta alone does not mend it.** With build 34's rows, the kind stayed right on 9 of 20 at betas 100, 300 and 1,000, because the new row and the cat both grow.
- **The pocket model** (unchanged; `run/everyday/pocket-2`, results `runs/everyday/6-pocket`, which now give every species and the dog's and cat's photos one by one). Away from the county its kind was right on 17 of the 20 and sure on 14, with 13 right and sure. In the county's time zone it was right on 18 but sure on only 2, and both of those were wrong. Its county list holds the cat and no dog, puts `Felis catus` first on 18 of the 20, and under the rule `mix` that keeps the dog's share at 0.70 or less (0.64 on average), below `kind_at` (0.87). Mending that needs a dog in the county list, which needs county records and a refit; it is left for later.

### The fix: a synonym takes the world row of the species GBIF accepts

`tools/species/everyday_build.py` now asks GBIF (on the build machine) for the accepted name of every species of the list that GBIF calls a synonym, and for its species (`data/everyday/accepted.json` keeps the answers). When the world table has a row for that species and the row carries the English name the species' own row would carry, the species takes that row, with its home share, and gets no new row of its own. Five of the nine synonyms qualify: the dog (`Canis lupus`, Domestic Dog), the African collared-dove (`Streptopelia roseogrisea`), the African violet (`Streptocarpus ionanthus`), the pea (`Lathyrus oleraceus`) and the loquat (`Rhaphiolepis bibas`). The other four keep their own rows because the world row has another English name: the ferret (European Polecat), the zebu (Domestic Cattle), the pig (Wild Boar; the pig's own row has no English name) and the orange (naartjie). So a pig is not named a wild boar. New rows fall from 24 to 19, and the database from 427,187 rows to 427,182. A wolf is named Domestic Dog, as it was before build 34, since that is the world table's English name for `Canis lupus`.

The build measures both rules (`own`, as build 34, and `accepted`) on the same photos and reads every beta in one pass: the rows without a home share are summed once a photo, and the home rows are added for each beta. Run with the rule `own` at beta 30, it gave build 34's numbers to the last digit (97.06% and 75.64% on the test photos in their own zones, 75.83% and 76.83% on the world test), so the new arithmetic agrees with the old.

### Beta

The grid now runs 0, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1,000 and 3,000, and the world test is read at every beta. Beta is chosen on the tune photos, by the species right in each photo's own zone, among the betas whose world-test drops are all within the mark of 1 point. With the rule `accepted`:

| Beta | Tune, species right | Tune, kind right | World drop, zone | World drop, zone and three met |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 50.5% | 92.2% | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| 10 | 74.3% | 95.7% | 0.06 | 0.06 |
| 30 | 76.0% | 96.2% | 0.23 | 0.20 |
| 100 | 77.5% | 97.0% | 0.66 | 0.57 |
| 300 | 78.4% | 97.3% | 1.34 | 1.29 |
| 1,000 | 78.9% | 97.7% | 2.14 | 1.97 |
| 3,000 | 78.9% | 97.7% | 3.43 | 2.97 |

With no prior the world test drops 0.11 points at every beta, from the new rows alone. The score flattens above 1,000, and from 300 the world test fails, so beta 100 was chosen. The rule `own` gave the same choice and nearly the same drops.
