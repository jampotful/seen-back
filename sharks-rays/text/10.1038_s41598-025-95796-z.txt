# Seismic and geomechanical characterization of Asmari formation using well logs and simultaneous inversion in an Iranian oil field

## Abstract
This paper presents a case study on the application of simultaneous inversion (SI) technique and well log analysis to accurately seismic-geomechanical characterization of Asmari formation in an Iranian oil field. To achieve this goal, a one-dimensional (1D) prediction of geomechanical properties such as Young’s modulus (E), bulk modulus (K), shear modulus (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\upmu\:}$$\end{document}), Poisson’s ratio (PR), Vp/Vs ratio and brittleness (BRI) was first generated from the analyzing the well log data. In the next step, the spatial distribution of the geomechanical parameters in the reservoir area was predicted on the basis of the results of the simultaneous inversion. Lithological facies discrimination and fluid detection of the Asmari formation were performed using simultaneous inversion of pre-stack seismic data and conventional cross-plotting analysis of well data. To accomplish this objective, elastic and geomechanical parameters were cross-plotted against conventional petrophysical logs along with BRI, water saturation (Sw), gamma ray (GR), acoustic impedance (Zp), \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\upmu\:}$$\end{document}, and lithology logs such as quartz and calcite volumes. As a result of this work, two reservoirs were identified in the Asmari formation: sandstone and carbonate. The carbonate reservoir consists predominantly of calcite and dolomite, with minimal shale content, while the sandstone reservoir is mainly composed of shale and contains less quartz. In addition, the carbonate section within the Asmari formation exhibits better reservoir quality due to lower water saturation and higher porosity compared to the sandstone zone. To determine the type of fluid in the Asmari formation of the studied oil field, LMR (lambda-mu-rho) scatterplots were employed in both well and seismic domains. The findings reveal that the sandstone reservoir in the Asmari formation is water-saturated, while the carbonate reservoir is oil-saturated. Furthermore, a gas cap is present at the top of the Asmari formation.

## Introduction
The accurate characterization of reservoir properties, including geomechanical parameters, is critical to the successful management of oil and gas reservoirs1–3. Geomechanical parameters such as rock strength, modulus of elasticity and Poisson’s ratio are important for reservoir engineering applications such as analyzing wellbore stability, hydraulic fracturing design and reservoir simulation4–9. Determining the lithology of reservoir zones, predicting petrophysical and geomechanical properties, and detecting pore fluids are essential topics in reservoir geophysics10–14. Typically, the analysis of core and well data is used for reservoir characterization, but the recording of this data is costly and time consuming and only available close to the well locations15,16.
Nowadays, 3D seismic data is widely available and provides information on the spatial distribution of subsurface layer properties15,17–19. The inversion of seismic data to calculate the elastic properties of subsurface formations has played an important role in petroleum engineering. The basic idea of seismic data inversion is to assume an acoustic impedance (Zp) of the subsurface formation at normal incidence angle18,20–24. However, in many cases it is difficult or even impossible to distinguish between hydrocarbon saturated zones and the surrounding shale rock at normal incidence25. Although normal-incidence (post-stack) seismic data is quite a useful tool in reservoir geophysics, some studies show that it is an ambiguous indicator for reservoir characterization26–28. To overcome these limitations, amplitude versus offset (AVO) analysis was introduced as an efficient and robust tool for seismic reservoir characterization and development25,26,29,30. The relatively recent advance in seismic data inversion and AVO analysis is based on the inversion of seismic data with a non-zero offset, referred to as simultaneous inversion (SI). Several studies show the discrimination between hydrocarbon saturated zones and the surrounding shale at non-zero offset data is possible in simultaneous inversion25,26,28,30–32.
The prediction of elastic properties (Vp, Vs and ρ) from AVO analysis by seismic data inversion was introduced by Connolly26. Overall, SI is a generalization of Zp for variable incidence angles. In seismic-geomechanics, the SI technique is a practical and useful tool for 3D determination of geomechanical properties such as shear modulus, Poisson’s ratio, bulk modulus and brittleness33. In recent years, interest in the inversion of seismic data to estimate geomechanical parameters of reservoirs has increased. The inversion of seismic data makes it possible to estimate the spatial distribution of geomechanical properties in the reservoir and to create a more accurate and detailed reservoir model. The SI technique for calculating elastic properties is considered a robust tool for predicting fluid and lithology11,26,28,30,34.
Numerous studies have been successfully conducted to estimate elastic properties, petrophysical parameters, and geomechanical properties using pre-stack seismic data inversion35–37. Alebouyeh and Chehrazi used pre-stack seismic data inversion to distinguish between reservoir and non-reservoir facies in the Ghar formation, identifying three lithologies: sandstone, dolomite sandstone, and shale. They found the middle Ghar reservoir to be the most porous due to lower lambda-rho (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\lambda\:\rho\:$$\end{document}) and mu-rho (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mu\:\rho\:$$\end{document}) values32. Sharifi et al. integrated pre-stack seismic inversion with well log data to determine geomechanical parameters like Young’s modulus, bulk modulus, Poisson’s ratio, and brittleness, revealing significant variations in reservoir zones with different lithologies and fluid saturations33. Ugbor et al. applied seismic pre-stack waveform inversion and empirical relationships to estimate geomechanical properties in the Ruby field38. Shakir et al. characterized thin gas sand layers in the lower Ranikot formation using seismic inversion techniques, with pre-stack seismic inversion providing better reservoir characterization than post-stack methods39. Masri and Takacs compared post-stack and pre-stack inversions of acoustic impedance using a 3D seismic dataset, finding that pre-stack inversion provides a more detailed model for discriminating lithology in a geothermal reservoir in fractured Triassic carbonates34. Taras and Riahi, and Mehdipour et al. identified hydrocarbon-bearing reservoir zones and assessed reservoir quality using geomechanical evaluation based on pre-stack seismic data inversion11,30.
The literature shows the potential of using simultaneous inversion results and geomechanical parameters to predict the lithology and fluid content of reservoirs. However, interpreting the results of the seismic inversion and accurately predicting the reservoir fluids and lithology remains a challenge. Further research is needed to develop more accurate and reliable methods for predicting reservoir properties using seismic data inversion and geomechanical parameters. By integrating seismic data and advanced inversion techniques, this study offers a promising approach for more accurate and efficient lithology and fluid prediction, which has the potential to increase exploration success and reduce risk in hydrocarbon resource development projects.

## Geological background and dataset
In this section, the most important aspects of the geological environment of the Asmari formation, especially with regard to stratigraphy, are presented. The Asmari formation in the investigated oil field, located in the Dezful embayment, is part of the Zagros folded belt, an important oil-producing region in Iran27,30,40,41. The Zagros fold-and-thrust belt is composed of two primary zones: the high Zagros (imbricated zone) in the north and the Zagros folded belt in the south. From northwest to southeast, the Zagros folded belt is laterally divided into the Lurestan, Dezful embayment, and Fars regions, as illustrated in Fig. 141–43.
The Asmari formation in the Dezful embayment is a significant geological formation from the Oligocene to Early Miocene epochs. The Asmari Formation extends broadly throughout the Zagros sedimentary basin, showcasing anticline structures that align along a northwest-southeast axis. Its sedimentary environment spans a spectrum from shallow marine areas to deeper aquatic settings. The Asmari formation is an important reservoir and its stratigraphy plays a crucial role in understanding the reservoirs properties and potential. The Asmari formation consists mainly of limestone and dolomite, with some sandstone and shale interbeds. The limestones are generally bioclastic and oolitic, while the dolomites are generally detrital and peloidal. The sandstones are mostly quartz-rich and arkosic, while the shales are predominantly silt and clay44–46. The Asmari formation is located in a complex structural environment characterized by folding and faulting. These structural features influence the architecture of the reservoir and the distribution of hydrocarbons40,47.

Different lithologies have different rock properties related to the fluid type and mineral properties. To identify the separation of lithology and fluid, the LMR (lambda-mu-rho) cross plotting was created. The application of the LMR was introduced by Goodway et al.52. This makes the cross-plot of these two parameters a powerful tool for discriminating lithology and fluids in the reservoir. Therefore, the cross plot of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\uplambda\:}{\uprho\:}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\upmu\:}{\uprho\:}$$\end{document} is created to identify the type of reservoir fluids (gas, oil, and water) of Asmari formation. A key aspect of applying LMR scatterplots is the selection of an effective attribute, such as the Vp/Vs ratio, used as a color bar to distinguish the fluid type. the range of Vp/Vs ratios in reservoir fluids can vary depending on lithology, clay content, rock porosity and type of pore fluid. The Vp/Vs ratio in reservoir fluids can vary, but typically the ratio is between 1.4 and 1.7 for gas, between 1.7 and 2 for oil, and between 2 and 2.5 for water. On the other hand, the Vp/Vs ratio is a useful tool to identify the type of fluid (water, oil or gas) in a reservoir because the ratio varies systematically with the fluid type due to the different effects on compressional and shear wave velocities. Note that variations in Vp/Vs ratio in some cases are not only indicative of the type of fluid, but can also be influenced by other factors such as rock properties, pore geometry and pore fluid distribution, leading to possible misinterpretations. Indeed, geologic heterogeneity within a reservoir can complicate the interpretation of the Vp/Vs ratio, which can lead to inaccuracies in fluid type identification. Changes in rock properties, such as porosity, clay content and mineral composition, can affect the Vp/Vs ratio, making it difficult to isolate the effects of fluid type on the ratio. ## Simultaneous pre-stack seismic inversion
The simultaneous seismic inversion method is used to identify fluid in the characterization of reservoirs. This method integrates seismic and well data to quantitatively interpret and evaluate uncertainties in subsurface reservoirs. Simultaneous inversion of seismic data and well logs is used to derive elastic properties such as P-impedance, S-impedance, density and Vp/Vs ratio. On the other hand, simultaneous inversion enables the differentiation of fluid types within the reservoir by analyzing the elastic properties derived from the inversion process. ## Conclusion
An integrated simultaneous inversion of pre-stack seismic data and well log analysis was employed to achieve seismic-geomechanical characterization of the Asmari formation in an Iranian oil field, enabling the discrimination of reservoir lithology and fluid content. Geomechanical parameters were estimated one-dimensionally in the well domain using empirical relationships, and their distribution was derived in the seismic domain via simultaneous inversion. Analysis of geomechanical properties in the lower section of the Asmari formation reveals a significant decrease in elastic properties and geomechanical parameters, coupled with an increase in brittleness, compared to the upper section. Consequently, the Asmari formation in the investigated field is divided into two distinct reservoirs: a carbonate reservoir in the upper section and a sandstone reservoir in the lower section. A scatter plot of compressional wave velocity (Vp) versus shear wave velocity (Vs), with shear modulus represented as a color bar, was used to evaluate the mineralogical composition of the Asmari formation. The findings demonstrate that the carbonate reservoir predominantly consists of calcite and dolomite, while the sandstone reservoir is mainly composed of quartz and shale. Furthermore, the carbonate reservoir exhibits superior reservoir quality, characterized by lower water saturation, higher porosity, and reduced shale volume compared to the sandstone reservoir. The results indicate that both lambda-rho (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\uplambda\:}{\uprho\:}$$\end{document}) and mu-rho (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\upmu\:}{\uprho\:}$$\end{document}) attributes decrease within the reservoir zones. However, the reduction in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\uplambda\:}{\uprho\:}$$\end{document} is more pronounced than that of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\upmu\:}{\uprho\:}$$\end{document}, as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\upmu\:}{\uprho\:}$$\end{document} is influenced solely by lithology, whereas \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\uplambda\:}{\uprho\:}$$\end{document} is sensitive to both lithology and the nature of reservoir fluids. To identify fluid types within the Asmari formation, a cross plot of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\uplambda\:}{\uprho\:}$$\end{document} versus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\upmu\:}{\uprho\:}$$\end{document} was utilized, with acoustic impedance represented as a color bar. The analysis reveals that the sandstone reservoir is water-saturated, while the carbonate reservoir is oil-saturated. Additionally, a gas cap is present at the top of the Asmari formation. Gas-saturated zones, primarily dolomitic in composition, exhibit higher acoustic impedance, whereas water-saturated zones, predominantly comprising quartz and shale, display lower acoustic impedance. Oil-bearing zones, which are primarily calcitic, show lower acoustic impedance compared to gas-bearing zones. This study underscores that the simultaneous inversion of pre-stack seismic data combined with well log analysis provides enhanced accuracy and detail regarding reservoir lithology and fluid distribution. These insights significantly improve the understanding of reservoir properties and behavior. The findings have important implications for reservoir characterization and management, emphasizing the utility of seismic inversion as a critical tool for successful exploration and production operations.
