# Structural, Optical, and Conduction Relaxation Properties of TlCrP2O7 Driven by Nonoverlapping Small Polaron Tunneling

## Abstract
This work provides an in-depth examination of the structural, optical, electrical, and dielectric characteristics of TlCrP2O7, prepared using a conventional solid-state reaction route. X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis confirms the formation of a highly crystalline single phase with monoclinic symmetry (P21/c). Complementary Raman and infrared spectroscopy further validate the structural framework and reveal the characteristic internal and external vibrational modes associated with the pyrophosphate network. Optical characterization revealed a direct band gap of 2.99 eV and an Urbach energy of 0.48 eV, reflecting significant electronic transitions within the lattice. Impedance measurements indicated thermally activated conduction, with activation energies of 0.52 eV for grains and 0.47 eV for grain boundaries, successfully modeled using an equivalent circuit incorporating constant phase elements to capture microstructural effects. The AC conductivity obeys Jonscher’s universal power law, indicating that charge transport occurs through a nonoverlapping small polaron tunneling mechanism. Dielectric studies reveal pronounced non-Debye relaxation and significant space-charge polarization, both strongly influenced by temperature, frequency, and grain-boundary effects. Overall, these results emphasize the crucial influence of microstructure on charge transport and dielectric behavior, highlighting TlCrP2O7 as a promising material for electronic and optoelectronic applications.

## Introduction
In recent decades, transition-metal phosphates, in both vitreous and crystalline forms, have attracted considerable research attention owing to the versatility of their structural and compositional features. − Crystalline members of this family consist of PO4 tetrahedra linked to MO n (n = 4, 5, 6) polyhedra, which can connect through multiple modes to generate a variety of architectures, including 1D chain-like arrangements, 2D layered frameworks, and 3D open networks. These materials are especially appreciated for their excellent thermal stability, low fabrication cost, environmental friendliness, ease of preparation, and adaptable structural features. Importantly, their crystal frameworks can accommodate postsynthetic modifications through the insertion of ionic or neutral species, enabling fine control over their physical and chemical properties. Extensive research efforts have focused on this class of materials, with examples including olivine-type RbZnPO4, NASICON-type Na3MnTi0.5Zr0.5(PO4)3, orthophosphate K3Tb­(PO4)2, metaphosphate NaCdP3O9, and pyrophosphate Li2CuP2O7.
Pyrophosphate compounds, typically represented by the general formula AMP2O7 (where A is an alkali or alkaline earth metal and M is a transition metal), have gained significant attention due to their suitability for a wide range of advanced technological applications. These materials have been successfully explored for use in energy conversion systems, − rechargeable batteries, , energy storage devices, supercapacitors, water treatment technologies, and various optical applications. Within this family, compounds of the type AICrIIIP2O7 have attracted particular interest because of their distinctive structural features and excellent tunability, which make them well-suited for diverse functional applications. − In addition, several studies have reported their remarkable optical and dielectric properties, particularly for compositions where A = Li, K, Na, Ag, or Tl. ,,− Despite its potential, TlCrP2O7 remains relatively underexplored, with most studies to date concentrating primarily on its crystal structure. Despite being less toxic than heavy metals like lead (Pb) or cadmium (Cd), thallium (Tl) has remained relatively underexplored in scientific studies. , Structurally, TlCrP2O7 is known to crystallize in the monoclinic system with the P21/c space group. Recently, Souissi et al. reported on the optical properties of NaCrP2O7, highlighting a direct optical band gap of 2.9 eV and an Urbach energy of 0.44 eV. In parallel, our research group has investigated the dielectric response of TlFeP2O7, which revealed a remarkably high dielectric constant (ranging from 102 to 3 × 104). These findings provide a strong motivation to further investigate the optical and dielectric behavior of TlCrP2O7 in order to assess its potential for functional applications. To date, the physical and dielectric properties of TlCrP2O7 have not been reported in the literature, leaving a significant gap in the understanding of this pyrophosphate system. In this work, we present the first comprehensive and systematic study of TlCrP2O7, covering its crystal structure, microstructural characteristics, optical band gap, dielectric relaxation phenomena, and charge-transport mechanisms. By establishing clear correlations between microstructure and functional response, this study uncovers how grain boundaries, lattice dynamics, and local structural disorder govern the electrical and dielectric performance of this compound. The insights obtained not only broaden the current knowledge of Tl-based pyrophosphates but also position TlCrP2O7 within the emerging class of phosphate materials with promising electronic and optoelectronic potential. This work therefore provides a solid foundation for future research on the design and application of pyrophosphate-based functional materials.
Various synthesis strategies have been developed for the preparation of oxide-based materials, offering tunable control over composition, particle size, morphology, and defect concentration in the resulting products. The selection of a suitable synthesis route is therefore fundamental to achieving high phase purity and optimizing the functional behavior of complex oxides. Phosphate ceramics, in particular, can be obtained through multiple approaches, such as the conventional solid-state reaction method. Among these, the solid-state route remains the most commonly adopted technique owing to its simplicity, scalability, and low cost. This process involves solid-state diffusion between precursor powders at elevated temperatures to ensure complete phase formation, often requiring prolonged heating for full reaction and densification. Moreover, it enables accurate control of stoichiometry and crystalline phase evolution, key parameters for tailoring structural and electrical performance. Due to its reproducibility and versatility, the solid-state synthesis method continues to be a reliable route for producing advanced functional materials with potential applications in dielectric, optical, and energy-storage devices.
The present work aims to provide a comprehensive investigation of TlCrP2O7, covering its synthesis, structural characterization, and functional properties. The compound was first prepared via a conventional solid-state reaction, and its phase purity and crystal structure were confirmed through X-ray powder diffraction (XRPD) analysis. Subsequently, key optical parameters, including the optical band gap energy (E g) and Urbach energy (E u), were determined to gain insight into the electronic structure and disorder within the material. Additionally, the work examines how different immittance functionsdielectric permittivity (ε), loss tangent (tan δ), complex impedance (Z), and electrical conductivity (σ)evolve with changes in frequency and temperature. The contributions of grains and grain boundaries to the overall transport mechanism were evaluated by modeling the experimental impedance data with an appropriate equivalent circuit, enabling the extraction of resistance and capacitance parameters. The results indicate that ionic conduction in TlCrP2O7 is dominated by a nonoverlapping hopping mechanism, accompanied by thermally activated, non-Debye-type relaxation processes.

## Device Assessment
X-ray powder diffraction (XRPD) was employed to determine the crystalline phases and obtain refined unit-cell parameters of TlCrP2O7. Data collection was carried out at room temperature using a Philips PW1710 diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å) over a 2θ range of 10°–60°. To achieve high-resolution results, the measurement spanned 12 h, acquired as 12 successive 1 h scans that were subsequently combined into a single diffractogram. Rietveld profile fitting was performed using the FullProf suite, allowing accurate extraction of lattice parameters and assessment of the refinement quality without executing a complete structural model refinement. The morphology was examined with a Schottky field-emission scanning electron microscope (EVO LS10 (Zeiss)). High-resolution scanning electron microscopy (SEM) imaging was used to probe the surface microstructure of the sample. The infrared spectrum of the powder, prepared as a KBr pellet, was measured at room temperature using a Thermo Scientific Nicolet 6700 FTIR spectrometer with the DRIFTS technique, covering the 400–1400 cm–1 range. The Raman spectrum was collected at room temperature over the range 100–1200 cm–1 using a Renishaw InVia Reflex spectrometer with a 633 nm laser operating at 10 mW. The absorbance spectrum was obtained at ambient temperature using a PerkinElmer Lambda 1050 spectrophotometer, covering the wavelength range from 200 to 1100 nm.
Electrical characterization was carried out by impedance spectroscopy (IS) using a Solartron SI-1260 analyzer coupled to a Solartron 1296 dielectric interface, with temperature control ensured by a Linkam LTS420 system during the heating sequence. The setup provided a temperature resolution of 0.1 K and stability within ±0.5 K. A closed-loop regulation device maintained consistent thermal conditions, guaranteeing homogeneous heating of the sample. The pellet was positioned inside a thermostated chamber, where continuous regulation minimized any temperature drift during measurement. Impedance data were collected in two-electrode mode across the 102 to 106 Hz frequency window while varying the temperature between 383 and 523 K.

The SEM micrograph acquired at room temperature and at a magnification of 2.00k (Figure a) reveals a dense polycrystalline microstructure consisting of grains with irregular morphologies and variable sizes. ## Assignment of Raman and IR Bands for TlCrP2O7
The primary vibrational bands are assigned to the internal motions of the [P2O7] unit. In particular, the IR bands observed at 1242 and 1183 cm–1, along with the Raman bands at 1168, 1158, 1129, and 1099 cm–1, are attributed to the asymmetric stretching vibrations νas(PO3). In IR, the symmetric stretching vibrations νs(PO3) occur at 1093, 1047, and 1022 cm–1, with corresponding Raman bands at 1064 and 1029 cm–1. Indeed, the band at 917 cm–1 in the infrared region and the band near 910 cm–1 in Raman spectroscopy are assigned to the asymmetric stretching vibration νas(POP), while the symmetric stretching mode νs(POP) is observed at 758 cm–1 in the IR spectrum and at 763 cm–1 in the Raman spectrum. The IR bands observed at 642 cm–1 and 587 cm–1, along with the Raman bands between 601 cm–1 and 580 cm–1, are assigned to the antisymmetric deformation mode δas(PO3), while the bands appearing in the range (437–547) cm–1 in the IR spectrum and (356–549) cm–1 in the Raman spectrum are attributed to the symmetric deformation mode δs(PO3). The 300 cm–1 Raman band corresponds to Cr–O stretching modes and resembles the Cr3+–O stretching features seen in Na3Cr2(PO4)3. The Raman bands appearing between 149 and 272 cm–1, as well as the band at 146 cm–1, are attributed to the external vibrational modes.

## Absorption Spectra and Band Gap Determination
Figure a illustrates the absorbance spectrum of TlCrP2O7 samples annealed at 800 °C, measured at room temperature over the 200–1100 nm wavelength range. The spectrum displays three prominent absorption bands. Band A1, located at 350 nm, was chosen as the excitation wavelength to prevent interference with Cr3+ transitions. The other two bands, A2 at 460 nm and A3 at 650–750 nm, are attributed to d–d electronic transitions of Cr3+ ions (3d3 configuration) occupying octahedral sites. , The optical band gap was evaluated using the Marotti method, which involves plotting the derivative of absorbance with respect to wavelength (dA/dλ) versus λ; the minimum of this plot indicates the band gap, as depicted in Figure b. This approach yielded a band gap energy of E g = 3.14 eV for TlCrP2O7.

From the absorbance spectra of TlCrP2O7, the absorption coefficient (α) can be calculated using the following relations: 4 α=2.303×Ad In this expression, A stands for absorbance, d indicates the layer thickness, and λ denotes the wavelength.
In a semiconductor, the band gap energy defines the energy necessary to move an electron from the valence band into the conduction band. An accurate determination of this parameter is essential for predicting the optical properties of semiconductors. Tauc proposed a method to determine the band gap energy of semiconductors based on their optical absorption spectra. , This technique relies on the absorption coefficient (α) being dependent on photon energy, as described by the following equations: 5 (α(E=hν))1/n=C((E=hν)−Eg)ifE≥Eg(α(E=hν))1/n=0ifE≤Eg In this context, h represents Planck’s constant, ν is the photon frequency, E g stands for the band gap energy, and C is a proportionality factor.
The photon energy is given by E = hν. The exponent n is determined by the nature of the electronic transition, taking a value of 1/2 for direct transitions and 2 for indirect transitions. As indicated by eq , the band gap energy can be obtained from the intercept of the linear portion of the Tauc plot on the energy (x) axis. This method is generally valid for semiconducting materials that exhibit negligible absorption in the region where (α­(E = hν))1/n = 0 for E ≤ E g. However, in materials with significant subgap absorption, the estimation may be affected by defects, doping, or surface modifications that introduce intragap states, leading to the appearance of an Urbach tail or additional absorption bands. Such effects distort the Tauc plot and must be considered to ensure an accurate determination of E g. Applying the Tauc method indiscriminately over the entire energy range may yield erroneous results by shifting the intercept toward lower energies, thereby underestimating the actual band gap. Figure presents the plots of (αhν)2 and (αhν)1/2 as a function of photon energy (hν). From these plots, the optical band gap values were extracted: the direct transition energy (E gd) was determined to be 2.99 eV, while the indirect transition energy (E gi) was found to be 2.57 eV.

## Urbach Energy
The Urbach energy (E u) represents the width of the exponential tail in the optical absorption edge and provides valuable insight into the degree of structural disorder and defect-induced localized states within the band gap. A higher Urbach energy indicates an increased density of localized states, often associated with lattice imperfections, compositional fluctuations, or electron–phonon interactions. The calculated E u values suggest that disorder and defect states significantly influence the optical absorption behavior of the compound, which in turn affects its optoelectronic performance. ## Complex Impedance Analysis
Impedance spectroscopy (IS) is a powerful analytical tool widely used to probe charge transport, polarization mechanisms, and relaxation dynamics in functional materials. , The electrical response of a ceramic is strongly influenced by its microstructural features, where well-conducting grains are typically separated by grain boundaries of comparatively higher resistance. In this study, impedance spectroscopy was employed to link the microstructural features of TlCrP2O7 with its electrical transport behavior across a broad range of temperatures and frequencies. The complex impedance (Z) provides valuable information on both the dielectric response (ε) and the capacitive contributions of different microstructural components. A detailed analysis of the complex impedance (Z*) allows for separating the effects of grains and grain boundaries and for revealing the relaxation mechanisms that control charge transport. Although Z* primarily reflects the material’s intrinsic characteristics, external factors such as electrode polarization, contact resistance, and sample geometry may also affect the measured impedance response. Figure illustrates how the real component of the impedance (Z′) changes with frequency at various temperatures. Two characteristic zones are evident: (i) a low-frequency plateau representing direct current (DC) conduction and (ii) a high-frequency region where the response becomes AC-dependent. The DC component arises from long-range charge carrier motion produced by efficient hopping between accessible sites. A marked decrease in Z′ with increasing frequency and temperature is observed, which is characteristic of materials exhibiting a negative temperature coefficient of resistance (NTCR). At low frequencies, Z′ exhibits a plateau that becomes more pronounced at higher temperatures, followed by a sharp drop, reflecting its strong thermal sensitivity. This trend demonstrates a pronounced reduction in the material’s overall resistance as the temperature increases. Beyond a characteristic frequency, Z′ continues to decrease with frequency, which can be ascribed to enhanced charge carrier mobility and the diminishing influence of trapped charges. The evolution of Z′ with frequency reveals a shift in Tl+-ion transport, moving from long-range diffusion in the low-frequency region to localized hopping at higher frequencies, a process enhanced by the diminished influence of trapping sites at higher temperatures.
Frequency-dependent behavior of the real component of impedance (Z′) measured between 383 and 523 K.
Figure shows the temperature-dependent variation of the imaginary part of the impedance (Z″). Each spectrum exhibits a distinct Z max ″ peak that becomes broader and moves toward higher frequencies as the temperature increases. This evolution reflects a wide distribution of relaxation times and confirms that the relaxation process in TlCrP2O7 is thermally activated. , At higher frequencies, the Z″ curves tend to merge, a behavior linked to the diminished influence of space-charge effects due to the lowering of interfacial potential barriers. This behavior suggests that charge carriers accumulate at grain boundaries or interfacial regions, and their restricted mobility or trapping strongly influences the impedance response. Such interfacial charge accumulation becomes more significant at elevated frequencies, thereby contributing to the enhancement of AC conductivity and the merging of Z″ values.
Frequency-dependent evolution of the imaginary impedance component (Z″) recorded between 383 and 523 K.
Figure presents the Nyquist diagram (Cole–Cole plot) showing the relationship between the real (Z′) and imaginary (Z″) components of the complex impedance at different temperatures. In dielectric materials, Nyquist plots often exhibit up to three separate semicircles, representing the effects of grains (bulk), grain boundaries, and the electrode–sample interface. The high-frequency semicircles are generally attributed to the grain response, whereas those appearing at lower frequencies are associated with grain boundary effects. Any semicircles that appear in the very low-frequency region are linked to electrode polarization effects. In the present case, the plot exhibits a broad semicircular arc, which appears to be the superposition of two partially overlapping semicircles, with its center lying below the real axis. This departure from the ideal shape signifies non-Debye behavior, since, according to Debye’s model, a material with a single relaxation time would generate a perfect semicircle centered precisely on the real axis. The observation of two overlapping semicircles indicates that the impedance response of TlCrP2O7 is primarily governed by contributions from grains and grain boundaries, with minimal influence from electrode polarization. The measured impedance data were modeled using ZView software with an equivalent circuit consisting of two parallel resistor–constant-phase element (CPE) pairs connected in series (R1//CPE1R2//CPE2), as depicted in the inset of Figure . The phase constant of the impedance is commonly employed to characterize the observed non-Debye behavior, as expressed by the following relation: 8 ZCPE=1/Q(jω)α Here, Q denotes the empirical exponent of the constant-phase element (CPE), and ω is the angular frequency. The CPE behaves as a capacitor when α = 1, as a resistor when α = 0, and as an inductor when α = −1. As illustrated in Figure , the experimental impedance data show excellent agreement with the theoretical fits obtained using ZView. ## Study of Electrical Conductivity
To investigate charge carrier transport and identify the prevailing conduction mechanisms across different temperatures and frequencies, the electrical conductivity of the sample was examined. It was calculated from the real (Z′) and imaginary (Z″) parts of the impedance using the following expression: 11 σ=esZ′(Z′2+Z″2) In this formula, Z′ and Z″ denote the real and imaginary parts of the complex impedance, respectively, whereas “e” and “s” refer to the thickness and cross-sectional area of the pellet, respectively.
Figure illustrates the influence of temperature and frequency on the electrical conductivity of TlCrP2O7. At low frequencies, the conductivity is dominated by the DC component, which is largely temperature-dependent and remains nearly constant with frequency, resulting in the characteristic plateau. The conductivity in this low-frequency region ranges from approximately 2 × 10–8 to 6 × 10–6 Ω–1·cm–1 as the temperature increases from 383 to 523 K, confirming the semiconducting nature of TlCrP2O7. This plateau is mainly due to the grain boundary effect, where the boundaries in polycrystalline ceramics function as resistive barriers or trapping centers that hinder the movement of charge carriers. Consequently, at low frequencies, carriers have sufficient time to interact with these barriers, leading to space charge accumulation, ionic atmospheres, and polarization effects that dominate the electrical response. At elevated frequencies, the conductivity rises with increasing frequency, signaling a shift toward conduction dominated by the grains (bulk). Here, the rapidly oscillating alternating field allows charge carriers to move between localized sites within the grains before encountering the resistive effects of grain boundaries. This frequency-dependent increase illustrates the dynamic relaxation of the ionic surroundings around the carriers, facilitating more efficient intragrain transport. The combined observation of a nearly constant conductivity at low frequencies and a dispersive, frequency-dependent response at high frequencies underscores the complex and heterogeneous electrical nature of TlCrP2O7. This behavior reflects the combined influence of microstructural features, with grain boundaries restricting charge carrier movement at low frequencies, while the inherent properties of the grains control conduction at higher frequencies. Importantly, this frequency-dependent response aligns with Jonscher’s universal power law, which provides a reliable description of the electrical conductivity in disordered and polycrystalline systems. The corresponding mathematical expression is given by 12 σ(ω,T)=σdc(T)+σac(ω,T)=σdc(T)+Pωs(T) In this context, σdc represents the dc conductivity at lower frequencies, while σac corresponds to the conductivity in the high-frequency region. The parameter “P” is a temperature-dependent constant, and “s” is the power-law exponent that reflects the degree of interaction between mobile ions and their surrounding environment. Physically, the exponent “s” reflects how charge carriers move by hopping over energy barriers or between localized sites, with their dynamics influenced by structural disorder, defects, and microstructural characteristics such as grain boundaries and porosity. At higher frequencies, carriers can utilize additional conduction pathways with lower resistance, which enhances the overall conductivity. Additionally, the surrounding ionic environment including local polarization fields and space-charge regions relaxes more effectively under high-frequency excitation, facilitating ionic hopping and producing the observed frequency-dependent dispersion in conductivity.

To gain deeper insight into the conduction mechanism, the temperature dependence of the power-law exponent s was examined. Several theoretical frameworks have been developed to explain AC conductivity in disordered materials, including the correlated barrier hopping (CBH), nonoverlapping small polaron tunneling (NSPT), overlapping large polaron tunneling (OLPT), and quantum mechanical tunneling (QMT) models. Each model exhibits a characteristic temperature dependence of the exponent “s”: in CBH, “s” decreases with rising temperature; in NSPT, it increases; OLPT shows a nonmonotonic trend; and QMT predicts an almost constant “s” value near 0.8. , In the case of TlCrP2O7, Figure a shows that “s” increases steadily within the 383–523 K range, which is consistent with the NSPT model, indicating that charge transport is dominated by a nonoverlapping small polaron tunneling mechanism. This behavior is in agreement with previous studies on phosphate-based semiconductors such as LiFeP2O7, K2CuP2O7, and NaMnPO4.

Table summarizes the activation energies (E a) reported for several chromium pyrophosphate compounds and highlights the influence of the A-site cation on charge-transport behavior. The E a values span a broad range (0.36–1.01 eV), ,,, reflecting differences in lattice rigidity and carrier mobility across the CrP2O7 family. NaCrP2O7 exhibits the highest activation energy (1.01 eV), indicating strongly localized carriers, whereas AgCrP2O7 shows the lowest values (0.36–0.50 eV), consistent with enhanced charge mobility facilitated by the highly polarizable Ag+ ion. KCrP2O7 and LiCrP2O7 display intermediate activation energies, suggesting mixed contributions from lattice distortions and thermally activated hopping mechanisms. In comparison, TlCrP2O7, investigated for the first time in this work, exhibits an activation energy of 0.45 eV in the 383–523 K range, placing it among the more conductive members of this family. This relatively low E a value implies that Tl+ incorporation into the pyrophosphate network reduces the energy barrier for charge transport, making TlCrP2O7 a promising candidate for functional applications within the broader class of phosphate-based materials.

## Investigation of Dielectric Behavior
The dielectric behavior of TlCrP2O7 can be further characterized using the complex permittivity expression: 13 ε*=ε′−jε″ In this formula, ε′ denotes the real component of the permittivity, reflecting the material’s dielectric constant, whereas ε″ represents the imaginary component, which accounts for dielectric losses. These contributions can be expressed mathematically as 14 ε′(ω)=Z″ωC0(Z′2+Z″2) 15 ε″(ω)=Z′ωC0(Z′2+Z″2) Within this context, C 0 represents the vacuum capacitance, given by C 0 = ε0 A/d, where ε0 is the permittivity of free space, A is the electrode area, and d is the thickness of the sample. Z′ and Z″ correspond to the real and imaginary parts of the impedance, respectively, and ω is the angular frequency.
Figure a shows the variation of the real part of the dielectric permittivity (ε′) with frequency at different temperatures. The observed enhancement of ε′ values in the low-frequency region can be ascribed to interfacial polarization and space-charge accumulation occurring at the interfaces between grains and grain boundaries. , Moreover, ε′ increases with temperature at a given frequency, suggesting thermally activated charge carrier dynamics that enhance polarization. The observed dielectric response can be attributed to different polarization mechanisms, including orientational and interfacial. Of these, interfacial and orientational polarizations are classified as relaxation components of polarizability. Based on Koop’s phenomenological model, the variation of ε′ with frequency is largely controlled by Maxwell–Wagner interfacial polarization. In this model, the ceramic microstructure consists of well-conducting grains separated by poorly conducting grain boundaries, where charge carriers accumulate at low frequencies. This accumulation generates interfacial polarization, leading to the elevated ε′ values observed in the low-frequency regime. At higher frequencies, the alternating electric field changes too quickly for charge carriers to move and accumulate at the grain boundaries. Consequently, polarization decreases, and ε′ levels off to an almost constant value. This high-frequency “unrelaxed” permittivity primarily reflects the contributions of atomic and electronic polarizations.

Likewise, the imaginary part of the dielectric permittivity (ε″), which reflects energy dissipation within the system (Figure b), exhibits a pronounced decrease with increasing frequency. This behavior suggests that dielectric losses are dominant at low frequencies, primarily due to space-charge effects and interfacial polarization. At elevated frequencies, ε″ decreases significantly because the charge carriers cannot respond quickly enough to follow the rapidly changing electric field. Consequently, less energy is dissipated as heat, resulting in lower dielectric losses and improved energy efficiency. As the frequency rises, the resistive component of the compound decreases, requiring less energy for charge carrier relocation and thereby further minimizing dielectric losses in the high-frequency regime. Such behavior highlights the potential of the synthesized compound for use in energy-efficient electronic and microelectronic devices. The dielectric constant (ε′) of a material indicates its capability to store electrical energy under an applied field, with higher ε′ values corresponding to greater charge storage capacity. Conversely, the dielectric loss (ε″) measures the portion of energy dissipated as heat in an alternating field, so lower ε″ values reflect improved efficiency, a critical property for microelectronic devices where reducing power loss is important.

## Conclusion
In this study, TlCrP2O7 was successfully prepared using a solid-state reaction method and thoroughly analyzed through a range of complementary techniques. X-ray powder diffraction verified the formation of a pure monoclinic phase (P21/c) with accurately refined lattice parameters and high-quality Rietveld fitting. SEM observations showed a compact microstructure consisting of submicron grains with a homogeneous elemental distribution. Structural integrity was further validated through Raman and infrared spectroscopy, which displayed characteristic internal and external vibrational modes of the pyrophosphate network. Optical investigations, including UV–vis absorption analysis, Tauc plots, and Urbach energy calculations (0.48 eV), indicated a direct band gap of 2.99 eV and highlighted the role of Cr3+ ions in introducing disorder and defect states. Impedance spectroscopy confirmed the semiconducting behavior of TlCrP2O7, displaying a typical negative temperature coefficient of resistance (NTCR) and non-Debye relaxation. The electrical transport in TlCrP2O7 was found to be thermally activated, exhibiting activation energies of 0.52 eV for the grains and 0.47 eV for the grain boundaries. An equivalent circuit including constant phase elements effectively captured the influence of microstructural features on the conduction behavior. The AC conductivity displayed a frequency dependence in agreement with Jonscher’s universal power law, indicating a nonoverlapping small polaron tunneling (NSPT) conduction mechanism. Dielectric studies showed clear non-Debye relaxation and notable space-charge effects, with their magnitude strongly affected by temperature, frequency, and the presence of grain boundaries. Overall, the structural, optical, and electrical analyses indicate that TlCrP2O7 is a stable and promising material for thermally activated ionic and electronic conduction. This study presents the first report of its physical properties, identifies its band gap energy and semiconducting behavior, investigates its dielectric relaxation and charge transport mechanisms, and correlates microstructural features with functional responses. These findings highlight the novelty of TlCrP2O7 and its potential relevance within the broader field of phosphate-based functional materials.
