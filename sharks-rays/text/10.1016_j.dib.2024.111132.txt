# BD-freshwater-fish: An image dataset from Bangladesh for AI-powered automatic fish species classification and detection toward smart aquaculture

## Abstract
There are about 33,000 different species of fish and they are visually identified using variety of traits, i.e., size and shape of body, head's size and shape, skin pattern, fin pattern, mouth pattern, scale pattern, and eye pattern etc. In traditional manner, identifying these fish species is always difficult with necked eye. Identification and detection of fish species from images using deep learning and computer vision based techniques is challenging topic among researchers worldwide as an interesting problem. Automatic fish species classification and detection has practical importance for both smart aquaculture and fish industry. AI powered deep learning and computer vision based automatic fish species recognition and sorting system becoming significant factor for making aquaculture industry more productive and sustainable. However, the performance of machine learning classifier greatly depends on the size of image dataset and the quality of the images in the dataset. This article demonstrate BD-Freshwater-Fish, an image dataset contain 4389 images of 12 different species captured in natural environment using HD mobile camera from local fish market of Sylhet and Jessore district of Bangladesh. Twelve (12) different data classes are: Rohu (Labeo rohita), Catla (Catla catla), Mrigal (Cirrhinus cirrhosus), Grass Carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella), Common Carp (Cyprinus carpio), Mirror Carp (Cyprinus carpio var. specularis), Black Rohu (Labeo calbasu), Silver Carp (Hypophthalmichthys molitrix), Striped Catfish (Pangasius pangasius), Nile Tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus), Long-whiskered Catfish (Sperata aor), Freshwater Shark (Wallago attu) has been included in the dataset with a different number of images of different species. The BD-Freshwater-Fish dataset is hosted by Department of Computer Science and Engineering mutually with the help of the Department of Aquaculture, Sylhet Agricultural University, Sylhet, Bangladesh.

## Background
The ultimate goal of this project is to build a large-scale image dataset of freshwater fish from Bangladesh which will be freely accessible for any interested researchers globally. This fish image dataset can be used to develop automatic fish species classification and detection system based on various computer vision and deep learning based algorithms for sustainable aquaculture and fisheries industry. Ichthyology students can learn and study about many features of fish species via fish images as various species are disappearing day by day due to variety of causes.

## Data Description
Fish are aquatic animals that are key source of high quality animal protein and other micronutrients for the human body [1]. Now a day's automatic fish species classification and detection using computer vision and machine learning based approaches are being very popular and active research areas among the researchers for sustainable fish farm management and precision fish farming. A large amount of labeled images dataset is mandatory for such machine vision based automatic classification and detection system [2]. But there are a limited number of fish image datasets available for deep learning and computer vision based application [3,4]. Fish species classification from images using machine learning and computer vision is a multi-class identification problem and attracting researchers worldwide as a popular domain.
This article presents an image-based dataset of 12 (twelve) distinct fish species, containing total 4389 images of fresh water fish from Bangladesh. Collected fish species for dataset are Rohu (Labeo rohita), Catla (Catla catla), Mrigal (Cirrhinus cirrhosus), Grass Carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella), Common Carp (Cyprinus carpio), Mirror Carp (Cyprinus carpio var. specularis), Black Rohu (Labeo calbasu), Silver Carp (Hypophthalmichthys molitrix), Striped Catfish (Pangasius pangasius), Nile Tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus), Long-whiskered Catfish (Sperata aor), Freshwater Shark (Wallago attu). Each image in the dataset shows the entire body of the respective fish where different dominant features (Head, Body, Fin and Tail) of a fish are clearly distinguishable in most of the images in the dataset. Photographs of both live and dead fish were considered for taking photos to building dataset, depending on the availability of the fish on the local fish markets. All the images of fish species were taken out of water, always one side of each fish were considered for photographed. In most cases, a fish is captured once; few fish are photographed multiple times with changing the orientation of fish on white board. ## Experimental Design, Materials and Methods
The images captured for this dataset are collected from various local fish markets of two separate district of Bangladesh, namely Jessore and Sylhet district of Bangladesh between December 2023 and April 2024 (Fig. 2). The imaging areas consists of eighteen local fish markets where ten markets are in Jessore and eight markets are in Sylhet district. The name, location and latitude-longitude of the local fish markets are also listed in the following figure (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Graph showed data collection areas where fish image were collected.Fig 2
Graph showed data collection areas where fish image were collected.
Fish images were all captured in the natural light environment from the local fish market using three different mobile cameras. The images were clicked from the above of board at different height, the angle at which the fish was photographed was constantly changed to catch the entire body of fish. After image collection, sort the defective images and remove them from the dataset. The photos are cropped manually to remove unwanted surrounding from the image before placing into specific fish folder. The data are scrutinized finally regarding fish species before data consolidation and publication.

## Limitations
Taking individual fish photos one after another from retail fishmongers takes a lot of effort and they frequently don't comply during business hours. Most of the time fisherman doesn't allow catching fish by hand as the fish can rot or spoil. It is also incredibly challenging to get still photo of live fish when they are out of water (Fig. 3).Fig. 3Diagrammatic view regarding steps involved in building dataset.Fig 3

The very few photos of Freshwater Shark (Wallago attu) and Long-whiskered Catfish (Sperata aor) fish in the dataset is a result of their non-season during the imaging period.
