Abstract

16

In electroreceptive jawed vertebrates, embryonic lateral line placodes give rise to

17

electrosensory ampullary organs as well as mechanosensory neuromasts. Previous reports

18

of shared gene expression suggest that conserved mechanisms underlie electroreceptor and

19

mechanosensory hair cell development and that electroreceptors evolved as a

20

transcriptionally related 'sister cell type' to hair cells. We previously identified only one

21

transcription factor gene, Neurod4, as ampullary organ-restricted in the developing lateral line

22

system of a chondrostean ray-finned fish, the Mississippi paddlefish (Polyodon spathula). The

23

other 16 transcription factor genes we previously validated in paddlefish were expressed in

24

both ampullary organs and neuromasts. Here, we used our published lateral line organ-

25

enriched gene-set (arising from differential bulk RNA-seq in late-larval paddlefish), together

26

with a candidate gene approach, to identify 23 transcription factor genes expressed in the

27

developing lateral line system of a more experimentally tractable chondrostean, the sterlet

28

(Acipenser ruthenus, a small sturgeon), and/or that of paddlefish. Twelve are expressed in

29

both ampullary organs and neuromasts, consistent with conservation of molecular

30

mechanisms. Six are novel electrosensory-restricted transcription factor genes (Irx5, Satb2,

31

Insm1, Sp5, MafA and Rorc), and five are the first-reported mechanosensory-restricted

32

transcription factor genes (Foxg1, Sox8, Isl1, Hmx2 and Rorb). However, as previously

33

reported, Sox8 is expressed in ampullary organs as well as neuromasts in a shark

34

(Scyliorhinus canicula), suggesting the existence of lineage-specific differences between

35

cartilaginous and ray-finned fishes. Overall, our results support the hypothesis that ampullary

36

organs and neuromasts develop via largely conserved transcriptional mechanisms, and

37

identify multiple transcription factors potentially involved in the formation of electrosensory

38

versus mechanosensory lateral line organs.

Introduction

41
42

In jawed anamniotes, mechanosensory hair cells are found in the inner ear, in the spiracular

43

organ associated with the first pharyngeal cleft (lost in amphibians, bichirs and teleosts; see

44

Norris and Hughes, 1920; von Bartheld and Giannessi, 2011), and in lateral line neuromasts:

45

small sense organs distributed in lines over the head and trunk, which respond to local water

46

movement (see e.g., Mogdans, 2019; Webb, 2021). In electroreceptive species (e.g.,

47

cartilaginous fishes, ray-finned fishes including the chondrostean paddlefishes and sturgeons,

48

and urodele amphibians like the axolotl), the lateral line system includes electrosensory

49

ampullary organs containing supporting cells and electroreceptors that detect weak electric

50

fields, primarily for hunting or avoiding predators (see e.g., Crampton, 2019; Leitch and Julius,

51

2019; Chagnaud et al., 2021).

52

Like hair cells, electroreceptors have an apical primary cilium (lost during maturation

53

in cochlear hair cells; Lu and Sipe, 2016) and basolateral ribbon synapses with afferent nerve

54

terminals (Jørgensen, 2005; Baker, 2019). However, electroreceptors lack the apical hair

55

bundle (staircase array of microvilli) where mechanoelectrical transduction occurs (Ó

56

Maoiléidigh and Ricci, 2019; Caprara and Peng, 2022). The main anamniote developmental

57

models - the teleost zebrafish and the frog Xenopus - lack ampullary organs: electroreception

58

was lost in the ray-finned bony fish radiation leading to teleosts, and in the lobe-finned bony

59

amphibian lineage leading to frogs (Baker et al., 2013; Baker, 2019; Crampton, 2019).

60

(Physiologically distinct lateral line electroreceptors evolved independently in some teleost

61

lineages; see e.g., Baker et al., 2013; Baker, 2019; Crampton, 2019.)

62

Neuromasts and ampullary organs, together with the neurons in lateral line ganglia

63

that provide their afferent innervation, develop from a series of cranial lateral line placodes

64

(Northcutt, 1997; Baker et al., 2013; Piotrowski and Baker, 2014). These either elongate to

65

form sensory ridges that fragment, with a line of neuromasts forming first in the ridge's centre

66

and ampullary organs (if present) forming later on the ridge's flanks (Northcutt, 1997;

67

Piotrowski and Baker, 2014). Alternatively, as in the ray-finned bony teleost zebrafish, lateral

68

line primordia migrate as cell collectives, depositing neuromasts in their wake (Piotrowski and

69

Baker, 2014). The lateral line placode origin of ampullary organs was first shown by grafting

70

and ablation in a lobe-finned bony tetrapod, the axolotl (Northcutt et al., 1995). Our DiI-

71

labelling studies in a chondrostean ray-finned fish (Mississippi paddlefish, Polyodon spathula)

72

(Modrell et al., 2011a) and a cartilaginous fish (little skate, Leucoraja erinacea) (Gillis et al.,

73

2012) showed that individual elongating lateral line placodes form ampullary organs and

74

neuromasts across all non-teleost jawed vertebrates (reviewed in Baker et al., 2013).

What molecular mechanisms underlie the formation of ampullary organs versus

76

neuromasts within the same lateral line sensory ridge? We have identified a range of

77

ampullary organ-expressed genes in different electroreceptive vertebrates using a candidate

78

gene approach (O'Neill et al., 2007; Modrell et al., 2011a; Modrell et al., 2011b; Gillis et al.,

79

2012; Modrell and Baker, 2012; Modrell et al., 2017a; Modrell et al., 2017b). More recently,

80

we took an unbiased differential RNA-seq approach, comparing the transcriptome of late-

81

larval paddlefish gill-flaps (covered in ampullary organs, plus some neuromasts) versus fins

82

(no lateral line organs) (Modrell et al., 2017a). The resultant lateral line organ-enriched dataset

83

of around 500 genes (Modrell et al., 2017a) is not exhaustive: it includes most, but not all, of

84

the genes identified in paddlefish ampullary organs via the candidate gene approach (Modrell

85

et al., 2011a; Modrell et al., 2011b; Modrell et al., 2017a; Modrell et al., 2017b). In situ

86

hybridization for selected candidate genes from this dataset suggested that electroreceptors

87

and hair cells are closely related, e.g., late-larval ampullary organs express genes encoding

88

proteins essential for neurotransmission specifically at hair-cell (but not photoreceptor) ribbon

89

synapses in the basolateral cell membrane (Pangrsic et al., 2018; Moser et al., 2020), such

90

as vGlut3, otoferlin and Cav1.3 (Modrell et al., 2017a). Cav1.3 has also been identified as the

91

electrosensitive voltage-gated Ca2+ channel in the apical electroreceptor membrane (Bennett

92

and Obara, 1986; Bodznick and Montgomery, 2005) in shark and skate species (i.e., in

93

cartilaginous fishes) (Bellono et al., 2017; Bellono et al., 2018).

94

Developing ampullary organs also express key 'hair cell' transcription factor genes

95

including Atoh1, Pou4f3 (Brn3c) and Six1 (Modrell et al., 2011a; Butts et al., 2014; Modrell et

96

al., 2017a). These are critical for hair cell formation and, in combination with Gfi1, can drive

97

an immature 'hair cell-like' fate in mouse embryonic stem cells, adult cochlear supporting cells

98

and fibroblasts (see Roccio et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2021; Iyer and Groves, 2021; Iyer et al.,

99

2022). Co-expression of Atoh1, Pou4f3 and Gfi1 is sufficient to drive a more mature hair cell

100

fate in postnatal cochlear supporting cells (Chen et al., 2021). The expression of Atoh1,

101

Pou4f3 and Six1 in developing ampullary organs, as well as neuromasts, suggests that

102

molecular mechanisms underlying electroreceptor development are likely to be highly

103

conserved with those underlying hair cell formation (Modrell et al., 2011a; Modrell et al.,

104

2017a). Nevertheless, hair cells and electroreceptors are morphologically and functionally

105

distinct (Jørgensen, 2005; Baker, 2019). Validation of multiple candidate genes from the late-

106

larval paddlefish lateral line organ-enriched dataset had identified only a handful of genes with

107

expression in developing ampullary organs but not neuromasts (Modrell et al., 2017a). These

108

were the basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) transcription factor gene Neurod4, plus two voltage-

109

gated potassium channel subunit genes (Kcna5, encoding Kv1.5, and Kcnab3, encoding the

110

accessory subunit Kvb3) and a calcium-chelating beta-parvalbumin, all presumably involved

111

in electroreceptor function (Modrell et al., 2017a).

In recent years, another chondrostean fish, the sterlet (a sturgeon, Acipenser

113

ruthenus), has been developed as an experimentally tractable non-teleost model (see e.g.,

114

Saito and Psenicka, 2015; Chen et al., 2018; Baloch et al., 2019; Stundl et al., 2020; Du et al.,

115

2020; Stundl et al., 2022). In contrast to the limited Mississippi paddlefish spawning season,

116

many hundreds of sterlet embryos are available each week for up to several months in fully

117

equipped laboratory research facilities. We have therefore turned to the sterlet as a tractable

118

model for studying the molecular control of lateral line hair cell and electroreceptor

119

development. In the current study, we describe lateral line hair cell and electroreceptor

120

differentiation in the sterlet, and the expression in sterlet and/or paddlefish of almost all of the

121

remaining transcription factor genes from the paddlefish late-larval lateral line organ-enriched

122

dataset (Modrell et al., 2017a), plus a few additional candidates. We report expression within

123

the developing lateral line system of 23 novel transcription factor genes. Twelve - including

124

the key 'hair cell' transcription factor gene Gfi1 - were expressed in both ampullary organs and

125

neuromasts, supporting conserved molecular mechanisms. Six transcription factor genes

126

proved to be electrosensory-restricted, while five represent the first-reported mechanosensory

127

lateral line-restricted transcription factors. These eleven genes, plus ampullary organ-

128

restricted Neurod4 (Modrell et al., 2017a), are good candidates to be involved in the

129

development of electrosensory versus mechanosensory lateral line organs.

Results

132
133

Characterizing lateral line hair cell and electroreceptor development in sterlet

134

In order to use the sterlet as a more experimentally tractable model for lateral line organ

135

development than the Mississippi paddlefish (Modrell et al., 2011b; Modrell et al., 2011a;

136

Modrell et al., 2017b; Modrell et al., 2017a), we first characterized the timing and distribution

137

of lateral line hair cell and electroreceptor differentiation (staging according to Dettlaff et al.,

138

1993). The development of lateral line placodes, neuromasts and ampullary organs had

139

previously been described in the shovelnose sturgeon, Scaphirhynchus platorynchus (Gibbs

140

and Northcutt, 2004a); ampullary organ formation had also been described in a sturgeon in

141

the same genus as the sterlet, the Adriatic sturgeon, A. naccari (Camacho et al., 2007). In S.

142

platorynchus, all lateral line placodes were present at stage 30 and had started to elongate

143

into sensory ridges (Gibbs and Northcutt, 2004a). By the time of hatching at stage 36,

144

neuromast primordia were present within the sensory ridges at the centre of all the lateral line

145

placodes but only a few mature neuromasts had formed, specifically in the otic lateral line

146

(Gibbs and Northcutt, 2004a). At stage 41, roughly midway between hatching (stage 36) and

147

the onset of independent feeding (stage 45), ampullary organ primordia were present in the

148

lateral zones of the anterodorsal, anteroventral, otic and supratemporal sensory ridges (Gibbs

three weeks (Gibbs and Northcutt, 2004a). By stage 45 in A. naccari, in contrast, ampullary

157

organs in sterlet, we focused on the stages between stage 35, i.e., the stage before hatching

158

159

at 14 days post-fertilization (dpf; Zeiske et al., 2003). Figure 1 shows a temporal overview of

160

167

'Hair cell' transcription factor genes expressed in developing ampullary organs include

202

Gfi1, Sox4 and Sox3

203

We previously showed that various transcription factor genes essential for hair cell

204

development - Six1, Eya1, Sox2, Atoh1, Pou4f3 (see Roccio et al., 2020; Iyer and Groves,

205

2021) - are expressed in developing paddlefish ampullary organs, as well as neuromasts

206

(Modrell et al., 2011b; Modrell et al., 2011a; Butts et al., 2014; Modrell et al., 2017a). We

207

confirm here that, in addition to Sox2 (Figure 1E-H; Figure 2A,B), Atoh1 and Pou4f3 are also

208

expressed in both types of lateral line organ in sterlet (Figure 2C-F). As noted earlier, Sox2

209

was expressed more strongly in neuromasts than ampullary organs (Figure 1G,H; Figure

210

2A,B). However, Atoh1 showed the converse pattern, with stronger expression in ampullary

211

organs than in neuromasts (Figure 2C,D). These differential expression patterns were also

212

seen at earlier stages (for Sox2, see Figure 1E-G; for Atoh1, see Supplementary Figure S2A-

213

D).

214

A key 'hair cell' transcription factor gene whose expression we had not previously

215

examined is the zinc-finger transcription factor gene Gfi1 (see Roccio et al., 2020; Chen et al.,

216

2021; Iyer and Groves, 2021; Iyer et al., 2022). Gfi1 was 12.0-fold enriched in late-larval

217

paddlefish operculum versus fin tissue (Modrell et al., 2017a). Sterlet Gfi1 proved also to be

218

expressed in developing ampullary organs, as well as neuromasts (Figure 2G,H).

219

In the mouse inner ear, the SoxC subfamily members Sox4 and Sox11 are co-

220

expressed in proliferating hair cell progenitor cells and newly born hair cells, and in

221

combination are essential for hair cell formation (Gnedeva and Hudspeth, 2015). Ectopic

222

expression of either gene converts supporting cells to hair cells (Gnedeva and Hudspeth,

223

2015). Although neither Sox4 nor Sox11 was present in the paddlefish lateral line-enriched

224

gene-set (Modrell et al., 2017a), we cloned sterlet Sox4, which proved to be expressed in both

225

ampullary organs and neuromasts, though more strongly in ampullary organs (Figure 2I,J).

226

This differential expression pattern was also seen at earlier stages (Supplementary Figure

227

S2E-H).

228

229

SoxB1 subfamily member Sox3, as well as Sox2, and that Sox3 is important for the formation

230

of the correct number of neuromast hair cells (preprint: Undurraga et al., 2019). A recent

231

single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) study showed Sox3 expression at homeostasis in

232

multiple neuromast support cell types (Baek et al., 2022) including central cells, the immediate

233

precursors of regenerating hair cells (Romero-Carvajal et al., 2015; Lush et al., 2019). We had

234

previously used a candidate gene approach for lateral line placode markers to identify Sox3

235

expression in paddlefish lateral line primordia, neuromasts and also ampullary organs (Modrell

236

et al., 2011b). (Sox3 was 5.2-fold enriched in late-larval paddlefish operculum versus fin

237

tissue; Modrell et al., 2017a.) As expected, sterlet Sox3 was also expressed in both types of

Additional transcription factor genes expressed in developing ampullary organs and

267

neuromasts

268

We cloned and analysed the expression of paddlefish and/or sterlet homologues of a further

269

33 transcription factor genes present in the late-larval paddlefish lateral line organ-enriched

270

dataset (Modrell et al., 2017a). (The paddlefish lateral line-enriched dataset also includes

271

fifteen other loci assigned to specific transcription factor/co-factor genes, for which cloning

272

and/or ISH failed in sterlet, or expression was inconsistent: Akna, Barx1, Egr2, Fev, Fhl2, Fhl5,

273

274

275

276

277

278

D). However, Insm2 expression was much stronger in ampullary organs than in neuromasts;

279

390

neuromasts was later even than Isl1, starting only at stage 41 (Figure 8M,N). It was intriguing

552

to see the mutually exclusive expression of Rorb in neuromasts (Figure 8O,P) and Rorc in

553

ampullary organs (Figure 4O,P).

554

Taken together, we have identified Sox8, Foxg1, Hmx2, Isl1 and Rorb as the first-

555

reported transcription factor genes restricted to the mechanosensory division of the lateral line

556

system in ray-finned fishes. Sox8 and Foxg1 are expressed in the central zone of sensory

557

ridges where neuromasts form and maintained in neuromasts, though apparently excluded

558

from differentiated hair cells. Hmx2 is expressed in sensory ridges and retained in neuromasts,

559

whereas Isl1 and Rorb are restricted to neuromasts as early as they can be detected.

Discussion

588
589

In this study, we used our paddlefish lateral line organ-enriched gene-set (generated from

590

differential bulk RNA-seq at late larval stages; Modrell et al., 2017a), together with a candidate

591

gene approach, to identify 23 novel transcription factor genes expressed in developing lateral

592

line organs in sterlet and/or paddlefish. These data, together with our previous work in

593

paddlefish (Modrell et al., 2011b; Modrell et al., 2011a; Modrell et al., 2017b; Modrell et al.,

594

2017a), suggest extensive conservation of molecular mechanisms involved in electrosensory

595

and mechanosensory lateral line organ development. However, they also reveal a set of

596

transcription factor genes with restricted expression that may be involved in the development

597

of mechanosensory versus electrosensory organs. Of the 40 transcription factor genes with

598

validated expression during lateral line organ development in paddlefish and/or sterlet, 28

599

(70%) were expressed in both ampullary organs and neuromasts (Table 1). These include the

600

key 'hair cell' transcription factor genes Six1, Eya1, Sox2, Atoh1, Pou4f3 and Gfi1 (see Roccio

601

et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2021; Iyer and Groves, 2021; Iyer et al., 2022). We also identified

602

seven electrosensory-restricted and five mechanosensory-restricted transcription factor

603

genes (Table 1), as discussed further below.

604

While this work was ongoing, a differential RNA-seq study of regenerating ampullary

605

organs and neuromasts in the Siberian sturgeon (Acipenser baerii) was published (Wang et

606

al., 2020). This study compared dissected tissue samples containing stage 45 ampullary

607

organs or neuromasts relative to general epidermis, which identified 2074 lateral line organ-

608

enriched genes, of which 1418 were shared by ampullary organs and neuromasts; 539 were

609

ampullary organ-enriched, and 117 were neuromast-enriched (Wang et al., 2020). The

610

'common' stage 45 lateral line-organ dataset from the Siberian sturgeon (Wang et al., 2020)

611

included many of the candidate genes encoding transcription factors and differentiation

612

markers whose expression we have validated in both ampullary organs and neuromasts at

613

stage 45-46 in paddlefish and/or sterlet, e.g. Six1, Eya1, Atoh1, Pou4f3, Gfi1, Otof, and

614

Cacna1d (Modrell et al., 2011b; Modrell et al., 2011a; Modrell et al., 2017a; this study). The

615

ampullary organ-enriched dataset from the Siberian sturgeon (Wang et al., 2020) included

616

Sp5, which we identified here in sterlet as ampullary organ-restricted, but also Hmx2 and Rorb,

617

which we identified here as neuromast-restricted. Conversely, the neuromast-enriched

618

dataset (Wang et al., 2020) included Insm1, which we found in sterlet to be ampullary organ-

619

restricted. Furthermore, the 'common' dataset (Wang et al., 2020) included genes whose

620

expression in sterlet and paddlefish was either ampullary organ-specific (e.g., the voltage-

621

gated K+ channel genes Kcna5 and Kcnab3, as well as Neurod4; this study; Modrell et al.,

622

2017a), or mechanosensory-specific (e.g., Foxg1 and Isl1; this study).
20

bioRxiv preprint doi: https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.04.14.536701; this version posted April 14, 2023. The copyright holder for this preprint
(which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. It is
made available under a CC-BY 4.0 International license.

623

Overall, we think that the stage 45 Siberian sturgeon tissue dissections (Wang et al.,

624

2020) were unable to separate ampullary organs and neuromasts completely. Like our own

625

stage 46 paddlefish lateral line organ-enriched dataset (Modrell et al., 2017a), the stage 45

626

Siberian sturgeon datasets are not exhaustive (Wang et al., 2020): some of the genes whose

627

expression we have validated in stage 45-46 sterlet and/or paddlefish lateral line organs were

628

missing (e.g., Satb2, Sox2 and Sox8; this study; Modrell et al., 2017a). Nevertheless, this

629

differential RNA-seq study in late-larval Siberian sturgeon embryos (Wang et al., 2020)

630

provides an invaluable, independent resource from which to identify additional candidate

631

genes for future validation and functional investigation in vivo.

633

Conserved

molecular

mechanisms

are

likely

involved

in

the formation

of

634

electroreceptors and hair cells

635

In this study, we identified twelve novel transcription factor genes expressed in both types of

636

lateral line organ in chondrostean ray-finned fishes, consistent with conservation of molecular

637

mechanisms. In particular, we highlight Gfi1 (see Roccio et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2021; Iyer

638

and Groves, 2021; Iyer et al., 2022) as being another key 'hair cell' transcription factor gene

639

expressed in developing ampullary organs as well as neuromasts, together with Atoh1,

640

Pou4f3, and Six1 (Modrell et al., 2011a; Modrell et al., 2017a). Gfi1-deficient hair cells fail to

641

mature and also upregulate neuronal differentiation genes such as Neurod1 and Pouf41,

642

suggesting that a key function of Gfi1 in hair cells is to repress neuronal genes that are initially

643

also expressed in hair cell progenitors (Matern et al., 2020). Gfi1 also acts indirectly to

644

increase Atoh1 transcriptional activity, by forming part of a transcriptional complex with Atoh1

645

and E proteins, in which neither Atoh1 nor Gfi1 binds the other directly and Gfi1 does not bind

646

DNA (Jen et al., 2022). Given shared expression in ampullary organs as well as neuromasts,

647

it seems likely that Gfi1 plays these roles in developing electroreceptors, as well as hair cells.

648

Intriguingly, however, Insm2 was recently reported as a direct target of both Atoh1 and Gfi1 in

649

mouse cochlear hair cells, and one of only a handful of genes (including Atoh1 itself) to be

650

repressed by Gfi1 during hair-cell maturation (Jen et al., 2022). Repression of Insm2 by Gfi1

651

in mature hair cells, but not electroreceptors, could explain the much weaker expression of

652

Insm2 that we saw in neuromasts versus ampullary organs at stage 45 (the onset of

653

independent feeding). This suggests the existence of both shared and divergent functions of

654

the same transcription factor within hair cells versus electroreceptors.

656

Six novel ampullary organ-restricted transcription factor genes

657

We have identified six novel transcription factor genes expressed in developing ampullary

658

organs but not neuromasts in chondrostean ray-finned fishes, in addition to previously

659

published Neurod4 (Modrell et al., 2017a). Two of these, Irx5 and Satb2, encode
21

bioRxiv preprint doi: https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.04.14.536701; this version posted April 14, 2023. The copyright holder for this preprint
(which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. It is
made available under a CC-BY 4.0 International license.

660

homeodomain transcription factors. In C. elegans, unique combinations of homeodomain

661

transcription factors define all 118 neuron classes (Reilly et al., 2020) (also see Vidal et al.,

662

2022). Hence, members of this class of transcription factors are potentially good candidates

663

to be involved in controlling divergent fate specification and/or maintenance in closely related

664

cell types.

665

Irx5 is required for the terminal differentiation of a subset of cone bipolar cells in the

666

mouse retina (Cheng et al., 2005). In mouse and chicken, Irx5 is expressed within the otic

667

vesicle epithelium, including some prospective sensory patches (Bosse et al., 2000; Cardeña-

668

Núñez et al., 2016). However, by stage 34 in chicken (embryonic day 8), when hair cells are

669

fully differentiated, Irx5 is not expressed in any sensory patch, unlike some other Irx family

670

members (Cardeña-Núñez et al., 2016). In mouse, chicken and zebrafish, Irx5 (together with

671

other Irx family members) is expressed in otic placode-derived neurons (Bosse et al., 2000;

672

Houweling et al., 2001; Cardeña-Núñez et al., 2016; Lecaudey et al., 2005). In zebrafish, the

673

only reported expression of Irx5a in the lateral line system is in the secondary posterior lateral

674

line primordium (prim II) (Lecaudey et al., 2005). This migrates later than the primary posterior

675

lateral line primordium and contributes post-embryonically to lateral and dorsal branches of

676

the trunk lateral line (Sapède et al., 2002). However, the function of Irx5 in the primordium is

677

not known (Lecaudey et al., 2005) and the lack of reported expression in neuromasts is

678

consistent with its restriction to ampullary organs in paddlefish and sterlet.

679

Ampullary organ-restricted Satb2 encodes a homeodomain transcription factor and

680

chromatin-remodeller that is important for craniofacial development, including osteoblast

681

differentiation (reviewed by Huang et al., 2022). Its expression has not been reported in the

682

inner ear or lateral line system. The Satb2 gene is directly bound by Smad1/5 and upregulated

683

following over-expression of Bmp4 in cranial neural crest cells, suggesting that Satb2 is a

684

direct target of the Bmp signalling pathway (Bonilla-Claudio et al., 2012). This raises the

685

possibility that Bmp signalling may be important for ampullary organ development. Indeed,

686

Bmp4, Bmp5, Brinp3 (encoding BMP/retinoic acid-inducible neural-specific protein 3) and

687

Bambi (encoding a Bmp/activin inhibitor) are present in the 'common' lateral line organ-

688

enriched gene-set from stage 45 Siberian sturgeon (Wang et al., 2020). Brinp3 is also in the

689

ampullary organ-enriched gene-set (Wang et al., 2020). Our stage 46 paddlefish lateral line

690

organ-enriched gene-set also includes Bmp5, together with genes encoding the dual Bmp/Wnt

691

inhibitors Sostdc1 and Apcdd1 (Modrell et al., 2017a). Thus, the Bmp pathway is a promising

692

target for studies of ampullary organ development.

693

The other electrosensory-restricted transcription factor genes were Insm1, Mafa, Rorc

694

and Sp5. In the inner ear, transient expression of Insm1 in developing outer hair cells prevents

695

them from transdifferentiating into inner hair cells, by repressing a set of genes usually

22

bioRxiv preprint doi: https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.04.14.536701; this version posted April 14, 2023. The copyright holder for this preprint
(which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. It is
made available under a CC-BY 4.0 International license.

696

enriched in early inner hair cells (Wiwatpanit et al., 2018). It is possible, therefore, that Insm1

697

also acts in developing ampullary organs to repress hair cell-specific genes.

698

MafA synergises with Neurod1 (and Pdx1) to activate the insulin promoter in pancreatic

699

beta-cells (reviewed in Liang et al., 2022). Given the ampullary organ-restricted expression of

700

Neurod4 in the paddlefish lateral line system (Modrell et al., 2017a), this raises the possibility

701

that MafA could similarly synergise with Neurod4 to activate ampullary organ-specific target

702

genes.

703

Rorc encodes two isoforms of a ligand-dependent transcription factor, RAR-related

704

orphan nuclear receptor gamma (RORγ and RORγt), primarily studied for its roles in regulating

705

Th17 cell differentiation and thus autoimmune and inflammatory diseases (see Fauber and

706

Magnuson, 2014; Meijer et al., 2020; Ladurner et al., 2021). Endogenous ligands for RORγ

707

have not been confirmed, but it responds to sterols including the cholesterol precursor,

708

desmosterol (see Hu et al., 2015; Meijer et al., 2020). Retinoic acid has also been reported to

709

inhibit RORγ activity (Stehlin-Gaon et al., 2003). In the axolotl, ampullary organs were missing

710

and far fewer cranial neuromasts formed after retinoic acid treatment for one hour at late

711

gastrula/early neurula stages (Gibbs and Northcutt, 2004b). However, this most likely reflects

712

an effect on the lateral line placodes themselves, rather than organ formation directly (Gibbs

713

and Northcutt, 2004b). In any case, the mutually exclusive expression of ampullary organ-

714

restricted Rorc and neuromast-restricted Rorb is particularly intriguing (also see next section).

715

Finally, Sp5 encodes a Wnt/β-catenin effector (Kennedy et al., 2016), suggesting that

716

this signalling pathway might be important for ampullary organ development. Indeed, one of

717

the other ampullary organ-restricted genes, Irx5, is directly upregulated by Wnt/β-catenin

718

signalling in somatic cells of the gonad (Koth et al., 2020).

719

Overall, the ampullary organ-restricted expression of these six transcription factor

720

genes provides a starting point for identifying molecular mechanisms that may be important

721

for the formation of electrosensory lateral line organs.

Five novel mechanosensory lateral line-restricted transcription factor genes

724

We identified five mechanosensory lateral line-restricted transcription factor genes: the first-

725

such genes reported in electroreceptive vertebrates. Of these, Hmx2, Isl1 and Rorb are

726

expressed in zebrafish lateral line placodes and/or neuromasts (Feng and Xu, 2010; Dufourcq

727

et al., 2006; Bertrand et al., 2007). Hmx2 and Isl1 both encode homeodomain transcription

728

factors. In zebrafish, Hmx2 is expressed throughout lateral line placode development, together

729

with the related gene Hmx3 (Feng and Xu, 2010). Morpholino knockdown experiments

730

suggested a redundant requirement for Hmx2 and Hmx3 for cell proliferation in the migrating

731

posterior lateral line primordium, and for normal neuromast formation (Feng and Xu, 2010).

732

Double mutant analysis of Hmx2 and Hmx3a suggested that the loss of neuromasts arises

made available under a CC-BY 4.0 International license.

from stalling of the migrating primordium adjacent to the first few somites, hence failure to

734

deposit neuromasts (England et al., 2020). Recent scRNA-seq data from zebrafish also show

735

that Hmx2 is expressed specifically in anterior-posterior (A/P) support cells in neuromasts

736

(Baek et al., 2022).

737

We cloned Isl1 because it promotes a more complete conversion by Atoh1 of mouse

738

cochlear supporting cells to hair cells than does Atoh1 alone (Yamashita et al., 2018). In

739

zebrafish neuromasts, Isl1 is expressed in multiple support cell types including central support

740

cells (Lush et al., 2019; Baek et al., 2022), which divide symmetrically to form new hair cells

741

after hair cells are ablated (Romero-Carvajal et al., 2015; Lush et al., 2019). In neural crest-

742

derived sensory ganglia, Isl1 is expressed in all neurons and is necessary for nociceptor

743

lineage-specific gene expression, for repressing earlier-acting neurogenic transcription factors

744

- including direct repression of Neurod4, which is ampullary organ-specific in the paddlefish

745

lateral line (Modrell et al., 2017a) - and for repressing lineage-inappropriate genes (Sun et al.,

746

2008; Dykes et al., 2011). In the pancreas, Isl1 is a direct transcriptional repressor of Mafa

747

(Du et al., 2009), which we identified here as ampullary organ-restricted (see previous

748

section). We hypothesize that, in electroreceptive species, Isl1 may promote a hair cell fate

749

within neuromasts at least in part by repressing an electroreceptor fate, including by

750

repressing Neurod4 and Mafa.

751

Rorb, encoding RAR-related orphan nuclear receptor beta (RORβ), is expressed by

752

supporting cells in adult and embryonic neuromasts in zebrafish (Dufourcq et al., 2006;

753

Bertrand et al., 2007). Retinoic acid is a confirmed inhibitory ligand for RORβ (Stehlin-Gaon

754

et al., 2003). The reciprocal expression of Rorb in neuromasts and Rorc in ampullary organs

755

(see previous section) suggests that these ligand-dependent transcription factors play specific

756

roles in the development of mechanosensory versus electrosensory organs.

757

In contrast to Hmx2, Isl1 and Rorb, mechanosensory lateral line-restricted Foxg1 and

758

Sox8 are not expressed in the developing lateral line system of zebrafish or Xenopus (e.g.,

759

Dirksen and Jamrich, 1995; Papalopulu and Kintner, 1996; Toresson et al., 1998; Eagleson

760

and Dempewolf, 2002; Duggan et al., 2008; Zhao et al., 2009; O'Donnell et al., 2006; Martik

761

et al., 2019). As zebrafish and Xenopus only have a mechanosensory lateral line system

762

(Baker et al., 2013; Baker, 2019), this suggests Foxg1 and Sox8 may play specific roles in the

763

developing mechanosensory lateral line system of electroreceptive bony vertebrates, rather

764

than in lateral line primordium or neuromast development per se. (In cartilaginous fishes, i.e.,

765

sharks, Sox8 is expressed in ampullary organs as well as neuromasts; this study and (Freitas

766

et al., 2006)

767

In paddlefish and sterlet, Foxg1 was expressed in the central zones of lateral line

768

sensory ridges where neuromasts form, though excluded from the central domains of

769

neuromasts where hair cells differentiate. In the mouse olfactory epithelium, Foxg1 maintains

777

783

784

785

786

787

788

789

line system in either Xenopus or zebrafish (O'Donnell et al., 2006; Martik et al., 2019). Given

790

the 'master regulator' role of Sox8 in otic placode development (Buzzi et al., 2022), it is

791

possible that Sox8 lies upstream of Foxg1 in lateral line primordium development specifically

792

in electroreceptive vertebrates (although it may play a separate, later role in ampullary organ

793

development in cartilaginous fishes).

Conclusion

796

The data presented here, taken together with our previous results in paddlefish (Modrell et al.,

797

2011b; Modrell et al., 2011a; Modrell et al., 2017b; Modrell et al., 2017a), show that most

798

transcription factor genes expressed in developing lateral line organs in chondrostean ray-

799

finned fishes, including many that are required for hair cell development, are expressed in both

800

ampullary organs and neuromasts. This supports the hypothesis that the molecular

801

mechanisms underlying electrosensory and mechanosensory lateral line organ development

802

are highly conserved, and that electroreceptors likely evolved as transcriptionally related sister

803

cell types to lateral line hair cells (Modrell et al., 2017a; Baker and Modrell, 2018). Moreover,

804

in addition to ampullary organ-restricted Neurod4 (Modrell et al., 2017a), we have identified a

805

further eleven transcription factors (six that are electrosensory-restricted; five that are

806

mechanosensory-restricted) that could be involved in the formation of electrosensory versus
25

(which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. It is
made available under a CC-BY 4.0 International license.

807

mechanosensory organs. These are good candidates for functional experiments using

808

CRISPR/Cas9-mediated mutagenesis in sterlet (e.g., Chen et al., 2018; Baloch et al., 2019;

809

Stundl et al., 2022), the next step to further our understanding of the development of these

810

sensory (sister) cell types.
