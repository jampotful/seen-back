Tuning Ferroelectricity in PVDF-TrFE through Thermal Treatment-Dependent Crystal Orientation.
Li L, Lin F, Meng X, Qi Z, Xiao H, Liu J, Liu Q. (2026). ACS omega. . doi:10.1021/acsomega.5c10199. Licence: cc-by-nc-nd. Text from europepmc-xml.

# Tuning Ferroelectricity in PVDF-TrFE through Thermal Treatment-Dependent Crystal Orientation

## Abstract
The ferroelectric properties of poly­(vinylidene fluoride-co-trifluoroethylene) (PVDF-TrFE) films are critically dependent on its molecular orientation, due to the dipole-electric field match problem. This study systematically investigates the impact of the recrystallization temperature and cooling rate on crystal orientation and resultant ferroelectric performance during melt recrystallization of PVDF-TrFE thin films on mica substrates. The collective analysis of two-dimensional (2D) grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray diffraction (2D-GIWAXD), atomic force microscope (AFM), Fourier transform infrared-grazing incidence reflection absorption spectroscopy (FTIR-GIRAS), and polarization-electric field (P-E) hysteresis measurement data reveals that recrystallization at lower temperatures (25 °C) with a faster cooling rate (100 °C/min) facilitates the formation of edge-on lamellar structures and enhanced ferroelectric performance. During the melt recrystallization of PVDF-TrFE films, mica substrates can promote the formation of more edge-on lamellae compared to glass or silicon substrates. These findings provide an effective approach to modulate the ferroelectric properties of PVDF-TrFE thin films from melt on mica substrates.

## Introduction
Ferroelectric polymers, particularly poly­(vinylidene fluoride-co-trifluoroethylene) (PVDF-TrFE), hold immense promise for flexible electronics, sensors, actuators, and nonvolatile memories due to their robust ferroelectricity, solution processability, and mechanical flexibility. − Their ferroelectricity originates primarily from the dipole moments which is perpendicular to the chain axis. , This dipole moment pointing from fluorine to hydrogen can result in a macroscopic polarization with their switching accomplished by rotating the polymer backbone around the chain axis upon the vertical electric field. − So the alignment of dipoles perpendicular to the substrate, matching the direction of the applied electric field, can result in optimal ferroelectric performance. Because the dipole moment of PVDF-TrFE is perpendicular to the molecular chain, manipulating the molecular chain oriented parallel to the substrate, forming an edge-on structure, is of great importance for the ferroelectric performance of PVDF-TrFE.
The ferroelectric PVDF-TrFE films are traditionally prepared via solution spin coating on glass or silicon substrate and then annealed below the melting temperature. , Such a preparation method generally induces edge-on lamellae in which molecular chains are parallel to the substrate and dipoles are perpendicular to the molecular chains. However, when the processing temperature exceeded 150 °C, interface interaction resulted in the formation of crystals with a flat-on structure (molecular chains perpendicular to substrate), which leads to a mismatch of applied electric field and dipoles. Extensive research has been dedicated to achieving the edge-on structure in PVDF-TrFE crystals directly from the molten state. In recent work, the edge-on orientation of PVDF-TrFE has been regulated in ultrathin films by vacuum-evaporating a carbon layer on the surface of its solution casting films to fix molecular chains parallel to arbitrary substrate in melt. Epitaxial crystallization has also been utilized to achieve an edge-on structure in PVDF-TrFE from melt. − The ordered epitaxial substrate could be friction-induced highly ordered poly­(tetrafluoroethylene) (PTFE) films, chitin films, and chemical vapor deposited graphene. Besides, mechanical shearing of the film under appropriate thermal conditions has also been employed to align the molecular chains of PVDF-TrFE to generate edge-on lamellae from melt. − However, the carbon-coating method, which involves depositing a carbon layer onto the PVDF-TrFE surface via vacuum evaporation, is a relatively complex process. Epitaxial approaches require lattice matching and may introduce interfacial dielectric layers like PTFE and chitin films in devices, inhibiting the polarization reversal process. Although graphene offers excellent electrode compatibility, its high production cost hinders its practical application. Furthermore, mechanical shearing easily introduces frictional damage into the film during processing. Thus, exploring a feasible method capable of fixing molecular chains parallel to the substrate from the melt is challenging. In this article, we discovered that on a mica substrate, the edge-on orientation of PVDF-TrFE molecular chains can be controlled from the melt state by tailoring the heat treatment conditions, thereby influencing the performance of its ferroelectric devices. This may expand the thermal processing range of PVDF-TrFE and enhance its potential applications in electronic devices.

## Experimental Section

## Materials and Film Preparation
PVDF-TrFE (70/30 mol %) was purchased from Piezotech Akema, and mica was obtained from Zhongjing Technology Instrument Co., Ltd. The schematic diagram of the film preparation method is shown in Figure . PVDF-TrFE was dissolved in methyl ethyl ketone (MEK) solvent and stirred for 24 h to prepare a 20 mg/mL solution. The 200 μL solution was spin-coated onto a freshly cleaved mica sheet (2 cm × 2 cm) at 1500 rpm for 60 s. The prepared samples were placed in a vacuum oven at 50 °C for 4 h to remove the solvent. The thickness of the prepared films, measured with a Dektak XT Profilometer, was approximately 180 nm. To investigate the effect of crystallization temperature, the prepared samples were heated to 200 °C and held for 10 min using a Linkam hot stage (THMS600/CI94) equipped with a liquid nitrogen cooling system, then cooled at a rate of 100 °C/min to 140 °C, 130 °C, 70 °C, and 25 °C, respectively, and then held at each temperature for 2 h. To examine the effect of cooling rate, the prepared samples were heated to 200 °C for 10 min, then cooled to 25 °C at rates of 5 °C/min, 20 °C/min, and 100 °C/min, respectively, and held at 25 °C for 2 h.
Schematic diagram of the PVDF-TrFE film preparation process using the spin coating method.

## Structure Characterization
The two-dimensional (2D) grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray diffraction (2D-GIWAXD) measurement was performed at beamline 1W1A of the Beijing Synchrotron Radiation Facility using an X-ray with a wavelength of 0.154 nm. The 2D-GIWAXD patterns were recorded with a CCD camera (MAR345). The surface morphologies of the PVDF-TrFE samples crystallized at different thermal treatment processes were monitored using an Agilent Technologies 5500 atomic force microscope in tapping mode. The silicon tips with a resonating frequency of 320–350 kHz and a spring constant of 20–30 N/m were used. Resolutions of 512 × 512 data points per image were used to collect the images. The field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was performed by using a JEOL JSM-6700F instrument. Fourier transform infrared-grazing incidence reflection absorption spectroscopy (FTIR-GIRAS) data were collected using a PerkinElmer Spectrum 100 spectrometer at an incidence angle of ca. 80° from the normal to the surface at a resolution of 4 cm–1. For FTIR-GIRAS measurement, the heat-treated PVDF-TrFE film was detached from the mica substrate with the help of hydrofluoric acid and mounted on a Au substrate.

## Capacitor Fabrication and Characterization
In order to measure the ferroelectric properties of the PVDF-TrFE films, a metal/ferroelectric polymer/metal (MFM) capacitor was fabricated, as shown in Figure . A strip-shaped aluminum (Al) bottom electrode approximately 80 nm thick was deposited by vacuum evaporation onto a freshly cleaved mica substrate using a strip-patterned mask. Subsequently, a PVDF-TrFE solution was spin-coated onto this bottom electrode and subjected to thermal treatment. Finally, a second set of strip-shaped Al electrodes, oriented perpendicular to the bottom electrodes, was deposited via evaporation onto the surface of the PVDF-TrFE sample as the top electrodes. Polarization hysteresis loops for the PVDF-TrFE films were measured using the multiferroic tester (Radiant Technologies Inc.) on unpoled films. The testing frequency was 10 Hz, and a Double Bipolar Sine waveform was applied.

## Results and Discussion

## Effect of Crystallization Temperature on the Edge-On Orientation
To investigate the influence of the crystallization temperature on the molecular chain orientation of the PVDF-TrFE samples on mica substrates, films with a thickness of approximately 180 nm (measured in Figure S1) were prepared via spin coating. This selected film thickness effectively balances the substrate influence and the subsequent electrical performance. In Figure , 2D-GIWAXD patterns provide direct visualization of the crystalline orientation within the PVDF-TrFE films as a function of crystallization temperature. As the PVDF-TrFE film is heat-treated first at 200 °C for 10 min and then quenched to 140 °C at a rate of 100 °C/min, the sample displays intensified equatorial reflections in the diffraction pattern (Figure a). This intensified equatorial reflection can be indexed as (110) or (200), because the β-ferroeletric phase of PVDF-TrFE crystal has a pseudohexagonal lattice with a = 9.12 Å, b = 5.25 Å, c = 2.55 Å (β = 93°) and in turn results in nearly equal (110) and (200) interplanar spacing. This pattern suggests the c-axis of PVDF-TrFE crystals is preferentially aligned perpendicular to the mica substrate surface with a- and b-axis parallel to the substrate after melt recrystallization, leading to formation of flat-on lamellae. When the PVDF-TrFE was melt-quenched to 130 °C (Figure b), the sample exhibits strong (110)/(200) diffraction signals along the equatorial direction. Concurrently, relatively weak diffraction signals emerge along the meridional direction. Analysis confirms that these meridional signals correspond to the (110)/(200) reflections. Their appearance indicates the formation of edge-on lamellae with c-axis aligned parallel to the substrate, while the a- and b-axes rotate freely around the c-axes. However, the significantly lower intensity of the meridional signals compared to the equatorial signals reveals that both flat-on- and edge-on oriented crystals coexist, with flat-on crystals dominating the structure. As the sample was quenched to 70 °C (Figure c), strong diffraction signals are present along both equatorial and meridional directions, with comparable intensities. This signifies that the sample contains approximately equal proportions of flat-on and edge-on oriented crystals. Quenching to 25 °C (Figure d) resulted in significantly stronger diffraction signals along the meridional direction compared with the equatorial direction. These features confirm the predominance of edge-on oriented crystals within the sample. At 25 °C, the majority of polymer chain axes (c-axis) lie parallel to the substrate plane. Consequently, the ferroelectric dipole moment of this film can freely rotate around the c-axis under an applied electric field. This specific arrangement is crucial for achieving strong out-of-plane ferroelectric polarization in thin films. From the 2D-GIWAXD results, it can be inferred that the melt recrystallization temperature is a key factor in controlling the crystal orientation of PVDF-TrFE films. Higher recrystallization temperatures favor the formation of flat-on crystals, while lower temperatures promote the development of edge-on crystals.
2D-GIWAXD patterns of PVDF-TrFE films heat-treated first at 200 °C for 10 min and then quenched to (a) 140 °C, (b) 130 °C, (c) 70 °C, and (d) 25 °C for 2 h at a rate of 100 °C/min.
The surface morphology of PVDF-TrFE films crystallized at different temperatures was characterized by AFM. As shown in Figure a,b, PVDF-TrFE films recrystallized at 140 and 130 °C have a similar planar and featureless surface. This planar morphology is consistent with the predominantly flat-on structure inferred from the 2D-GIWAXD data. At 140 and 130 °C, the flat-on crystals occupy almost the full images. With a decrease in the crystallization temperature to 70 °C, fibrous-like structures appear on the PVDF-TrFE surface in Figure c. The thickness of those fibrous-like structures is about 35 nm. When the crystallization temperature decreased to 25 °C, those fibrous-like structures became more numerous and finer. The average thickness of those fibers is approximately 25 nm. This confirms an increase in the content of edge-on oriented crystals as the crystallization temperature decreases. The SEM results shown in Figure S2 reveal the same trend in morphological evolution. Those morphological evolutions observed by AFM and SEM correlate well with the 2D-GIWAXD results, collectively indicating that edge-on lamellae are likely to form at lower crystallization temperatures in PVDF-TrFE films. Besides, Figure S3 displays that the PVDF-TrFE films consistently formed fibrous edge-on crystals after melting at 220, 240, 260, and 300 °C, respectively. This indicates that the melting temperature has minimal influence on the crystallization morphology of PVDF-TrFE film.
AFM phase images of PVDF-TrFE films heat-treated first at 200 °C for 10 min and then quenched to (a) 140 °C, (b) 130 °C, (c) 70 °C, and (d) 25 °C for 2 h at a rate of 100 °C/min.
FTIR-GIRAS is a highly sensitive technique for studying the molecular alignment and dipole orientation of PVDF-TrFE. In the GIRAS mode, the p-polarized component (E P) of the vibrational mode is enhanced, whose electric vector is perpendicular to the substrate surface. Consequently, GIRAS primarily detects vibrational modes whose transition dipole moments are oriented normal to the substrate. The relationship between the transition dipole moment (μ) and the unit cell axis of the β PVDF-TrFE crystal and vibrational symmetry species (A 1, B 1, and B 2) are as follows: μ//b at A 1, μ//c at B 1, and μ//a at B 2. The FTIR-GIRAS spectra of PVDF-TrFE samples recrystallized at different temperatures show several absorbance bands in Figure , which are sensitive toward changes in polymer chain orientation. With decreasing crystallization temperature, the significant decrease in the absorbance band B 1 at 1404 cm–1 for the PVDF-TrFE samples indicates the preferential orientation of the polymer chains parallel to the substrate surface at low crystallization temperature. To quantitatively compare the orientation of polymer chains and dipoles in PVDF-TrFE recrystallized at different temperatures, the ratios of absorption peak intensity observed at 1404 cm–1 (B 1) and 1198 cm–1 (B 2) were calculated. The I B1/I B2 ratios are 1.17, 1.01, 0.77, and 0.55 in PVDF-TrFE samples recrystallized at 140, 130, 70, and 25 °C, respectively. The gradually decreasing I B1/I B2 ratio further demonstrates that during isothermal recrystallization from the melt, crystallization at lower temperatures facilitates the parallel alignment of PVDF-TrFE molecular chains with respect to the mica substrate.
FTIR-GIRAS spectra of PVDF-TrFE films heat-treated first at 200 °C for 10 min and then quenched to different crystallization temperatures for 2 h at a rate of 100 °C/min.
It should be pointed out that the PVDF-TrFE films spun on glass and silicon substrates obviously exhibit a planar structure even after melt crystallization at 25 °C, as shown in Figure S4. Substrate is a major factor for PVDF-TrFE films to form edge-on oriented lamellae when the heat treatment is above the melting temperature. Previous studies have demonstrated that van der Waals epitaxy has been applied to the epitaxial growth of various materials on mica substrate, such as MoS2, VO2, ZnO, GaN, and so on. − We speculate that temperature-dependent van der Waals epitaxy exists between mica and PVDF-TrFE, which may promote the formation of edge-on lamellae in PVDF-TrFE. At higher crystallization temperature, the van der Waals force between mica and PVDF-TrFE is weaker, resulting in less epitaxial influence. While at lower crystallization temperatures, the van der Waals force is stronger, enhancing the epitaxial effect and thereby promoting the formation of more edge-on lamellae in the PVDF-TrFE film. However, the mechanism of mica substrates in promoting edge-on lamellae growth in PVDF-TrFE films remains under further investigation.

## Effect of Cooling Rate on the Edge-on Orientation
In order to investigate the effect of different cooling processes on the chain orientation of PVDF-TrFE films, 2D-GIWAXD patterns of PVDF-TrFE films cooled from the melt state to 25 °C at different cooling rates are obtained and shown in Figure . For films cooled at 5 °C/min (Figure a), a strong (110)/(200) diffraction signal is observed along the equatorial direction, while the meridional signal is very weak. This confirms the dominant formation of flat-on oriented crystals. As the cooling rate increased to 20 °C/min (Figure b), the diffraction strength in the meridional direction increased compared to the samples cooled at 5 °C/min, indicating a higher content of edge-on structure crystals. Nevertheless, the diffraction intensity along the equatorial direction remains significantly higher than that along the meridional direction, indicating that the flat-on structure still predominates in the sample. When the PVDF-TrFE melt is directly quenched to 25 °C at 100 °C/min, the meridional diffraction intensity is significantly higher than the equatorial intensity (Figure d), proving the primary formation of edge-on oriented lamellae. These results demonstrate that melt-quenched and melt-slowly cooled PVDF-TrFE films exhibit drastically different chain orientation. Rapid cooling favors the formation of edge-on oriented crystals in the PVDF-TrFE melt. A rapid cooling rate implies a shorter residence time in the higher temperature regime, which is consistent with the finding that the edge-on structure is promoted at lower melt-recrystallization temperatures.
2D-GIWAXD patterns of PVDF-TrFE films heat-treated first at 200 °C for 10 min and then cooled to 25 °C at rates of (a) 5 and (b) 20 °C/min.
The 2D-GIWAXD results are further correlated with surface morphology characterized by AFM. As shown in the phase images (Figure a,b), samples cooled at 5 and 20 °C/min exhibit a planar microstructure. In contrast, the sample quenched at 100 °C/min develops distinct strip-like features in Figure d, indicating that the rapid cooling promotes an edge-on orientation of the crystalline lamellae along the substrate. This interpretation is consistent with the FTIR-GIRAS findings in Figure c, which show a decrease in the B 1 band intensity with an increasing cooling rate. Together, these data confirm that higher cooling rates preferentially align the polymer chains parallel to the substrate surface.

## The Ferroelectric Properties Characteristics
The ferroelectric properties of PVDF-TrFE thin films were systematically investigated by preparing MFM capacitors under distinct thermal conditions. Figure a presents the polarization-electric field (P-E) hysteresis loops for PVDF-TrFE films recrystallized at distinct temperatures (140 °C, 130 °C, 70 °C, and 25 °C) under the same cooling rate (100 °C/min). The sample recrystallized at 140 and 130 °C displays low remnant polarization (P r) values and poorly saturated hysteresis loops, due to the flat-on crystals dominating the structure. In the flat-on lamellae, the polymer chain axis (c-axis) is oriented predominantly perpendicular to the substrate surface, while the polar b-axis lies within the plane of the substrate. Reorienting these in-plane dipoles along the out-of-plane electric field direction requires rotation of the entire chain backbone (c-axis). Such a process demands extremely high energy barriers, which are rarely overcome in practical fields. Thus, high-temperature recrystallization favors thermodynamically stable flat-on crystals but results in a severely limited dipole mobility and low P r. The sample recrystallized at 70 °C shows an intermediate Pr value and an improved loop saturation. This enhancement in ferroelectric performance is attributed to the higher prevalence of edge-on lamellae in samples crystallized at 70 °C compared to those crystallized at 130 and 140 °C, as demonstrated by previous XRD, AFM, and FTIR-GIRAS analyses. In the edge-on configuration, the c-axis lies parallel to the substrate surface, which allows the CF2 dipoles to rotate around the c-axis with significantly lower energy requirements compared to the flat-on lamellae. The most pronounced enhancement in ferroelectric performance was observed in the sample quenched directly to 25 °C after melting. This film exhibits the highest remnant polarization (P r = 5.8 μC/cm2) at an applied voltage of 19 V, corresponding to a coercive field (E c) of approximately 90 MV/m, and displays the most saturated hysteresis loop. The markedly enhanced P r arises from the optimal alignment and switch ability of the CF2 dipoles. Low-temperature crystallization promotes the formation of edge-on crystallites where the c-axis lies parallel to the substrate, facilitating efficient dipole rotation under the electric field. Furthermore, our research demonstrates that the cooling rate equally exerts a significant influence on the ferroelectric performance of the samples. Figure b provides a direct comparison of the ferroelectric performance for PVDF-TrFE films reaching 25 °C via different cooling pathways: 5 °C/min, 20 °C/min, and 100 °C/min. The sample cooled at 5 and 20 °C/min exhibits poorer ferroelectric performance, characterized by substantially reduced P r and less saturated hysteresis loop. The slow cooling rate favors the growth of flat-on lamellae, where the c-axis aligns perpendicular to the substrate. This severely impeded dipole switching under the vertical electric field applied in the capacitor, resulting in the observed ferroelectric degradation. The results shown in Figure obviously revealed that ferroelectric properties are strongly dependent on thermal treatment condition. Besides, the Pr values of PVDF-TrFE films reported in previous studies are summarized in Table . Compared with prior experimental results, the Pr value achieved in this work demonstrates competitive advantages. More importantly, our high-Pr PVDF-TrFE ferroelectric devices were fabricated via melt processing, which significantly broadens the thermal processing window for PVDF-TrFE-based devices.

## Conclusion
In summary, the thermal treatment conditions significantly influence the ferroelectric performance of PVDF-TrFE thin films on a mica substrate. Quenching to 25 °C at 100 °C/min yields the highest Pr and most saturated hysteresis loop, making it the optimal condition for MFM capacitor applications. The results underscore the importance of controlling crystallization kinetics to achieve desirable ferroelectric properties for PVDF-TrFE thin films in the mica substrate. Rapid quenching to low crystallization temperature is the most effective method for enhancing the polarization performance of PVDF-TrFE films. These findings provide valuable insights for the design of high-performance ferroelectric polymer memories and other flexible electronic devices from the melt.
