A vision attention driven Language framework for medical report generation.

# A vision attention driven Language framework for medical report generation

## Abstract
This study introduces the Medical Vision Attention Generation (MedVAG) model, a novel framework designed to facilitate the automated generation of medical reports. MedVAG integrates Vision Transformer (ViT)-based visual feature extraction and GPT-2 language modeling, enhanced by graph-based feature fusion and multiple attention mechanisms (co-attention, cross-attention, memory-guided attention), to ensure semantic coherence and diagnostic accuracy. Evaluated on IU X-Ray and COV-CTR datasets, the model achieved state-of-the-art performance across natural language generation metrics (BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE, CIDEr) and clinical effectiveness measures. Ablation studies highlighted the critical role of attention mechanisms and feature fusion in aligning visual and textual features. MedVAG demonstrates strong potential as an assistive technology, aiming to support radiologists by reducing workload and enhancing diagnostic accuracy.

## Introduction
Rapid advances in medical imaging technologies have led to an unprecedented increase in radiology data, creating both opportunities and challenges for modern healthcare systems. Radiologists play a crucial role in the accurate interpretation of medical images; however, increasing imaging demands, combined with a shortage of trained specialists, have significantly increased the workload of radiologists. This trend can lead to increased error rates, longer reporting times, and delays in clinical decision-making, which can adversely affect patient outcomes1.
To overcome these challenges, automated medical report generation systems have emerged as a promising solution. These systems aim to bridge the gap between large medical image datasets and the need for timely and accurate reporting by automatically generating descriptive and diagnostic text from medical images. Such systems can significantly reduce the workload of radiologists, increase efficiency and minimise human error by streamlining the radiology reporting process1,2.
Advances in deep learning, particularly in encoder-decoder architectures, have facilitated the development of automated reporting systems. These models have demonstrated the ability to extract visual features from medical images and transform them into meaningful linguistic descriptions3,4. However, traditional approaches often fail to fully capture the complex relationships between images and clinical text, limiting the accuracy and clinical utility of the generated reports. This challenge has resulted in the adoption of transformer-based models, such as the Vision Transformer (ViT), which have been shown to excel at learning both local and global relationships in medical images through self-attention mechanisms5,6. ViT models process images by dividing them into patches, thus enabling the model to discern intricate visual patterns that may not be recognised by conventional convolutional neural networks (CNNs)6.
A significant challenge in the realm of automated medical report generation pertains to the integration of multi-view and multi-modal data. Medical imaging frequently encompasses multiple perspectives, including front and side chest X-rays, and combines several modalities, such as CT scans, MRIs, and radiology reports. Effective integration of this information is essential to create comprehensive and accurate diagnostic narratives7,8. To address this, recent studies have utilised cross-view attention mechanisms and multimodal memory networks that allow visual and textual features to be combined while maintaining contextual coherence9,10. These approaches show promise in capturing nuanced relationships between modalities and improving the completeness and reliability of generated reports.
Incorporating memory mechanisms into transformer architectures further improves the performance of automated reporting systems by enabling long-term contextual information retention. Memory-driven transformers have been shown to produce more consistent and diagnostically accurate reports by aligning visual features with clinical text11,12. Similarly, graph-based feature fusion techniques facilitate the conversion of visual information into structured graphical representations, which are then integrated with textual data to enhance the clinical relevance of the generated reports7.
In the context of automated medical report generation, it is imperative to prioritise the principles of clarity, accuracy, conciseness, completeness, consistency, and coherence to ensure the integrity and reliability of the generated reports. Although there has been considerable success in the generation of comprehensive reports by existing approaches, concerns remain regarding the coherence, consistency and conciseness of these reports. The challenges associated with the generation of medical reports are multifaceted. Firstly, in contrast to natural images, medical images exhibit high structural similarity, with only subtle differences present in regions where pathological anomalies are present. Consequently, it is imperative for the model to capture these fine-grained visual features to prevent redundant or overly generic report generation. This necessitates a robust encoder architecture capable of effectively distinguishing these subtle variations. Secondly, the model must be capable of accurately identifying multiple coexisting abnormalities within a medical image and understanding the relationships between them. Consequently, it is imperative to capture the spatial dependencies and pixel-level interactions inherent within medical images to facilitate the generation of precise reports. Conventional image captioning is a relatively straightforward process; by contrast, medical report generation is a critical task with direct implications for human health. Accordingly, it is imperative to adopt an approach characterised by meticulous precision, necessitating rigorous evaluation based on multiple objective criteria to ensure reliability and clinical applicability. The utilisation of both natural language generation (NLG) and clinical effectiveness (CE) measures is paramount for the assurance of the reliability of automated reporting systems, as rigorous evaluation is essential for the validation of such systems. Standard metrics such as BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR and CIDEr assess the overlap between the generated reports and the underlying real text, while clinical metrics such as precision, recall and F1 score assess the robustness and relevance of the generated content3,13. However, recent studies have emphasised the need to incorporate semantic similarity metrics and retrieval-assisted generation (RAG) approaches to better capture the clinical accuracy and contextual fit of generated reports3,14.
This study proposes the implementation of a Medical Vision Attention Generation (MedVAG) model, a system designed to systematically address challenges identified in the field through the integration of multiple advanced components. The primary objective of this integration is to enhance the accuracy, coherence and clinical relevance of automatically generated radiology reports. The proposed model has been subjected to a comprehensive evaluation using both Natural Language Generation (NLG) metrics and Clinical Efficacy (CE) metrics. This evaluation has demonstrated the superiority of the proposed model in comparison to existing state-of-the-art methodologies. The proposed approach aims to enhance the efficiency of clinical workflows and alleviate the workload of radiologists by ensuring contextual consistency, clinical accuracy, and diagnostic relevance in the generated radiology reports. The primary contributions of our research are as follows:

## Related works
The integration of image captioning in the generation of radiology reports signifies a substantial advancement in the domain of medical imaging and documentation. This process entails the automated generation of textual descriptions from radiological images, with the potential to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of radiology reporting. Recent studies have demonstrated the efficacy of various artificial intelligence (AI) and natural language processing (NLP) techniques in automating this task, thereby reducing the workload of radiologists and enhancing patient outcomes. One of the principal benefits of leveraging artificial intelligence (AI) in the context of radiology report generation lies in its capacity to automate administrative tasks, including report writing and data entry. This automation enables radiologists to allocate more time to critical analysis and patient care, as opposed to being encumbered by documentation obligations. For instance, Temperley emphasises that tools such as ChatGPT can significantly reduce the burden of documentation, enabling radiologists to focus on their core responsibilities15. In a similar vein, Chen et al. expound on the strides made in automated radiology report generation models, underscoring a discernible trend towards automation in this domain13.
Furthermore, the utilisation of natural language processing (NLP) techniques for the extraction of clinical findings from unstructured radiology reports has been identified as a scalable solution for the labelling of image data for machine learning algorithms. As highlighted by Lau et al., the majority of radiology reports are composed of unstructured free text. The utilisation of natural language processing for clinical finding extraction has been demonstrated to be a successful method of overcoming barriers associated with data labelling16. This capability is of pivotal significance for the training of machine learning models, which require labelled datasets in order to facilitate effective learning and performance. As evidenced by the development of sophisticated models, such as the Cross-modal Memory Networks proposed by Chen et al., there exists considerable potential for the application of deep learning techniques to generate coherent and clinically relevant captions from radiological images13. These models leverage the relationship between visual data and textual descriptions, allowing for improved feature extraction and more accurate report generation. Jaiswal further supports this notion by providing a detailed account of how dynamic convolution and attention mechanisms can enhance the consistency and relevance of generated captions, thereby emulating the expertise of radiologists12. In addition to enhancing the generation of reports, artificial intelligence (AI) has the capacity to streamline the identification and quantification of biomarkers in radiological images. Palm et al. explore the potential of AI-supported algorithms to detect and quantify biomarkers of diseases in chest CT scans, a process that is of paramount importance in the field of preventive medicine17. This capability has been demonstrated to facilitate diagnostic procedures whilst concomitantly enhancing the quality of radiology reports by furnishing detailed insights into patient conditions.
The issue of labelling large datasets for the training of artificial intelligence models is another critical aspect of automated report generation. Wood et al. have highlighted that the labour-intensive nature of this task has presented a considerable barrier to the clinical adoption of AI technologies in the field of radiology18. However, recent advancements in natural language processing (NLP) have rendered the automation of the labelling process a viable proposition, thus facilitating the streamlined training of image analysis algorithms18. This automation is essential for developing robust AI systems capable of generating accurate and reliable radiology reports. In addition, the integration of structured reporting formats in the field of radiology is gaining traction as a means to enhance communication and clarity in reports. The Radiological Society of North America (RSNA) has recommended the utilisation of structured reports with a view to enhancing the communication of radiological examination results19. As Kuling et al. have demonstrated, this systematic approach facilitates improved comprehension amongst healthcare professionals, as well as aiding in the automated extraction of pertinent reports20.
The employment of large language models (LLMs) in the domains of report simplification and creation has demonstrated considerable potential. It has been demonstrated by studies that LLMs have the capacity to effectively generate reports which are coherent and relevant within their context21. This has the potential to enhance the readability and accessibility of radiological findings for both clinicians and patients. The present findings are in alignment with those previously reported by Alqahtani, who emphasised the potential of CNN-Transformer approaches to generate comprehensive radiology reports22. Furthermore, the employment of multimodal models which incorporate visual and textual data is proving to be an effective strategy for the enhancement of report generation. Shetty et al. propose a deep learning framework that utilizes chest X-ray images to produce diagnostic reports, highlighting the importance of integrating visual features with textual modeling23. This approach has the dual benefits of improving the accuracy of report generation and ensuring that critical visual information is adequately captured in the textual descriptions.
The ongoing research in the field of automated radiology report generation also addresses the requirement for continuous enhancement of the model’s performance. Yu et al. evaluated various state-of-the-art methods for automatic chest X-ray report generation, emphasizing the importance of using multiple metrics to assess the effectiveness of these models24. This rigorous evaluation process is crucial for ensuring that the generated reports meet the clinical standards required for effective patient care.
The generation of radiology reports through automated systems has become a pivotal research area, especially with the advent of transformer-based architectures. The objective of these systems is to alleviate the burden on radiologists by converting complex visual data from medical images into structured and clinically relevant textual reports. The advent of increasingly sophisticated medical imaging techniques has brought to the fore the deficiencies of conventional image captioning methodologies, which frequently yield unadvanced portrayals, unable to do justice to the intricate and nuanced character of radiological findings. This has resulted in the necessity for the development of increasingly advanced models that have the capacity to accurately interpret visual data and to generate reports that are relevant to the specific context.
Transformer-based architectures have emerged as a potent solution to the aforementioned challenges, offering substantial advancements in the domain of automated radiology report generation. The ability to capture long-range dependencies within data is of particular critical importance in the field of radiology, where a single image may contain multiple findings that must be articulated coherently. As demonstrated in the research conducted by25, the efficacy of conditioned transformers in managing contextual information is paramount for the generation of precise reports. Furthermore, the integration of multimodal data, encompassing the combination of visual features from medical images and textual information, has led to a notable enhancement in the quality of reports. In this context23, proposed an encoder-decoder framework that utilised chest X-ray images with a view to improving diagnostic accuracy. In this regard, Zhang et al. highlighted the merits of incorporating multiple image types for the purpose of generating superior reports26. The seminal contributions of researchers such as11] and [27 underscore the significance of nuanced approaches to translating visual input into textual output. These contributions lay the foundation for the development of more sophisticated multimodal models that address the unique demands of medical report generation.
The transformer architecture has been particularly beneficial in this context due to its ability to process sequential data and its effectiveness in capturing long-range dependencies within the input data. To provide an illustration, the research by Li et al. introduces a knowledge-driven methodology that emulates the process of report writing by radiologists. This approach utilises a transformer framework for the purposes of encoding, retrieving and paraphrasing information from medical imagery28. It has been demonstrated that this method enhances both the comprehensibility of generated reports and their alignment with clinical workflows, thus improving their practical application in authentic healthcare environments. Furthermore, the incorporation of knowledge graphs into the report generation process has been investigated as a strategy to enhance the contextual comprehension of the analyzed medical images, thereby improving the accuracy and clinical relevance of the generated reports. Zhang et al. explore the potential of knowledge-driven methodologies to enhance the generation of comprehensive reports by integrating structured medical knowledge with visual data26. This approach facilitates the generation of reports that are both accurate and relevant to the specific context, thus addressing the particular requirements of healthcare professionals. The evaluation of these automated systems is critical, and various metrics such as BLEU, METEOR, and ROUGE have been employed to assess the quality of generated reports. However, the challenge remains in ensuring that these metrics align with clinical relevance, as the ultimate goal of radiology report generation is to support clinical decision-making effectively.
Recent years have witnessed significant advancements in the field of deep learning, resulting in the emergence of highly sophisticated models that employ attention mechanisms and hierarchical generation techniques. For instance, the work by Nooralahzadeh et al. introduces a progressive transformer-based framework that compartmentalises the report generation process into manageable steps, thereby improving the overall quality and coherence of the generated text30. The present method mirrors a mounting tendency towards more methodical and systematic approaches to report generation, a development with the potential to substantially improve the interpretability and usability of the generated outputs. Furthermore, the incorporation of multimodal data—combining both visual and textual inputs—has been shown to improve the performance of radiology report generation systems. Research by Kalimuthu emphasizes the importance of integrating various data sources to enhance the robustness of the generated reports31. This multimodal approach enhances not only the data available for report generation but also facilitates a more comprehensive understanding of the clinical context surrounding the images.
The transformer architecture’s inherent flexibility enables the integration of diverse techniques, including attention mechanisms. These further enhance the model’s capacity to concentrate on the most pertinent sections of the input data. As demonstrated in the study by Liu et al., the utilisation of a BERT-based deep learning approach for the extraction of pertinent evidence from radiology reports can facilitate the development of a computer-aided diagnosis framework32. This emphasises the capacity of transformer models to function not solely as a means of generating reports, but also as a tool to facilitate clinical decision-making by offering pertinent insights derived from the data. In addition to enhancing the precision and pertinence of generated reports, transformer-based models have the capacity to augment the efficacy of the report generation process. The work by Nagendran et al. underscores the importance of deep learning algorithms in radiology, noting that these systems can significantly streamline workflows by automating routine tasks33. This is especially advantageous in high-volume settings, where the demand for timely and accurate reporting is paramount. Moreover, the implementation of deep learning methodologies within the domain of radiology report generation is accompanied by a series of substantial challenges. The requirement for extensive annotated datasets to train these models presents a considerable challenge to their widespread adoption. Nevertheless, as demonstrated by Zhang, recent advancements in weakly supervised learning approaches show promise in overcoming this limitation by enabling models to learn from less structured data34. This has the potential to broaden the scope of transformer-based systems in a variety of clinical settings.
The role of explainability in automated radiology report generation is another critical aspect that has attracted considerable attention. Beddiar et al. highlight the necessity for models to provide interpretable outputs, ensuring that healthcare professionals can trust and understand the generated reports35. This is of particular significance within the medical domain, wherein decisions founded upon automated outputs may bear substantial ramifications for patient care. The field of radiology report generation is rapidly evolving, driven by advancements in transformer-based architectures and deep learning techniques. The integration of multimodal data, knowledge graphs, and attention mechanisms has led to the development of more sophisticated models capable of producing clinically relevant and coherent reports. As the research progresses, the emphasis is expected to transition toward the enhancement of explainability and usability of these systems. This will ensure that healthcare professionals receive effective support in their decision-making processes.
In conclusion, the integration of image captioning in radiology report generation is a transformative development that leverages AI and NLP technologies to improve the efficiency, accuracy and clarity of radiological documentation. As the field continues to evolve, ongoing research and advancements in deep learning models will play an important role in shaping the future of radiology reporting, ultimately leading to improved patient outcomes and streamlined workflows for radiologists. In addition, transformer-based architectures are revolutionising radiology report generation by providing powerful tools to automate the conversion of complex visual data into structured text reports. Advances in the integration of multimodal data, attention mechanisms and learning techniques are improving the accuracy, relevance and efficiency of these systems. As research continues to progress, it is crucial to address challenges related to data requirements and evaluation metrics if the potential of these transformative technologies in clinical practice is to be realised.

## Methodology
The present work describes the development of a framework for generating text from images, which is based on the utilisation of tags from medical reports. The general framework of the study is shown in Fig. 1. The framework consists of two separate modules and the part of these modules that undertakes the task of medical report generation has an encoder-decoder architecture in terms of its general structure. The other part of the framework, Tag Guided GradCam Heatmap Extraction module, is used to generate gradcam heatmap based on patient tags. The encoder stage of the model responsible for generating medical reports aims to extract high-level image features. To achieve this objective, a pre-trained ViT (Vision Transformer Model) is utilised. The decoder stage includes a pre-trained GPT2 model to be used for report generation. The alteration to the model’s architectural design was implemented within the encoder-decoder structures. Accordingly, after two images (frontal and lateral) of each patient are passed through ViT, these image features are combined and given to the graph-base feature fusion structure and a graph-based feature fusion is provided. In this way, it is aimed to learn the relationships and context information between the features of both images. Then, the inputs given on the transformer layers of the GPT-2 based language model are processed to return the hidden states. This process was applied for both ground truth reports and reports retrieved with RAG (Retrieval-augmented Generation) in the datasets used. Here, in fact, just like feature extraction on images with ViT, feature extraction was performed on words with the same justification. In this way, instead of directly giving the input words to the report generation model, their hidden states extracted with GPT2 are given.
As a next step, the CoAttention mechanism is integrated into the model to capture the dependencies between the latent states of the input texts and the latent state vectors of the texts retrieved by RAG. As stated in the ablation studies given in the following sections, it has been determined that the inclusion of only the CoAttention mechanism is not sufficient to reach a superior level on the basis of the metrics used, and the need to learn image features simultaneously with text features has emerged. For this reason, text features from CoAttention, image features from graph fusion were subjected to Cross Attention in order to process image, and text features together. In the text generation task, a memory representation is required in order to more effectively model long-term dependencies in the context. The Memory Guided Attention mechanism was also included in the system, considering that this structure can contribute to more consistent, meaningful and comprehensive report generation by combining the information from the current input with the information in the memory. The following sub-headings provide information about the details of the model.

## Tag determination
COVID-19 CT Reports (COV-CTR) and IU-XRay, which are among the datasets used as benchmark datasets for medical report generation task in the literature, were employed. The COV-CTR dataset originally consists of Chinese reports and XRay images of these reports. An English translation of the dataset has been made publicly available by reference in36. In this study, the English reports of the dataset provided by reference36 were used for COV-CTR. In order to extract tags for each patient’s report in the COV-CTR dataset, the TF-IDF Vectorizer method was used to identify the 50 most frequently occurring unique words in all reports and check whether these words were present for each report. In the event of the 50 most frequently used words (tag) being included in the report in the relevant row, the tag was coded 1. Conversely, if the words were not included, the tag was coded 0. The tag acquisition for the IU-XRay dataset was provided by the ‘Manual Tags’ column provided by the reference in25 for this dataset. Among all the tags in this column, only the unique ones were taken and a total of 105 tags were obtained for the IU-XRay data. For the reports in each row of this dataset, the presence or absence of tags in that row was checked. The one-hot encoding method is employed through the allocation of a value of 1 to indicate the presence of a tag, and 0 to denote its absence. For both datasets used in the study, any report text can contain multiple tags.
Following the acquisition of the encoded tag values of the datasets utilised, the GradCAM heatmap extraction process, which is oriented according to these tags, was initiated. Each patient in the datasets is represented by a pair of X-ray images. Gradcam extraction was applied to each of these images. Consequently, a gradcam heatmap image was generated for each of the tags with an encoding value of 1. These images were derived from the patient’s existing tag information. During this process, the pretrained ChexNet (densenet121)37 model trained on ImageNet38 was used. Tag-guided gradcam extraction is achieved by back-propagation of the target class (tag id) using the id number of each tag. The extraction of heat maps was achieved by the analysis of the tag values present in each patient’s X-ray image.
It was initially observed that both datasets were devoid of any coordinate information pertaining to the regions exhibiting signs of disease or abnormality. For this localisation, a labelling and localisation study is needed in the presence of a radiologist. The objective of this study is to generate a report which encompasses both disease or anomaly symptoms and normal conditions, including the condition and size of organs. In other words, if the objective was to produce a report that focused exclusively on specific disease states, the presence of a specialist physician would be necessary for the localisation of these regions. However, in order to produce reports that can be valid for both disease and normal conditions, that is, to produce reports that aim to identify the most prominent areas on an Xray image as well as every object in the image, it is preferred to create gradcam according to tags. Since the tags extracted for this study contain values specific to both disease and normal conditions, they serve the above-mentioned purpose.

## Tag guided gradcam heatmap extraction
In the final output of the study, it is aimed to show the tag values of the conditions (disease/normal) of a given XRay image of a patient, and it is also intended to to ascertain the presence of this tag information in the generated report, thereby ensuring a coordinated report formation with the tag. In order to achieve this goal, a given image must be subjected to a multi-label image classification process by an image classification model whose output value is the number of tags. In order to perform the multi-label image classification task, a multi-modal classification model has been developed in which the embeddings of the tag information of the image extracted by the pre-trained Clinical BERT Model are given as input at the same time as the image itself and the output value is the tag information. The pre-trained densenet121 was used as the image feature extractor of this model. The aim of this phase of the general framework is to perform gradcam heatmap extraction based on the tag information of an XRay image.

## High level feature extraction with vision transformer
The Vision Transformer (ViT), which was originally designed to be used in various NLP (Natural Language Processing) tasks, has been found to provide significant gains in the field of computer vision in the following periods. In this study, a ViT-based feature extractor module has been created in the visual feature extraction process. In the working principle of ViT, images are divided into fixed size patches and then linearly arranged in token sequences. Each token is supported with positional encodings to make sense of the placement logic of the patches in the image39. The core structure of the ViT architecture consists of multiple transformer blocks and each block consists of multi-head self-attention and feed-forward networks. The fact that the structure is designed in this way allows the weights of different image patches to vary relative to each other, in other words, to learn the weight distributions between various regions in the image and to extract the spatial relationship between these patches40.

## Cross attention
The Cross Attention mechanism enables the models to understand complex data representations and improve their ability to produce them by learning embeddings from different modalities such as image and text in an integrated manner. At the core of this technique is the ability to focus on relevant features from one modality while simultaneously processing information from other data types, thereby facilitating seamless interaction and integration between different data modalities43.
In the working mechanism of the cross-attention mechanism, input from any of the different modalities is treated as a query and input from the other modality is treated as a key-value pair. The way the structure works allows the model to calculate attention scores that determine how much focus should be given to different parts of the key input when processing the query input. This calculation is performed by performing a dot product between query and key and then applying the softmax operation to normalise44. The obtained normalised attention scores are then used to weight the values in the next stage. This weighted sum produces a new representation that extracts relevant information from the keys depending on the query45.
The rationale behind the utilisation of the cross-attention mechanism in this study is to ensure that the model focuses on the relevant parts of the image while generating text, and to generate aligned with image report words by focusing on these images. Thanks to the ability of this mechanism, which allows enhanced feature fusion, to prioritise certain features over others, more efficient learning can be achieved and as a result, high performance can be achieved46. ## Memory guided attention
Memory-guided attention mechanism is a mechanism that allows the model to focus on the relevant stimuli of the current time by utilising past experiences and stored information. The mechanism’s ability to predict possible future situations based on previous information is very important for effective visual search and decision-making47. In addition to the performance improvements provided by this attention mechanism in tasks based on visual data processing, it has also been suggested that it can improve long-term memory encoding by prioritising relevant information and reducing interference from distracters48. The working principle of memory-guided attention mechanism is based on the interaction between long-term memory (LTM) and working memory (WM)49. Memory-guided attention works by retrieving relevant information from memory to inform current attentional states. Here, a flexible approach is offered by adapting the orientation of the attention mechanism based on newly acquired information stored in WM or well-established information in LTM49.
The memory guided attention mechanism used in this study was implemented following Cross attention. This has brought many benefits, especially in increasing the efficiency and effectiveness of attention processes. The first of these benefits is that it can preserve important patterns and long-term dependencies that cross attention may neglect. Furthermore, the simultaneous recall of information from two different modalities could be improved, thus enhancing overall cognitive functioning. One of the superior aspects of the mechanism is that the model can provide more predictive responses to relevant stimuli.

## GPT-2 decoder
After the inclusion of feature extraction, graph-based fusion strategy and various attention mechanisms in the system, the decoding of the encoded information was initiated. In this study, the GPT-2 LM Head Model, which is a pre-trained model in the last layer of GPT-2 and performs the function of producing the model output in text format, was used for decoding. GPT-2 LM Head focuses on predicting the next token in the sequence by considering the tokens in the given sequence. This autoregressive feature allows GPT-2 to generate text sequentially by predicting one word at a time based on the context provided by previous words50.
Unlike bidirectional models such as BERT, GPT-2 is a unidirectional model that processes the input from left to right51. The GPT-2 architecture consists of multiple transformer layers, each of which contains a multi-head self-attention mechanism that focuses on different parts of the input sequence, followed by a feed-forward neural network52,53. This architectural design of the GPT-2 language model enables the model to capture complex relationships and dependencies in the input text. Each transformer block contains layer normalisation and residual connections that help to stabilise the training and improve the flow of gradients54.

## Dataset structure
In this study, IU X-Ray55 and Cov-CTR56 datasets, which are widely used as benchmark datasets, were used. The IU X-ray dataset consists of 7470 chest X-ray images and 3955 reports. The same way of separating the data as in57 was adopted here and 10% reports were randomly selected for testing. The Cov-CTR dataset consists of 728 images (349 with covid and 379 healthy) and English translations of the corresponding Chinese reports. For all datasets used, a 7:1:2 approach was adopted as train-val-test separation format.
In the IU X-Ray dataset, there is a section called ‘Impression’, which is considered as the title or summary of the report, and a section called ‘Findings’, which contains a more detailed description of the report. In this study, the ‘Findings’ and ‘Impression’ sections were combined as in reference58 and used as the target real report.
Cider (Consensus-based Image Description Evaluation) metric is among the metrics used in the evaluation of the study. This metric is an evaluation scale frequently used in image caption tasks and looks at the n-gram overlap between the text produced about the image and multiple sets of reference text for the same image. There is a single reference text with the combination of Findings and Impression columns (Findings_Impression). For a more accurate calculation of the Cider score, more than one actual report text is needed for each image. The document embeddings of patient-based reference texts from the Findings_Impression column were initially extracted using the pre-trained BioBERT model59. Subsequently, a search index was constructed with the Faiss (Facebook AI Similarity Search) library, which facilitates semantic similarity searches and is utilized in the retrieval phase of the Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approach. Findings_Impression text was given as a query and the two most similar texts were returned. For each patient, the texts returned from the Rag retrieval stage constitute another reference text of the data. Therefore, a total of 3 reference texts were created for each patient, one Findings_Impression and two information retrieved from Rag. A similar formation was also implemented for the COV-CTR dataset using the same methodology.

## Baselines and evaluation metrics
In order to demonstrate the accuracy of the proposed model by comparison, three basic models were identified. The first one, called Base, consists of a pretrained GPT2LMHeadModel, which is used as an encoder with a pretrained ViT (Vision Transformer) as a feature extractor, and a pretrained GPT2LMHeadModel, which is used as a decoder and returns the probabilities of the next words. The second model, Base + CoAttn, was created by adding the CoAttention mechanism between the encoder and decoder stages of the Base model. In the last model, MedVAG (Medical Vision Attention Generation), which is also the proposed model, in addition to the Base model, Graph Based Feature Fusion, CoAttention, CrossAttention and Memory Guided Attention, which transform the image features into graph structure, are included among the encoder and decoder structures, respectively.
BLUE60, METEOR61, ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L62, CIDEr29 and clinical efficacy (CE) metrics such as precision(P), recall(R), and F1 scores were used to evaluate the performance of the proposed model. It is insufficient to compare the generated texts with the reference texts only with metrics such as BLUE, METEOR, ROUGE, which are used as word overlap metrics. In the task of image-based text generation, texts that may have similar meanings with the reference text can be produced by using different words from the reference text. Therefore, the degree of semantic similarity should also be measured. Since the reference reports contain texts from the Findings_Impression and Rag retrieval process together, the measurement of the semantic similarity of the generated reports to these combined reference reports is considered as the Rag Answer similarity metric. For the calculation of this metric, the pretrained Sentence Transformer model was used to capture the cosine similarity between the reports. Rag answer correctness metric was used to determine the level of overlap of the generated answers with the ground truth texts.
For the qualitative evaluation of the study, images of 3 patients were randomly taken from the test set and the similarity of the reports produced for these images with the ground truth reports were evaluated. Accordingly, firstly, the XRay image of the patient is given to a multimodal classifier model to determine which tag information those images have. The classifier used here is a multilabel classifier and an image can have more than one tag value. Then, it was checked whether this tag value was present in both the generated and ground truth reports, and if this tag value was present in both reports, the word containing the tag information was underlined in the generated report. The 14 category values in Chexpert Labeler were used for the evaluation of diseased or normal conditions in the generated report. The comments on the evaluation of the colour relationships between the reports are considered as a qualitative measure of the study and these results are given in Sect. 5.4.

## Implementation details
The proposed framework consists of two main modules: the tag classifier and the medical report generation module. In the tag classifier stage, embeddings generated via pretrained ClinicalBERT were combined with visual features extracted from DenseNet121, serving as input for classification. Adam optimizer83 was selected for this module primarily due to its robustness in handling gradient noise and sparse label distributions, which are common in multi-label classification tasks. Experiments conducted with different learning rates indicated that a learning rate higher than 1 × 10−4 caused training instability, while lower rates prolonged training unnecessarily without significant accuracy improvements84,85. Thus, the optimal learning rate was empirically set at 1 × 10−4. A batch size of 8 was selected as it provided an optimal balance between GPU memory usage, gradient stability, and training speed, ensuring effective gradient updates without memory overflow. To avoid overfitting, a dropout of 0.4 and early stopping with patience of 3 epochs were applied, and automatic stopping typically occurred at the 20th epoch.
For the medical report generation module, the AdamW optimizer63 was specifically chosen due to its effective decoupled weight decay mechanism, which is critical in regularizing long-sequence generation tasks prone to overfitting. Experiments showed that a learning rate of 5 × 10−5 provided stable convergence and optimal performance, balancing rapid training and model accuracy. Higher rates caused divergence, while lower rates slowed convergence excessively. Batch size was constrained to 2 due to the increased memory requirements associated with sequential generation tasks. A dropout of 0.3 and early stopping with the same patience settings ensured that the model generalized effectively without compromising the computational efficiency.

## Abnormality
IU X-Ray dataset, one of the datasets used in the study, contains Manual Tags information in its original form. Nevertheless, the original COV-CTR dataset is devoid of tag information. In order to use the COV-CTR dataset for the purpose of the study, the tag extraction process detailed in Sect. 3.2 was carried out. The tag values of both datasets include both diseased and normal conditions. The 105 tag values for IU X-Ray and 50 tag values for COV-CTR constitute the number of output neurons of the tag classifier module that determines which tag information a patient’s X-ray image has. In other words, in the medical report generation phase, only the images of the patient, Findings Impressions and Rag Retrieval Texts constitute the input values of the model, while tag values and patient images are considered as input in the classification module. The ultimate goal of the study is to first determine which tag values a given Xray image has through the classification module, and then to ensure that this detected tag information is included in the report produced by the report generation module. Therefore, a coordinated report generation with tag has been the first point to be achieved. In addition, the only goal here is not only to detect diseased conditions and to provide report generation according to the disease, but also to provide a more comprehensive report generation that can cover all conditions (diseased, normal). For this reason, it is important that the tag values generated include both disease and normal conditions.
The colourings indicating the diseased or normal/healthy conditions in the patient-based reports given in Fig. 4 are based on the 14 key values in Chexpert labels and a large number of value information belonging to each key. Out of these 14 categories, the conditions belonging to the value values of the keys ‘support devices’ and ‘no findings’ were considered as ‘normal/healthy’, while the conditions other than these were considered as ‘diseased/need attention’. Accordingly, the similarity ratios between the words in the generated report and Chexpert Label’s key and corresponding value information were analysed. In the similarity calculation between words, word vectors were created with the pretrained Sentence Transformer, and then the similarity of this word vector to each value information belonging to the key was analysed using cosine similarity. The threshold value used here was taken as 0.5 as a result of the experiments. If the words are similar to each other above this threshold value, colouring was performed by looking at the colour code corresponding to the key value to which that value information belongs.

## Ablation studies
This section details an ablation study carried out to analyse the impact of various modules on the performance of the MedVag architecture for medical report generation. In this type of study, the performance results of the MedVag architecture, which includes Base, Base + CoAttn and finally all components, whose model contents are given in Sect. 4.2, on the basis of NLG and CE metrics are given in Table 2, and the results of RAG answer correctness and RAG answer similarity metrics are given in Table 3. Accordingly, it is seen that the MedVag model has a much superior performance on the basis of all NLG and CE metrics used in both datasets. MedVag, which belongs to Ablation3, achieved high levels in the production of clinically correct and meaningful words (according to Blue scores) as well as in the maintenance of fluency and coherence (according to Rouge metrics) compared to the remaining ablation studies. When the reason underlying this performance is evaluated on the basis of metrics, it is understood that the improvement of the Cider score is due to the addition of the Cross-Attention attention mechanism, which provides the alignment of textual and visual modalities. Cross-Attention has been demonstrated to have a significant impact on the production of image-related content, through the integration of clinically critical features derived from visual features with textual features. The significant improvement in CIDER compared to Ablation2 emphasises this alignment. Cross-Attention also ensured that the generated text was structurally consistent and fluent by maintaining the logical flow between multimodal (image + text) features.
The benefits of using the Memory Guided Attention mechanism are evident in the Recall, F1-score and Blue-4 metrics. The inclusion of the memory component in this attention mechanism allows the model to better capture clinical details and provides a balance between precision and recall. In addition, the memory component maintains contextual coherence over long word sequences, enabling the accurate generation of longer utterances.
The improvement that the Co-Attention mechanism brings to the MedVag architecture is that the model allows reasoning between these modalities by considering the semantic alignment between the input texts and the texts brought from the Rag retrieval-based approach of the model, and accordingly, various nuances of the clinical language can be understood, leading to an increase in the Meteor metric. The Co-Attention mechanism has resulted in a high precision of the generated texts by including only relevant details between texts and excluding irrelevant ones.

## Limitations
During the realisation of this study, it was observed that the IU X-Ray dataset is highly dependent on the random seed values and environment settings, resulting in noticeable overfitting problems. These issues appear closely related to the limited number and type of samples in the IU X-Ray dataset. Similarly, the COV-CTR dataset presents significant structural limitations, particularly due to the repetitive nature of the reports. Specifically, the repeated usage of common phrases such as ‘Thorax’ across nearly identical patient reports severely restricts the linguistic variability and reduces the model’s capacity for generating diverse, innovative text.
Moreover, the tag extraction methodology used in this study poses further limitations. Disease-relevant tags are only extracted if their corresponding terms occur above a predefined frequency threshold within the textual reports. Consequently, disease information may be inadvertently omitted in the generated reports, potentially affecting clinical accuracy and completeness. Hence, improving model performance, although already high according to NLG and CE metrics, heavily relies on enhancing the internal consistency, comprehensiveness, and expert-driven tag extraction of the datasets used.
To overcome these limitations and enhance generalization, future studies should incorporate larger, more diverse, and multicentric datasets representing various patient populations and conditions. Employing advanced data augmentation techniques and ensuring expert involvement in tag extraction processes could significantly improve the model’s capacity to generate clinically accurate and diverse medical reports.

## Discussion and conclusion
In this study, a new framework is presented that enables detailed report generation using Graph Based Future Feature Fusion and Vision Transformer, which are spatial and positional feature extractors that allow advanced visual detail capture of chest X-ray images. The developed structure has two stages. In the first of these stages, Tag Guided GradCam Heatmap Extraction module, gradcam heatmap generation based on patient tags was performed. The objective of this study is to demonstrate the tag values associated with the disease or normal conditions in the X-ray image of a patient, thereby ensuring that these tags are incorporated within the generated report. The subsequent aim is to generate a report that is compatible with the tags.
The module responsible for report generation of the proposed structure is based on encoder-decoder architecture and is focused on extracting spatial and high-level features between frontal and lateral images of a patient. The degree to which the model makes sense of the image features determines the quality of the generated report. Accordingly, the primary focus was on the extraction of detailed information from the image. For this purpose, high-level image features were extracted for each image (front-side) of the patient with a pre-trained ViT model, and then these features were converted into a graph structure and the spatial relationship on these 2 images of a patient was captured through a graph-based fusion module. In this way, the relationships between the features of both images and context information were learnt. The section up to this stage constitutes the encoder phase of the model. This study encompassed a multifaceted approach, incorporating not only the examination of image features but also the extraction of features from textual elements. For this purpose, both the ground truth reports and the retrieved reports that are most similar to the ground truth reports with RAG Retrieval are passed through the Transformer block of GPT2 and word embeddings are obtained. The resulting embeddings were passed through the Co Attention mechanism to capture the interdependencies of these two different modalities (ground truth reports and RAG Retrieval ones).
Since the general goal of the study is to produce text from the given image, visual features should be combined with text features at some point. On the basis of Cross Attention Mechanism, while the information coming from one modality (image) is made sense of, the information coming from the other modality (text) is learnt at the same time. Therefore, image features and text features are considered in their entirety. This part constitutes an important point that serves the purpose of the study.
With the Memory-guided attention mechanism applied after Cross Attention, important information in the past learning of the report generation model is stored and the ability to predict the possible future words to be produced is gained, thus paving the way for more stable production. In addition, the general cognitive functioning was strengthened by improving the simultaneous recall of information from 2 different modalities with memory-guided attention. The attention mechanisms used up to this stage were added between the encoder-decoder parts of the model. After extracting all necessary visual and textual features and the parts of these features to be paid attention to, the report generation process was initiated. For this purpose, a pre-trained GPT2 Transformers language model was used.
The focus of the study is to provide more detailed extraction of visual features and to produce tag compatible reports. The results obtained from various experiments show that the goal was achieved in terms of the metrics used. Standard natural language generation (NLG) metrics (BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE and CIDEr) and clinical effectiveness (CE) metrics including precision, recall and F1 score were used to evaluate model performance. In addition, these trials were performed on 2 different benchmark datasets, IU X-Ray and COV-CTR.
The MedVAG model proposed here achieved a BLEU-4 score of 0.595 on the IU X-Ray dataset, significantly outperforming previous models such as R2Gen (BLEU-4 0.165) and CMN (BLEU-4 0.170). This metric emphasises the fluency and consistency of the generated reports. It is clear from the values in Table 1 that the proposed model achieves a higher performance in all BLEU metrics.
The model also achieved a METEOR score of 0.748, reflecting strong semantic similarity with the reference texts, and a ROUGE-L score of 0.762, indicating that the generated reports preserve the structural and contextual integrity of the reference texts. The CIDEr score of 5.054, which surpasses all previous approaches, demonstrates the content richness and diagnostic accuracy of the generated reports. The clinical effectiveness measures of precision (0.775), recall (0.780), and F1 scores (0.776) confirmed the robustness of the model and underlined the ability of the generated reports to accurately reflect clinically relevant information.
The MedVAG model achieved similarly high performance on the COV-CTR dataset. A BLEU-4 score of 0.611 outperformed the Vision-BERT (BLEU-4 0.558) and R2Gen (BLEU-4 0.521) models, and a METEOR score of 0.807 demonstrated an exceptional ability to capture the nuanced terminology and semantics of medical texts. Furthermore, the model achieved a ROUGE-L score of 0.805, ensuring structural consistency in the generated reports. The CIDEr score of 2.226 confirmed the model’s ability to produce diagnostically relevant and content-rich text. The clinical effectiveness measures of precision, recall and F1 score in this dataset were 0.759, 0.788 and 0.772, respectively, indicating high reliability in identifying clinically relevant information.
The measurement of model performance was not only based on numerical metrics but also on qualitative evaluation. In the IU X-Ray dataset, the model correctly identified important medical terms such as ‘aorta, mild, thoracic, tortuous’ and included them in the generated reports. These terms were consistent with actual values, demonstrating the model’s ability to generate diagnostically meaningful content. A similar performance was observed in the COV-CTR dataset; however, this dataset is inherently less diverse and the existing reports are mostly similar, with differences between reports being limited to a few words. Despite this limitation of the COV-CTR dataset, the model proposed here was able to produce a consistent production that is tag compliant and does not semantically deviate from the reference report.
The proposed MedVAG model achieved state-of-the-art performance in automated medical report generation, effectively producing radiology reports with superior semantic accuracy, contextual richness, and diagnostic relevance on both IU X-Ray and COV-CTR datasets. Nevertheless, inherent limitations related to dataset diversity, repetitive textual patterns, and the quality of tag extraction could impact the model’s generalization capability across diverse clinical environments. Specifically, the repetitive nature and limited diversity in datasets restrict the variability and innovativeness of generated reports, potentially affecting their broader applicability. Moreover, the reliance on frequency-based tagging can inadvertently omit clinically relevant information, highlighting the importance of expert-guided labeling processes. Additionally, although the attention mechanisms significantly improved text-image alignment and diagnostic accuracy, they may introduce biases due to dominant patterns in the training data. To address these limitations, future research should employ larger, multi-institutional datasets, advanced data augmentation strategies, and federated learning approaches to enhance model robustness and generalizability, particularly for rare or complex conditions. Implementing MedVAG in real-world clinical settings may pose additional challenges, including data privacy concerns and variability in imaging and reporting standards across institutions. Addressing these challenges through standardized protocols, multi-center validations, and privacy-preserving methodologies is essential. Given these considerations, MedVAG should be primarily used as an assistive technology to support radiologists, maintaining essential human oversight to ensure clinical accuracy and patient safety.
