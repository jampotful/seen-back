# Multimodal imaging analysis of 40 cases of combined hamartoma of the retina and retinal pigment epithelium: a retrospective observational study

## Abstract
BackgroundThis study aimed to analyse the multimodal imaging features of combined hamartoma of the retina and retinal pigment epithelium (CHRRPE).MethodsThis retrospective observational study included patients (n = 40) who were diagnosed with CHRRPE at the Second Hospital of Jilin University between March 2008 and March 2024. The characteristics of the multimodal images of the fundus of the affected eyes, including the patients’ fundus images, optical coherence tomography (OCT), fundus autofluorescence (FAF), fluorescein fundus angiography (FFA) and indocyanine green angiography (ICGA) imaging, were evaluated and summarised.ResultsThe study analyzed 40 eyes from patients (mean age: 26.88 years, 47.5% male). Macular involvement was observed in 80% of cases, with lesions primarily yellowish-white (67.5%) and located at the posterior pole (87.5%). Retinal staging revealed 85% in stage 2, with 97.5% exhibiting proliferative membranes. Of these, 34 eyes underwent OCT, and their characteristic manifestations were small peaks (mini-peaks) in 23 eyes (67.6%), large peaks (maxi-peaks) in 23 eyes (67.6%), shark teeth sign in seven eyes (20.6%), omega sign in six eyes (17.6%), and double-retina sign in eight eyes (23.5%). And those involving the optic disc are more likely to invade the nasal retina of fovea (n = 10, 71.4%). Furthermore, while FAF showed hypofluorescent lesions with hyperfluorescent margins in some cases. FFA highlighted vascular abnormalities and late leakage in 90% of eyes, and ICGA demonstrated early hypofluorescence with late mottled fluorescence.ConclusionMultimodal image analysis of 40 patients with CHRRPE revealed some unique structural features of CHRRPE. Among them, optical coherence tomography provided an accurate qualitative analysis of CHRRPE features, which facilitated disease diagnosis and guided treatment.

## Background
Combined hamartoma of the retina and retinal pigment epithelium (CHRRPE) is a rare, isolated benign retinal tumour that was first reported by Gass in 1973 [1]. Histopathologically, it is characterised by abnormal proliferation of retinal pigment epithelial cells, retinal blood vessels, and dysplastic neuroglial components [2]. One of these components is usually predominantly present in the retina and retinal pigment epithelium, resulting in a differently coloured tumour. Consequently, a bulging pigmented lesion can occur in the optic disc, macula, and midperipheral retina [3]. It is most common in children and develops in one eye. In a few cases, it may be associated with type II or type I neurofibromatosis [4].
In 2018, Dedania VS et al. [5] proposed new classification criteria for CHRRPE: ZONE: Zone 1: macula and its periphery (centred on the optic nerve and macula, 1.5 mm beyond the optic disc and paracentral concave area); Zone 2: mid-peripheral area (ranging up to the equator); and Zone 3: peripheral area (ranging up to the serrated rim). STAGE: Stage 1: no retinal detachment; Stage 2: retinal detachment and/or retinal cleavage; and Stage 3: retinal detachment. OCT presentation: grade A: superficial retinal involvement; grade B: partial retinal involvement; and grade C: retinal and RPE involvement.
With the continuous development of ophthalmic imaging technology, methods such as optical coherence tomography (OCT), ultra-wide angle laser fundus photography, fluorescein fundus angiography (FFA), and indocyanine green angiography (ICGA) have improved the accuracy of CHRRPE diagnosis. In this study, we proposed to retrospectively analyse the multimodal images of CHRRPE and summarize the various imaging features of CHRRPE to provide reference for clinical diagnosis and treatment.

## Methods

## General information
This retrospective case analysis included 40 patients (40 eyes) with CHRRPE diagnosed at the Second Hospital of Jilin University between March 2008 and March 2023. The inclusion criteria were as follows:(1)patiets with typical multimodal imaging features consistent with CHRRPE,including fundus photography,OCT and FFA;(2) patients with clinical manifestations such as retinal pigment epithum and retinal abnormalities. The exclusion criteria included:(1) patients with other ocular diseases that could mimic CHRRPE,such as idiopathic epiretinal menbrane(ERM) or retinal vascular tumors confirmed by comprehensive clinical examination and multimodal imaging;(2) patiens with incomplete imaging data or fowllow-up information. All affected eyes met the diagnostic criteria for CHRRPE. The study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was reviewed and approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Second Hospital of Jilin University (Approval No. 259, 2024).

## Methodology
Each patient’s age, race, gender, and associated ocular symptoms, including tumour size, shape, colour and location were evaluated. We also evaluated retinal vascular changes, haemorrhages, exudates, traction, fibrosis/gliosis or retinal detachment, macular oedema, anterior retinal membranes or holes, central pits or optic disc traction, retinal or choroidal neovascularisation and vitreous haemorrhage and systemic syndromes including neurofibromatosis.
Imaging tests included: (1) colour fundus images (TRC-5EX, Topcon, Japan); (2) FAF examination (Spectralis HRA, Heidelberg Engineering, Germany); (3) FFA examination (Spectralis HRA, Heidelberg Engineering, Germany); (4) ICGA examination (Spectralis HRA, Heidelberg Engineering); (5) OCT examination (Spectralis OCT, Heidelberg Engineering, Germany).

## Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)
OCT images of 34 eyes revealed several tumour-associated features including a hyperreflective surface, prominent preretinal membrane, and retinal folds or streaks caused by membrane contraction. Other findings included retinal bulges at the lesion, cavity-like hyporeflectivity and structural disturbances underneath, and normal outer retina with vitreous proliferation. Additionally, we observed the following signs: inner layer damage may show sawtooth signs (mini-peaks) (n = 23, 67.6%), whole retina damage may show large peaks (maxi-peaks) (n = 22, 64.7%). Furthermore, the whole retina may have large folds, however, the outer plexiform layer may remain intact and clear in morphology, resembling the shape of"ω", i.e. the omega sign (n = 6, 17.6%). A small triangular highly reflective sign in the outer plexiform layer (OPL) was defined as the shark teeth sign (n = 7, 20.5%), and the double-retina sign (n = 8, 23.5%) (Fig. 1, e); none of the above signs were present in the remaining five eyes (12.5%). Those without optic disc involvement were less likely to have RPE migration and less likely to have RPE hypertrophy. In addition, we observed that in cases where the lesions were located in the optic disc (n = 14), the lesions mostly affected the retina on the nasal side of the central concavity more (n = 10, 71.4%) (Fig. 2).

## Fundus Autofluorescence (FAF)
The autofluorescence of the tumour in the affected eyes was uniformly attenuated to varying degrees (Fig. 1, d), and in eight cases (20%), the autofluorescence of the affected eyes showed marginal patchy or linear hyperfluorescence, which was considered to be the accumulation of lipofuscin due to the malfunctioning of the peripheral RPE.

## Fluorescein Fundus Angiography (FFA)
The lesion area of the affected eyes in the early stage of FFA contrast had different degrees of masked fluorescence due to tumour occlusion or retinal thickening, retinal vascular tugging, and deformation; moreover, capillary dilatation were seen in the tumour area, with fluorescence leakage in the late stage (Fig. 1, b, c). Furthermore, patchy translucent fluorescence was seen in some of the affected eyes. No obvious fluorescein leakage was seen in the late stage of FFA in three eyes (7.5%). Microscopic Angiomas and capillary dilatation showed pinpoint and punctate fluorescence, while strong fluorescence leakage with blurred borders was seen in the late phase of the imaging. There were no obvious abnormalities in blood vessels outside the lesion area.

## Indocyanine Green Angiography (ICGA)
ICGA images showed that the tumour in the affected eye obscured the choroidal fluorescence to varying degrees in the early stage, and in some of the late stages, the central part of the lesion were seen to be hypofluorescent, with mottled fluorescence at the periphery (Fig. 5). Furthermore, in only one case, the affected eye showed a large patchy hyperfluorescence at the central part of the lesion in the late stage, with hypofluorescence at the periphery, and linear hyperfluorescence at the edges. ## Complication
In this study, out of 40 eyes, choroidal neovascularisation (CNV) was seen in four eyes (10%), retinal tear in two eyes (5%), and retinal pigment epithelial detachment (PED) in one eye (2.5%) (Fig. 6). Most of the cases required only regular observation as the lesions were stable. ## Discussion
CHRRPE is a rare ocular tumour that commonly occurs in children. Although cases are benign isolated lesions, they can lead to other complications, such as progressive vision loss. Currently, studies have indicated that CHRRPE may be associated with type II neurofibromatosis, type I neurofibromatosis, Gorlin-Goltz syndrome, Poland syndrome, branchio-ocular facet syndrome, and juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma [3].
Histopathological features include a single layer of overlapping retinal pigment epithelial cell hyperplasia that invades the tumour tissue above it to form mottled pigment deposits. Neuroglial hyperplasia on the retinal surface constricts and pulls the tumour vessels and retinal blood vessels towards the centre of the tumour and forms retinal folds or striations [1]. The difference in tumour colour may reflect the histopathology of the tumour, which may be an abnormally proliferating pigment epithelium, blood vessel, or dysplastic glial cells [2]. FFA findings further support that a defective vascular system serves as the primary pathological basis [6].The clinical presentation of CHRRPE varies from flat to bulging lesions with varying degrees of hyperpigmentation, tortuous vascularity, and epiretinal membrane (ERM) formation with vitreous haemorrhage, macular tear, CNV, and retinal traction, which progressively affect vision. CHRRPE is commonly seen in young patients, presenting with hamartomatous malformations, retinal structural disorder on OCT, and no significant vascular abnormalities or fluorescein leakage. In contrast, idiopathic epiretinal membrane is more prevalent in the elderly, without pigmentary disturbances and with relatively intact retinal structure, while retinal vascular tumors are often accompanied by dilated vessels, arteriovenous malformations, bleeding, and significant fluorescein leakage on FFA.
In multimodal imaging at CHRRPE, fundus photography provides an objective picture of the fundus of the affected eye, including tumour colour, lesion size, extent of involvement, and surface proliferation, allowing for the analysis of the tumour’s main pathological structural components and prognosis. FFA allows assessment of the blood supply to the tumour and the degree of retinal vascular involvement. ICGA is more penetrating than FFA and can be better observed through the thickened retina.
OCT has the most diagnostic value in visualising the hierarchy and extent of lesions. In 2002, Ting et al. [7] first described the presentation of CHRRPE on OCT as ERM visible at the lesion, retinal thickening that may be wavy or associated with folds, and structural disturbances within the retina that were hyperreflective, with extensive shadowing of the tissue underneath. OCT of 11 cases reported by Shields et al. [8] revealed significant anterior retinal membranes and retinal folds or streaks caused by membrane contraction in almost every case; Tiny retinal folds were jagged protrusions on the inner surface of the retina; ERM and inward retinal pulling, and full-layer retinal disorganisation. Subsequent studies used OCT to identify mini-peaks, maxi-peaks(large peaks) [9], shark teeth sign [10], omega sign [11], double retina sign [12] and concentric macular rings (CMR) [13]. They also showed disruption of the retinal layers including the following: disruption of the inner retina (mini-peaks), full-layer retinal folds with distorted vertical and lateral stretching of the superficial and deep layers of the retina (maxi-peaks/omega sign), or the OPL into the outer nuclear layer (ONL) forming a small, highly reflective triangular alteration (shark teeth), respectively. It has been suggested that the identification of the ‘Omega sign’ provides new insights into the distinction between macular CHRRPE and idiopathic ERM [11].
Speculation about the mechanism of CHRRPE occurrence has also been supported by the OCT. Ledesma-Gil et al. [14] retrospectively analysed the evolution of CHRRPE, which progresses from the inner retina to the outer retinal layers, and affected the normal anatomy and vascular structure of each retinal layer. The absence of retinal self-folding in the interstices filled with gliotic hyperplasia can be seen in the double retinal sign on OCT, further confirming that CHRRPE is the result of a gradual, full-layer involvement of the retina from the inner to the outer retinal layers [12]. Another recent OCT report of CHRRPE describes CMR, in which the OPL and Henle fibre layer (HFL) on SD-OCT cross-sections are in a toothed pattern in the macular area. Moreover, OCTA showed the absence of the FAZ. A similar feature is also thought to be formed by traction-induced changes at the interface of the OPL and HFL [13].Gass classified the CHRRPE into two categories based on whether the optic nerve was involved [15]. Furthermore, those with optic nerve involvement were more likely to have RPE damage [16]. Most of the OCTs showed that the outer layer of the retina and the RPE were intact (29 eyes, 72.5%), and only 11 eyes had significant damage to the outer layer and the RPE, of which eight (72.7%) had optic disc or paracentral lesions. Furthermore, it was observed that cases in which the lesion was located in the optic disc had a relatively minimal impact on the temporal retina of the central concavity, possibly related to the anatomy of the central concavity.
OCT is also an important reference for surgical treatment. If the retinal structure is still good and there is a gap between the pulled retinal prefovea and the retina, it suggests that the surgery may be less challenging and there is a high probability of improvement of visual acuity; otherwise, the surgery is not worthwhile and may lead to complications.
In recent years, optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) has rapidly evolved, and the clinical application of OCTA can deepen our understanding of related diseases. Gupta et al. described the characteristics of OCTA of CHRRPE, and observed a high density in fully disorganised retinal foci of filamentous vascular pattern, which is thought to result from a lack of structural organisation within the dysplastic tissue [17].
CHRRPE needs to be differentiated from idiopathic pre-retinal, retinoblastoma, and choroidal melanoma. In recent years, some scholars believe that CHRRPE needs to be differentiated from unilateral retinal pigment epithelium dysgenesis (URPED) [18].
Complications of CHRRPE can progressively affect visual function, e.g. CNV [19, 20]. When there are no significant complications in visual function in patients with CHRRPE, no interventional treatment is required, and only regular follow-up is performed. In cases of ERM that affect visual function, membranectomy was performed. The main purpose of surgery is to release the retinal pull in order to restore the anatomical structure and function of the retina. In children, postoperative interventions for amblyopia are also very important.
The objective of this study was to gain insights into the clinical features, pathological mechanisms and their association with multiple syndromes through multimodal imaging of CHRRPE. The novel aspects of our study include the identification of multiple characteristic OCT manifestations of CHRRPE, the observation that lesions involving the optic disc have a greater impact on the retina of the central concave nasal side, and the exploration of the association between these manifestations and pathological mechanisms. Furthermore, our study emphasises the value of OCT in guiding the diagnosis and management of CHRRPE. However, this study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design may introduce selection bias. Second,the lack of functional outcomes,such as visual acuity changes,limits the assessment of the impact of CHRRPE on visual function.Third, the reliance on qualitative imaging interpretation may lead to subjective variability in the evaluation of lesions.Future studies should consider a prospective design, include functional outcomes, and explore quantitative imaging analysis to address these limitations.

## Conclusions
CHRRPE is a benign neoplastic disease of unknown origin that has been described by multimodal imaging with various features of its lesions. However, given the rarity of the disease and limited observations, more evidence is needed to elucidate its true pathogenesis. Our study provides new perspectives for future research and emphasises the importance of multimodality imaging in the diagnosis and management of CHRRPE.

Early-phase fluorescein angiography (c, f) reveals retinal vessel traction, capillary dilation, and punctate hyperfluorescence. In the late phase (d, g), there is obvious leakage. OCT (e) examination shows retinal thickening with a high-reflective signal on the surface and tractional elevation (arrows), disordered inner layer structure (asterisks), and intact outer retina and retinal pigment epithelium

## Figure Fig. 4: Multimodal image of a patient in Zone 3. Fundus photographs (a, b) show large patchy blue-gray lesions (arrows) in the superotemporal peripheral retina with superficial proliferative membrane formation; Telangiectasia and punctate hyperfluorescence can be seen in the early stage (c) on fluorescein angiography, and in the late stage (d) there is significant leakage (asterisks) surrounded by an area without perfusion

## Figure Fig. 5: A case of CHRRPE multimodal imaging. Fundus color photograph (a) shows the optic disc and the yellowish-white lesion (triangle) beneath it with proliferative membranes, extensive mottled pigmentation around the mass, tortuous surface vessels, retinal folds due to traction, and involvement of the macula; OCT (b) shows thickened retinal tissue at the lesion with high-reflective signals on the surface and tractional elevation, high signals in the inner retinal layer, disordered inner layer structure, and masking of the signals from the underlying tissues. Fundus fluorescein angiography (c) shows early patchy hypofluorescence, dilated capillaries, and punctate hyperfluorescence, and late (e) leakage of fluorescence. Indocyanine green angiography (d) shows early hypofluorescence and (f) late mottled fluorescence

## Figure Fig. 6: Complications of CHRRPE. Retinal tear: Lesions below the optic disc (arrow), involving the macula, surface proliferative membrane formation, pulling the retina. Retinal tears can be seen next to the blood vessels below the optic disc are showed in the fundus photographs (a); OCT (b) shows that high reflection signals on the surface of the retina, pulling the retina to bulge, separating the neuroepithelial layer from the pigmentary cortex, disordered temporal retina structure, and a full-thickness defect of the neuroretina can be seen on the nasal side (arrow). PED: Elevated lesions in the optic disc area, circular orange red elevations of blood vessels above the optic disc (c), OCT (d) shows increased thickness of the nasal retina, high reflection signal on the surface; focal elevation of the RPE (star), and a liquid dark area below it, obscuring the choroid below. CNV: Macular area lesions (e). The lesion appears yellow white, with disappearance of reflection in the macular fovea. OCT (f) shows that high reflectivity signal on the surface of the retina, with macular tissue protrusion and interlayer edema, RPE layer protrusion, and visible clustered medium to high reflectivity (triangle)

## Figure Fig. 7: Multimodal images of one case of CHRRPE after vitrectomy. Preoperative images: fundus photographs (a) show a slightly elevated macular lesion (arrow) with proliferation of the surface membrane, pulling of the peripheral retina, tortuous vascular alignment, OCT (b) shows hyperreflectivity of the macular surface, thickening of the retina, structural disturbances and folding phenomena. Postoperative imaging: fundus photographs (c) show less elevation of the macular area, with less vascular tortuosity than before, and OCT (d) shows thinning of the nerve fibre layer, improved retinal thickness in the macula compared to before, and blurred retinal structure
