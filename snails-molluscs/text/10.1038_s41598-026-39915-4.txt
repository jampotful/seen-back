# RBP4 interferes with tongue squamous cell carcinoma progression by inhibiting the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and promoting macrophage M1-type polarization

## Abstract
AbstractTongue squamous cell carcinoma (TSCC) is a common type of oral mucosal epithelial malignancy that can severely affect patient quality of life. Therefore, novel therapeutic strategies for TSCC are needed. This study aimed to investigate the role and mechanism of action of retinol-binding protein 4 (RBP4) in TSCC progression and its effect on tumor-associated macrophages. To this end, TSCC cell lines with RBP4 overexpression and knockdown were constructed, and effects of RBP4 expression on the proliferation and invasive abilities of TSCC cells were verified using ex vivo experiments. Furthermore, a tumor cell-macrophage co-culture model was established to assess the effect of RBP4 on macrophage polarization. RBP4 overexpression reduced the phosphorylation of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway and inhibited tumor cell proliferation by regulating Snail levels; RBP4 inhibited TSCC cells from undergoing epithelial-mesenchymal transition. In addition, RBP4 promoted the activation of NF-κB signaling pathway, leading to macrophage polarization toward M1 type and inhibiting TSCC growth. We found for the first time that RBP4 affects the proliferation and migration of TSCC cells by inhibiting the activation of the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, and that RBP4 promotes the M1-type polarization of tumor-associated macrophages, which contributes to their antitumor effects.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-39915-4.Keywords: RBP4, Tongue squamous cell carcinoma, PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, NF-κB signaling pathway, Macrophage polarizationSubject terms: Cancer, Cell biology, Oncology

## Introduction
Tongue squamous cell carcinoma (TSCC) is a common type of oral mucosal epithelial malignancy, and the loss of tissue and part of the tongue function after surgery severely affects patient quality of life1. Thus, there is an urgent need to develop novel therapeutic strategies.
Retinol-binding protein 4 (RBP4) is a transport protein for lipophilic vitamin A retinol in the blood. RBP4 expression is downregulated in epithelial ovarian cancers2, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)3, and colorectal cancers4. PI3K is the starting node of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway, and aberrant activation of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway is closely associated with tumorigenesis and metastasis5. Activated Akt regulates various substrates required for cell growth, including mTOR6. In addition, PI3K/Akt pathway regulates epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in tumors7. RBP4 can impact the activation of the PI3K pathway; however, whether RBP4 activates the PI3K pathway in TSCC cells has not been reported.
Macrophages are involved in inflammatory and immune responses and have a wide range of functions8. Macrophages involved in the formation of the tumor microenvironment (TME) (tumor-associated macrophages [TAMs])9are the most abundant innate immune population in the TME10. Furthermore, activation of the nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) signaling pathway plays a key role in the induction of an important factor in the polarization of TAMs to M1-type macrophages11. RBP4 is a secreted RBP12, and it is not clear whether tumor-secreted RBP4 plays an inhibitory role in tumor progression in the TME by modulating the polarization. We, therefore, aimed to examine the role of RBP4 in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway activation and Snail-mediated EMT in TSCC, to develop new strategies for targeting TAMs in cancer therapy.

## Bioinformatics analysis
Based on the TCGA-TSCC (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov) cohort (158 tumors + 14 paraneoplastic tissues), immune subtypes were classified using ssGSEA/hierarchical clustering, and their reliability was verified using ESTIMATE/t-SNE. EdgeR was applied to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs), which were combined with the ImmPort immune gene library to screen for TME immune-related DEGs (TIDEGs).
A TME immune-related genes prognostic signature (TIPS) was constructed by single/multifactorial Cox regression after gene ontology (GO)/Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) annotation13, and high and low-risk groups were differentiated according to the risk score formula: (Risk score = Σ(βi×Gi)). The Kaplan–Meier survival analysis and time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to verify the predictive efficacy of the model. Key prognosis-related genes, KLRK1, LTB, and RBP4, were screened for tumor/paraneoplastic differential validation, and RBP4 was confirmed to be differentially expressed in three Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets (193 cases in total), GSE13601, GSE52915, and GSE34105, using the limma package.
The Hallmark gene set was obtained from the MSigDB database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb), which was analyzed by Gene Set Variation Analysis to show a positive correlation with PI3K/Akt pathway activity (using databases GSE13601 and GSE31056); Gene Set Enrichment Analysis suggested that the high expression of the RBP4 group was enriched for inflammatory/immune pathways (using databases GSE13601, GSE78060, GSE31056, and GSE52915). CIBERSORT analysis suggested that RBP4 was associated with M1 macrophage polarization (using databases GSE13601 and GSE31056); finally, a correlation analysis was performed between RBP4 expression in TSCC-HNSCC data and TLR4 expression.
To further explore the relationship between genes and signaling pathway activity, this study loaded the TCGA GSVA enrichment score dataset (tcga_gsva_hmk.Rdata) and merged it with the TCGA gene expression matrix. Using RBP4 as the core gene of interest, the signaling pathway HALLMARK_PI3K_AKT_MTOR_SIGNALING was selected. Spearman correlations between these two factors across different cancer types were calculated and visualized. Subsequently, the expression correlation between them in HNSC cancers was further analyzed using the sincorplot function.

## Immunohistochemistry
We used paraffin-embedded samples from patients who underwent surgical treatment in the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University, Harbin, China, and were diagnosed with TSCC by the Department of Pathology in 2023–2024. We included 20 clinical samples and matched paracancerous tissues of TSCC, none of which received radiotherapy or immunotherapy prior to surgery.
Tissue blocks were fixed using 4.0% paraformaldehyde, and the embedded tissues were cut into 4 μm sections. After the addition of an endogenous peroxidase blocker for 10 min, three rinses were performed using phosphate buffered saline (PBS); a non-specific staining blocker was added for another 10 min.
After overnight incubation with RBP4 antibody (1:200, Abclonal) or CD86 antibody (1:200, Abclonal), sections were incubated with rabbit secondary antibody for 30 min at room temperature. The sections were incubated with rabbit secondary antibody for 30 min using 3-diamino benzidine tetrahydrochloride (DAB kit, Maixin Biotechnology Development Co., Ltd.) to develop the color, and were then stained with hematoxylin. The staining intensity was evaluated under a microscope. Two pathologists evaluated the staining intensity of the sections without prior knowledge of the basic patient information or clinical characteristics. During high-power microscopic examination, five fields of view were selected, and the number of positive cells was counted to obtain an average. The specific scoring criteria are as follows: Evaluate the average staining index (SI) across five randomly selected fields of view (SI = staining area score × staining intensity score). The staining area scoring stand-ards are: 0 points (staining area nearly 0%); 1 (staining area < 10%); 2 (staining area 10–35%); 3 (staining area 35–75%); 4 (staining area 76–100%). The staining intensity scoring criteria are as follows: 0 points (no staining), 1 point (weak staining), 3 points (moderate staining), 4 points (strong staining). An SI ≥ 8 indicates high expression, while SI < 8 indicates low expression.

## Cell lines and culture
Human TSCC cell lines (CAL27 and SCC-15), a human monocyte cell line (THP-1), and a mouse monocyte cell line (RAW264.7) were purchased from the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). CAL27 and SCC-15 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA) supplemented with 100 U/mL streptomycin, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, ScienCell). THP-1 cells were grown with RAW264.7 cells in Roswell Park Memorial Institute medium (RPMI)-1640 medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco). Cells were all cultured in an incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2.

## Cell transfection and stable cell line construction
Human RBP4 cDNA (Table. S2) was constructed using GeneChem (Shanghai, China), and a lentiviral plasmid vector was constructed using a plasmid with the sequence Ubi-MCS-3FLAG-CBh-gcGFP-IRES-puromycin. After lentiviral transfection of cells according to the instructions, the cells were treated with 2.5 µg/mL of puromycin, and the stably transfected cells were collected for subsequent experiments. TSCC cells overexpressing RBP4 and negative control cells were named the OE-RBP4 and OE-NC groups, respectively. TSCC cells with RBP4 knockdown and negative control cells were named the sh-RBP4 and sh-NC groups (Table. S3), respectively, and the Control group comprised normal TSCC cells.

## CCK8 assay
Cells were counted and inoculated into 96-well plates at a density of approximately 3 × 103 cells/well, and the total volume was made up to 100 µL. Three wells from each group were used, and the cells were incubated for 12, 24, 36, and 48 h. The cells were added to 10% CCK-8 (Dojindo) assay solution, and were incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. The absorbance (optical density [OD]) was determined using an enzyme marker at 450 nm.

## Cell scratch assay
The cells in the logarithmic growth phase were inoculated into six-well plates; when the cell growth fusion was approximately 95%, we used a 10-µL sterile tip to make a scratch perpendicular to the bottom of the plate; washed with PBS 2–3 times and then added serum-free RPMI 1640 medium; we placed the plates in an incubator (37 °C, 5% CO2) and pictures were taken at 0 h and 24 h using a Leica DMI1 microscope in brightfield mode. The formula scratch healing rate was calculated as [(0 h scratch area-24 h scratch area)/0 h scratch area]×100%.

## Transwell experiment
Transwell experiments were performed using a transwell chamber (Corning Inc., Corning, NY). For cell migration experiments, 1 × 105 TSCC cells were inoculated in the upper chamber with 200 µl of McCoy’s 5A medium without FBS, while 600 µL of medium containing 15% FBS was placed in the lower chamber. Cells were incubated at 37 °C for 48 h. Unmigrated cells were removed using a cotton swab on top of the filter membrane. The cells at the bottom of the membrane were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min at room temperature and stained with 0.1% crystal violet (Beyotime, China) for 20 min. After washing with PBS, the cells were observed under a microscope and photographed in three randomly selected fields of view at 200× magnification (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). For invasion experiments, 50 µL of diluted Matrigel (BD Bioscience, San Jose, CA) was applied to the transwell filter membrane.

## Western blotting experiment
After total protein extraction, proteins were lysed using RIPA buffer, separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (10%), and transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (Biosharp, China). The membranes were incubated with 5% skimmed milk solution in PBS solution for 2 h at room temperature before incubating with primary antibodies (Table S1) overnight at 4 °C. Diluted horseradish peroxidase-coupled secondary antibody was added and incubated for 2 h at room temperature before protein blotting was performed using an electrochemiluminescence assay kit (Epizyme, China). The results were obtained from three independent experiments, and the intensity of the protein blots was determined using the ImageJ software.

## qRT-PCR experiments
Total cellular RNA was isolated from the cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) and reverse-transcribed with a SevenCleverTM First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (with dsDNase). The PCR primer sequences are listed in Table S4. A SevenFast® Two Step RT & qPCR Kit was used for real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR (qRT-PCR). Relative quantitative analysis was performed using the 2-ΔΔCT method.

## TSCC cells co-cultured with THP-1-derived M0 macrophages
Cells were seeded at a density of 7 × 105 cells/well and treated with 100 ng/mL phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA; Sigma Chemicals, St. Louis, MO) for 48 h to allow the THP-1 cells to differentiate into M0 macrophages. TSCC cell lines overexpressing or knocking down RBP4 were co-cultured with THP-1-induced M0 macrophages in the transwell chambers of six-well plates, with cancer cells in the upper chamber and M0 macrophages in the lower chamber, inoculated with 5 × 105 cells each. The cells were collected after 48 h of culture for subsequent experiments.

## Flow cytometry
The co-culture system cells were collected and washed twice with ice-cold PBS to obtain a single cell suspension in 100 µl PBS and incubated with FITC Anti-CD206 (rabbit, 141703, 1:1000, Biolegend) with PE Anti-CD86 (rabbit, 105007, 1:1000, Biolegend) at 4 °C in the dark for 30 min according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The surface markers were then washed twice with PBS and resuspended in 300 µL PBS, and the average fluorescence intensity of each group of surface markers was analyzed on a flow cytometer.

## Animals and experiments
We purchased male Balb/c nude mice aged 4–6 weeks with initial weights between 20 and 23 g from Saiye Biotechnology Co. All animals were housed in individually ventilated cages at 23 °C with a 12-hour light/dark cycle and fed with a specific pathogen-free diet.
Nude mice were randomly divided into four groups (n = 5 per group): overexpression, knockdown, overexpression co-culture, and knockdown co-culture. According to the types of cells injected subcutaneously in nude mice, the overexpression group was divided into Control (CAL27), OE-NC (CAL27-OE-NC), and OE-RBP4 (CAL27-OE-RBP4) groups; the knockdown group, Control (CAL27), sh-NC (CAL27-sh-NC), and sh-RBP4 (CAL27-sh-RBP4) groups; and the knockdown co-culture group was divided into Control (CAL27), sh-NC (CAL27-sh-NC), and sh-RBP4 (CAL27-sh-RBP4) groups. Each group was injected with 2 × 106 cells. The overexpression co-culture fractions included four groups as follows: RAW264.7 + Control (CAL27 + RAW264.7), RAW264.7 + OE-NC (CAL27-OE-NC+RAW264.7), and RAW264.7 + OE-RBP4 (CAL27-OE-RBP4 + RAW264.7); knockdown co-culture fractions, RAW264.7 + Control (CAL27 + RAW264.7), RAW264.7 + sh-NC (CAL27-sh-NC+RAW264.7), and RAW264.7 + sh-RBP4 (CAL27-sh-RBP4 + RAW264.7). Each group of macrophages was injected 1:1 with TSCC cells, and a total of 2 × 106 cells were injected into the right axillary region of the nude mice.
The longest and shortest diameters of the tumors were measured every 7 days to calculate the volume of the tumor, and the weight of the nude mice was recorded. The mice were kept until day 28, then euthanized by carbon dioxide inhalation, and the tumor tissues were isolated.

## Immunohistochemical fluorescence assay
Nude mice transplanted with tumor tissues were washed with PBS, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 1 h, treated with 0.2% TritonX-100 (Sigma, St. Louis, MO) at 4 °C for 1 h, and closed with normal goat serum (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 1 h. The cells were stained with antibodies against CD86 (1:200, A21198, Abclonal) and CD 206 antibody (1:200, A21014, Abclonal). PE-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L and FITC-labeled goat anti-mouse IgG H&L were added, respectively, and then incubated for 2 h at 37 °C in the dark. Finally, the cell nuclei were stained with DAPI, observed under a fluorescence microscope, and photographed.

## Transcriptome sequencing
Transcriptome sequencing was performed by Genechem, based on the Illumina sequencing platform for transcriptome sequencing (RNA-Seq) of a recombinant human RBP4 (2 µg/ml, HY-P70471, MCE) protein-treated M0 macrophage cell model. A KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed using the R software “clusterProfiler” package after obtaining the differential genes based on gene expression analysis.

## Statistical analysis
All experiments were repeated at least three times. The data are summarized as mean±standard error of the mean and were statistically analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA). Differences between two groups were determined using Student’s t-test, while differences between multiple groups were assessed using one-way analysis of variance. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

The training set was divided into high-risk (n = 40) and low-risk (n = 39) groups according to median risk score. The Kaplan–Meier analysis showed that the low-risk group had a significantly longer survival (p < 0.05). The AUC of TIPS at 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years were 0.7, 0.704, and 0.739, respectively, indicating good predictive performance. The survival status of each sample, the distribution map of risk scores, and the expression levels of TICPGs in the training set are sequentially presented in the figure, indicating that TICPGs correlate with patient prognosis (Fig. 2c). To validate the prognostic value of this risk model across different cases, we performed TIPS assessments on both the test set and the entire TSCC cohort. Cases were categorized into high-risk and low-risk groups based on their respective optimal cutoff points. Analysis of KM curves revealed that TSCC samples in the low-risk group demonstrated significantly superior prognosis compared to the high-risk group, both in the test set and the entire cohort, with p < 0.05. ROC curves demonstrated that TIPS exhibited excellent prognostic predictive performance for both the test set (1-year AUC = 0.737, 3-year AUC = 0.64, 5-year AUC = 0.647; Fig. 2H) and the entire cohort (1-year AUC = 0.713, 3-year AUC = 0.677, 5-year AUC = 0.711; Fig. 2I) demonstrated excellent prognostic predictive performance. Furthermore, based on the survival status of each sample, the risk score distribution plot, and the TICPG expression heatmap, TICPGs were found to be closely associated with patient prognosis in both the test set and the entire cohort(Fig. 2d, e).
To validate the independent prognostic value of the TIPS risk score, univariate analysis showed that pathological stage, T, N, and TIPS were significantly associated with survival (Fig. 2f). The predictive efficacy (AUC) in TIPS was superior to that of conventional metrics at 1/3/5 years (Fig. 2g-i). Multifactorial analysis further identified N and TIPS as independent prognostic factors (Fig. 2j).

## RBP4 expression in TSCC
In TCGA-TSCC, the TIPS-related genes KLRK1 and LTB were not differentially expressed in tumor and paracancerous tissues, whereas RBP4 was significantly under-expressed (p < 0.001, Fig. 3a). RBP4 was significantly downregulated in GSE13601 and GSE31056. GSE78060 showed a decreasing trend (Fig. 3b). Concurrently, immunohistochemical staining analysis of human TSCC tissues revealed that 16 cases (86.7%) exhibited low RBP4 expression, while 4 cases (13.3%) showed high expression. In contrast, among adjacent non-cancerous tissues, 18 cases (96.7%) demonstrated high RBP4 expression, and 2 cases (3.3%) exhibited low expression. The difference was statistically significant (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3c, Table S5). To explore the effects of RBP4 on TSCC cell proliferation, invasion, and migration, we constructed TSCC cell lines overexpressing and knocking down RBP4, and validated the effects of knockdown and overexpression via qPCR and Western blotting. Results demonstrated that both RBP4 knockdown and overexpression were effective (Fig. 3d-g). By CCK-8 assay, we found that the proliferation ability of TSCC cells was reduced after overexpression of RBP4. And knockdown of RBP4 enhanced the proliferation ability of TSCC cells (Fig. 3h). Subcutaneous tumor formation experiments in nude mice also demonstrated that RBP4 overexpression inhibited tumor growth. In contrast, RBP4 knockdown promoted tumor growth (Fig. 3i). In addition, by scratch and Transwell assays, we found that the migration and invasion ability of TSCC cells was reduced after overexpression of RBP4. On the contrary after knocking down RBP4, the migration and invasion ability of TSCC cells was enhanced (Fig. 3j, k).

## PI3K/Akt pathway regulation
To explore the mechanism by which RBP4 inhibits TSCC cell proliferation, we first analyzed it using the GEO database, and the results suggested that the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway had a higher activity level in tumor tissues of the RBP4 low-expression group (Fig. 4a). Furthermore, analysis of the TCGA database using KEGG pathway mapping revealed significant activation of the PI3K pathway in cancer tissues (FigureS1a). The function `getpancordata` was used to calculate the Spearman correlation coefficients between RBP4 and PI3K across various cancer types. The pancorplot function was used to generate a pan-cancer correlation distribution plot, visually illustrating differences in gene pair correlations across cancer types (FigureS1b). Additionally, the sincorplot function was applied to generate a scatter plot for head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). This revealed a negative correlation between RBP4 gene expression and PI3K pathway activation in HNSCC tissues (FigureS1c). In addition, western blotting experiments suggested that the protein expression levels of p-PI3K, p-Akt, and p-mTOR were significantly decreased in TSCC cells overexpressing RBP4. The opposite result was observed in TSCC cells knocking down RBP4 (Fig. 4b). p-PI3K agonist 740 Y-P (0.2 mM) reversed the inhibitory effect of RBP4 overexpression on p-PI3K/p-Akt/p-mTOR and enhanced cell proliferation (Fig. 4c, d). To explore the mechanism by which changes in RBP4 levels affect TSCC cell migration and invasion, we determined changes in Snail expression in RBP4 overexpressing and RBP4 knockdown TSCC cells. The results suggested that RBP4 overexpression down-regulated Snail expression, up-regulated E-cadherin and inhibited N-cadherin/Vimentin; knockdown of RBP4 showed the opposite trend (Fig. 4e, f).PI3K agonist treatment reversed the effects of RBP4 overexpression on Snail/E-cadherin/N-cadherin /Vimentin regulation (Fig. 4g), while enhancing cell migration and invasion (Fig. 4h, i), suggesting that RBP4 suppresses the TSCC malignant phenotype by inhibiting the PI3K/Akt/mTOR-Snail axis.

## Macrophage M1-type polarization
To investigate the association between RBP4 expression levels and tumor-infiltrating macrophage phenotypes in TSCC tissues, we performed a correlation bioconfidence analysis using the GEO database, which demonstrated that elevated RBP4 expression promotes macrophage transformation to the M1 type (Fig. 5a, b). Immunohistochemical staining experiments also showed that the proportion of M1-type macrophages was significantly increased in tumor tissues with high RBP4 expression (Fig. 5c). In addition, by flow cytometry to detect macrophage phenotypes in the co-culture system of TSCC cells and macrophages, we found that the M2/M1 of macrophages co-cultured with RBP4 overexpressing TSCC cells was significantly decreased (p < 0.01). In contrast, macrophage M2/M1 was significantly elevated in RBP4 knockdown TSCC cells co-cultured with macrophages (Fig. 5d).

## TLR4/NF-κB pathway activation
To investigate the regulatory mechanism of RBP4 on macrophage polarization, human recombinant RBP4 protein was used to co-culture with THP1-derived M0 macrophages. Transcriptome sequencing showed that RBP4 activated the TLRs/NF-κB pathway (Fig. 6a) and that TLR4 and RBP4 expression were positively correlated (Spearman, Fig. 6b).Western blotting showed elevated expression of TLR4 and p-NF-κB in macrophages incorporating the human recombinant RBP4 protein (with no NF-κB changes) (Fig. 6c).QRT-PCR and Western blotting confirmed that RBP4 significantly up-regulated M1 markers (CD86, iNOS) and inhibited M2 markers (CD206, Arg-1) (Fig. 6d, e), whereas the TLR4 inhibitor TAK242 (0.2 Mm, HY-11109, MCE) reversed this effect ( Fig. 6c, e). Furthermore, in a co-culture system with TSCC cells overexpressing RBP4, macrophage TLR4/p-NF-κB levels were elevated; whereas co-culture with TSCC cells knocking down RBP4 resulted in decreased macrophage TLR4 and p-NF-kB levels (Fig. 6f, g).TAK242 treatment blocked RBP4 overexpression induced by TSCC cells in the macrophage TLR4/p-NF-κB upregulation and M1-type polarization (Fig. 6h, i), suggesting that RBP4 promotes macrophage transformation to the M1 phenotype through the TLR4/NF-κB signaling axis.

## TSCC tumor growth inhibition
To further demonstrate that the altered expression level of RBP4 in TSCC cells can promote macrophage M1 polarization through regulating the TLR-4/ NF-κB signaling pathway, and thus inhibit tumor progression, we co-injected CAL27 cells with macrophages in nude mice for subcutaneous culture. The results showed that the tumors in the RBP4 overexpression co-culture system grew slowly, and the immunofluorescence results showed that the number of M1 macrophages was the highest in the tumor tissues, and the number of M2 macrophages was lower. On the contrary, tumors in the RBP4 knockdown co-culture system grew rapidly, with the lowest number of M1 macrophages and the highest number of M2 macrophages (Fig. 7a, b). Meanwhile, western blotting analysis of tumor tissues showed that the expression levels of TLR4 and p-NF-kB were elevated in the RBP4 overexpression co-culture system, while the expression of NF-kB was not significantly changed. In the co-culture system with knockdown of RBP4, the expression levels of TLR4 and p-NF-kB were decreased, while the expression of NF-kB was not significantly altered (Fig. 7c).

## Discussion
In this study, we found that RBP4 overexpression reduced the phosphorylation of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway and inhibited tumor cell proliferation by regulating Snail levels; furthermore, RBP4 inhibited TSCC cells from undergoing EMT. Additionally, RBP4 promoted the activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway, leading to macrophage polarization toward M1 type and inhibiting TSCC growth. This is the first study to show that RBP4 interferes with the proliferation and migration of TSCC cells by inhibiting the activation of the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. RBP4 also promotes the M1-type polarization of TAMs, contributing to their oncogenic effects.
TSCC is the most common malignant tumor of the oral mucosa, with a high risk of metastasis and recurrence14. Despite continuous improvements in treatment, the 5-year survival rate in patients remains poor15. The role of the TME in TSCC has been revealed, and targeted treatments are likely to improve the prognosis in patients with TSCC. Here, we successfully constructed a TIPS for TSCC, which was validated in test and full TSCC sets. Among the three immune-related prognostic genes constituting the TIPS, RBP4 was identified as a TSCC TME immune-related prognostic gene.
RBP416, is a novel adipokine and host immune response protein candidate in OSCC17. RBP4 also has an impact on polycystic ovary syndrome, pancreatic islet resistance, and cardiovascular disease, and its level is significantly reduced in cancers such as epithelial ovarian cancers, HCC18, and colorectal cancer4. However, whether the level of RBP4 expression is abnormal in patients with TSCC has not been reported. We found that RBP4 was minimally expressed in TSCC tissues and further analyzed our results using the GEO dataset. The activation level of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway was higher in tumor tissues with low expression of RBP4. PI3K is a catalytically active intracellular signaling pathway that regulates the downstream Akt phosphorylation level, constituting the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. mTOR is a downstream protein in this pathway. mTOR is the downstream protein of the pathway, and the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway plays a crucial role in cell proliferation, growth, and survival and is activated in various cancers19. Using western blotting, we found that the expression of PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway-related proteins was upregulated in RBP4 knockdown TSCC cell lines and downregulated after RBP4 overexpression. In addition, CCK8 experiments showed that RBP4 overexpression inhibited TSCC cell proliferation, whereas knockdown of RBP4 promoted TSCC cell proliferation. Following interference with PI3K activators, the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway—inhibited by RBP4 overexpression—was reactivated. Furthermore, the activators were observed to reverse the proliferation suppression in TSCC cells caused by RBP4 overexpression. These results suggest that RBP4 inhibits TSCC proliferation by regulating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway.
EMT is a key biological process in tumor cell migration and invasion20. The molecular mechanism of EMT activation is complex21, and Snail22, a member of the zinc finger 1 family of transcription factors, is involved in the regulation of the EMT process. Snail is a downstream protein of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway23. Here, we found that in TSCC cells, the overexpression of RBP4 resulted in the downregulation of Snail expression, a decrease in N-cadherin and Vimentin expression, and an increase in E-cadherin expression. The knockdown of RBP4 resulted in the opposite effects. These results indicated that RBP4 negatively regulated Snail-mediated EMT. In addition, transwell and scratch assays of TSCC cells showed that overexpression of RBP4 inhibited TSCC cell migration and invasion, and knockdown of RBP4 promoted TSCC cell migration and invasion. The inhibitory effect of RBP4 overexpression on Snail protein was reversed after interference with the PI3K activator. Furthermore, the inhibition of TSCC cell migration and invasion caused by RBP4 overexpression was also reversed by the PI3K activator. These results suggest that the potential mechanism by which RBP4 inhibits TSCC migration and invasion may involve the inhibition of Snail protein expression downstream of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, which in turn regulates the EMT process.
Since RBP4 is a secreted protein, it can be secreted by tumor cells into the TME24. As the most abundant immune cells in the tumor microenvironment, macrophages can exert oncogenic and pro-oncogenic effects through different polarization phenotypes25. However, the role of RBP4 in the polarization has not yet been reported. In the present study, bioinformatics analysis revealed that RBP4 was associated with the transformation of M1-type macrophages. Immunohistochemical results also suggested that M1 macrophage infiltration was higher in the pathological tissues of TSCC patients with high RBP4 expression. Moreover, after using the human recombinant protein RBP4 to stimulate THP-1 cell-induced M0 macrophages, we detected M1 and M2 macrophage markers using western blotting and found that RBP4 could promote macrophage M1 polarization. Meanwhile, after co-culture RBP4 overexpressing, RBP4 knockdown, and in normal TSCC cells with THP-1-induced M0 macrophages, we detected the changes in macrophage phenotype using flow cytometry and confirmed that TSCC-secreted RBP4 promoted M1 polarization of TAMs. Related in vivo experiments also showed that overexpression of RBP4 in TSCC cells promoted the conversion of TAMs to the M1 type and inhibited tumor growth.
Macrophage polarization is affected by a variety of signaling pathways, among which activation of the nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) signaling pathway is an important factor in inducing the polarization of TAMs to M1-type macrophages11. TLR4, an innate immune receptor expressed on the surface of macrophages, can exert a pro-inflammatory effect through activation of NF-κB, thereby inducing macrophage polarization to pro-inflammatory M1 type26. We examined the changes in the expression of TLR4, p-NF-κB, and M1/M2 surface-specific proteins in each group of macrophages using western blotting assay, and found that the human recombinant protein RBP4 promoted the expression of TLR4 and p-NF-κB in macrophages, while RBP4 led to an increase in M1-type macrophages and a decrease in M2-type macrophages. These changes were reversed by the TLR4 inhibitors. Subsequently, in further western blotting experiments, we found that the expression of TLR4 and p-NF-κB was increased in macrophages co-cultured with RBP4 overexpressing TSCC cells, whereas the expression of TLR4 and p-NF-κB was decreased in macrophages co-cultured with RBP4-knockdown TSCC cells. After disruption of the overexpression of the RBP4 co-culture system using a TLR4 inhibitor, the expression of TLR4 and p-NF-KB proteins was suppressed, and effect of RBP4 on macrophage M1-type polarization reversed by the TLR4 inhibitor, suggesting that tumor-secreted RBP4 may modulate the polarization of TAMs. In in vivo experiments, we observed that activation of the TLR4/NF-κB pathway promoted macrophage transformation to the M1 type, which exerted an anticancer immune effect. Thus, our study provides preliminary evidence that RBP4 affects macrophage phenotypic changes through the TLR4/NF-κB pathway (Fig. 7d).
However, this study has not fully elucidated the mechanism of action between RBP4 and TLR4, failing to demonstrate whether RBP4 directly binds to TLR4 or exerts its effects by indirectly influencing TLR4 expression levels. Furthermore, the mode of RBP4 transfer between tumor cells and macrophages remains unexplored; it may be transported via exosomes or influence macrophages through paracrine signaling. Finally, while RBP4 inhibits PI3K pathway activation in tumor cells, its ability to suppress this pathway in macrophages requires further validation. This is because some studies suggest the PI3K pathway may activate NF-κB27, whereas our findings indicate RBP4 conversely activates NF-κB. In summary, this study represents a preliminary observation that RBP4 plays a role in the mutual influence between tumor cells and macrophages. However, further investigation is required to elucidate its underlying mechanisms.
This is the first study to identify RBP4 as an immune-related prognostic gene in the TSCC tumor microenvironment with significantly reduced expression in TSCC. In addition, RBP4 affects TSCC cell proliferation by negatively regulating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway and is involved in the Snail factor-regulated EMT process, which reduces TSCC cell migration and invasion. This study preliminarily demonstrates that RBP4 secreted by tumor cells inhibits TSCC progression by promoting macrophage M1 polarization through positive regulation of the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway in TAMs.
