# Investigation of slug catcher vessel degradation by hydrogen sulfide (H2S) damage

## Abstract
AbstractSlug catchers offer effectively multiphase flow management in the field of oil and gas industry. critical equipment required for isolates and confines liquid, gases, and solid particles in slugs under environmental conditions that observed to incoming production stream. This work examines the case study that undergoes corrosion failure in vessels of slug catcher, concentrating on (H2S) damage, hardness, and microscopic corrosion in different locations. The methodology of this study involved visual inspections, hardness, and microscopic testing in order to evaluate the level of nature corrosion. The results refer that while the failure of corrosion pits present, there were not cracks, microcracks, stress corrosion cracking (SCC), or Hydrogen Induced Cracking (HIC) recognized in the structural of vessel. The structures of ferrite and pearlite of the HIC were presented in main components, and there are no cracks or microcracks detected.

## Introduction
A wet H2S environment causes carbon steel to blister and crack, in pipes and pressure vessels, hydrogen blistering appears as a bulge on the inner diameter, outer diameter, or inside the wall thickness. Additionally, blisters can be formed when hydrogen atoms diffuse into steel during the sulfide corrosion process. As hydrogen atoms combine, they form hydrogen molecules that are too dense to diffuse. The pressure builds up, causing local deformation and blistering. Corrosion produces hydrogen that causes blistering. The hydrogen that causes blistering is produced by corrosion, not by the hydrogen present in the process stream [1]. Stepwise cracks connect hydrogen blisters on the metal's surface. External stress is not required for HIC formation. Due to internal pressure, hydrogen blisters crack due to high stress within their circumference. Steel blisters occur in different planes when these high-stress fields interact, causing cracks to form [2]. Metal cracking due to hydrogen sulfide and tensile stress is described as sulfide stress cracking (SSC). Hydrogen stress cracking results from the absorption of atomic hydrogen by sulfide corrosion. The high hardness zones in the weld metal and heat-affected zones can initiate SSC on the metal surface of the steel. There can be zones of high hardness in attachment welds and weld cover passes that aren't tempered (softened) by subsequent passes. Steel is more susceptible to SSC when treated with PWHT. SSC can also affect steels used in refining. The residual elements in heat-affected zones can prevent some carbon steels from tempering at stress-relief temperatures. Preheating minimizes these hardness problems. H2S damage, which primarily affects low alloy and carbon steels, cannot occur on stainless steel. Hydrogen permeation and absorption in steels are the main mechanisms of wet H2S damage. The environment (pH, H2S level, contaminants, temperature) and material properties (hardness, microstructure, strength significantly identify wet H2S damage [3].
Engineering materials fail due to stress-corrosion cracking (SCC). Corrosion reactions and mechanical stress are combined and synergistic to cause crack propagation. As with many simplifications, this definition does not identify the boundaries of the subject. For a detailed discussion of this problem, it is essential to clearly define the type of loading involved, the type of materials involved, the environment causing this type of crack propagation, and the nature of the interactions [4,5].
Modern manufacturing relies heavily on stainless steel. It has been widely used in corrosive environments as a reliable substitute for carbon steel in various of industries. Stainless steel has long been considered an expensive, high-tech alloy. Due to advancements in manufacturing and fabrication techniques, stainless-steel components can be produced on a large scale. Stainless steel is widely used due to all its benefits, as well as its ability to withstand harsh environments and the cost of corrosion [6,7].
An aqueous environment, such as condensed moisture layers or bulk solutions, can cause SCC. Stainless steels and aluminum alloys crack when chloride ions are present. SCC might not occur in some alloys if the environment causes it in others. Changing the temperature, aeration level, and/or flow rate of SCCs can fail in environments with high concentrations of ionic species [[8], [9], [10]]. There are many studies on the corrosion behavior of carbon steel in H2S environments, Tang et al., 2020 [11], explored the effect of H2S concentration on the corrosion of carbon steel at elevated temperatures. It studies comprehensions into how various extents of H2S can affect the damage of carbon steel. Qi et al., 2014 [12], examined how carbon steel corrodes in H2S environments and focused on the effect of temperature. Wei et al., 2016 [13] investigated the corrosion of carbon steel in CO2 and H2S environments, demonstrating how even a slight of H2S can markedly influence the rate of corrosion. In damage of the vessel, finite element analysis can be used to simulate the concentration of stresses and strains, considering the influence of corrosion and H2S caused by material damage [14]. Wang et al., 2022 [15] explored the corrosion behavior in CO2/H2S/Cl environments for steel under high pressure. Ye et al., 2022 [16] examined the corrosion behavior of carbon steel in CO2 and H2S environments, it offers practical results for the gas and oil factories, where mixed CO2 and H2S interactions are affected by complexity damage in industries. Liu et al., 2022 [17] focused on the corrosion behavior in saturated solutions of H2S low alloy steel. Zhang et al., 2022 [18] discussed the effect of fluid flow and existing H2S on corrosion of carbon steel. Sun et al., 2023 [19] studied the effect of H2S on the corrosion in supercritical CO2 of N80 steel. Talukdar et al., 2023 [20] in vestigated the effect of acetic acid and H2S on CO2 corrosion of carbon steel at elevated temperatures. These researches mutually strengthen our awareness of the corrosion of materials in H2S environments. Despite extensive ]studies on the corrosion of the effect of H2S and CO2 in carbon steel, there is a notable lack of studies on the effects of H2S. The limitations and possible sources of uncertainty for predicting the remaining life of a slug catcher vessel, when dealing with H2S damage; involve several factors because of the intricate effect of corrosion under different conditions, where H2S can cause localized corrosion like SSC or HIC, which are caused by difficult prediction due to many factors; (microstructural, stress concentration, environment), fluid dynamics within the vessel also causes variations in corrosion rates.
Specifically, an investigation of failures of slug catcher vessels is presented in this paper, as well as leaks of hydrogen sulfide (H2S). ## Methodology
The vessel 95-V-10 was not designed and constructed to be NACE MR0175/ISO 15156-2 compliant. The assessment of the slug catcher vessel and material certification discovered that the risk of non-compliance was related to H2S stress cracking degradation mechanisms. The lack of NACE design compatibility for use in a containing environment required qualification of the materials (and welds) for H2S service.

## Experimental design matrix
A detailed experimental design was developed to assess the qualification of materials for H2S service. The design matrix included the following factors:
H₂S Concentration: Three levels (Low: 10 ppm, Medium: 50 ppm, High: 100 ppm)
Temperature: Two levels (Room Temperature: 25 °C, Elevated Temperature: 60 °C)
Flow Velocity: Two levels (Low Flow: 0.5 m/s, High Flow: 1.5 m/s)
Each experimental run was conducted based on a full factorial design, resulting in a total of 12 unique experimental combinations. Three repetitions were performed for each combination to ensure statistical reliability, leading to 36 total experimental runs.

## Inspection and testing Protocols
A detailed written scheme of examination was developed for an on-stream non-intrusive inspection scope, specifying extensive automated ultrasonic wall thickness mapping on potential liquid-wetted parts of weld categories and joints of the vessel. This assessment depended on how quickly the metal was passivated after film rupture compared to how fast the metal dissolved in the solution.
The internal inspection plan and NDT methods used to qualify the materials included:
Wet Fluorescent Magnetic Particle Testing: Applied for longitudinal and circumferential welds, including the heat-affected zone (HAZ).
Hardness Testing: Conducted for the shell, longitudinal and circumferential welds (including, where possible, in the HAZ of the weld), as well as hardness indentations on the weld of one nozzle attachment.

## Microstructure verification
A replica test was carried out to verify the microstructure of the material. This was accomplished by polishing the surface to the desired level, and a replica of the microstructure was taken at the locations of the shell, the circumferential weld, the long seam welds, and on the weld of one nozzle attachment.
Hardness testing was conducted to provide Vickers HV 10 or HV equivalence. The ultrasonic contact impedance method was used to measure microhardness, and hardness devices were calibrated against standard hardness testing, which used a polished sample to provide a more accurate assessment of the actual hardness.

## Environmental control
H₂S Concentration Control: The levels of H₂S concentration in the test environment were controlled using precise gas mixture systems or by exposing the samples to known concentrations of H₂S gas.
pH Control: Buffer solutions or pH adjustment methods were employed to maintain consistent pH levels throughout the experimental duration.
Temperature Control: The temperature of the test environment was controlled using thermostatic chambers or controlled heating systems.
Flow Velocity Control: Flow velocity was managed using flow meters and adjustable pumping systems, allowing for the simulation of different flow conditions relevant to the slug catcher vessel's operation.

## Results and discussion

## Comprehensive internal inspection and NDT analysis of slug catcher vessel

## Inspection results of the on-stream non-intrusive have been completed
The primary containment wall thickness was assessed, and over 35 % of the vessel's cylindrical shell was covered by ultrasonic wall thickness scanning. The inspection focused on the bottom half and therefore covered 80 % of the length. Additional scans were made to provide coverage of the heads. This coverage provided highly reliable insight into the presence and nature of aqueous sour corrosion. In general, minimum isolated pitting was observed on the bottom section, not more than 2 mm deep, with an aspect ratio of around 10. Only one area of localized thinning was observed exceeding the design corrosion allowance of 3 mm. The localized thinning measured 3.68 mm with a width and length of 35 × 35 mm2. The actual wall thickness was measured at 30.5 mm against the design thickness of 30.0 mm. Hence, at the location of maximum corrosion, the actual minimum wall thickness of 26.82 mm only marginally exceeded the design corrosion allowance of 3 mm.

## Corrosion mitigation method in the bottom during the visual inspections
Wet fluorescent magnetic particle testing was completed for longitudinal and circumferential welds, including the heat-affected zone (HAZ). The results showed that there was no evidence of cracks in the inspected locations.

## Corrosion mitigation method in the bottom during the visual inspections
Mitigation action was carried out on the corroded area; internal coating repair with coating filler metal was applied to the observed corroded area. Fig. 6 shows the corrosion mitigation after the coating applied. The corroded surface was blasted with grit blast, and the required cleanliness and roughness were achieved (average 90 μm). The BELZONA coating system was applied to the corroded area at the vessel bottom, and all the required inspections based on the coating manufacturer's data sheet were followed, including the pinhole inspection.

## Conclusion
Based on the first stage of inspection results, the main experienced mode of degradation has been aqueous sour corrosion resulting in localized pitting. Due to lake of document information to confirm that the vessel materials can be used in a sour H2S environment, an intrusive inspection (internal) plan has been developed to carry out during the planned shutdown. The main purpose of this inspection stage is to carry out the required inspection to confirm and qualify the materials are comply with NACE MR0175 (Materials qualification to use in H2S service).
The maximum material hardness for weld and base metal shall not exceed 250 HV (Vickers hardness). The NDT methods that have been used were nondestructive which is no need to take the sample from the vessel metal and send it to the lap for inspection, this has been reduced the cost of the inspection in case of the sample of metal was require to cut from the vessel and send to lap. Therefore, these inspections methods had high economic and satisfactory output results.
Application of the internal coating is very important to keep the corrosion rate low increase the equipment life and reduce the cost of unplanned shutdown to fix the failure. It is very important to provide materials that are resistant to the Wet H2S service when the equipment planned to be used in H2S service to reduce the risk of environmental cracks that can lead to cardiotropic failure. All weld categories are free from linear flaws.
