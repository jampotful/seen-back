HIGHLIGHT

36

Pollen tube growth requires tight control of apical wall expansion. We present evidence for a

37

mechanosensitive ion channel, MSL8, as a braking signal in growth dynamics through cell wall

38

regulation.

ABSTRACT

41

The male gametophyte in flowering plants, pollen, both performs the critical role of fertilization

42

and represents a unique and accessible system for interrogating plant cell mechanics. Pollen

43

endures multiple mechanical hurdles during its lifecycle: desiccation in the anther, rapid

44

rehydration on the stigma, and germination to produce a rapidly growing pollen tube that will

45

eventually reach the ovule. A key component in this robust mechanical system is MscS-Like 8

46

(MSL8), a mechanosensitive ion channel. We previously proposed that that MSL8 serves as an

47

“osmotic safety valve”, regulating pressure in the germinating pollen tube by releasing anions in

48

response to plasma membrane tension, thereby preventing pollen tube rupture. However, we

49

subsequently identified defects in the cell walls of msl8 mutant pollen, suggesting that it plays a

50

role independent of osmoregulation, a conclusion also supported by mathematical modeling.

51

Here, we show that pollen tubes lacking MSL8 channel function by genetic knockout or channel-

52

blocking point mutation lose major growth pauses, have altered pectin esterification patterns,

53

and are sensitive to pectin crosslinking. Together, these data suggest a mechanism whereby

54

tension-gated ion release through mechanosensitive channels regulates apoplastic function and

55

cell wall dynamics.

INTRODUCTION

74

Pollen is the male gametophyte of flowering plants. Successful pollination is required for the

75

production of all seed and fruit crops we reap from angiosperms, as well as for the propagation

76

of the next generation (Johnson et al., 2019). During the process of pollination, pollen grains

77

undergo a drastic mechanical journey: they develop and fully desiccate in the anther, then travel

78

to the female pistil where they rapidly rehydrate. After rehydration, the pollen grain germinates a

79

pollen tube, one of the fastest growing cells in the plant kingdom (Shamsudhin et al., 2016). The

80

tube then travels to the ovule to deliver the sperm cells to the egg cells for fertilization. This

81

sequence of events requires tight regulation of pollen grain and tube development and growth,

82

which must be additionally coordinated with the perception of, and adaptation to, huge changes

83

in osmotic conditions and substantial mechanical constraints (Williams et al., 2016; Cameron

84

and Geitmann, 2018; Reimann et al., 2020).

85
86

Unlike most plant cells, pollen tubes are tip-growing, meaning that they elongate by cell wall

87

deposition at the cell apex rather than across the cell length as in anisotropic expansion (Hepler

88

et al., 2001; Winship et al., 2021). Controlled tip-growth requires a balance between increased

89

cell volume and the delivery and incorporation of new cell wall material. Cell expansion and cell

90

wall deposition are coordinated by numerous intracellular factors, including ion fluxes,

91

cytoskeleton dynamics, the accumulation of reactive oxidative species, changes in turgor, and

92

multiple signaling pathways (Johnson et al., 2019). Imbalance between expansion and cell wall

93

deposition can result in a loss of cell integrity or a lack of growth (Johnson et al., 2019).

94
95

Pollen tubes can grow continuously or in a pulsatile manner. Their rate of growth varies widely

96

both between and within a species, with rates ranging from 100-600 nm/s in lily (Cárdenas et

97

al., 2008), 10-50 nm/s in tobacco (Michard et al., 2008), and 30-150 nm/s in Arabidopsis

98

thaliana (Rounds et al., 2011; Damineli et al., 2017). The robustness of growth oscillations also

99

varies: lily pollen exhibits highly periodic elongation in 20-50 second cycles (Cárdenas et al.,

100

2008), while tobacco pollen has ~80 second periods (Michard et al., 2008). Pollen tubes from A.

101

thaliana show relatively less uniform oscillatory characteristics, with reports ranging from ~30-90

102

second cycles, ambiguous oscillations, or steady growth rates (Rounds et al., 2011; Damineli et

103

al., 2017).

105

The internal expansive force that drives the pollen tube tip forward has long been attributed to

106

turgor. However, turgor does not appear to be responsible for growth oscillations. Multiple

107

studies have suggested that turgor pressure remains constant in growing pollen tubes, even

108

those exhibiting pulsatile behavior. This has led to the hypothesis that cyclic changes in the cell

109

wall, either through deposition or modification of cell wall components, modulate the pulsatile

110

growth dynamics of pollen tubes (Benkert et al., 1997; Zerzour et al., 2009; Winship et al., 2010,

111

2021; Rojas et al., 2011; Van Hemelryck et al., 2018; Dumais, 2021).

112
113

The wall composition of pollen tubes differs from most plant tissues in several respects. Callose

114

is a significant component of pollen tube walls, where it is found along the tube shank and

115

making up thick plugs that segment the growing pollen tube, keeping cytoplasmic space

116

compacted towards the tip (Parre and Geitmann, 2005; Chebli et al., 2012). In addition,

117

in pollen tubes the deposition, modification, and crosslinking of pectin, rather than the

118

orientation of cellulose microfibrils, stabilizes and directs growth (Hepler et al., 2001; Aouar et

119

al., 2010).

120
121

Pectins are a large class of diverse cell wall polysaccharides (Wolf et al., 2009; Harholt et al.,

122

2010). The most common pectin found in pollen tubes is homogalacturonan. This ɑ-1,4-linked

123

galacturonic acid homopolymer is synthesized in the Golgi with a high degree of methyl-

124

esterification. The degree of homogalacturonan esterification directly impacts the extent to

125

which that homogalacturonan polymer is integrated and crosslinked with other polymers in the

126

cell wall. The highly esterified homogalacturonan delivered in vesicles to the tube apex has a

127

low capacity for crosslinking, making a more extensible wall (Giovane et al., 2004; Bosch and

128

Hepler, 2005; Guo et al., 2022).

129
130

Pectin-modifying proteins, such as pectin methylesterases (PMEs) and pectin methylesterase

131

inhibitors (PMEIs), are co-delivered in vesicles with esterified homogalacturonan. When active,

132

PMEs cleave the methyl group of homogalacturonan, leaving negatively charged, de-esterified

133

homogalacturonan. Calcium ions in the wall space complex with these negatively charged

134

pectins, crosslinking them into an “egg box” configuration, and thereby stiffening the pectic gel

135

matrix, and making the wall less extensible (Bosch et al., 2005; Bosch and Hepler, 2006; Leroux

136

et al., 2015).

This enzymatic conversion from esterified homogalacturonan to de-esterified, cross-linkable

139

homogalacturonan is spatially regulated to maintain an extensible tube tip and rigidified tube

140

shank. While PMEs appear to localize throughout the pollen tube wall, their activity is tightly

141

regulated, with lower activity at the tube apex (Bosch et al., 2005; Röckel et al., 2008). For

142

example, changes in cell wall microenvironment, particularly pH and ionic strength, can cause

143

PMEs to conjugate with the cell wall or PMEIs and restrict their catalytic activity (Bosch and

144

Hepler, 2005; Bonavita et al., 2016).

145
146

Mechanosensitive (MS) ion channels also play an important role in pollen. It is well-established

147

that, by opening a pore in response to lateral membrane tension, MS channels can function as

148

osmotic regulators (by allowing ions and osmolytes to exit the cell) and as signal transducers

149

(by allowing calcium to enter the cell) (Peyronnet et al., 2014). Two Arabidopsis MS ion

150

channels, MscS-Like 8 (MSL8) and close homolog MSL7, are expressed in pollen grains and

151

pollen tubes. Loss of MSL8 results in increased rates of pollen grain germination and bursting,

152

with altered callose deposition in germinating grains (Hamilton et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2022).

153

A bona fide mechanosensitive channel, MSL8 shows a slight preference for anions (Hamilton et

154

al., 2015; Hamilton and Haswell, 2017). Single amino acid mutations are sufficient to disrupt

155

MSL8 function, particularly F720L, which blocks channel conductance (Hamilton and Haswell,

156

2017). MSL8F720L mutants showed similar phenotypes as a msl8 knockout, indicating that

157

channel activity is required for proper function (Hamilton et al., 2015; Hamilton and Haswell,

158

2017). No phenotypes have yet been shown in plants lacking MSL7.

159
160

While osmoregulation is a core property of MscS-like proteins across systems (Hurst et al.,

161

2008), and we previously proposed that MSL8 may function as an osmotic safety valve

162

(Hamilton et al., 2015), recent evidence suggests that MSL8 may play a role beyond or

163

independent of simple osmoregulation. Germinating msl8 mutant pollen grains have altered cell

164

wall deposition, with increased callose at the germination site and a non-uniform distribution of

165

cell wall material in the germination plaque (Wang et al., 2022). Overexpressing MSL8 leads to

166

excess callose deposition in the periphery of ungerminated grains, possibly as part of a genetic

167

interaction between MSL8 and genes that encode components of the cell wall integrity (CWI)

168

pathway (Wang et al., 2022).

169
170

Finally, mathematical modeling of pollen germination also suggested that MSL8 may have

171

functions in addition to or instead of osmoregulation. During rehydration, msl8 mutant pollen

grains swell and deform to a greater extent than wildtype, which plateaus in volume (Miller et al.,

173

2022). Mathematical modeling can simulate this process with contributions of various factors,

174

such as MSL8 tension-gated osmoregulation, turgor, and cell wall deformation. Kinetic models

175

best support a scenario where cell wall modification leads to the observed changes in hydration

176

dynamics, rather than simple osmoregulation (Miller et al., 2022).

177
178

Here, we build on these data to investigate the relationship between MSL8 ion channel function,

179

pollen tube growth, and cell wall composition. These experiments serve to further test the

180

hypothesis that MS ion channels are capable of altering cell wall extensibility as well as

181

osmoregulation and intracellular signaling.

METHODS

185
186

Plant growth. All lines were in the Col-0 Arabidopsis thaliana ecotype and validated in previous

187

publications (Hamilton et al., 2015; Hamilton and Haswell, 2017; Wang et al., 2022; Miller et al.,

188

2022). Seeds were sown directly on hydrated Berger BM2 growth medium and stratified for 24

189

hours at 4°C. Flats were then moved to a Conviron MTPS120 growth chamber and grown in

190

long day conditions (16h light, 8h dark) at 21°C, 175 µm light, and 50% relative humidity. Plants

191

began to bolt ~5 weeks after sowing, with pollen collected 5-10 days after bolting.

192
193

In vitro pollen germination. For each genotype, 20-40 freshly opened flowers were collected

194

into 1.7 ml Eppendorf tubes containing 750 µl of liquid pollen growth medium (LPGM, 18%

195

sucrose, 2 mM CaCl2, 2 mM Ca(NO3) 2, 0.49 mM H3BO3, 1 mM MgSO4, 1 mM KCl, pH’d to 7.05

196

with KOH (Bou Daher et al., 2009; Wang et al., 2022)). Tubes were vortexed at maximum speed

197

for 1 min to release pollen, then centrifuged at 10,000xg for 4 min to pellet pollen. Forceps were

198

used to remove excess floral debris and the supernatant was carefully removed by pipette.

199
200

For germination and growth in Eppendorf tubes, the pollen pellet was resuspended in 200 µl

201

LPGM and tubes transferred to a petri dish humidified with a wet Kimwipe. For germination and

202

growth on microwell dishes, coverslip bottom microwell dishes (MatTek Corp, no. P35G-1.5-14-

203

C) were coated with 150 µl of poly(ethyleneimine) solution (1:30 in ddH2O) for 10 min, washed

204

twice with 200 µl ddH2O, and coated with 200 µl of LPGM. Following the pollen centrifugation

205

described above, the pollen pellet was resuspended in 20 µl LPGM. The 200 µl LPGM was

206

removed from the dish and the resuspended pollen added with a cut pipette tip. The pollen was

207

left for 1 min to adhere to the plate, then 200 µl fresh LPGM was added carefully by pipette.

208

Microwell dishes were placed in petri dishes humidified with a wet Kimwipe in an incubator at

209

23°C.

Pollen tube growth dynamic assays and analysis. Pollen was germinated in microwell

212

dishes as described above and grown for 4 hours before imaging on an Olympus IX73

213

microscope equipped with a Hamamatsu C11440 digital camera. Brightfield images were

214

captured with a 10x / NA0.3 objective at 10 second intervals over a 40-minute period.

215

Kymograph analysis was carried out in FIJI, using a segmented line with width = 1 drawn down

216

the center longitudinal axis of the pollen tube, and processed with the Multi Kymograph function.

RESULTS

309
310

MSL8 channel function is required for pulsatile growth dynamics.

311

To explore the effects of MSL8 channel function on pollen tube growth, we leveraged a suite of

312

genetic reagents including functional MSL8 (WT), a msl8 CRISPR knockout mutant (msl8-5,

313

(Wang et al., 2022)), a msl8 mutant complemented with natively expressed functional MSL8

314

(msl8-5 + pMSL8::MSL8, (Miller et al., 2022)) and a msl8 mutant complemented with natively

315

expressed non-functional MSL8 (msl8-5 + pMSL8::MSL8F720L,(Hamilton and Haswell, 2017;

316

Miller et al., 2022)) (Figure 1A, left panels). Pollen tubes were germinated and grown in

317

microwell dishes for 3 hours before time-lapse imaging every 10 s for 40 min. From each time-

318

lapse series we created kymographs of individual tubes elongating (Figure 1A, right panels).

319
320

We observed wildtype A. thaliana pollen tubes with both pulsatile and linear elongation patterns,

321

with most tubes (~75%) having some degree of pulsatile behavior and a minority (~25%)

322

elongating continuously (Figure 1B-C, Supplemental Figure 1). In msl8-5 mutants, however,

323

nearly all pollen tubes elongated continuously without major growth pauses (Figure 1B-C,

324

Supplemental Figure 1). We observed this loss of pulsatile growth in two other msl8 mutant

325

lines, while single msl7 mutants retained pulsatile growth patterns similar to wildtype

326

(Supplemental Figure 1). Functional complementation of msl8-5 with pMSL8::MSL8 restored

327

pulsatile growth in three independent lines, while three independent lines of the channel-blocked

328

MSL8F720L failed to restore pulsatile growth to msl8-5 mutant pollen tubes (Figure 1B-C,

329

Supplemental Figure 1).

330
331

To quantify changes in growth rate, we further analyzed our kymograph results using the

332

CHUKKNORRIS R scripts (Damineli et al., 2017). Representative growth rate traces of wildtype

333

and msl8-5 pollen tubes are depicted in Figure 1C, upper and lower panels respectively. The

334

average instantaneous maximum growth rate across all genotypes was not significantly different

335

between genotypes (Figure 1D, left panel). However, pollen tubes lacking MSL8 channel

336

function (both msl8-5 and msl8-5 + pMSL8::MSL8F720L) had a higher average instantaneous

337

minimum rate than wildtype or functionally complemented lines (Figure 1F, right panel). These

338

data provide an explanation for our previous observation that msl8 pollen tubes grow longer

DISCUSSION

509
510

There is substantial correlative data suggesting that the pattern of cell wall deposition and

511

homogalacturonan esterification contributes to pollen tube growth dynamics (Bosch et al., 2005;

512

Tian et al., 2006; McKenna et al., 2009; Winship et al., 2010; Rojas et al., 2011). Here, we add

513

to these data and introduce the concept of a MS channel influencing these processes by

514

releasing ions to the extracellular space in a membrane tension-dependent manner. Plant MS

515

channels have clearly defined roles in osmoregulation and internal signaling (Basu and Haswell,

516

2017), but this is the first time that a function for ion flux into the apoplast by plant MS ion

517

channels has been proposed. We found that without MSL8 channel function, pollen tubes lose

518

major growth pauses and have altered homogalacturonan accumulation and esterification at the

519

tube apex. These data support a model whereby MSL8-mediated ion flux modulates pectin

520

extensibility, and thereby elongation dynamics, in the pollen tube apex. This model is described

521

in more detail below.

522
523

Overview of Working Model

524

We propose that MSL8 is activated in an oscillatory manner during tube elongation. Deformation

525

of the plasma membrane during cell expansion could provide the increase in membrane tension

526

needed to open MSL8 and possibly other MS channels (Dutta and Robinson, 2004; Hepler et

527

al., 2013). According to this model, ion flux through MSL8 reduces local cell wall extensibility,

528

which acts as a brake to reduce elongation and initiate a growth pause. The MSL8 braking

529

signal—a membrane tension-induced change in the apoplastic ionome—could promote PME

530

activity or increase pectin crosslinking in order to stiffen the local cell wall.

531
532

As mentioned above, it is hypothesized that cell wall deposition or modification rather than

533

turgor fluctuations underlie pulsatile pollen tube growth (Benkert et al., 1997; Zerzour et al.,

534

2009; Winship et al., 2010, 2021; Rojas et al., 2011; Van Hemelryck et al., 2018; Dumais,

535

2021). Our results support this hypothesis and position a mechanosensitive ion channel as a

536

possible contributor to oscillatory regulation (Figure 1). Notably, pulsatile behavior was not

537

restored in msl8 pollen tubes provided with osmotic support, indicating that osmotic control does

538

not underlie growth dynamics (Figure 2).

539
540

was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. It is made

542

Ion flux through MSL8 is required for its function in oscillatory growth of pollen tubes.

543

MSL8F720L mutant channels, which disrupt ion flux but not channel localization or assembly

544

(Hamilton and Haswell, 2017) do not complement loss of pulsatile dynamics and the altered

545

pectin deposition of msl8 mutants. (Figure 1A, Figure 3, Figure 5A), suggesting that it is ion

546

flux through the MSL8 pore that is the key function of MSL8 in this context. However, we cannot

547

rule out the alternative explanation that the F720L lesion not only blocks the channel pore, but

548

also prevents it from adopting its open conformation, thereby disrupting a normal interaction

549

with a putative protein signaling partner. While the exact ions passed through MSL8 in the

550

pollen tube apex is not known, it is anion-preferring and Cl- and NO3- are likely candidates.

551
552

Anion efflux as a mechanism of pulsatile growth control

553

The plant cell wall acts as an ion exchanger between itself and free ions in the apoplastic space,

554

with the wall consisting mainly of negative charges (Shomer et al., 2003). For example, H+ efflux

555

into the wall is key to the acid growth theory, where acidification of the cell wall results in

556

polymer loosening and extension. However, anions have largely been ignored as potential

557

regulators of cell wall mechanics. While anion efflux at the tube tip appears to be part of the

558

pollen tube growth cycle (Zonia et al., 2002), potential roles in the apoplast were not discussed.

559

In one study, chloride treatment of maize leaves resulted in transient alkalinization of the

560

apoplast, which in turn remodeled the apoplastic proteome and stiffened the cell wall (Geilfus et

561

al., 2017), suggesting a possible role for anions in cell wall mechanics.

562
563

Apoplastic microenvironment chemistry is one area of interest when considering how MSL8-

564

mediated anion efflux might impact pollen tube growth dynamics and pectin status. PMEs are

565

ionically bound to the negatively charged cell wall matrix, and their association and function can

566

be modulated by cation displacement and pH levels (Moustacas et al., 1991; Bordenave and

567

Goldberg, 1994; Bosch and Hepler, 2005). Furthermore, PMEI interaction with PMEs has also

568

been shown to be pH sensitive (Bonavita et al., 2016). While these studies used tissues and

569

systems other than A. thaliana pollen, it is interesting to consider how an anion efflux could

570

locally shift the apoplast electrostatic chemistry in similar but opposing manner, possibly

571

complexing metal cations and reducing their impact on wall bound proteins. Misregulation of

572

PME activity could explain the excess esterified homogalacturonan we observe in tubes lacking

573

MSL8 channel function (Figure 5A-B) but does not account for the presence of de-esterified

574

homogalacturonan (Figure 5C-D).

Pectin esterification and crosslinking status as a means of pulsatile growth control

577

Our observations that tubes lacking MSL8 channel function elongate continuously (Figure 1A,

578

Supplemental Figure 1) and have higher levels of esterified homogalacturonan at the tube

579

apex than the WT (Figure 5A-B, Supplemental Figure 3A) are consistent with reports that

580

esterified homogalacturonan is more extensible than de-esterified homogalacturonan (McKenna

581

et al., 2009; Vogler et al., 2015). Together, the observed increase in LM20 signal and the

582

atypical distribution of LM19 signal in msl8 knock-out and channel-blocked mutant pollen

583

indicate that MSL8 channel function is required for normal pollen tube pectin patterning.

584
585

The presence of de-esterified homogalacturonan at the apex of msl8 mutant pollen tubes could

586

contradict our model. De-esterified homogalacturonan may be present, but not crosslinked into

587

a stiffened matrix that can be detected with our method. Rojas et al (Rojas et al., 2011)

588

proposed that chemical relaxation of the wall simultaneously disrupts existing pectic networks

589

while forming new crosslinks with freshly de-esterified polymers as the tube elongates, shifting

590

the load bearing forces to the new pectins until sufficient crosslinking stiffens the pectic gel.

591

Despite the presence of more de-esterified homogalacturonan at the apex of msl8 mutant pollen

592

tubes, if it is not sufficiently crosslinked the tube would remain extensible and thus not reach a

593

growth pause, perhaps indicating an imbalance in available Ca2+ ions or an overall increase in

594

pectin deposition that outweighs the normal Ca2+ pool available.

595
596

Our data on PI sensitivity support this, as adding PI inhibits msl8 tube elongation. PI is proposed

597

to bind the same de-esterified sites as Ca2+ on homogalacturonan, but not form crosslinks

598

between polymers (Rounds et al., 2011). Thus, increasing PI concentrations can outcompete

599

Ca2+ and lead to a destabilized wall that is prone to rupture. Our WT pollen was insensitive to PI

600

treatment, suggesting that there was a sufficient Ca2+:de-esterified homogalacturonan ratio that

601

was not disrupted by PI competition. The msl8 pollen tubes were highly sensitive to PI, showing

602

reduced growth associated with tube tip rupture (Figure 6). This indicates an imbalance in

603

Ca2+:de-esterified homogalacturonan as mentioned above. One possibility in this scenario is

604

that the excess de-esterified homogalacturonan we detect in msl8 tubes is already outweighing

605

the amount of Ca2+ available to crosslink it, and even a modest amount of PI competition can

606

cause tube rupture.

609

Anion efflux might modulate CWI signaling in response to membrane tension and

610

elongation.

611

The CWI pathway monitors and regulates cellular integrity, and in previous work we found a

612

complex genetic relationship between MSL8 and genes encoding components of the CWI. In

613

germinating pollen grains, MSL8-YFP overexpression could compensate for the bursting defects

614

seen in CWI pathway mutants, and upregulation of CWI signaling could rescue the bursting of

615

msl8 pollen (Wang et al., 2022). The altered cell wall composition at the apex of msl8 mutant

616

pollen tubes shown here (Figure 5), is also a phenotype associated with CWI misregulation

617

(Mecchia et al., 2017; Fabrice et al., 2018). It is possible that MSL8 anion efflux alters the

618

microenvironment chemistry of the pollen apoplast, thereby impacting interactions between CWI

619

factors such as leucine-rich repeat extension proteins (LRX) and rapid alkalinization factor

620

peptides (RALFs). These interact with each other and with plasma membrane bound receptor

621

proteins to monitor wall status (Ge et al., 2017, 2019; Mecchia et al., 2017; Fabrice et al., 2018)

622

(Moussu et al., 2020).

623
624

Future directions

625

Much remains to be uncovered in defining the mechanism behind MSL8 control of pollen tube

626

growth dynamic and cell wall composition. Dynamic imaging of chloride, pH, and Ca2+ fluxes in

627

msl8 pollen tubes could further define the ionic growth control patterns at play. Defining the

628

specific flux composition of MSL8 channels will be key to formulating testable hypotheses about

629

how MSL8 may be modifying the apoplast microenvironment. Future work could also explore

630

the effect of MSL8 anion efflux on interactions between CWI signaling components, potentially

631

providing an alternative mechanism for regulating cell wall deposition and growth. We feel the

632

results presented here open up a new area of study for the apoplast ionome as a regulatory

633

component of plant cell biology. This positions MS channels as dynamic modulators of the plant

634

cell wall and creates a link translating plasma membrane mechanics to cell wall mechanics.

635

Further exploration of the MSL8-cell wall interaction, as well as the effects of other MS channels

636

in other tissue types is ripe for unlocking greater insights of cell wall dynamics and growth

637

regulation.
