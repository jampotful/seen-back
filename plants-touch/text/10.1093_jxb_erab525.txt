ABSTRACT

20

Cells employ multiple systems to maintain cellular integrity, including mechanosensitive (MS) ion

21

channels and the cell wall integrity (CWI) pathway. Here, we use pollen as a model system to ask

22

how these different mechanisms are interconnected at the cellular level. MscS-Like (MSL)8 is an

23

MS channel required to protect Arabidopsis thaliana pollen from osmotic challenges during in

24

vitro rehydration, germination and tube growth. New CRISPR/Cas9 and artificial microRNA-

25

generated msl8 alleles produced unexpected pollen phenotypes, including the ability to germinate

26

a tube after bursting, dramatic defects in cell wall structure and disorganized callose deposition at

27

the germination site. We document complex genetic interactions between MSL8 and two

28

previously

established

components
R240C

of

the

CWI

pathway,

MARIS,

and

ANXUR1/2.

29

Overexpression of MARIS

-FP suppressed the bursting, germination, and callose deposition

30

phenotypes of msl8 mutant pollen. Null msl8 alleles suppressed the internalized callose

31

structures observed in MARISR240C-FP lines. Similarly, MSL8-YFP overexpression suppressed

32

bursting in the anxur1/2 mutant background, while anxur1/2 alleles reduced the strong rings of

33

callose around ungerminated pollen grains in MSL8-YFP over-expressors. These data show that

34

MS ion channels modulate callose deposition in pollen and provides evidence that cell wall and

35

membrane surveillance systems coordinate in a complex manner to maintain cell integrity.

INTRODUCTION

39

Cell mechanobiology includes the study of mechanical properties of cell constituents and the

40

forces applied to or generated by cells and their microenvironment (Bidhendi & Geitmann 2019).

41

Plant cells perceive and respond to multiple external or internal forces important for their growth,

42

survival, and development (Monshausen & Haswell 2013; Trinh et al. 2021). Pollen, the male

43

gamete of flowering plants, is an excellent model system for the study of plant cell

44

mechanobiology due to its relatively simple structure, its unique biology, and the availability of in

45

vitro growth systems (Johnson, Harper & Palanivelu 2019).This single cell harbors two sperm

46

cells and germinates a tube to deliver the sperm to the female ovule for double fertilization,

47

navigating multiple osmotic and mechanical challenges during rehydration, germination, tube

48

growth, and fertilization (Firon, Nepi & Pacini 2012; Adhikari, Liu & Kasahara 2020).

49
50

The plasma membrane is an important platform for mechanoresponses and it is expected that

51

changes in the physical properties of the membrane result from changes in turgor or in cell wall

52

integrity (Ackermann & Stanislas 2020). A conserved mechanism for force perception at the

53

membrane is the activation of mechanosensitive (MS) channels through increased membrane

54

tension (Naismith & Booth 2012; Peyronnet, Tran, Girault & Frachisse 2014; Ranade, Syeda &

55

Patapoutian 2015). In plants, members of the MscS-like (MSL) family of mechanosensitive ion

56

channels are localized to mitochondrial, plastid, and plasma membranes (Basu & Haswell 2017),

57

where they function as tension-regulated osmotic safety valves (Wilson, Jensen & Haswell 2011;

58

Hamilton et al. 2015; Hamilton & Haswell 2017), some with additional complex functions (Veley et

59

al. 2014; Maksaev, Shoots, Ohri & Haswell 2018). MSL8 is localized to the plasma membrane of

60

pollen grains and tubes, and mediates mechanosensitive channel activities with a slight

61

preference for anions (Hamilton et al. 2015). MSL8’s tension-sensitive ion transport activity is

62

required to protect Arabidopsis pollen from osmotic challenges during in vitro pollen rehydration,

63

germination and tube growth (Hamilton & Haswell 2017). Loss of MSL8 causes pollen grain to

64

burst (Hamilton et al. 2015).

65
66

The plant cell wall provides tensile strength and protection against both internal and external

67

mechanical stresses (Höfte & Voxeur 2017). The cell wall in a mature pollen surface comprises

68

an inner intine wall, an outer exine wall, and a pollen coat. Callose, a polysaccharide in the form

69

of β-1,3-glucan with some β-1,6-branches (Chen & Kim 2009), is abundant in immature pollen

70

grains, but disappears during pollen maturation and desiccation (Xu, Zhang, Zhou & Yang 2016).

71

Once a pollen grain has rehydrated and is about to germinate, tube emergence begins with the

72

formation of a germination plaque (Johnson & McCormick 2001; Hoedemaekers et al. 2015). This

73

lens-shaped plaque is typically restricted to one site on the grain periphery(Johnson & McCormick
3

bioRxiv preprint doi: https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.08.24.457556; this version posted August 25, 2021. The copyright holder for this preprint
(which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. It is made

74

2001; Hoedemaekers et al. 2015). It contains callose, which is thought to add flexibility to the cell

75

wall and increase its load-bearing ability (Parre & Geitmann 2005), During germination, the

76

germination plaque protrudes and extends, eventually forming the tip-growing tube that will

77

eventually deliver the male gamete to the female egg cell (Hoedemaekers et al. 2015). A tightly

78

controlled balance between cell wall deposition and internal pressure allows the pollen tube to

79

rapidly reach the ovules for fertilization (Cameron & Geitmann 2018).

80
81

In plants, the cell wall integrity (CWI) signaling pathway serves to maintain mechanical

82

homeostasis during cell expansion and in response to cell wall weakening (Gigli-Bisceglia,

83

Engelsdorf & Hamann 2019). Members of the Catharanthus roseus RLK1–like (CrRLK1L) protein

84

family are proposed to sense wall conditions and transduce information to cytosolic signaling

85

factors (Li, Wu & Cheung 2016).The CrRLK1Ls involved CWI signaling pathway is vital to

86

maintaining cellular integrity in multiple processes, including pollen grain germination and pollen

87

tube growth (Li et al. 2016; Li & Yang 2018; Ge, Cheung & Qu 2019; Vogler, Santos-Fernandez,

88

Mecchia & Grossniklaus 2019). In Arabidopsis thaliana pollen, ANXUR1 and ANXUR2 (ANX1/2)

89

and Buddha’s Paper Seal (BUPS)1/2 are membrane localized CrRLK1L receptors. The ANX1/2-

90

BUPS1/2 complex, as well as wall-anchored Leucine-rich repeat extensins (LRX8/9/11), bind the

91

autocrine small peptides known as Rapid Alkalinization Factor (RALF)4/19 to sense alterations in

92

the cell wall. These signals are transduced to downstream signaling components, including the

93

receptor-like cytoplasmic kinase MARIS (MRI), to regulate pollen tube growth rate and maintain

94

cell integrity (Liao et al. 2016; Li & Yang 2018; Ge et al. 2019). Mutants that disrupt the pathway,

95

such as anx1/2, mri, lrx8/9/11, bups1/2 and ralf4/19, show increased grain bursting (Boisson-

96

Dernier et al. 2009, 2013; Boisson-Dernier, Franck, Lituiev & Grossniklaus 2015; Miyazaki et al.

97

2009; Liao et al. 2016; Ge et al. 2017; Fabrice et al. 2018; Sede et al. 2018) and some have

98

elevated callose deposition (Fabrice et al. 2018; Sede et al. 2018). Over-expressing either

99

ANX1/2 or a dominant MRI allele (MRIR240C) results in membrane invagination and

100

hyperaccumulation of cell wall material (Boisson-Dernier et al. 2013, 2015). MRI functions

101

downstream of ANX1/2, as overexpression of MRIR240C suppresses grain bursting in anx1/2

102

mutants (Boisson-Dernier et al. 2015).

103
104

It has been proposed that the CWI pathway could act in synergy with mechanosensitive

105

channels, to integrate mechanical information from the cell wall and plasma membrane and

106

maintain cellular mechanical homeostasis (Hamant & Haswell 2017). The similar pollen bursting

107

phenotypes of msl8 mutants and CWI mutants supports this idea. Here we further test this

108

hypothesis with new mutant alleles of MSL8 and two components of the pollen CWI pathway. Our

109

data indicate a novel function of MSL8 in cell wall organization during pollen germination and

RESULTS

113

Creation of new msl8 and msl7 msl8 mutant lines

114

We previously characterized pollen hydration and germination defects in two MSL8 T-DNA

115

insertion mutants (Hamilton et al. 2015). However, the null msl8-4 allele is in the Landsberg

116

erecta (Ler) background, while most mutants available from publicly available stock centers are in

117

the Columbia-0 (Col-0) background. Furthermore, MSL8 (At2g17010) has a close homolog,

118

MSL7 (At2g17000), located in tandem on Chromosome 2 (SI Appendix, Fig. S1A). MSL7 and

119

MSL8 have partially overlapping expression pattern, as MSL8 is expressed specifically in mature

120

pollen gains and pollen tubes (Hamilton et al. 2015) and an MSL7p::GUS reporter was expressed

121

in pistil stigma cells and pollen tubes when grown through the stigma and style (SI Appendix, Fig.

122

S1B-C), consistent with microarray data (Swanson, Clark & Preuss 2005; Qin et al. 2009). We

123

thus identified msl7-1 (SALK_133223) as a null T-DNA insertion allele in the Col-0 background

124

(SI Appendix, Fig. S1A, G-H). We then used CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing of the MSL8 gene to

125

generate two null msl8 alleles (msl8-5 and msl8-8) and two double msl7 msl8 mutants (msl7-1

126

msl8-6 and msl7-1 msl8-7) in the Col-0 background (SI Appendix, Fig. S1D-F, Table S1). We also

127

used artificial microRNA (amiRNA)-based silencing to reduce MSL8 transcripts in the msl7-1

128

mutant background. Both msl7-1 amiR-MSL8-1 and msl7-1 amiR-MSL8-2 showed reduced MSL8

129

transcript levels in flowers (SI Appendix, Fig. S1G-H, Table S2).

131

We next germinated wild type and mutant pollen grains in vitro and monitored their morphology

132

after 4 and 7 hours (h). We observed increased pollen grain bursting (with the visible release of

133

cytosol) at both timepoints, and increased germination rates at the earlier timepoint, in the single

134

msl8 and double msl7 msl8 mutant lines when compared to pollen from WT or msl7-1 single

135

mutant plants (Fig. 1A). Similarly, pollen grains from msl7-1 amiR-MSL8-1 and msl7-1 amiR-

136

MSL8-2 showed significantly more bursting and early germination compared to WT or msl7-1 (SI

137

Appendix, Fig. S2A). There was no detectable difference between msl8 single and msl7 msl8

138

double mutant pollen in bursting or germination at either time point. The small but statistically

139

significant difference in germination rates between msl7-1 and WT pollen shown in Fig. 1A was

140

not reproducible in other experiments (SI Appendix, Fig. S2).

MSL8 is required for normal intine structure and callose deposition at the germination site

168

To investigate a possible novel role for MSL8 in controlling cell wall properties, we used

169

transmission electron microscopy to examine the ultrastructure of germinating WT and msl7-1

170

msl8-7 pollen grains (i.e. with visible protrusions ranging from a small bump to a fully extended

171

tube). We did not see obvious differences between the two genotypes with respect to pollen coat

172

or exine but did observe striking changes in the intine at the germination site (Fig 2A). WT pollen

173

had a relatively regular intine layer that thickened evenly at the germination site as reported

174

previously (Hoedemaekers et al. 2015). In msl7-1 msl8-7 pollen, the intine was dramatically

175

disrupted; in some cases, it was abnormally thick (two headed arrows), while in other cases it

176

looked discontinuous or had inclusions (black arrowheads). The inclusion could be cytoplasm as

177

previously reported (Hoedemaekers et al. 2015). All these phenotypes indicate a role for MSL8 in

178

establishing cell wall structure at the germination site.

Overexpression of MRIR240C suppresses msl8 mutant phenotypes

206

The discovery of defective pollen cell walls in the msl7-1 msl8-7 mutant led us to hypothesize that

207

the pollen CWI pathway might be disrupted in these lines. We predicted that increased grain

208

bursting and other phenotypes of msl8 and msl7 msl8 mutant pollen might be suppressed if CWI

209

signaling was enhanced. To test this, we overexpressed the gain-of-function MRI allele MRIR240C

210

in WT and msl7-1 msl8-7 mutant plants. We identified three independent lines expressing yellow

211

fluorescence protein (YFP)- or cyan fluorescence protein (CFP)-tagged MRIR240C under the

212

pollen-specific LAT52 promoter (Twell, Yamaguchi & McCormick 1990) in the msl7-1 msl8-7

213

background (msl7 msl8 MRIR240C-FP). Both fluorescence imaging (SI Appendix, Fig. S5) and

214

immunoblotting (Fig. 3A) indicated a range of expression levels; lowest in line 5, intermediate in

215

line 4 and highest in line 14. All msl7 msl8 MRIR240C-FP lines were backcrossed to WT plants, and

216

siblings harboring homozygous MRIR240C-FP in either the WT or the msl7 msl8 background were

217

isolated. As expected, in the WT background, intermediate and high expression of MRIR240C-FP

218

resulted in low bursting and low germination rates (Fig. 3B). Also as expected, msl7 msl8 mutant

219

pollen had high bursting and germination rates. Both phenotypes were significantly suppressed

220

by intermediate and high MRIR240C-FP overexpression at both 4 and 7 h of incubation in

221

germination media. The low level of MRIR240C-FP in line 5 also suppressed bursting and

222

germination at the early time point, though not to WT levels. It is unclear why the low levels of

223

MRIR240C-FP in line 5 in the WT background had higher bursting rates than the WT. We further

224

tested if the abnormal germination phenotypes observed in msl7 msl8 were also suppressed by

225

overexpression of MRIR240C-FP. Indeed, expression of MRIR240C-FP suppressed abnormal

226

germination compared to msl7 msl8 in all three lines (Fig. 3C), primarily through reducing the rate

227

of burst grains with a tube (Fig. 3C).

DISCUSSION

280
281

Plant cell expansion relies on a finely tuned balance between intracellular osmotic pressure and

282

extracellular resistance from the cell wall, and dysregulation on either side of this equation can

283

result in faulty growth behavior. In tip-growing cells, such as germinating and growing pollen

284

tubes, the dynamic regulation of ion fluxes and of cell wall mechanical properties have been well-

285

studied, but mostly separately (Michard, Simon, Tavares, Wudick & Feijó 2017; Cameron &

286

Geitmann 2018). The work reported here shows that these two key mechanical contributions are

287

inter-related, providing a novel avenue of mechano-homeostatic feedback and growth regulation.

288
289

We have proposed that MSL8 functions as an osmotic valve (Basu & Haswell 2017). According to

290

this model, MSL8 is required to relieve internal pressure during pollen hydration and germination;

291

in its absence, grains germinate more quickly and burst more readily. In overexpression lines,

292

excess MSL8 releases pressure too efficiently, preventing the build-up of turgor that is required to

293

drive germination. This “osmotic safety valve” model for MSL8 function in pollen is complicated by

294

the phenotypes and genetic interactions presented here. Consistent with previous work (Hamilton

295

et al. 2015), msl8 or msl7 msl8 mutants exhibited higher germination and bursting rates

296

compared to the WT (Fig. 1A), while MSL8-YFP overexpression resulted in very low germination

297

rates (Fig. 4C). However, we documented some new germination phenotypes, including double

298

tubes and burst grains growing new tubes (Fig. 1A-C, SI Appendix, Fig. S2). A null MSL7 allele

299

did not affect any of these phenotypes.

300
301

Evidence for a new role for MS ion channels in cell wall organization. The fact that mutant

302

pollen grains could burst and then recover and germinate a growing tube while WT grains that

303

had burst rarely did so (Fig.1D, Movies S1-3) suggested that there is more to MSL8 function than

304

simply osmoregulation. Indeed, Transmission electron microscopy and aniline blue staining

305

showed that MSL8 is required for normal cell wall organization in ungerminated pollen grains and

306

at the germination site (Fig. 2, SI Appendix, Fig. S4). Pollen from msl8 mutants showed a

307

disrupted and disorganized germination plaque and patchy callose deposition at the germination

308

site (Fig. 2, SI Appendix, Fig. S4). Furthermore, the accumulation of callose in a ring at the

309

periphery of ungerminated pollen was much more frequently observed and much more dramatic

310

in pollen from MSL8-YFP overexpressing plants than in pollen from the WT (Fig. 2C, Fig. 4D, SI

311

Appendix, Fig. S4). Thus, based on TEM and if the presence of callose is used as a proxy for cell

312

wall components, MSL8 unexpectedly modulates local cell wall organization in pollen grains

313

before and during germination.

315

Genetic interactions between MSL8 and the CWI pathway are complex. MSL8’s role in pollen

316

integrity and cell wall deposition was at least superficially similar to that of the cell wall integrity

317

pathway (Boisson-Dernier et al. 2009, 2013, 2015; Miyazaki et al. 2009), prompting us to test for

318

genetic interactions between MSL8 and two such components, MRI and ANX1/2. The results of

319

these studies (summarized in Figure 5A) do not easily conform to linear genetic pathways. The

320

grain bursting and abnormal germination phenotype of msl8 pollen was suppressed by

321

overexpression of MRI

322

by MSL8-YFP overexpression (Fig. 4A-C). In the anx1/2 background, MSL8-YFP overexpression

323

produced less intensely staining callose rings than they did in the ANX1/2 background (Fig. 4D),

324

suggesting that ANX1/2 are required for the full effects of MSL8-YFP overexpression and that

325

MSL8 functions upstream of ANX1/2. However, other data suggest that MSL8 acts downstream

326

of or parallel to ANX1/2. For example, excess callose was deposited to the periphery whether or

327

not ANX1/2 were present in MSL8-YFP overexpressing pollen (Fig. 4D).

R240C

-FP (Fig. 3A-C), while the grain bursting of anx1/2 pollen was rescued

330
331

Working model. Both MSL8 and the CWI pathway affect pollen germination, pollen bursting rate,

332

and the pattern of callose deposition, and these phenotypes were affected in complex patterns

333

that defy any linear relationship (Fig. 5A). As a result, we propose a simple model (Fig. 5B)
16

bioRxiv preprint doi: https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.08.24.457556; this version posted August 25, 2021. The copyright holder for this preprint
(which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. It is made
available under a CC-BY-NC-ND 4.0 International license.

334

wherein the MSL8 and the CWI signaling pathways are independent but impinge on a central

335

common component, referred to here as an “integrator”, which can also feedback to either of

336

them to ensure normal pollen germination and tube growth (Fig. 5B). This component could be a

337

mechanical status, such as turgor, or a molecular signal, such as ions or reactive oxygen species,

338

or a post-translational event such as the formation of a complex. Future work is needed to

339

determine the exact mechanism by which MSL8 and the cell wall integrity pathway interact and

340

how their signals are translated into downstream effects.

341
342

While it has become clear over the past few decades that mechanical forces play a huge role in

343

plant development, how they play out at the cellular scale is not well understood. The results

344

presented here indicate that in pollen, and likely in other plant cells, cell integrity maintenance

345

systems require coordination between cell wall- and plasma membrane-based perception

346

pathways and provide a foundation for future research into the mechanisms controlling

347

mechanical homeostasis at the cellular scale.

362

363

364

369

371

372

373

374

transplanted to soil. The absence of Cas9 was further confirmed by genotyping. Alternatively,

375

used to detect Cas9 and other genotyping information for the new msl8 and msl7 msl8 CRISPR
