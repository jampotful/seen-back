# Domain knowledge-integrated reinforcement learning control of nonlinear tunable vibration absorber under nonstationary excitation

## Abstract
AbstractThis paper proposes a novel model-free reinforcement learning (RL) control algorithm for a semi-active tunable vibration absorber (TVA) with nonlinear properties that are operated under nonstationary and multi-frequency excitations. The research addresses two critical challenges in vibration absorber control that are often treated separately: (i) the time-varying and nonlinear stiffness-damping characteristics, and (ii) the complex and nonstationary nature of real-world excitations. To address these challenges, a modified Q-learning algorithm is proposed by integrating vibration-domain knowledge derived from Parseval’s theorem and frequency response functions. This integration not only enables the controller to effectively minimize vibration energy without requiring an explicit model of the plant but also significantly reduces the computational complexity of the learning process. The proposed controller is experimentally validated under nonstationary multi-frequency excitation using a semi-active TVA with highly time-variant stiffness and damping properties. Experimental results demonstrated accurate real-time control performance, achieving an R-squared value of 0.994 compared to an optimal control baseline, and up to 58% reduction in vibration energy. These results provide strong evidence that reinforcement learning control strategies, when guided by vibration-domain knowledge, can offer generalizable, efficient, and adaptive solutions to complex mechanical vibration control problems.Keywords: Composite vibration control, Domain knowledge-integrated, Model-free control, Nonstationary excitation, Reinforcement learning, Smart materials, Semi-active vibration control, Time-varying characteristics, Tunable vibration absorberSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

## Introduction
A practical approach to mitigating shock and vibration is the use of a vibration absorber, which is generally classified as a passive, active, and semi-active system. Passive systems are only effective at a narrow range of excitation frequencies, and active systems require higher power and system complexity. On the other hand, a semi-active tunable vibration absorber is a balance between passive and active systems, in which it controls the stiffness or damping for a larger range of operation while consuming less power than active systems.
Recently, smart materials have been utilized in controlling the stiffness or damping of the semi-active tunable vibration absorber due to the easy implementation and fast responses1–3. These intelligent materials with controllable material properties include piezoelectric ceramic, shape memory alloys, and magnetorheological elastomers (MREs)4–6. Particularly, extensive research has been conducted on MREs, also referred to as smart rubber, in various types of vibration control systems, including dynamic vibration isolators7–11, rail dampers12, and absorbers for aircraft seats13, milling robots14, and drilling systems15.
Despite extensive research on these semi-active, tunable vibration absorbers, significant challenges remain in achieving robust and effective vibration control. The main issues include the time-varying nonlinear characteristics of smart materials and the need for adaptability under nonstationary excitation as in real industrial environments.
First, the vibration absorber based on smart material can exhibit time-varying nonlinear properties, thereby complicating the design of effective control systems. This phenomenon is easily observed in elastomer-based vibration absorber systems. Environmental factors such as temperature16,17 and humidity18 can dynamically shift the material stiffness and damping over time. Additionally, non-linear characteristics such as the stress–strain hysteresis and a complex response under varying magnetic field strengths19,20 underscore the inherent complexity of the smart material behavior. Therefore, these challenges necessitate advanced control strategies to maintain consistent system performance.
To address these issues, conventional model-free strategies such as skyhook or bang-bang control have been widely adopted for their simplicity in various vibration systems, including automotive suspensions with nonlinear stiffness21 and seismic isolation using piezoelectric friction dampers22. Extensive reviews indicate that these strategies remain dominant in structural vibration control despite the inherent nonlinearity of modern damping devices23. However, these fixed-rule approaches are inherently heuristic and fail to ensure control optimality in time-varying systems, as they lack the adaptive capacity to autonomously update their control laws against the dynamic variations in smart material properties and nonstationary environments. Beyond these methods, researchers have developed more sophisticated adaptive control algorithms. These include combinations of fuzzy-logic control with semi-active control24 and integrations of fuzzy-logic control with neural network-based control25–27. However, these approaches are also limited because they rely on model-based designs that are optimized for predefined initial conditions and do not consider for the time variance of smart materials. A study in28 proposed a model-free reinforcement learning algorithm to address the time-varying characteristics of smart rubber; however, that study was designed only for a single-frequency excitation environment.
The second challenge is that the vibration excitation in an actual industrial environment can be a complex form of multi-frequency bandwidth with nonstationary dynamics. For examples, aircraft, ships, and trains are exposed to nonstationary disturbance factors, such as wind, waves, and rail states, which can be a complex form of multiple frequency components that change over time. Therefore, an advanced vibration control algorithm that adaptively suppresses the nonstationary vibration excitation is necessary to enhance reliability and efficiency across various industrial applications.
Although most of the previous research on tunable vibration absorbers28,29 was mainly focused on a single-frequency excitation, there have been some attempts that addressed under multi-frequency conditions30,31. However, their testing environment was confined to simple scenarios with random amplitudes of three to four distinct frequency components. These approaches do not adequately reflect the complex vibration conditions of industrial environments, and the developed control algorithms do not simultaneously address the time-variant property of the vibration absorber based on smart materials. While recent mechanical network synthesis has introduced inerters to enhance isolation through mass-amplification32, their focus remains distinct from the real-time stiffness and damping modulation required for tunable vibration absorbers (TVAs).
Consequently, despite these diverse advancements in both control strategies, there is a lack of an integrated approach to solve both the main challenges of robust control of a time-varying and nonlinear tunable vibration absorber and effective control under nonstationary multi-frequency excitation. Therefore, this study proposes a model-free vibration control system that comprehensively addresses both the challenges of time-varying nonlinearity and nonstationary excitation.
By utilizing a model-free reinforcement learning (RL), a robust vibration control is achieved to continuously adapt and optimize control strategies on the vibration absorber with the time varying and nonlinear properties. Furthermore, the proposed system integrated domain knowledge in vibration signal processing on the reinforcement learning model to control adaptively under nonstationary excitations. In particular, the concept of Parseval’s theorem and the vibration frequency response function (FRF) are utilized to generalize the RL model to control the vibration adaptively under the unknown environment of excitations. Also, this has shown to efficiently reduce the computational complexity while enhancing the effectiveness in suppressing a complex of multi-frequency excitations.
To evaluate the proposed system’s performance, this study used the smart rubber-based tunable vibration absorber (TVA) from28 as a case study. Given the highly nonlinear and time-dependent stiffness and damping properties of elastomers, the MRE-based TVA was selected as a representative system. Experiments under nonstationary and multi-frequency excitations demonstrated that the system exhibited fast learning capabilities and achieved excellent vibration suppression performance, confirming its strong potential for real-world applications.
The key contributions of this paper are as follows:
We proposed a reinforcement learning-based model-free control algorithm that integratively addresses the time-varying and nonlinear dynamics as well as the complex nonstationary excitation control of semi-active tunable vibration absorbers.
We integrated vibration-domain knowledge of Parseval’s theorem and frequency response functions (FRFs) into the RL algorithm to improve adaptive control performance and significantly reduce computational complexity.
We experimentally validated the proposed control system using a semi-active vibration absorber in a nonstationary multi-frequency vibration environment, demonstrating that the algorithm achieves fast and accurate learning performance.

## Issues of time-varying and nonlinear characteristics
Recent semi-active TVAs consist of smart materials with variable material properties. The variable properties, and , are determined by a set of influencing factors , where the system is actively controlled via the parameter (e.g., magnetic field or current).
However, the material properties can also be influenced by various environmental factors such as usage-time28, humidity18, and working environment temperature16,17,33,34. Furthermore, some materials such as smart rubber exhibit nonlinear properties such as hysteresis19,20. Due to these nonlinear and time-varying material properties, it is highly complex to model the TVA system accurately, for it is influenced by the working environment. In addition, it is challenging to design an optimal vibration control on the TVA system that can have the stiffness value drifted unexpectedly over time. Such a time-varying characteristic of the system can lead to a decrease in vibration control performance over time, even if the controller were optimally designed initially.
For example, a semi-active TVA developed in a previous study28 is observed that the stiffness drifted over time, which caused the change in the FRF curve from the initial state as shown in Fig. 2a. Specifically, the time-varying drift in and causes a shift in the absorber’s natural frequency as governed by Eq. (2). This shift in the frequency response necessitates the re-tuning of the optimal control parameter to minimize vibrations at each frequency, as illustrated in Fig. 2(b).

One approach to addressing this issue of time-varying characteristic nonlinear property is to design a model-free control system, which can adapt to the changed parameters in the system model. Such robust control systems also need to continuously and adaptively explore the optimal control actions in response to time-varying characteristics and interactions with new environments. Therefore, this study proposes a model-free vibration control system based on reinforcement learning to enable the TVA quickly adapt to the time-varying and nonlinear characteristics of the TVA system.

## Issues of multi-component and nonstationary excitation environment
Industrial systems, such as automobiles, railway vehicles, and aircraft, can experience composite vibration signals due to the system-level complexity of interconnected rotating machineries and external disturbances. These mechanical vibration signals consist of multiple frequency components, with dominant frequencies dynamically changing over time. The complexity arises when the amplitude, phase, and frequency of the excitation vary concurrently. This paper defines such complex excitation as the multi-component non-stationary signals.
Most previous research on tunable vibration absorbers focused mainly on single-frequency excitation28,29. Although there have been studies for multi-frequency excitation30,31, they have tested with a few distinct frequency components.
Therefore, the proposed model-free vibration control algorithm is designed to efficiently isolate the complex vibration signals with presence of multiple frequency components in which the dominant frequencies dynamically change over time. Specifically, the domain-knowledge of Parseval’s theorem and the vibration frequency response function (FRF) are included in the reinforcement learning model to enhancing the suppressing the nonstationry excitations with a low computational complexity.
The detailed design principles of the algorithm are discussed in “Reinforcement learning-based vibration control system for tunable vibration absorber”, and its performance evaluation on composite vibration signals is presented in “Evaluation on isolating nonstationary and composite vibration”.

## Reinforcement learning-based vibration control system for tunable vibration absorber
This section introduces the proposed model-free vibration control system based on Q-learning of reinforcement learning (RL), designed to address the integrated challenges of time-varying nonlinearity and composite vibration control. Prior to the detailed design, the operational envelope of the proposed system is defined based on physical validity and control complexity: First, the range of disturbance amplitude is defined as a regime where the frequency response function (FRF) characteristics are preserved, ensuring the smart material response remains within a predictable engineering regime. Second, to ensure real-time adaptability, the frequency variation of the disturbance is assumed to follow a quasi-stationary evolution. Accordingly, discontinuous transient signals, such as impulses or pure white noise, are beyond the scope of this study. Within these conditions, the target of this study is high-dimensional complex non-stationary vibrations, characterized by the superposition of multi-frequency interference and non-deterministic amplitude modulation.
For the control of such composite vibrations, the block diagram of the proposed reinforcement learning (RL)-based vibration control system is shown in Fig. 3. The operational sequence of a control cycle in the proposed system consists of four distinct stages: First, the system acquires input vibration signals () from the base and output vibration signals () from the target plant (TVA) in the time domain. Second, these signals are transformed into frequency-domain spectra () via Fast Fourier Transform (FFT). Third, the proposed RL algorithm learns the frequency response function from these spectra and determines the control input to minimize the predicted vibration energy for the current state. Finally, the magnetic field generator applies the selected to the smart material, which modulates the stiffness () and damping () properties to alter the vibration characteristics of the TVA.
Considering the time and environment dependent characteristics of the tunable system and the diverse patterns of composite vibrations, the control model must learn across a vast number of potential scenarios. To overcome these challenges, this study focused on reducing learning complexity and enhancing efficiency by actively incorporating domain knowledge in vibration mechanics. Specifically, Parseval’s theorem was employed in designing the state, action, and reward for optimal control. This helped the system to be simplified as an episodic task, enabling the learning process to follow a structured and streamlined approach. Furthermore, the proposed Q-learning algorithm leveraged the frequency response functions (FRF) to adaptively address the challenges posed by composite vibration disturbances.

## Formulation of state, action, and reward using Parseval’s theorem
The vibration signals collected using accelerometers are used as the training data for the proposed model. The vibration signals can be analyzed in either the time-domain or frequency-domain. In the time-domain, the raw acceleration signal is used directly that eliminates the need for data preprocessing.
However, it has a computational complexity in understanding the dynamic changes of the frequency and phase-delay components over time. In contrast, the frequency-domain analysis provides a more intuitive understanding of the frequency, magnitude, and phase information of the signals.
Therefore, this study also converted the vibration signals into the frequency domain and used them to define the state, action, and reward in reinforcement learning.

## State
The proposed system must perform optimal control that adapts to disturbances so the reinforcement learning agent monitors the base vibrations, as shown in Fig. 3. The vibration signal data is obtained from the time-domain signal sampled times at the sampling frequency .
Then, the data is transformed into the frequency domain using FFT. The states of the RL model are defined as vectors of the spectrum magnitude across different frequencies.

## Action
The principle of vibration isolation of a semi-active TVA is to control the stiffness of the absorber module such that to minimize the vibration of the target plant. Consequently, the action space of the RL model is discretized based on the control input values, which can be the current and the magnetic field, that directly affect the stiffness change.
The proposed algorithm applied the -greedy algorithm35 to select the best action. During the learning process, the algorithm prioritizes exploration in the early stages and exploitation as learning progresses. The goal is to maximize the Q-value, which measures the expected future rewards based on past experiences.

## Reward
The reward for the TVA is directly linked to the policy learning direction. Thus, it must correlate with the vibration magnitude of the target system, which is the primary goal of vibration suppression. Therefore, this study designed the reward to increase as the vibration energy of the target plant decreases.
According to Parseval’s theorem, which is a fundamental principle in vibration analysis, the signal in the frequency domain retains the same energy value as in the time domain. Using this domain knowledge, the reward is defined as the negative value of the target system’s vibration energy that is measured in the frequency domain.

## Configuration of episodic tasks based on vibration mechanics analysis
Reinforcement learning problems can be broadly categorized into episodic and continuous tasks35. Episodic tasks are characterized by distinct episodes, each beginning in an initial state and concluding in a terminal state, where states, actions, and rewards are reset at the end of each episode. In contrast, continuous tasks involve ongoing interaction with the environment without a defined endpoint, with learning occurring indefinitely.
Since the TVA must continuously adjust its optimal vibration control values in response to environmental changes and disturbances, it may appear to be a continuous task. However, a deeper analysis grounded in vibration mechanics reveals that the system can be regarded as an episodic task.
The disturbance vibrations applied to the target system are independent of the learning model’s actions, and the control values (actions) of the stiffness tuning only influence vibration suppression (rewards) without impacting subsequent states. Furthermore, the relationships among states over time are also independent; subsequent disturbance vibrations can change significantly regardless of the current disturbance status.
Due to this independence of states and actions, the TVA can treat each point of disturbance vibration measurement as a terminal state. Consequently, the system can be modeled as an episodic task, where each episode concludes with a single state-action-reward tuple. The state-action value function (Q-value) is updated at the end of each episode using the Monte Carlo method35, which represents the simplest form of Q-learning36:
The episodic task framework combined with the Monte Carlo update method enables the system to efficiently learn from immediate rewards in each episode. This approach allows the proposed system to adapt quickly to the time-varying characteristics of the nonlinear TVA modeling.

## Experiment and discussion
For the experiment to evaluate the proposed system, this study used the smart-rubber-based tunable vibration absorber (TVA) system as a case study. The evaluation focuses on analyzing whether the smart rubber can adaptively learn its nonlinear and time-varying characteristics, and whether the system can achieve optimal control through experiments involving composite vibration disturbances.

## Experimental setup: smart rubber based TVA
The proposed vibration control algorithm was experimentally tested and evaluated using actual smart rubber vibration absorber hardware. This study utilized the system designed in a previous study28. The stiffness-variable smart rubber, also known as the magnetorheological elastomer(MRE), has highly nonlinear and time-dependent stiffness and damping properties, which makes it a suitable system for the case study. The MRE is composed of carbonyl iron powder (CIP) mixed with silicone elastomers, and exhibits rapid change in the elasticity and viscosity in response to an external magnetic field.
As shown in Fig. 4a, the system consists of a linear compressor as the target plant and a smart rubber absorber, with vibration signals acquired via accelerometers. As illustrated in Fig. 4b, the absorber applies a magnetic field ranging from 0 to 340 mT to the material in 8 discrete steps (level 0 to 7) through motor rotation. The magnetic field values for each step are presented in Table 1. The system’s response characteristics vary with the intensity of the magnetic field when it is excited by external vibrations transmitted from the base, and the results are shown in Fig. 1b. Therefore, the frequency range of the external vibrations was set to 40 to 110 Hz for the magnetic field control experiment. The vibration experiment’s measurement environment and learning parameters were configured as follows. The sampling frequency was set to 1 kHz, with each episode consisting of 1000 data points . To accommodate both the mechanical response of the magnetic generator and the stabilization of MRE properties, a delay time of 0.5 s was applied28,37,38, resulting in an episode length of 1.5 s. The learning rate for the experiment was fixed at 0.1.

## Evaluation on adaptability to time-varying characteristics
The change in the stiffness and damping of smart rubber is highly influenced by various environmental factors such as usage-time28, and working environment16,17. Due to these nonlinear and time-varying material properties, the system modeling of the semi-active TVA can drift unexpectedly over time. Therefore, the optimal vibration controller requires continuous adaptation to the time-varying stiffness and damping characteristics.
The experiment TVA was operated for five days under vibration disturbances and the actual frequency response function (FRF) of the TVA system were continuously recorded. It was observed that the FRF has been drifted over five days, mainly due to the time-variance in the nominal stiffness property, as shown in Fig. 7a.

Even though the TVA had a time-variant system modeling, the proposed RL algorithm could continuously track and estimate the new system modeling of FRF with high accuracy in Fig. 7a. The tracking error of the FRF in terms of R-square value was evaluated as 0.999, confirming that the proposed model-free RL can continuously adapt to the time-varying characteristics of the target system. It is noted this study assumes the rate of changes in the system’s stiffness and damping characteristics occur much more slowly than the learning convergence time, about 100 min for 4k episodes, as mentioned in “Evaluation on model-free learning model”.
Whenever there is a change in the system modeling (i.e., system FRF), the optimal control input to tune the TVA also needs to be updated accordingly. The optimal level of the magnetic field, which is the control action to tune the test TVA to minimize the vibration, is configured differently based on the system’s FRF and vibration frequencies. The action values of the proposed model, after learning the system FRF, are also plotted as circles on the same figure. It is confirmed that the proposed model also generates very close to the optimal action values of the magnetic field with a low error of 0.9936 R-square values. This result also confirmed that the proposed method has high adaptability and robustness to the time-varying characteristics of the TVA.

## Conclusion
This study presented a domain knowledge-integrated, model-free reinforcement learning (RL) control framework for a nonlinear tunable vibration absorber (TVA) subjected to nonstationary composite excitations. A robust RL-based controller was developed for a semi-active TVA exhibiting time-varying stiffness and damping characteristics, which pose significant challenges to conventional model-based controllers. Furthermore, the proposed controller was designed to adaptively suppress vibrations under nonstationary excitations with multiple frequency components, enabling versatile applicability in dynamic environments.
This proposed control algorithm, the FRF-based Q-learning controller, is a modification of the Q-learning model that incorporates frequency-domain vibration mechanics of Parseval’s theorem and frequency response functions (FRFs). The controller is trained to minimize the total vibration energy while adaptively learning the FRF of the target plant, without knowing the system modeling. In additions, an episodic learning strategy is proposed to improve the learning convergence speed and reduce computational complexity for adaptive control under dynamic environments.
Experimental results confirmed the controller’s ability to accurately track system dynamics, adapt to long-term material property drift, and optimally suppress complex multi-frequency excitations. The proposed approach exhibited control performance nearly equivalent to that of an ideal solution, the reference optimal control value. These results highlighted the potential of domain-aware RL strategies for robust, adaptive, and model-independent vibration control in complex industrial systems. Since this study assumes discrete action spaces, the proposed system can be improved further by focus on the development of control systems that incorporate continuous action spaces.
