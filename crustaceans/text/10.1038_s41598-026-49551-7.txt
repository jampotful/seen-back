# An owl-inspired temporal transformer for enhanced shrimp detection in aquatic environments

## Abstract
This study introduces NOCT-A-VIS, which stands for Nocturnal Adaptive Vision System, symbolizing a bio-inspired framework that mimics owl-like visual adaptations for detecting underwater objects in low-light environments. From the methodological point of view, the physical characteristics of owls’ vision from the NOCT-A-VIS framework are incorporated as dedicated computational modules. The proposed shrimp detection transformer analyzes light reflection to emulate the function of the Tapetum Lucidum (TL), which is a deep reflect part of retina exists in owls, improves the visual capability to view the scene and identify the various object under the poor lighting by retinal light trajectory. The TL mechanism focus on the light reflection from retina to the scene or object. The Sensory Enhancement deals with Rod-Inspired Analogous to the high sensitivity of rod cells, the algorithm incorporates a pre-processing step to enhance weak signal detection from underwater sensors must use noise filtering techniques to amplify subtle signals, mimicking the owl’s ability to capture minimal light. The visual pigment of the rod cells detects the variations of the object color segments under the water and the captured information transferred to the outer segment of the rod cells. To improve the detection rate of the object a pre-processing step called Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) is used to filter the signal noise and adopting the owl visionary technique to identify the objects in the dark mode or in the dim light conditions. The Sequential Process includes Spatial Awareness aspects deals with Large Eye Size-Inspired depends on the biological features of the owl eye and it size is larger than its head so that the perception is good in receiving light to retina for identifying the objects. The complete process working under the curated dataset which is underwater scenes. The proposed pipeline is biologically grounded in three owl-inspired mechanisms. In particular, the sensitivity of rod cells provides insights for weak signal denoising, while a retroreflective role of the tapetum lucidum breeds temporal attention mechanisms in the interest of robustifying low light feature extraction, and finally, binocular visual processing in owls informs the architecture of stereo-depth estimation modules and increases the system’s ability for wide-angle spatial awareness.

## Research novelty
The novelty of the research is a bio-inspired transformer-based underwater shrimp detection system (NOCT-A-VIS) that explicitly employs multiple analogies of owl vision and provides them as different computational modules: (i) tapetum lucidum-inspired light “recycling” operationalized in terms of a temporal attention mechanism cooption that serves to fuse cues across consecutive frames, thus stabilizing under fast changing low light conditions, (ii) rod-cell-inspired sensory enhancement realized through empirical mode decomposition (EMD) denoising process with weak non-ignoring underwater signals taken into consideration, for high frequency noise reduction, (iii) owl-like binocular depth perception with stereo-matching disparity-based 3D depth reconstruction and improved localization in turbid scenes, and (iv) large-eye spatial awareness translated into wide-angle (≈ 270°) multi-scale tiling and feature integration to expand context coverage beyond typical underwater detector setups. Critically, the contribution of this study is the system-level integration of these biologically grounded modules set in a unified “shrimp detection transformer” designed for real-time, low-illumination, dynamic, noisy, aquatic environments and supporting the claim with comparative and ablations-style evidence attributing gains to each module instead of simply suggesting another variance.

## EMD-based signal decomposition
The visual pigment of the rod cells detects the variations of the object color segments under the water and the captured information transferred to the outer segment of the rod cells. To improve the detection rate of the object a preprocessing step called Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) is used to filter the signal noise and adopting the owl visionary technique to identify the objects in the dark mode or in the dim light conditions. The main use of the Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) is for noise filtering. The EMD ability to separate noise, as high-frequency Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs) which is regularly characterize the noise and later discarded. After reconstruction of the signal on utilizing the valid IMFs (omitting non-factual IMFs) in association with the essential residuals, actual signal components are preserved. Input is given as the noisy signal defined it as Ï(t) depicted in Eq. (1) and the output is generated as Ready-to-process signal. To ensure the signal is sampled and formatted appropriately for processing to Identify Local Extrema, is used to identifying all local maxima and minima of Ï(t).1
By finding the nodes (the points where the first derivative of the noisy signal is zero), the local maxima and minima are detected, where the difference between maxima and minima is then determined by the sign of the second derivative. The identified extrema are then used during the construction of the upper and lower envelopes in the Empirical Mode Decomposition sifting procedure.

## Results and discussion
A few recent and relevant existing studies are chosen for comparative evaluations. YOLOv7 is considered because of its real-time processing and the ability to detect various-sized objects in challenging underwater situations. It provides high precision with low computational overhead, making it appropriate for assessing performance in low-light conditions similar to NOCT-A-VIS’ scope. With IF-RCNN, accurate object localization in complex underwater scenes is achieved as a result of optimized anchor box design (K-Means++) and region-based proposal mechanisms. It is as used with NOCT-A-VIS because it favors a detailed recognition especially in scenes with many and differently sized objects, in order to reproduce the owl-like spatial resolution of NOCT-A-VIS. VBOD is based on shape-based detection with stereo and mono pose estimation on MARIS and TRIDENT datasets, showing real underwater scenes. Compared to NOCT-A-VIS, it distinguishes itself by placing emphasis on pose recognition, localized features, and robustness in the noisy underwater environment. BI- RL optimizes for real-time decision making and energy conservation in aquatic agents by integrating bio-inspired principles with learning mechanisms. It aligns with NOCT-A-VIS, where owl-inspired vision and temporal attention are integrated, showcasing its biologically inspired strategy of task learning and object tracking. E-YOLOv5 uses special preprocessing approaches and a hybrid data augmentation approach governed in order to enhance the detection of hidden and poorly illuminated underwater objects. By enhancing generalization and recognizing subtle features, E-YOLOv5 equates to the rod-inspired sense enhancement and analysis of how light reflects on the surface found in NOCT-A-VIS.

The limited utilization of those advanced denoisers and dependence on stable environmental scenarios compromise the performance of BI-RL (−2.5%) and VBOD (−4.3%).

## Conclusion and future enhancement
NOCT-A-VIS is an owl-inspired bio-inspired vision transformer for advanced underwater shrimp detection. It combines rod- and cell-based empirical mode decomposition and temporal attention to extract light, similar to rod- and cell-based attenuation, tapetum lucidum-inspired sight, to enhance the signal with dynamic light correction, binocular stereo-matching, and combine depth estimation, and wide-angle multi-scale tiling for spatial awareness. A comparison with state-of-the-art models (YOLOv7, E-YOLOv5, VBOD, IF-RCNN, and BI-RL) demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed approach, signifying a 9.2% increase in detection accuracy in low light, 7.8% improvement in noise reduction, 6.4% improvement in depth estimation accuracy, and 8.6% increase in object detection stability under dynamic conditions. Unlike standard Yolo or CNNs variants, limited by narrow sighting ranges, inferior denoising, or loss of temporal memory, NOCT-A-VIS emulates the sensing abilities of an owl to guarantee strong, real-time-path detection under fluctuating conditions of haziness, lighting, or image blurring. By combining biological sensory analogies and transformer-based attention mechanisms, the system addresses key limitations in the detection of underwater objects and hence establishes itself as a multi-functional platform for ecological monitoring applications, aquaculture bio-surveillance, and autonomous marine robotics. NOCT-A-VIS shall be validated in real ocean conditions departing from a laboratory environment. By combining it with autonomous underwater vehicles, the system will enable wide-area, long-term monitoring of shrimp and marine life. Finally, the system will be optimized regarding the use of lightweight hardware accelerators and deployment in real-time in resource-scarce marine environments.
