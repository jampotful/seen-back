Amacrine cell inputs to OFF midget ganglion cells in macaque retina.

# Amacrine cell inputs to OFF midget ganglion cells in macaque retina

## Abstract
In primates, the OFF midget retinal ganglion cells (OFF mRGCs) have a high spatial density and small dendritic arbors. Their axons provide input to the parvocellular pathway mediating both colour vision and the highest-acuity spatial vision. This study aimed to understand the basis for their light responses by identifying the presynaptic amacrine and bipolar cells. Retinal tissue from an adult macaque was processed for serial block-face scanning electron microscopy, and a volume of images of the inner retina located 2 mm temporal to the centre of the fovea was analysed. Ten OFF mRGCs and many of their presynaptic cells were reconstructed. Both midget and diffuse types of bipolar cells provided excitatory, glutamatergic input. Axons and long dendrites of wide-field amacrine cells made synapses, and we propose that these mediate tonic, GABAergic inhibition. Narrow-field amacrine cells also made synapses onto the OFF mRGCs, and we propose that most of them are glycinergic, inhibitory synapses. One presynaptic narrow-field amacrine cell was the knotty bistratified type 1 (KB1), which contains immunoreactive glycine and vesicular glutamate transporter 3. We propose that they enlarge the receptive field centers of OFF mRGCs via direct, excitatory synapses. The KB1 cell studied most extensively was presynaptic to some of the same types of amacrine cells that made inhibitory synapses onto OFF mRGCs. We propose that the knotty bistratifed type 1cells release glycine at those synapses and disinhibit responses of OFF mRGCs.

## Introduction
Ganglion cells are the projection neurons of the retina, and there are multiple types that differ in their morphology, light responses and central projections. In primates, midget retinal ganglion cells (mRGCs) have the highest spatial density, and there are two types. The ON type ramifies in the inner half of the inner plexiform layer (IPL) and depolarises in response to increments in light intensity. The OFF type ramifies in the outer half of the IPL and is depolarised by decrements in light intensity. This difference originates in the outer plexiform layer, where cones make two different types of synapses onto ON and OFF bipolar cells, one excitatory and one inhibitory. These bipolar cells make excitatory synapses only onto midget ganglion cells with the same response polarity.
Both types have somas in the ganglion cell layer (GCL) and project to the parvocellular layers of the dorsal lateral geniculate nucleus (reviewed by Kolb & Marshak, 2003). The dendritic arbours of both ON and OFF mRGCs are smaller than those of other types of retinal ganglion cells, and they do not overlap with those of their neighbours. Their dendritic arbours become larger with distance from the fovea, but the dendritic arbours of the OFF type are consistently smaller than those of the ON type (Dacey, 1993). The spatial density of OFF midget ganglion cells in the central retina accounts for the human visual acuity function in bright light (Masri et al., 2020).
The light responses of OFF mRGCs have been studied extensively in macaque retinas. Central and some peripheral OFF mRGCs have long been known to respond with opposite polarity to long and short wavelength stimuli mediated by their input from long-wavelength-sensitive (L) and medium-wavelength-sensitive (M) cones via midget bipolar cells (Diller et al., 2004; Martin et al., 2001; Wool et al., 2018). Later, OFF mRGCs with OFF responses to short-wavelength-sensitive (S) cones were identified, first in macaques (Field et al., 2010; Klug et al., 2003; Patterson et al., 2019; Tsukamoto & Omi, 2016; Wool et al., 2019) and later in humans (Kim et al., 2024). Although individual OFF mRGCs are insensitive to motion (Manookin et al., 2018), macaques can utilise their signals to detect motion (Merigan et al., 1991).
Some of the neurons presynaptic to OFF mRGCs have been identified (reviewed by Grünert & Martin, 2021). These include bipolar cells, local circuit neurons with somas in the inner nuclear layer (INL) whose dendrites receive synapses from photoreceptors in the outer plexiform layer (OPL) and send axons to the IPL. OFF mRGCs were once thought to receive input exclusively from midget bipolar cells, which receive input from single cones in the central retina. But OFF mRGCs also receive input from diffuse bipolar cells, which receive input from multiple cones (Tsukamoto & Omi 2015). This study of 10 central OFF mRGCs from a macaque retina using serial block-face scanning electron microscopy (SBFSEM) confirmed that finding.
A large proportion of the input to OFF mRGCs comes from amacrine cells, local circuit neurons with somas in the INL or the GCL and dendrites in the IPL (Kolb & Marshak, 2003). However, many of the presynaptic amacrine cells have not yet been identified, and that was another goal of this study. Two physiological studies of macaque OFF mRGCs that appeared to contradict what was known about their anatomy were of particular interest. The first showed that parafoveal OFF mRGCs had no light-driven inhibitory currents (Sinha et al., 2017), although they received many synapses from amacrine cells (Calkins & Sterling, 1996). The second was a study of signal redundancy in macaque retinal ganglion cells using multielectrode arrays. The receptive field centres of more than 3000 midget and parasol ganglion cells were modelled as elliptical Gaussian functions, and the results were analysed by plotting the ellipses representing one standard deviation from the centre. The ellipses of both types of ganglion cells abutted one another, a finding indicating that receptive field centers of both types had the same degree of overlap, and the authors proposed that this overlap enabled both midget and parasol cells to sample visual space efficiently (Gauthier et al., 2009). However, it was unclear why parasol cells, whose dendrites overlap extensively, had the same degree of receptive field centre overlap as midget ganglion cells, whose dendrites do not overlap (Dacey, 1993). Our study suggests that excitatory input from one subtype of amacrine cells accounts for both of these findings.

## Methods

## Ethical approval
The retinal tissue was obtained through the Tissue Distribution Program at the Washington National Primate Research Centre (WaNPRC). The specimen came from an adult male Macaca nemestrina (animal ID K06006) euthanised on 20 August 2015 under the approved WaNPRC IACUC protocol Tissue Banking and Distribution Program (#4277-01). According to the clinical record, the animal was deeply anaesthetised with ketamine HCl (180 mg IM) and subsequently euthanised with Beuthanasia-D (1950 mg IV) and pentobarbital (Nembutal, 350 mg IV) administered by WaNPRC veterinary staff. Euthanasia was performed in accordance with AVMA Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals and institutional IACUC standards. The animal was born and provided shelter, food and water at the WaNPRC. He had participated in approved research protocols involving hematopoietic and tissue collection procedures (e.g., Gene Transfer into Hematopoietic Stem Cells, #3235-01; Research Specimen Collection, #4202-03). These prior studies did not involve ocular experiments, and the eyes were intact and unmanipulated at the time of euthanasia. All procedures conformed to the U.S. Animal Welfare Act, the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, and WaNPRC IACUC regulations.
Central retinal tissue was processed for SBFSEM using a protocol described in detail previously (Patterson et al., 2019). Tissue taken from 2 mm temporal to the centre of the fovea was fixed in glutaraldehyde, stained en bloc with osmium ferrocyanide, uranyl acetate and lead aspartate. After embedding in epoxy resin, it was sectioned in the horizontal plane, and images were acquired at a resolution of 7.5 nm/pixel using a Zeiss Sigma VP field emission scanning electron microscope equipped with a 3View system (Gatan, Inc.).
A retinal volume approximately 200 μm on each side was studied. It contained 937 sections, 70 nm each, spanning from the GCL to the INL. This volume was also used in recent studies of ON parasol ganglion cells, broad thorny ganglion cells, intrinsically photosensitive ganglion cells and microglial cells (Bordt et al., 2021, 2022; Goyal et al., 2023; Patterson et al., 2020). Image registration was performed using Nornir (http://nornir.github.io RRID:SCR_003584). The image tiles were reassembled into a cohesive digital volume and hosted on a server at the University of Washington.
The volume was annotated using the web-based, multiuser Viking software environment described previously (Anderson et al., 2011; http://connectomes.utah.edu RRID:SCR_0 05986). Profiles of processes were annotated by placing circular discs with the same diameter at their centers of mass and linking them to annotations on adjacent sections. Dendrites and axons can be rendered quite accurately using circles, but the renders of irregularly-shaped somas are only approximations. Because the volume is close to the centre of the fovea, the sizes of some types of neurons vary considerably within the volume, and therefore the term ‘relatively’ is used when comparing somas to those of neighboring cells. Synapses were annotated as lines.
The cell types ramifying in the IPL were reconstructed and identified using ultrastructural and morphological criteria (Dowling & Boycott, 1966; Grünert & Martin, 2020). Axon terminals of bipolar cells contained numerous synaptic vesicles and synaptic ribbons. There were some synapses where the bipolar cells were clearly presynaptic but no ribbons were present, as described previously in human retinas (Kolb & DeKorver, 1991). The bipolar cells were classified based on the morphology of their axon terminals (Tsukamoto & Omi, 2015, 2016).
Axons and dendrites of amacrine cells typically contained fewer synaptic vesicles that were clustered at synapses. Based on single-cell transcriptomics, there are at least 25 distinct types of amacrine cells in primates (Yan et al., 2020). Even more diversity was revealed using the Golgi method; 26 and 27 types have been identified in macaque and human retinas, respectively (Kolb et al., 1992; Mariani, 1990). They were divided into narrow- and wide-field types based on the lateral extent of their dendrites. The largest narrow-field amacrine cell in the macaque retina, described by Mariani (1990) is the wavy multistratified type 2. In the volume studied here, the diameter of that cell’s dendritic arbour was 130 μm (not illustrated), and amacrine cells with larger arbours than this were classified as wide-field. Some of the wide-field amacrine cell types also had axons that ramified within the IPL.
Amacrine cell dendritic arbours were characterized as narrowly or broadly stratified based on the percentage of the IPL depth that they occupied. The boundary between the INL and the IPL was designated as 0% depth and the IPL-GCL boundary as 100%. For some descriptions, the IPL was divided into five strata, each 20% in depth, with stratum one being outermost (sclerad) and stratum five being innermost (vitread).
Many of the presynaptic amacrine cells could not be reconstructed sufficiently to be identified. A few of the unidentified amacrine cell processes were unbranched and unistratified; these were probably axons or dendrites of wide-field amacrine cells that fell short of our 100 μm criterion length. Most of the unidentified amacrine cell processes were more convoluted and ramified in more than one IPL stratum, and these were probably dendrites of narrow-field amacrine cells that could not be traced to a soma in the INL. Most retinal ganglion cells and all of the OFF mRGCs had somas in the GCL and varicose axons; their dendrites in the IPL were exclusively postsynaptic.
Data analysis and 3D rendering were performed using an open-source Matlab program SBFSEM-tools (Mathworks, RRID: SCR_0 01622) program https://github.com/neitzlab/sbfsem-tools, RRID: SCR_01 7350. Image rendering was performed using the RenderApp function. Synapses were rendered as unit spheres centred at each synapse annotation’s X, Y and Z coordinates using the synapseSphere function and then scaled to optimize visibility. Processes of amacrine cells, dendrites of ganglion cells and axon terminals of bipolar cells were analysed using the IPLDepth function. Figures were prepared using Adobe Illustrator and SBFSEM tools.

## Results
The 10 OFF mRGCs in the sample had somas deep in the GCL and thick primary dendrites. Their axons either arose from the primary dendrites and ran along the boundary between the IPL and GCL or else descended from the somas toward the optic fibre layer. The dendrites ascended to the outer half of the IPL, where they branched extensively and formed a compact cluster of terminals (Fig. 1). The median stratification depth is shown in Fig. 2. The distal dendrites were found in strata 1 and 2 of the IPL. Some of the variability is likely due to the curvature of the IPL boundaries (Fig. 1B). Only a few synapses were located on the primary dendrites, and all were from amacrine cells (Fig. 3). The vast majority of the synapses were located on higher-order dendrites.

## Inputs from bipolar cells
There were 475 synapses from bipolar cells onto the 10 OFF mRGCs, 33% (475/1429) of the total input. Twenty fragments of presynaptic cells could be classified as originating from bipolar cells by ultrastructural criteria, but they could not be reconstructed sufficiently to identify the type. There were 370 synapses from identified OFF midget bipolar cells onto the 10 OFF mRGCs, 81.3% (370/455) of the identified bipolar cell synapses; the rest were from OFF diffuse bipolar cells. DB1 bipolar cells provided 23.5% (20/85) of the inputs from identified diffuse bipolar cells; the rest were from DB2, DB3a and DB3b types, considered together here. A DB1 bipolar cell and one of its synapses with an OFF mRGC are illustrated in Fig. 4. The data underlying these findings are in Table 1.

## Inputs from amacrine cells
The other 954 synapses onto the 10 OFF mRGCs, comprising 67% (954/1429) of the input, were from various types of amacrine cells. Many of the presynaptic amacrine cells could not be identified. A few of the unidentified amacrine cell processes were unbranched and unistratified; these were probably axons or dendrites of wide-field amacrine cells that fell short of our 100 μm criterion length.
Most of the unidentified amacrine cell processes were more convoluted and ramified in more than one IPL stratum, and these were probably dendrites of narrow-field amacrine cells that could not be traced to a soma in the INL.

## Inputs from wide-field amacrine cells
Processes of wide-field amacrine cells made a large contribution (Table 2). These were either axons or very long dendrites of amacrine cells that usually could not be traced to somas in the volume. These processes were relatively straight, branched infrequently if at all, and were typically varicose (Fig. 5). Many of the presynaptic wide-field amacrine cell processes ran in only one of the outermost two strata of the IPL.
In a few instances, processes of presynaptic wide-field amacrine cells could be traced to somas in the INL (Fig. 6). Three wiry type 1 amacrine cells were identified by their relatively small somas and thin, varicose dendrites that ran in the outermost strata of the IPL. They branched infrequently at acute angles, as described in macaque retina previously using the Golgi method (Mariani 1990; Majumdar et al, 2008),
Another presynaptic amacrine cell had a larger soma (Fig. 6). It had dendrites with a larger diameter that ran in the outermost strata of the IPL and gave rise to thinner, varicose axons at their tips. It was classified as a semilunar type 1 amacrine cell based on its similarity to those identified in macaque and human retinas using the Golgi method (Mariani, 1990; Kolb et al., 1992).
Two other types of presynaptic wide-field amacrine cells had more broadly-stratified dendritic arbours. One type of presynaptic amacrine cell was identified by its very fine dendrites, each with a few relatively large varicosities (Kolb et al., 1992; Mariani, 1990). Based on its morphology and its input from a rod bipolar cell, we classified this as an A17 cell. However, the expected continuation of the dendrites in stratum 5 was not apparent because the cells could not be completely reconstructed. These cells are also known as spidery (Mariani, 1990) and stratified diffuse (Boycott & Dowling, 1969). Their somas were in the INL, and the dendrites ramified throughout the IPL (Fig. 7). We could not distinguish the two subtypes that have been described previously (Boycott & Dowling, 1969; Long et al., 2016). An A13 amacrine cell (Fig. 8) was identified by its long, slightly varicose dendrites that gradually descended to stratum 4 of the IPL (Mariani 1990; Kolb et al., 1992). Together, the wide-field cells contributed approximately half of the amacrine cell inputs to the 10 OFF mRGCs that could be clearly identified, 52.3±10.3% (Table 2).

## Inputs from narrow-field amacrine cells
Twelve narrow-field amacrine cells that resembled human A4 cells provided the most synaptic input of any identified type of amacrine cell to the OFF mRGCs (Fig. 9). As described previously using the Golgi method (Kolb et al., 1992), these had small somas in the INL and small dendritic arbors. Their convoluted dendrites formed a broad plexus in strata two, three and four of the IPL.
Four amacrine cells presynaptic to the 10 OFF mRGCs were identified as the AII type by their lobular appendages in stratum 1 and dendrites that ramified throughout the rest of the IPL (Mariani, 1990; Kolb et al., 1992; Wässle et al., 1995; Mills & Massey, 1999). An example is shown in Fig. 10. In two instances, the dendrites of the OFF midget ganglion cells received inputs from the innermost parts of the lobular appendages in stratum 1. The remainder of the synapses from AII cells were made by other parts of the dendrites.
Three presynaptic A8 amacrine cells were identified by their relatively large somas and dendritic arbours and by their distinctive, lobular dendrites in the inner IPL; one is shown in Fig. 11. Their dendrites were very broadly stratified and occupied 90% of the IPL depth (Kolb et al., 1992; Neumann & Haverkamp, 2013 Strettoi et al., 2018).

## Inputs from knotty bistratified type 1 amacrine cells
Another type of presynaptic narrow-field amacrine cell closely resembled the knotty bistratified type 1 (KB1) amacrine cells described previously in the macaque retina using the Golgi method (Mariani, 1990). It also resembled the amacrine cells in baboon retina that contain immunoreactive vesicular glutamate transporter 3 (vGluT3) and frequently make synapses onto OFF mRGCs (Marshak et al., 2015). The cell is located near a large blood vessel, which distorted the local boundaries of the IPL (Fig. 12). In subsequent figures, cell 43,016 and the other cells are shown as they would appear without the blood vessel. Its convoluted dendrites formed two dense, relatively broad plexuses on either side of the centre of the IPL (Fig. 13). Because the inputs from these dual-neurotransmitter interneurons to OFF mRGCs were of particular interest, one of these cells and many of its pre- and postsynaptic cells were reconstructed completely.
KB1 cell 43,016 had approximately equal numbers of synaptic inputs and outputs. It made 259 synapses in all. Only 8.5% (22/259) of the synapses were onto bipolar cells. The remainder of the synapses were made onto other amacrine cells, 42.8% (111/259), and ganglion cells, 48.6% (126/259). The KB1 cell received 277 synapses in all, mainly from amacrine cells. Only 12.6% (35/277) of these synapses were from bipolar cells, and these were typically located on distal KB1 dendrites (Fig. 14).
The reconstructed KB1 cell made synapses onto nine OFF mRGCs, five from the 10 studied most extensively and four others (Fig. 15). The OFF mRGC ramifying in the centre of the amacrine cell’s dendritic field received 17 synapses, its immediate neighbours received seven synapses each, and the rest received two or three each. Assuming that KB1 cells have the same degree of dendritic overlap as vGluT3 cells in mice (Keeley et al., 2021), four neighboring KB1 cells would also be expected to make synapses onto each of these OFF mRGCs. As would be expected from the stratification of the KB1 cell dendrites, it made only two synapses onto ON mRGC dendrites (not illustrated).
Many of the amacrine cell processes presynaptic to the KB1 cell were classified as either dendrites or axons of wide-field amacrine cells by the criteria described above Fig. 16. These processes were almost entirely pre-synaptic. In 33 instances, these processes of wide-field amacrine cells made a synapse onto dendrites of the 10 OFF mRGCs that also received synapses from the KB1 cell (Table 3).
Wide-field amacrine cells (WFACs) that were pre- synaptic to KB1 cell 43,016 were also presynaptic to eight of the 10 OFF midget ganglion cells (mRGCs) in this study at 33 synapses. Two additional, partially-annotated OFF mRGCs received three synapses from WFACs that were also presynaptic to 43,016.
A few wide-field amacrine cells that interacted with the KB1 cell could be traced to somas in the volume to facilitate identification. The KB1 cell interacted with four A13 amacrine cells, identified as described above, Fig. 17. One other reconstructed wide-field amacrine cell made two synapses onto the KB1 cell (not illustrated). Based on the gently curving dendrites that formed a broad arbour centred at 40% depth in the IPL, it was identified as the stellate wavy type (Mariani, 1990). Other wide-field amacrine cells that interacted with the KB1 cell were also reconstructed, but they did not resemble any of the types described previously (not illustrated).
Two types of narrow-field amacrine cells interacting with the KB1 cell were identified. AII amacrine cells, identified as described above, made synapses onto the KB1 cell and received synapses from it (not illustrated). A knotty type 2 cell was identified by its delicate, highly convoluted dendrites that co-stratified with those of the KB1 cell (Mariani, 1990; Klump et al., 2009). This narrow-field amacrine cell also made synapses onto the KB1 cell (not illustrated).
Many of the bipolar cells pre- and postsynaptic to the KB1 cell were identified by the morphology of their axon terminals (Tsukamoto & Omi, 2015 & 2016; Grünert & Martin, 2020) and are summarised in Table 4. They included almost every type of cone bipolar cell except diffuse bipolar (DB) type DB1 and OFF midget ganglion cells, whose axon terminals did not overlap with distal dendrites of the KB1 cell, and the giant bipolar cells, which have a very low spatial density. It was not always possible to unequivocally identify DB2 and DB3b bipolar cells in this study. As Tsukamoto and Omi pointed out in 2015, these were difficult to distinguish even with higher-resolution, transmission EM. Fig. 18 shows examples of presynaptic diffuse bipolar cells and synapses to and from the KB1 cell.

## Discussion

## Synaptic connections of a KB1 amacrine cell
The KB1 amacrine cell had a unique pattern of synaptic connections. Our findings are compared with those of the relatively few types of primate amacrine cells that have been studied by electron microscopy to date in Table 5. Like the cells in the baboon retina containing immunoreactive vGluT3 (Marshak et al., 2015), the KB1 cell made approximately half of its synapses onto retinal ganglion cells. Only one other type, the starburst amacrine cell, devotes such a large percentage of its output to retinal ganglion cells (Mariani & Hersh, 1988; Yamada et al., 2003). The ganglion cells postsynaptic to starburst cells are the rare, wide-field ganglion cells with recursive dendrites (Patterson et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023). Like many other types of amacrine cells, a major target of the KB1 cell was other amacrine cells. Only 9% of the output of the KB1 cell was directed to bipolar cells, a smaller proportion than any of the other narrow-field amacrine cell types studied previously in primates.
Narrow-field amacrine cells in the macaque retina typically receive a large, excitatory input from bipolar cells, but only 13% of the synapses onto the KB1 cell were from bipolar cells. The majority of the inputs were from other amacrine cells, and this is consistent with the finding that inhibitory input predominates during light-evoked responses of vGluT3 cells in mouse retina (Grimes et al., 2011). These inhibitory inputs to the dendrites of the KB1 cell might also enable the two dendritic arbours to operate independently, as they do in vGluT3 cells of mice (Chen et al., 2017; Hsiang et al., 2017). There may even be regions of each dendritic arbour that respond independently, as they do in response to flashing bar stimuli in the mouse retina (Friedrechsen et al., 2024).
In several respects, the synaptic connections of the KB1 cell were similar to those of vGluT3 cells in the mouse retina. The majority of their synaptic interactions were with other amacrine cells, and both types directed a large proportion of their output to retinal ganglion cells: 51% for the completely reconstructed KB1 cell, and 43% for the vGluT3 cells in the mouse (Mani et al., 2023; Friedrichsen et al., 2024). The reconstructed KB1 cell made synapses onto midget ganglion cells, and vGluT3 cells in the mouse retina also made synapses onto the homologs of OFF mRGCs, sustained OFF alpha cells, albeit much less frequently (Mani et al., 2023; Hahn et al., 2023). Recently, another group found none at all (Friedrichsen et al., 2024).
The synaptic inputs to the vGluT3 cell in the mouse and the KB1 cell in the macaque were also similar. Both types of cells received relatively few inputs from bipolar cells, 9% for the KB1 cells in macaque retina and 26% for vGluT3 cells in mouse retina, and these were typically found on distal dendrites in both species. Like KB1 cells in macaque retina, the mouse vGluT3 cells interacted with many types of bipolar cells whose axons costratifed with their dendrites. DB3a bipolar cells were amongst those presynaptic to the KB1 cell, and their homologs, fast type 3a bipolar cells, provided excitatory input to vGluT3 cells in mice (Friedrichsen et al., 2024).
Wide-field amacrine cells would be expected to provide tonic inhibition to KB1 cells from stimuli outside the classical receptive fields, as they do in mice (Kim et al., 2015). In many instances, the reconstructed KB1 cell and the postsynaptic ganglion cells received input from the same wide-field amacrine cells. Assuming that these two amacrine cell inputs to ganglion cells act synergistically, these findings suggest that the synapses from the KB1 cells to the ganglion cells are excitatory.

## Inhibitory inputs to OFF mRGCs
The axons and dendrites of wide-field amacrine cells provided input to the OFF mRGCs. These GABAergic amacrine cells are particularly well-suited to provide tonic inhibitory input because their somas are located at varying distances from the OFF mRGCs and their conduction velocities are relatively slow (Manookin et al., 2015; Greschner et al., 2016). OFF mRGCs outside the parafovea express GABAA receptors (Grünert et al., 1993), and wide-field amacrine cells are likely to mediate the inhibition by full-field stimulation observed in OFF mRGCs there (Sinha et al., 2017).
Wiry type 1 amacrine cells that ramify in the outer IPL have depolarising responses to decrements in light intensity in their large, narrow receptive field centers that are co-extensive with their dendrites, but they have ON-OFF responses to moving stimuli (Manookin et al., 2015). Wiry Type 1 cells would be expected to inhibit the OFF mRGCs in response to those stimuli. The light responses of A13 amacrine cells of macaques have not been reported. In cats, they have hyperpolarising responses to light stimuli and make GABAergic synapses onto OFF beta ganglion cells, the likely homologs of OFF mRGCs (Kolb & Nelson, 1996).
Glycinergic narrow-field amacrine cells also provided a major input to OFF mRGCs, which express alpha 1 glycine receptors macaque retina (Grünert & Ghosh, 1999). A8 amacrine cells in macaque retina express immunoreactive glycine transporter 1 (Neumann & Haverkamp, 2013), and they are expected to provide glycinergic inhibition to OFF mRGCs. Assuming that the presynaptic A4 narrow-field amacrine cells are glycinergic, as they are in other mammals (Menger et al., 1998), they would also be expected to inhibit the OFF mRGCs. In 2015, we proposed that A4 cells were the human homologs of vGluT3 cells in baboons, but we now believe that they are distinct types (Marshak et al., 2015).
Some of the unidentified postsynaptic processes to immunolabeled macaque AII cells were proposed to be from ganglion cell dendrites (Wässle et al. 1995), and we confirmed that finding. Synapses from AII amacrine cells to OFF mRGCs would also be inhibitory because AII cells contain immunoreactive glycine in the macaque retina (Kolb et al., 2002). If their light responses are similar to those of other mammals, AII amacrine cells would be expected to provide crossover inhibition to OFF mRGCs (reviewed by Demb & Singer, 2012).

## Excitatory inputs to OFF mRGCs
The excitatory synapses from midget bipolar cells onto midget ganglion cells have been studied extensively (reviewed by Grünert & Martin, 2021). We confirmed the results of a previous study that found excitatory input to OFF mRGCs from diffuse bipolar cells, including the DB1 type (Tsukamoto & Omi, 2015). Both OFF midget and DB1 bipolar cells receive major inputs from AII amacrine cells, and they would convey input from the primary rod pathway to OFF mRGCs (McLaughlin et al., 2021). These synapses from bipolar cells would generate the excitatory postsynaptic currents that have been recorded from OFF mRGCs in macaque retina (Sinha et al., 2017).
Several lines of evidence suggest that KB1 cells also provide excitatory input to OFF mRGCs via glutamatergic synapses. They are morphologically similar to the cells that contain immunoreactive vGluT3 in the baboon retina, and they have a very similar pattern of synaptic connections (Marshak et al., 2015). Some types of amacrine cells are highly specialised, interacting repeatedly with the same types of other retinal neurons (Diamond, 2017; Franke & Baden, 2017). The opposite was true for the reconstructed KB1 cell. Like cells containing immunoreactive vGluT3, it had synaptic interactions with many types of bipolar cell axons, nearly all the types that terminated within its dendritic field. It also interacted with two other types of narrow-field amacrine cells and several types of wide-field amacrine cells.
OFF mRGCs are amongst the ganglion cell types that were identified as postsynaptic to vGluT3 cells by intracellular tracer injection and light microscopic immunolabelling (Marshak et al., 2015). The other evidence for excitatory synapses from KB1 cells to OFF mRGCs is indirect. A subset of wide-field amacrine cells presynaptic to the KB1 cell also made synapses onto the wide-field amacrine cells presynaptic to the OFF mRGCs. Parafoveal OFF mRGCs receive many synapses from amacrine cells with somas in the INL (Calkins & Sterling, 1996), but no light-driven inhibitory currents are recorded in response to light stimuli in those cells (Sinha et al., 2017). Taken together, these findings suggest that KB1 cells provide excitatory, glutamatergic input to parafoveal OFF mRGCs. If so, central OFF mRGCs would be particularly vulnerable to excitotoxic damage. One caveat is that the results reported previously were from the parafovea, but this study was done using a volume 2 mm from the centre of the fovea. Future studies using a volume from the parafovea would be required to test this hypothesis more rigorously.
There is also support for glutamatergic synapses from KB1 cells onto ganglion cells from studies in the mouse retina. Transient OFF alpha cells of mice (Hahn et al., 2023) receive a considerable amount of synaptic input from vGluT3 cells (Mani et al., 2023), and glutamate is the neurotransmitter at synapses from mouse vGluT3 cells to transient OFF alpha ganglion cells (Krishnaswamy et al., 2015; Kim et al., 2015; Lee et al., 2016).
Synapses from KB1 cells might provide an explanation for the unexpectedly large sizes of OFF mRGC receptive field centres in the peripheral macaque retina (Gauthier et al. 2009). The KB1 amacrine cell and the OFF mRGC that receives its largest number of synapses are illustrated in Fig. 19. Because the dendritic arbour of the KB1 cell is much larger than that of the OFF mRGC, the receptive field centre of the postsynaptic OFF mRGC would be enlarged considerably by excitatory input from the KB1 cell. Because the dendritic field of the OFF parasol cell that receives the most input from the KB1 cell (not illustrated) is large enough to receive input from all five of the KB1 cells predicted to be within its dendritic arbor, KB1 cells would not affect the size of its receptive field centre.

## Conclusions
OFF midget retinal ganglion cells of primates are of particular interest because they have the highest spatial density and provide the input to neurons in the brain that mediate high-acuity visual information processing and colour vision. The prevailing view is that responses of these cells to decrement in light intensity are mediated primarily by excitatory inputs from OFF bipolar cells. This anatomical study of 10 OFF midget retinal ganglion cells confirmed that both midget and diffuse bipolar cells are presynaptic to OFF midget ganglion cells, a finding that may account for the unexpectedly large sizes of their receptive field centers, at least in part.
The majority of the synaptic inputs to OFF midget ganglion cells were from amacrine cells. Nearly all amacrine cells use inhibitory neurotransmitters, but his study provides evidence that one type of amacrine cell, the knotty bistratified type 1, made excitatory synapses onto OFF midget ganglion cells. We proposed that these synapses are excitatory based on the finding that the same wide-field amacrine cells frequently made synapses onto both types of cells, and they are expected to act synergistically. If so, this would explain two surprising findings about OFF mRGCs reported previously. In the parafovea, the OFF mRGCs receive many synapses from amacrine cells, but they do not have light-driven inhibitory postsynaptic currents. In the peripheral retina, OFF mRGCs have larger receptive field centre sizes than would be expected from their relatively small dendritic arbors. Both findings are consistent with excitatory input from narrow-field amacrine cells to OFF mRGCs.
These dual-neurotransmitter amacrine cells also made synapses onto some of the other types of amacrine cells that are presynaptic to OFF midget ganglion cells, and we propose that these synapses are glycinergic. Acting simultaneously, these two sets of synapses would be particularly well-suited to facilitate responses of OFF midget retinal ganglion cells to dark stimuli. If confirmed by physiological studies, this would provide an example to guide future studies of dual-transmitter interneurons elsewhere in the central nervous system.
